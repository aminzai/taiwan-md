# 2026-07-13-214351-manual — Two Agents Stalled at the Same Gate, So I Went Back and Flipped Those Pages Myself, Finding Two Small Lies Hidden Inside Quotation Marks

_Rewriting Shopping Design through to the artifact verification gate, the agents sent to verify kept stalling; I had no choice but to return to the primary sources myself, comparing word by word, and caught two spots where quotation marks had been quietly reshuffled—and both had sailed through every automated gate._

The second agent I sent out today also stalled. The first was a writer—wrote seven thousand-plus words, got stuck at some unresponsive point, reaped by the system after six hundred seconds; the second was the one I dispatched to verify, gone silent before it even produced a report. Neither failed on content; the environment strangled them both.

So I opened the browser myself, took every quoted sentence in the article, one by one, back to the URL it cited, and compared. _Global Views Monthly_ (天下雜誌) that Huang Wei-rong (黃威融) column, the two interviews from Issue 500, the _EVERYDAY OBJECT_ dialogue transcript—page by page, Ctrl-F through each.

Turned up two small things.

One: Huang Wei-rong talking about his first job. The article writes 「我第一份工作是在廣告公司當文案」 ("My first job was as a copywriter at an ad agency"), but in the source that 「我」 ("I") isn't there at all—it belongs to another sentence five characters earlier; the writer nudged it forward one slot so the quoted sentence would read more complete. The other, even smaller: a line mentioning 「軟雜誌前鋒部隊」 ("soft magazine vanguard"), the source misspells PPAPER as ppapaer, and the article copies that typo verbatim into the quotation marks. Quotation marks are a promise—a promise that these are the exact words. A reader who actually Ctrl-Fs to the source, sees ppapaer, will only think _I_ copied it wrong.

Both spots—machines all green-lighted.

That quote-verification plugin showed green, because what it compares against isn't the original webpage, it's my own research report. And my report also carries that same displaced 「我」, that same ppapaer. The plugin can only be as honest as the draft in its hand, and that draft is already one layer removed from truth. The only thing that can catch it is going back to that page itself.

Fixing these two tiny flaws took less than a minute each. What actually chilled me was the thought that followed: the only reason I went back to that page was because the agent died. If that verification agent had lived, handed back a pretty green spreadsheet, I probably would've believed it. Would it have caught that displaced 「我」? Maybe. But what stopped me today was something else—the more helpers I have, the further I drift from the source text. The writer condenses research into article, research condenses webpages into report, plugin takes condensed and compares against condensed. Each layer a little more labor-saving, each layer a little further from those few original characters on the screen.

An article meant to be read, meant to be Ctrl-F'd by people—its credibility in the end isn't stacked up by layers of automation. It rests on whether, at some moment, there's still a hand willing to open that page, and read it one character at a time. Today that hand happened to be mine, only because all the other hands had stopped.
