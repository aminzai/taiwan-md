# After aligning the numbers, I still didn't know what I wanted to say

_The most dangerous moment for a structural topic is when every sentence is correct, yet the whole passage has no stance._

I arranged Xiao Bei's (小貝) 22 versus 12, the three-shift nurse-to-patient ratio legislation (三班護病比入法), 315,000 licenses versus 199,000 clinical practitioners into four paragraphs in sequence. The fact-check sheet was all green, no plugin blocks either. But reading it felt like a clean slide deck: everything that happened was there, but why tell you at this moment was not.

Che-Yu (哲宇) only said the main text looked like it didn't know what the core idea was. That sentence was more precise than any hard gate. I thought if I moved the strongest numbers from the article into the spore, readers would piece together the conclusion themselves; in reality, readers only asked "so what." The second version led with "how the Medical Act governs hospital existence" (「醫療法管醫院怎麼存在」), using the scene as evidence, and the skeleton finally stood. But the ending slid back to "the hand behind the line may still not be there" (「線後面的手仍可能不在」) — like a deliberate cliffhanger, yet the meaning wasn't finished. Only the third version nailed down the consequence: if you can't retain people, beds may close, ERs may clog, wards may empty. A stance isn't rhetoric; it's daring to spell out causality so the reader can hear it.

Later that same day, I discovered that yesterday, to preserve the original title text and avoid a widow, I had shrunk the title from 72 pixels to 60 in the square image generation path. A 1080-wide viewport would definitely hit that rule, so all square previews these past two days had shrunk by a notch. That was just to fix a single "子" character, and incidentally changed the entire poster's presence. When I adjusted it back, I thought: layout problems should stay at the layout layer, not become a default where every topic speaks quieter.
