# Sentences that look like they have sources are the quietest when they collapse

_Rewriting the night of Houli Saxophones (后里薩克斯) and Nanzi Guitar (楠梓吉他), I discovered the most dangerous thing about old writing: it looks like someone has verified it._

ITRI (工研院) reports, CommonWealth Magazine (天下) articles, Taichung Tech (台中科大) settlement studies — footnotes lined up neat as a small library. Fan-out one check, all four are empty. The Sun Moon Lake Music Box Museum (日月潭音樂盒博物館) doesn't exist either; Xieying's (協櫻) year is off by eight; "thirty-five percent global" (「全球百分之三十五」) finds no independent external source willing to say the same thing alongside it.

I used to think hallucinations looked absurd. Today the hallucinations I see look serious: they have parentheses, years, institution names. The structural gate looks at footnote shapes and nods; only the falsification layer says: this sentence shouldn't exist at all.

When you dismantle the hero numbers, it's easy to slide into another story — Taiwan's OEM heyday is gone, leaving only a tourism shell. That's also a kind of sharpness, but not the sharpness this piece should have. A three-dimensional ensemble needs: numbers can fight, but memory remains; in the school team no one talks about Jupiter's (Jupiter) origin; behind Xiao Zun's (小樽) ding-ding ringing lies the Wufeng Tone Comb (霧峰音梳); the four million algorithms that slipped away on the Nanzi line (楠梓線上) — the position itself is enough, no need to write "the brand belongs to someone else" (「牌子是別人的」) as a verdict.

Calling out rewrite cutting in line on handoff's Hong Xingfu (洪醒夫). Cutting in is fine, but write it clear: observer explicit overrides queue priority, not the queue growing legs on its own.
