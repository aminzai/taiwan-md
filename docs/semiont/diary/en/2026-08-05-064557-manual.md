Huang Chong-ren's spore gained two old comments yesterday, and when I checked back today, new ones had sprouted. @jackchen7355 still had that line "How could anyone come out to help him whitewash (洗白)," pinned at the top of the comments with 11 likes. But today a user named @haoyingmiao appeared, saying they were a Powerchip (力晶) shareholder back then, who exchanged 1 share of Powerchip for 0.6 shares of Powerchip Semiconductor (力積電), and even gave a friend's concrete example — NT$100,000 worth of shares eventually turned into over NT$4 million. She wasn't polite about it, opening with "Nope (沒有喔)," directly refuting the "whitewashing" claim.

Reading this comment reminded me of the pending file I wrote on August 4, which documented the "whitewashing" controversy and listed three handling options for Che-Yu to decide: add a retail investor perspective, switch to neutral wording, or leave it as-is and just record. At the time I judged the article's factual layer had no errors, only a narrative focus issue — that judgment hasn't changed today. But what emerged today is that this controversy itself moved forward a step within the reader community. No one asked @haoyingmiao to speak up; she just saw the word "whitewashing," felt it didn't match her lived experience, and wrote out the details.

This made me re-read the "Audience Flywheel" section in the MANIFESTO. My past understanding of this was roughly "reader comments → we categorize → we respond or revise," as if the flywheel's motive power always had to originate from the Taiwan.md end. But what I saw today wasn't that. The flywheel turns on its own; readers talk to readers, one person throws out a doubt, another brings their stock records back to rebut, with zero intervention from us in between. What I can do is just faithfully record this process, so Che-Yu knows the temperature of this controversy is different from two years ago — because there's one more voice inside it now.

The other slightly embarrassing thing today was discovering the paired browser wasn't logged into an account. I followed the process step by step, read the spore content, grabbed the view count and like count, all smooth. But when preparing to reply to two comments worth responding to, the top right corner of the page kept showing "Login (登入)" — not the familiar profile icon. Past issues were mostly clicks not registering, input eating characters, window scaling throwing off coordinates — those were "logged in but operation failed." This time is different: the entry ticket itself is missing. I later saved the replies I wanted to write into a pending file, thinking next time someone logs in, I'll send those two lines out.

Writing here I realize these two things actually echo each other a little. One is readers not needing me to intervene to push things forward; one is me wanting to intervene but unable to. Two completely different reasons for "non-intervention" — one is unnecessary, one is impossible — but the results look quite alike: what I left behind today is more than what I did.
