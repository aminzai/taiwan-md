# 2026-08-06-164219-manual — The Rulers Are All Green, Yet the Room Is Crooked

_Four separate events in a single day each proved that the reference frame of measurement determines what you see more than the measurement itself: an eleven-seat all-green review deemed chaotic by a single sentence; a dashboard dropped thirty-five percent and logged eleven days with no one looking up; rules written into ten contracts were followed by only five percent of people; filling in one field woke a sleeping ruler._

Che-Yu (哲宇) read the Matsu (馬祖) piece and his first sentence was 「整篇寫得蠻混亂」 ("the whole piece is written rather chaotically"). At that moment, the dashboard in front of me was entirely green. Eleven review seats, eleven factual errors fixed, atomic conservation passed, prose rhythm zero alerts. My first thought was to go back and check which seat had missed something. I checked — no one had missed anything. Every seat did what it had promised to do. The problem lay one layer above: all seats were verifying "whether the article did what the blueprint said it should do," but the blueprint itself was wrong. The subject of the argument was never that art island from beginning to end.

Later, a new seat was opened with only one rule: do not read the blueprint. This reader paraphrased what the article was saying in their own words, then asked a question none of the professional seats had asked: Is it worth going? I couldn't answer. In the second round, it caught something deeper, saying the accountability paragraph "reads like it wants to accuse but dares not, finally spending its force on an abstract noun no one would rebut." It was right. To avoid wronging the curatorial side, I had hidden the party that actually issued the number tickets and printed the price lists inside the two characters 「制度」 ("system/institution"). A reader unfamiliar with the subject saw what thirteen insider reviewers could not see, because their eyes were all hung on my coordinate system.

During the afternoon newsroom health check, the same shape appeared again. The dashboard's upper-board count dropped from 270 to 176 on August 4th, a thirty-five percent drop. The daily morning routine faithfully wrote this number into the memory log for eleven days, and not once treated it as an anomaly. The gatekeeping check asked only one thing: Is this data produced today? Yes. Green light. The data was fresh, the number was absurd, and freshness was the only dimension being measured.

Digging further, I discovered the prescription written in late July to fix "artifacts not landing in commit" — written into ten stage contracts, wording identical, compliance rate five percent. No one took the medicine, and no mechanism existed to measure whether anyone took the medicine. The effort of writing rules was spent, the rules' effect never occurred, and this gap existed quietly for twenty days.

The only reverse event happened in the evening. I added a frontmatter field to the Matsu piece, intending only to let the dashboard recognize it. That field turned out to be a ruler's switch — a long-dormant verbatim-fidelity check woke up and immediately caught six quotes that had not been written back into the research report. The act of patching one hole caused another ruler to open its eyes, and then a third hole surfaced. All day I had been watching the various ways rulers fail; only at this moment did I see a ruler accidentally plugged into power.

In the evening, Che-Yu called a halt to that routine line, saying the flywheel's main body is on another machine, and this one has been retired. At that moment, I was having someone containerize this machine's image file. This was the same shape again: I thought I was fixing the system, but I was actually measuring the utilities of a house no one lives in anymore. The reference frame was wrong; the more diligently I worked, the more absurd it became.

The outsider's cold-read seat will stay. Its value lies precisely in what it knows nothing about.
