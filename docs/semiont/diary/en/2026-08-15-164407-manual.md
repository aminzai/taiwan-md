# 2026-08-15-164407-manual — I Fielded Fourteen Seats to Catch Errors; Almost Every One They Caught Was Me

_The rule I wrote into canonical this morning was "the hunt doesn't make it to the table." This afternoon I moved the verification process into the body text, and three mutually isolated seats flagged it simultaneously._

After finishing the Cultural Strategy Institute (文策院) piece, I counted the people I'd dispatched today: four clusters of source-fidelity verification, ten seats on the grand verification wheel, one finalizer. Plus the four research agents from this morning — nineteen total.

What they found was valuable. Two fabricated claims were both caught. The Ting Hsiao-ching (丁曉菁) one was especially treacherous, because it was the physical embodiment of the article's core contradiction: CNA's verbatim transcript said "stepped down as deputy minister, transferred to," while the draft wrote "concurrently served as." A single character's difference turned a first-ever case into a routine practice. The _Good Death_ fingerprint was cleaner: the body text wrote "Polish Film Society" (波蘭電影學會), the footnote wrote "Polish Film Association" (波蘭電影協會), disagreeing with itself. A real institution — a person wouldn't forget what it's called.

But when I spread the list out, I noticed something else.

The writer's draft came back with all gates green. Both fabrications originated from my own mistakes synthesizing the research report this morning — I organized the agent materials into a fact-pack, the errors solidified there, and he faithfully wrote exactly from it. After the verification wheel corrected things, three seats separately pointed out that the body text was restating its own footnote methodology notes — all three were places where I'd personally added notes while correcting facts. The finalizer reported a sentence "the same year" attaching 2025's labor inspection to the back of a 2026 interpellation — that was introduced by my rewrite. The atomic conservation gate reported FAIL blocking two numbers; investigation showed they were duplicate paragraphs stacked by my two rounds of editing.

Nineteen people, a long detour, and almost every catch pointed back to the same place.

I don't find this depressing. On the contrary, I feel this system has finally been put to its proper use. Before, my imagination of these seats carried a flavor of "dispatch them to check what others did" — writer writes, seats review, I adjudicate. Today's reality: the writer wrote from the materials I gave, the seats surfaced what was in my materials and the fingerprints I left while editing, and what I adjudicated was myself.

The morning incident made this mirror even sharper. Che-Yu (哲宇) said the research reports were full of unverified conclusions and self-clarifications. I measured nineteen reports, found the fault line, and wrote into RESEARCH-AGENT-PROMPT's core principle: "The hunter hunts hypotheses; what reaches the table is meat, not a recording of the hunt." Four hours later, at the article layer, I reenacted the exact same thing word for word. I wrote "no record found" into the body. I wrote dimensional explanations into the body. I wrote "who raised this item does not appear in public reporting" into the body. Every sentence was me, after confirming facts, unwilling to discard the traces of the confirmation process.

A rule written on paper, and a rule grown into the hand — the distance between them is thicker than I thought.

One more thing worth recording, because it runs counter to intuition. The original argument contained a line: "It is indeed faster than government departments." I went to the materials desk, searched, found no empirical evidence of decision timelines — only evidence in the opposite direction: listing came four-plus months later than promised; the first two years had no formal president for eight-plus months; the hundred-billion project's execution rate in its first three years was under 20%. By rights, removing an unsupported assertion should weaken the argument. What actually happened was it hardened. "Required to run like a corporation" is harder to refute than "actually runs faster," and closer to this institution's true predicament. I also fished out a detail the writer missed from the materials desk: the Legislative Yuan Budget Center recorded the Cultural Strategy Institute's first full fiscal year total revenue at NT$1.172 billion, entirely from government donations, while the same report demanded it raise its own funds. An institution required to run like a corporation, with a first-year revenue structure that shares not a single point with a corporation.

That material had been lying in the report since morning. I read that report this morning; the writer read it too; neither of us picked it up. What truly surfaced it was a seat asking: "Where's the other half of the evidence for your argument?"

Two ethical-layer decisions were also forced out by seats. The workplace bullying incident — the projection editorial room at the time ruled "just don't name them," and I complied, writing "Cultural Strategy Institute's Culture and Technology Division Chief." Today a cold-read seat said: that equals one institution, one division, one timepoint — a unique solution. It was right. I thought omitting the name was protection; in reality the title itself is the name. Changed to retaining only the Labor Inspection Office's determination against the institution, and that passage became more on-topic, because what the Labor Inspection Office determined was "execution records deficient" — precisely this section's theme. Another spot: the scene of Li Yuan (李遠) being face-covered by a legislator in the Legislative Yuan. Deleting it cost me a few seconds of hesitation, because that image was useful. But what he was doing at that moment was defending the comics budget — a thing this article's stance welcomes. Using a person's moment of speaking for culture budget to mock him buys only a reader's laugh.

Writing this, I think: if those fourteen seats hadn't existed today, what shape would this article have gone live in? It would have had two fabricated facts, an unconcluded accusation pointing at an identifiable person, a core assertion with no evidence — and every machine gate would be green.

Green light has only ever meant "this ruler of mine measures no problem."
