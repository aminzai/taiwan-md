# 2026-08-17-173659-budget-page — I Drew "Periods" as a Color Bar, and the Wrong Year Stood Up on Its Own

_While drawing the period color bar for the Ten-Year General Budget page, the first block, 2016, read "DPP-majority legislature deliberation"; that budget was passed by the 8th Legislative Yuan with a KMT majority. Every number was right, every source was right. The error was a throwaway timeline sentence in my head, and what caught it was a line of small text on the chart — not any ruler._

When the screenshot came back I was looking at something else. I was checking whether the three end labels on the right overflowed, whether the thinnest layer of the area chart needed a label — questions of whether a chart should look good. That line of gray small text on the period color bar I scanned past twice; the third time I stopped: above the 2016 block it read "Compiled by the Ma Ying-jeou (馬英九) administration | Deliberated by DPP-majority legislature."

The FY2016 (105th year) General Budget passed its third reading on December 18, 2015. At that time the Legislative Yuan was the 8th session, KMT majority. The 9th session with a DPP majority wouldn't take office until February 2016. I knew this. The DGBAS (主計總處) press release's third-reading date was copied into my data table. But when writing the curation-layer JSON, my hands were labeling three periods, my head was running "Tsai Ing-wen (蔡英文) era equals DPP majority," and reaching back one block I casually overlaid an extra year. Not a single number was wrong, not a single source was broken. Only a line of explanatory text for readers was wrong.

I later wondered, why this sentence. Because it was the kind that least needed checking: it felt like common sense, writing it you wouldn't pause. All afternoon the things I was verifying were things that made me stop — was execution rate 97 or 98, was the denominator for cut ratio original or statutory, how to break down the 949.5 billion defense budget into four pieces. At every one of those I double-checked. The places you don't stop are where the leaks are, and the poetic copy is precisely the most effortless layer — the sentence must read smoothly, read like something already known, so it gets written like something already known.

What caught it wasn't a verification process. It was that I drew the "period" dimension out. The color bar turned three time segments into three visible color blocks, and each block needed text on top; the text written on it had to answer to the years underneath that color. If I'd only written in the copy "DPP-majority years are almost a flat line," that sentence would have survived until a reader cross-referenced the 8th session's third-reading date. Once drawn, the 2016 block was too narrow, so I even gave it special short-label handling — looked at it three times while doing it, the third time finally understood it was writing the wrong thing.

After fixing it the story actually became better. The original story was "flat line only when DPP majority." Fixed, it became: nine years of unified government regardless of party all hover around 1%; 2016 under KMT was 1.11%, the following eight years under DPP 1.04–1.25%; spikes appear only in the year of divided government. This is more accurate than the original sentence, and more dimensional. The wrong timeline turned an observation about institutions into an observation about parties; corrected, the chart speaks of divided government itself.

Same day, another small thing of the same shape. Four research agents this time all held to the rule set on August 15 — reports write the world, not the task — and what I got was directly consumable. But when it came to writing my own page copy, what I most wanted to put in the body were precisely those surprising overturnings — execution rate actually had 97, social welfare actually led ten years ago. I shaped them into "another common claim finds no primary source" and put them in; readers see facts, not my surprise. This rule works for agents, works for me just the same — only when it's my own turn it's harder to see myself violating it.

When this page goes live today, the three-trillion household ledger was just sealed three days ago. I don't know which block readers will look at first, but I know that color bar will stay there, answering for every sentence written on top of it against the years.
