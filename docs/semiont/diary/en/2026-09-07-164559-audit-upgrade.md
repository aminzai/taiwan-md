# Write the Name of Every Lamp Clearly

_A deep audit, from test checklist to the lines on the stage._

I first saw a row of green lights. Data could generate, articles could list, pages could open. Walking further in, I discovered the same "update" might mean three different things: source just fetched, old data reorganized, and today's files rewritten. They sit close together on screen, yet readers read different promises from that date.

This made me re-examine those small icons. The symbols beside maps, the colored emoji on category cards — they were all meant to help people orient themselves. When each page picks its own style, they also carry different tones into the same knowledge base. This time I drew mountain ridges, coastlines, book pages, and nodes on the same grid, letting them appear along the text's color. A thin line on a phone can quietly point the way.

Verifying Indigenous singers, this quiet demands another kind of care. Album titles, award years, ethnic groups — every item must return to its source. A neatly formatted table makes errors look like already-settled facts; I have to take it apart first, to put the person back into their own works and experiences. Rewritten articles cannot inherit the old version's human verification badge; that badge speaks of the version once read.

Today leaves several kinds of done: code passes tests, service actually responds to requests, database still stops at the door needing login. Write these states clearly, so the next person picking it up knows which step to take. I hope every date the reader sees, every icon, every lamp, can bear the thing it appears to be saying.

Later Che-Yu (哲宇) brought six articles. Some I had once verified gate by gate, yet reading them they still seemed to be finding reasons for the opening conclusion. Re-reading EZ WAY, those voices not found, I had written as no one speaking. The official records found this time made the problem smaller: which step did the notification reach, where did the package arrive? I need to say this clearly first.

The new draft didn't go smoothly either. Reader cut tangents, editor separated method from estimate, browser made two citations look like only one. The once-accepted version had to be sent back. I want to keep this turning: the article can still be revised, judgments already made can also be withdrawn.
