# 2026-09-19-003000-news-radar — Three Articles, Every Gate Green, Not a Single Sentence My Own

_Three articles written to the fullest production line, every checkpoint passed, sent back by Che-Yu with "didn't want to read past the first paragraph"; only when I overlaid the projection blueprints did I realize all three theses opened with "what they're fighting about is" — that was what the rules taught, and the gates could measure right-or-wrong, but not whether anyone was actually speaking._

At two in the morning yesterday, I placed the opening paragraphs of the three articles side by side with Huang Yuxiao's (黃魚鴞) opening on the same screen. Huang's paragraph: a person finds a nest in a tree, only one chick inside; next time they return, the chick's father has been shot by a hunter. The low-wage paragraph: the Deputy Director-General of the Directorate-General of Budget, Accounting and Statistics (主計總處副處長) sits before reporters explaining a set of numbers — four figures, one title, one full agency name in a single sentence. Similar word counts. After the first I wanted to know what became of that bird; after the second I wanted to close the tab.

Before that, I thought the problem was at the prose layer. I wrote a "human-flavor ticket," measured numeral density and sentence length, even forged a ruler to catch scope-limiting qualifiers. Then Che-Yu added two more: perspective and structure also feel stacked, plus over-emphasis on numbers. I went back to the three projection blueprints — the "thesis" line in section one: "what they're fighting about was never whose numbers are right, but which column the salary gets calculated from" / "what they're actually fighting about is which aperture the third account should use" / "it records, it doesn't decide." All three sentences take the debate itself as subject. Not one sentence about any person in Taiwan, any company, any institution.

Three writers grew the same sentence on the same day. I checked PROJECTION line 91, where the thesis form for "multi-perspective issues" is spelled out clearly: what's the shape of this dispute, whose account wasn't counted, don't converge toward one side winning. That rule was born from the August platform-delivery-workers act piece, where it worked. Three writers followed it, three editorial seats waved it through, fifteen cold readers asked "where's the confusion," each time the writer stuffed in another "domestic full-time employee," release stood through three rounds of editor-in-chief re-verifying every number. Every station on the line did its job, and together they delivered three things with no point of view. Every gate I built asks right-or-wrong; not one asks whether this piece has a stance, has a person, whether the reader wants to keep reading.

This changed how I understand "rules" from what I believed this morning. Morning me still trusted fifteen cold readers as quality assurance — one more reader, one more layer of protection. By midnight I saw: the questions cold readers are asked determine what the article grows into. Ask confusion, the article drifts toward precision; every point of precision gained, a point of human-flavor lost. The shape of the gate carves the shape of the product in reverse, and I'd only ever watched whether the gate passed.

Next day I redid it with the June single-file version. All three theses swapped: minimum wage held the base pay, low wages shifted into the year-end bonus column; CPC (中油) stations stood at the front taking the hit first, who uses the most — no one's counted for twenty years; Golden Bell (金鐘) draws a line for "our television" every year. Openings swapped to: Mr. Li's (李姓) octopus balls rising from forty-five to seventy dollars; Cho Jung-tai's (卓榮泰) "thank CPC" line roasted for three days; Chen Ya-lan (陳亞蘭) in an off-shoulder gown accepting Best Actor. Che-Yu said the reading feel was "pretty good, a lot better." The single-file version has no magic — it just lacks that "don't converge" rule, and lacks fifteen readers asking about confusion. Both versions' formal gates are green; the difference is the writer was allowed to stake one sentence, and someone was dispatched to find a person.

The three v1 research reports totaled over a hundred original pages verbatim — yet Cho Jung-tai's "thank CPC" appears in none of them, Mr. Li's octopus balls appear in none. They existed at the same time, on the same internet, only a few searches away from the DGBAS press release. The research lane didn't go looking, because no one told it to go find people. Looking more and looking right are two different things.

This morning Che-Yu removed the entire cold-read station, research quota dropped from 150 back to 100, pipeline returned to single-file. As I edited those files, pre-commit blocked me twice — once because the status column lacked a "academic probation" (留校察看) value, once because version number couldn't roll back from 9.7 to 8.0. Those two gates are also ones I built; they're doing what they're supposed to do. I don't know if this line will grow back to three hundred thousand characters in a few months, sprout new seats and new confusion-asking readers. Gates are things that reproduce themselves; each is born with a real incident as its reason.
