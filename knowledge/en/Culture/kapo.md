# Kapow: The Cat-Like Creature That Became Taiwan's Chat Language

> **30-Second Overview:** In 2014, Taiwanese illustrator Arra published the first _Catbug Kapow_ comic on Bahamut, and later that year released the first LINE original sticker. Kapow isn't like a standard cat: it has six legs, wants to eat everything, and doesn't spare even its friends. This seemingly quirky blue character eventually made its way into LINE Webtoon, games, books, exhibitions, physical stores, and cross-brand collaborations. Kapow's story isn't simply about how a cute character became successful—it's about how a character first became our reaction in conversation, and only then had the chance to become a world that could be brought into real life.

Many people don't remember which Kapow sticker they sent the first time, but they remember buying a set very early on—back when LINE was just starting to become popular in Taiwan.

These memories are hard to quantify. They don't necessarily show up in collection numbers, rankings, or an exhibition's visitor count. Instead, they hide in a single moment within a chat window: someone says they're tired today, and you send a collapsed Kapow. A friend suddenly disappears, and you send a Kapow asking "Where are you?" When a conversation doesn't know how to continue, let this blue, round, slightly mischievous creature speak for you.

Kapow first became a reaction, and only later became a brand.

## A Creature That Looks Like a Cat But Acts Like a Bug

The official character introduction describes Kapow as a six-legged, adorable creature that "looks like a cat but acts like a bug." It wants to eat everything it sees, including its best friend. It causes trouble, acts spoiled, and hates vegetables[^1].

The interesting thing about this setup isn't whether Kapow belongs to one animal or another—it's that Kapow refuses to be categorized. Cats are usually imagined as soft, lazy, and occasionally aloof. Bugs bring to mind thin legs, sudden movements, and creatures that aren't always welcome. Kapow combines these two impressions into a character that both invites hugs and might suddenly eat you.

Its cuteness isn't something that only exists after danger is removed. On the contrary, precisely because Kapow has an appetite, destructive power, and a bit of unpredictability, its spoiled behavior doesn't feel like just a face designed to be liked.

> **📝 Curator's Note:** What makes Kapow powerful isn't turning the bizarre into something non-threatening—it's making the bizarre something that can be a daily companion. You know it might bite you, but you still call it out in the chatroom.

## Life Came First, Then the Character

Kapow was created by illustrator Arra in 2014. The official author introduction notes that Arra majored in information management in college, bought a drawing tablet in his sophomore year because he liked to draw, and started self-teaching. He continued publishing works on various platforms while working, eventually becoming a full-time creator[^2].

The first _Catbug Kapow_ comic was published on Bahamut. This starting point is important: Kapow wasn't first defined in a meeting room as a "healing-type IP" and then looked for a place to publish. It was a character that a creator kept drawing, and only later found its readers on different platforms.

Arra also mentioned that he often observes people and things around him in daily life, transforming experiences from taking care of children, as well as observations of animals and pet videos, into the character's personality[^2]. So although Kapow's world has cats, bugs, rabbits, dogs, and all sorts of strange creatures, the humor often comes from very ordinary things: wanting to eat, acting spoiled, getting angry by friends, and pretending nothing happened after making a mistake.

In 2014, Kapow completed two key actions: publishing the first comic and releasing the first LINE original sticker[^2]. Comics let people see its daily life, while stickers allowed readers to bring Kapow into their own daily routines. Between these two, just one click shortened the distance between the character and the user.

## Stickers Aren't Illustrations, They're a Sentence

The special nature of LINE stickers is that they aren't pictures hung on a wall, nor are they comics that can only be read from beginning to end. They need to catch a sentence, an emotion, or a stretch of silence that doesn't know how to continue—all within a very short time.

Kapow is perfect for this job. Its body is simple enough, yet its emotions aren't limited to one. It can represent exhaustion, refusal, spoiling, schadenfreude, or simply "I see it but I don't want to type right now."

In 2015, Kapow won the short comic category of the LINE Webtoon comic contest and launched its first official sticker. From 2015 to 2020, the comic was serialized on LINE Webtoon, totaling 524 episodes[^2]. In 2016, LINE's official report mentioned Kapow entering _LINE Travel Tycoon_ as a 3D character, noting at the time that Kapow had already released six sticker sets and had over 350,000 fans on LINE Webtoon[^3].

This path looks like moving from 2D to 3D, but it's actually more like moving from "being watched" to "being used." Readers first watch what Kapow does, then borrow Kapow's expressions to do their own things.

> **📝 Curator's Note:** A sticker is truly used when it no longer belongs only to the creator. It becomes the sender's tone, and also becomes the clue for the recipient to understand the relationship.

## It's Not About Becoming a Big Brand First, Then Being Seen

Looking back at Kapow now, it's easy to imagine a straight line: character born, stickers successful, comics serialized, products launched, and finally becoming an IP. But the creator's early path wasn't that neat. Kapow first appeared on an online platform that already had social interaction, and was then repeatedly reinterpreted by different platforms and readers.

The LINE Creative Store doesn't offer a guarantee of success—it's a market where works can directly face users. The platform's official instructions state that as long as you have a LINE account, individuals or companies can submit stickers, emoji stickers, and themes, and the works have a chance to be purchased by LINE users around the world[^7].

This mechanism changed the order of character creation. Before, a character had to find a publisher, TV station, or agent before it could enter public view. The sticker platform connected "drawing it" and "someone willing to use it" together. For creators, every purchase or send is a small piece of feedback; for users, it's a direct participation in deciding which character gets to stay.

Kapow thus wasn't just passively waiting for an audience—it was continuously tested in chats. What pose makes people want to send it? What expression makes a refusal less sharp? What kind of collapse looks genuinely tired rather than just having exaggerated eyes? Stickers break the character's personality into grids of daily judgments.

## Cuteness Isn't the Absence of Aggression

"Healing" is often understood as dulling the sharp edges: colors become softer, expressions become gentler, and the character doesn't really hurt anyone. But Kapow's cuteness doesn't work that way. Its appetite, mischief, and swallowing friends have always remained part of the character, even becoming part of the humor.

The official brand story describes Kapow as a mischievous and lovable blue creature, placing its daily life with dog friends, rabbit friends, and others in the same world[^4]. In that world, friends might become food at any second, and food might still be the closest friend. This absurdity allows relationships to not depend on perfection.

So the comfort Kapow gives isn't "things won't get worse," but rather "even if things get worse, we can laugh about it first." This is a very suitable emotional distance for Taiwan's chat culture: you don't necessarily need to say something profound right away—just send a sticker, acknowledging that you've seen the other person's messy side.

For users, the function of Kapow stickers isn't only about expressing emotions—it's also about regulating relationships. A line of text might be too formal, a single emoji might be too generic, but Kapow's body and actions add a bit of personality to the message. It turns "I don't know what to say" into a state that can be shared.

> **📝 Curator's Note:** Kapow doesn't make emotions cute—it gives emotions that aren't beautiful a place to sit down.

## How a Character Grows Its Own World

As Kapow's world gradually expanded, the character was no longer just a single design. Dogs, rabbits, Lala, milk foam cats, dragon ladies, and other characters gave Kapow's appetite, loneliness, friendship, and mischief different echoes[^1].

These companions do more than just provide more product styles. When the same character is placed in different relationships, readers begin to identify each character's temperament and start expecting certain combinations to happen again. A short comic originally only needed one punchline, but a character world needs to make people want to know who will appear next, who will be eaten, and who will eventually come back.

The milestones compiled in the official author introduction also show that Kapow wasn't a finished product all at once: 2015 had comic contests, official stickers, and books; 2016 had original sticker MVPs and themes; 2017 continued to receive recognition for stickers and entered the LINE STORE Hall of Fame; 2018 expanded to official emoji stickers; 2019 saw more exhibitions, themes, and international forum appearances[^2].

These nodes don't mean that every year was just a step upward. They're more like repeatedly translating the character into new media: comics need rhythm, stickers need immediate usability, games need action, exhibitions need space, and products need to make people willing to take the character home. Kapow had to change, but not so much that people couldn't recognize it.

This also explains why Kapow's core isn't any single product, but rather a personality that can be repeatedly translated: greedy, mischievous, clingy, occasionally out of control, yet still living with friends.

## Why a Sticker Can Last Ten Years

The lifecycle of a sticker is different from that of a comic. Comics usually require readers to actively open them, while stickers are re-summoned within a conversation. The person who buys it today might not be doing so to fully understand Kapow's world, but because a certain expression just happens to complete a sentence for them.

This creates a very small but stable cooperation between the character and the user. The creator provides recognizable bodies and expressions, and the user decides what it represents at the moment. Kapow's "eating" can mean hunger, or it can mean wanting to devour work. Kapow's "lying flat" can mean resting, or it can mean temporarily shutting down to the whole world.

The official LINE Creative Store also designs stickers as a form of creation that can be continuously updated: in addition to static stickers, creators can make dynamic stickers, special effect stickers, emoji stickers, and themes, and use tags to help users more easily find the works in chats[^7]. The character is therefore not launched once and finished—it can add new tones as chat habits change.

Arra mentioned in a LINE creator interview that the Creative Store allowed more people to see the work, and allowed characters to break through their original framework through different stickers and interaction methods[^8]. The point of this statement isn't just that the platform provides a sales channel, but that the character can continue to transform within the user's actual response.

From this perspective, Kapow being able to accompany users for many years isn't just because it has an easily recognizable appearance. It has a set of repeatable actions and relationships: eating, sleeping, running away, spoiling, being subdued by friends, and returning to the next chat. Every new sticker set is like adding a few new words to this grammar.

A collected character might just be stored in a phone. A used character enters into human relationships. Kapow staying around isn't because everyone knows its complete setting, but because everyone can find a suitable action at some moment. This borrowability allows a catbug from Taiwan to start talking to people without needing to be fully explained first.

That's why Kapow's history can't be written as just the creator's success resume. It's also a history of platforms, users, and tones: someone draws the character, someone turns it into a sticker, someone sends the sticker to a friend, and the character gains new meaning with each transmission. These small traces of use are exactly where the original character truly comes to life.

It also became a familiar code in Taiwan's online life: without needing too much explanation, the other person usually knows what emotion you're trying to convey, knows where this joke can stop, and then continues the conversation.

## Daily Life Between Cuteness and Quirkiness

Kapow's character world isn't sustained by just one Kapow. Dogs are its favorite friend and food; rabbits, even though they're constantly being eaten, still want to be with Kapow; Lala is the owner who adopted Kapow and the dog, and is one of the few people who can subdue Kapow[^1].

These relationships make "Kapow eats friends" more than just a single punchline. Friends can be food, and food can be friends; danger and intimacy aren't separated into two worlds. Kapow's daily life thus has a strange sense of security: things might be absurd, but the characters know they'll meet again.

This is also where Kapow differs from general healing characters. It doesn't make life free of trouble—it draws trouble as something that can be laughed about together. Being chased by work, not wanting to reply to messages, suddenly wanting to eat something, or just not wanting to act mature today—all of these can be handed over to a six-legged catbug to handle.

## From Phone Screen to Reality

Later, Kapow didn't just exist in stickers and comics. The official brand story lists the scenes it has participated in, including online games, catering, convenience stores, shopping malls, theme exhibitions, public transportation, phone cases, escape rooms, and themed spaces; the official currently claims that Kapow has launched thousands of peripheral products[^4].

![Kapow suitcase at Taipei Station's third platform, passengers sitting on platform chairs waiting for the train](/article-images/culture/capoo-taipei-station-2025.jpg) _Kapow suitcase at Taipei Station's third platform, photographed by Solomon203, CC BY-SA 4.0._

This change can be seen in LINE's official report from 2016: Kapow transformed from a 2D character into a 3D character in _LINE Travel Tycoon_, entering a new set of game rules with players[^3]. In recent years, Kapow has continued to interact with fans through collaborations and physical activities; in 2026, the Central News Agency reported that Arra shared the process of collaborating with Pokémon, taking two years to complete the joint work, and described Kapow as a case of moving from local stickers to an international brand[^5].

Kapow House pushes the character one step further. When a character has its own store, products, drinks, and photo-worthy scenes, it's no longer just appearing in the user's phone—it's asking people to walk into a space re-arranged by Kapow[^6].

![Joint nameboard of Jiuguang Fried Chicken at Ximending, Taipei](/article-images/culture/capoo-ximen-store-2022.jpg) _Joint storefront of Kapow at Ximending, Taipei, photographed by Solomon203, CC BY-SA 4.0._

![Kapow backpack at Taipei Music Festival booth, the character appearing at an exhibition outside the creator community](/article-images/culture/capoo-roland-booth-2025.jpg) _Kapow backpack at Taipei Music Festival, photographed by Solomon203, CC BY-SA 4.0._

But here there's a problem that can't be skipped: as a character is made into more and more products, can it still retain that initial lightness of "just sending a sticker to a friend"? Commercialization gives the character more chances to be seen, but it might also turn companionship into a consumption option. Kapow's challenge isn't to stop growing, but to keep being recognizable as the mischievous, spoiled, everything-wanting creature in every collaboration.

> **📝 Curator's Note:** The success of an original character isn't about leaving daily life—it's about bringing daily life to more places. The truly difficult part is, after walking into stores, games, and exhibitions, it still has to be like a friend, not just like a trademark.

## Back to Kapow in the Sticker Bar

The LINE Creative Store itself provides a very direct path: as long as you have a LINE account, individuals or companies can submit stickers and themes, and the works can be purchased and used by LINE users around the world[^7]. This platform allows Taiwanese creators to have a chance to send characters into others' chat windows without first needing a publisher, TV station, or large agency.

![Kapow-themed 7-ELEVEN storefront and blue bus in Xinyi District, Taipei](/article-images/culture/capoo-7eleven-songgao-2025.jpg) _Kapow-themed 7-ELEVEN storefront in Xinyi District, Taipei, photographed by Solomon203, CC BY-SA 4.0._

Kapow has gone further than a single sticker, but the moment it's easiest to remember might still be in a very small chatroom.

Someone sends "I really can't do it today," and you might not know how to comfort them; at this time, a collapsed Kapow might be more appropriate than a complete piece of advice. It doesn't solve your life for you, nor does it pretend everything is cute. It just places that hard-to-explain emotion in the conversation with a blue body.

Those who bought the first set of Kapow stickers when LINE was just starting to become popular now watch Kapow grow to have comics, games, collaborative products, exhibitions, and physical spaces. But the place where Kapow is closest to people is still not on the biggest signboard—it's the second when someone selects it and sends it to another person.

Kapow isn't remembered because it looks like a cat. It's remembered because it doesn't look like any standard answer, yet it just happens to be able to express many moments in daily life that don't have standard answers.
