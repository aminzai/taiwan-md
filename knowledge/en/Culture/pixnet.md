# PIXNET: How a Platform Preserved Taiwanese Daily Life as Public Memory

## Thirty-Second Overview

> **Theme:** How PIXNET evolved from National Taiwan University campus photo albums into a platform carrying the daily writings, life information, and digital memories of Taiwan. **Timeline:** Starting with online photo albums in 2003, adding blogging in 2004, and becoming independent from NTU in 2006. It subsequently moved toward advertising revenue sharing, data analytics, interest communities, and personal media alliances.[^1] [^2] [^3] [^9] [^12] [^13] [^14] [^15] **Core Conflict:** The platform must update its old architecture and handle security and operational issues, but users still expect articles, photos, comments, links, and traffic data to be preserved.[^4] [^5] [^6] [^7] **Counterintuitive Sentence:** For a platform whose value lies in preserving users' lives, the hardest thing to manage is often preservation itself. **Takeaway for Readers:** PIXNET is not just a blogging website; it is a storage layer of Taiwan's digital life memory; understanding its redesign and feature retirement reveals that digital preservation does not equal keeping text on a server.

**One-Sentence Takeaway:** The history of PIXNET is not just about how the platform grew, but how Taiwanese daily life sought a place between platform updates and content preservation without being lost.

In 2003, PIXNET began from the need to share photos within National Taiwan University's campus community. In 2004, the platform added blogging functionality; in 2013, PIXNET was reported as one of Taiwan's top five most visited websites.[^1] [^2] [^3] These three time points connect its transformation from a tool to a platform and then into a life database.

This timeline seems like the growth story of a startup, but what is truly special is that it turned a dinner, a trip, a child's photo, or an uncelebrated personal reflection into searchable, citable, and revisit-able public memory.

However, memory does not last forever just because it was put online. In 2024, PIXNET ended its "New Type Article" service. At the end of 2025, the platform faced functional issues due to system restructuring, data repair, and cloud migration. In 2026, the official continued to issue announcements regarding system maintenance and database optimization.[^4] [^5] [^6] [^7] [^8] **The counterintuitive thing is: for a platform whose value lies in preserving users' lives, the hardest thing to manage is often preservation itself.**

## A Photo Before a Company

PIXNET's beginning was not based on a grand media blueprint, but on very specific needs within the campus. Co-founder Liu Hao-en recalled that after digital cameras became common, students needed a place to share photos; he also noticed that campus activity information delivery lacked immediacy. Photo sharing and campus information became two entry points of the same idea.[^2]

This origin explains why PIXNET started as an online photo album before adding blogging the following year. A 2007 report by the _Liberty Times_ also noted that PIXNET first provided online albums in 2003, transitioned to blogging services in 2004, and became independent from NTU in 2006.[^3]

| Year      | Platform Change                                                | Verifiable Significance                                         |
| :-------- | :------------------------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 2003      | Started with online photo albums                               | User needs preceded commercial product                          |
| 2004      | Added blogging functionality                                   | Photo sharing extended to long-form writing                     |
| 2006–2007 | Independent from NTU and introduced corporate funding          | Campus service entered the commercial phase                     |
| 2012      | Launched MIB blog advertising revenue sharing plan             | Creator content began linking with income systems[^12]          |
| 2017–2018 | Launched Bangbang, Interest Wall, and New PIXNET               | Shifted from single articles to interest communities[^13] [^14] |
| 2024      | Ended "New Type Article" service                               | Feature retirement became a data preservation issue[^4]         |
| 2025–2026 | System restructuring, content repair, and database maintenance | Platform updates clash with memory preservation                 |

📝 Curator's Note: PIXNET's first innovation was not turning ordinary people into celebrities, but making "ordinary lives worth keeping" a web habit.

## From Campus Service to Local Content Infrastructure

The 2007 report about the City-State Group acquiring PIXNET marked a turning point after the platform left campus: while the operating team remained shareholders, new capital partners were also introduced.[^3] This was not simply a student startup story; it was a common exchange in local internet services: funding allowed the platform to continue growing, and capital brought revenue, scale, and governance expectations.

In 2013, the _Taipei Times_ recorded a peak of PIXNET's Alexa ranking: the report stated that at that time it was one of Taiwan's top five most visited websites, citing the platform's claims of accumulating about 190 million articles and 230 million photos, with 300,000 articles produced daily.[^1] These were historical snapshots from 2013, not the current situation in 2026.

A review by _Digital Age_ in 2020 placed this database back into platform strategy: PIXNET extended from albums and blogging to social commerce, digital marketing, and data analytics. When the "New PIXNET" was launched in 2018, features like the Interest Wall and Bangbang were also added.[^9]

This meant the problems the platform faced had changed. The early problem was "how to get people to upload photos"; later, the problem became "how to make content discoverable, interactive, and convertible into new services." The platform was no longer just a container; it began intervening in content distribution, social relationships, and commercial value.

📝 Curator's Note: When a website accumulates enough life details, it is not just providing pages, but providing the infrastructure of "how Taiwanese people search for life."

## From Traffic Platform to Creator Ecosystem

The commercialization of PIXNET was not merely placing ads on pages; it gradually transformed "content being viewed" into an entire ecosystem concerning "whether creators can continue producing, whether brands can find suitable partners, and whether the platform can bear storage and transmission costs." The MIB advertising revenue sharing plan in 2012 made creator income part of the platform's product. A 2016 report described PIXinsight as a system that organized article and social behavior data to assist with advertising and content cooperation. These were specialized statements from the platform or companies during specific periods, not guarantees of today's revenue system.[^12]

The "New PIXNET" in 2018 placed blogging, Interest Wall, and Bangbang within the same product architecture. The report at the time stated that the Interest Wall offered 80 categories of interests and noted that the platform had over 5,000 Bangbang; these numbers only represented data from when they were released in 2018.[^13]

The platform also expanded creator evaluation beyond its own site. A 2018 report on the Personal Media Alliance described PIXinsight as analyzing creators across dimensions such as search power, popularity power, diffusion power, call power, and interactivity, mentioning that nearly 300 independent blogs had joined the plan.[^15] For creators, this offered exposure and collaboration; for the platform, it meant incorporating personal writing into a quantifiable, distributable, and tradable content ecosystem.

## A Platform Playing Four Roles Simultaneously

| Role                      | Function Seen by Users                                                | Responsibility Borne by the Platform                                     |
| :------------------------ | :-------------------------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Personal Publishing Tool  | Posting, editing, illustration, categorization, and long-term updates | Content must be storable, modifiable, and traceable                      |
| Search Database           | Finding old articles and life experiences via keywords                | Link stability, clear indexing, content not disappearing arbitrarily     |
| Interest Community        | Comments, sharing, following, and topic aggregation                   | Notifications, interaction, violation handling, and community governance |
| Commercial Infrastructure | Advertising, cooperation, traffic, and creator income                 | Revenue transparency, system stability, communication of redesign risks  |

The concept of Bangbang was to allow users to browse and interact autonomously based on interest classification, highlights, stars, discussion level, and date.[^14] This design reduced the monolithic feeling of large platforms' single recommendation mechanisms, but it did not mean governance issues disappeared: garbage content, plagiarism, malicious attacks, commercial disclosure, illegal content, and sorting power still needed to be managed.

The platform therefore had to explain both freedom and limitations: Who can post? What content will be removed? Who manages the community? Is the basis for sorting understandable? Can a user appeal after their content is removed? These are not just product interface issues; they are the governance contract formed when users entrust their daily lives to the platform.

## Taiwanese Life in Search Results

The function of PIXNET that is most underestimated is its ability to make experiences that would not be reported by traditional media searchable. Restaurant reviews, parenting experiences, accommodation ratings, appliance comparisons, and local travel—these did not need to be deemed major news to qualify for preservation.

The value of this content lies not in the authority of each piece, but in the map of life perception formed when a large volume of individual experiences overlaps. Someone records how a shop queues; someone supplements the pedestrian flow during rain; someone writes what a certain spot means to families with children. Readers are not just looking for answers; they are comparing whether others' lives are similar to their own circumstances.

In 2021, Liu Hao-en described his role at PIXNET as being a "lighthouse" that brought external needs back to the company, emphasizing that ordinary Taiwanese people could also contribute works.[^2] This highlights the difference between PIXNET and pure media platforms: its content ecosystem is not solely supported by a few professional authors, but relies on a large number of users without fixed titles.

However, life experience does not equal fact itself. Blogger reviews may be influenced by bias, business relationships, or memory deviation; search rankings do not equate to content quality. PIXNET provides a space where experiences are seen, not an official stamp of truth for every experience. Readers should still identify the author, date, and relationship, and cross-verify with other sources.

📝 Curator's Note: The public nature of blogging is not that everyone becomes an expert, but that everyone's experience can become a starting point for someone else.

## Why Feature Retirement Feels Like Moving Memories

In 2024, PIXNET announced the termination of the "New Type Article" function on June 28. The announcement specifically stated that this was not a general blogging article, and the blogging functionality could still be used, advising users to back up data in advance.[^4]

From a product management perspective, this is feature retirement; from a user's perspective, it feels like a room in their house has suddenly been locked. The content might still be in the account, but the original layout, image arrangement, comment context, and link relationships may not be preserved in the same way.

Digital preservation involves at least four layers: first, files such as text, photos, and videos; second, structures like categorization, tags, comments, internal links, and article versions; third, visibility such as URLs, search indexing, and front-end presentation; and fourth, portability such as backup, export, and cross-system reading. Any interruption in any layer makes "the article still exists" an incomplete answer.

For users, important articles can retain the original text and image files, noting the publication date, original URL, and category, and regularly checking external links. This is not shifting platform responsibility to the individual; it is acknowledging that digital memory requires multiple copies to mitigate losses from a single platform failure.

## 2025–2026: Redesign Puts Platform Conflicts Front and Center

In December 2025, PIXNET released a public letter explaining that the system-wide restructuring and cloud migration caused inconvenience to members, listing the old architecture, insufficient core module support, and security risks as reasons for rebuilding.[^6] The same announcement mentioned the repair of comments and popularity data, album display, front-end style, back-end management, and website indexing. Another announcement addressed login anomalies and advertising system issues.[^5] [^8]

On December 19, the official announced that old article content and symbols had been restored, explaining that for older articles before a specific date, they used a repair method of "retaining original links and synchronizing latest data." Articles edited during the anomaly period retained both the original repaired version and an added backup version.[^10] This revealed that what was hardest for the platform to fix was not a single file, but "which version counts as the original article."

In February 2026, the official listed items still under adjustment or testing, such as editors, system albums, popular articles, latest comments, popularity statistics, custom CSS, image hosting performance, and article export.[^7] A May announcement warned of member database optimization and system maintenance, suspending login and back-end services during maintenance, but allowing front-end browsing of articles.[^11]

As of the date of this review, these announcements prove that the platform is still maintaining, repairing, and restructuring; one cannot conclude that all functions are permanently stable, nor can official repair progress be treated as independent external verification. The safest understanding for readers is: PIXNET is operational, but its technology and product form are continuously changing.

📝 Curator's Note: For the platform, redesign is engineering; for the user, redesign is handing ten years of their writing over to an unfamiliar system for recognition.

## Conclusion: Preservation Is Not Stasis, But Portability

PIXNET's history has two speeds. The company's speed moves from campus albums, commercialization, and social services to cloud architecture; the user's speed is a half-hour article that might solve a small problem for an unfamiliar reader ten years later.

These two speeds cannot always align. A platform cannot refuse to deal with old systems and security risks because its old interface holds memory; users should not be forced to bear the entire burden of preservation just because the platform needs to update. Mature digital culture should treat "how to update" and "how to preserve" as the same problem.

Therefore, what is worth remembering about PIXNET is not a specific traffic ranking or whether a certain feature succeeded, but that it once allowed Taiwanese life to accumulate in a low-barrier way. Starting from a photo on an NTU campus, many things that would not appear in annual records were preserved in search engines and readers' memories.

PIXNET may not always maintain the same appearance, but as long as it understands that what users hand over is not just content, but a small piece of life evidence that can be revisited, every product decision is not just a technical one, but also a cultural responsibility.
