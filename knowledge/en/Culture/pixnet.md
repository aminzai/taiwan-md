# PIXNET: How a Platform Preserves Taiwanese Daily Life into Public Memory

## 30-Second Overview

> **Topic:** How PIXNET evolved from a National Chiao Tung University campus photo album into a platform carrying Taiwanese daily writing, lifestyle information, and digital memory. **Timeline:** Started as an online photo album in 2003, added blogging in 2004, and became independent from National Chiao Tung University in 2006. It later expanded into ad revenue sharing, data analytics, interest-based communities, and personal media alliances. [^1] [^2] [^3] [^9] [^12] [^13] [^14] [^15]**Core Contradiction:** The platform must update its outdated architecture and handle security and operational issues, yet users expect their articles, images, comments, links, and traffic data to remain preserved. [^4] [^5] [^6] [^7]**Counter-intuitive Insight:** For a platform whose value proposition is preserving users' lives, the most difficult thing to handle is often preservation itself. **Takeaway:** PIXNET is not just a blogging website; it is also a storage layer for Taiwan's digital life memory. Understanding its redesigns and feature sunsets reveals why digital preservation is not merely about keeping text on a server.

**One-sentence takeaway:** PIXNET’s history is not just about how a platform grew up, but about how Taiwanese daily life finds a place that won’t be lost between platform updates and content preservation.

In 2003, PIXNET began from the need to share photos on the National Chiao Tung University campus. In 2004, the platform added blogging features; in 2013, PIXNET was reported as the fifth most-visited website in Taiwan. [^1] [^2] [^3] These three time points trace its evolution from a tool, to a platform, to a lifestyle database.

This timeline might look like the growth story of a startup, but what makes it truly special is this: it turns a dinner, a trip, a child’s photo, and an unsolicited review into public memory that can be searched, cited, and revisited.

But memory does not exist forever simply by being put online. In 2024, PIXNET ended its "New Type Article" service. By the end of 2025, the platform faced functional issues due to system restructuring, data repair, and cloud migration. In 2026, the official team continues to publish announcements on system maintenance and database optimization. [^4] [^5] [^6] [^7] [^8] **Counter-intuitively, for a platform whose value is preserving users' lives, the most difficult thing to handle is often preservation itself.**

## A Photo Precedes a Company

PIXNET’s starting point was not a grand media blueprint, but a very specific need on campus. Founder Liu Hao-en recalls that after digital cameras became popular, students needed a place to share photos; he also noticed that campus activity information was not disseminated in a timely manner. Photo sharing and campus information became two entrances to the same idea. [^2]

This starting point explains why PIXNET was first an online photo album, adding blogging only the following year. A 2007 _Liberty Times_ report also records that PIXNET first provided online photo albums in 2003, transformed into a blogging service in 2004, and became independent from National Chiao Tung University in 2006. [^3]

| Time      | Platform Change                                                | Verifiable Significance                                                |
| --------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |

| 2004      | Added blogging features                                        | Photo sharing extended into long-term writing                          |

| 2025–2026 | System restructuring, content repair, and database maintenance | Platform updates and memory preservation face direct conflict          |

📝 Curator’s Note: PIXNET’s first innovation was not turning ordinary people into stars, but making "ordinary people’s lives are worth leaving behind" a web habit.

## From Campus Service to Local Content Infrastructure

A 2007 report on Cheng & Co. Group taking over PIXNET left a turning point for the platform after leaving the campus: the existing management team remained shareholders, but the platform also introduced new capital partners. [^3] This is not simply a student entrepreneurship story, but a common exchange in local web services: capital allows the platform to continue growing, while capital brings revenue, scale, and governance expectations.

In 2013, the _Taipei Times_ recorded a peak for PIXNET using Alexa rankings: the report stated it was then the fifth most-visited website in Taiwan, quoting the platform’s claim that it had accumulated approximately 190 million articles and 230 million photos, producing 300,000 articles daily. [^1] These are historical snapshots from 2013, not the current status in 2026.

A 2020 retrospective in _Digital Times_ placed this database back into platform strategy: PIXNET extended from photo albums and blogs to social commerce, digital marketing, and data analytics. When launching the "New PIXNET" in 2018, it also added features like the Interest Wall and BangBang. [^9]

This means the problems the platform faces have changed. The early problem was "how to get people to upload photos," while the later problem became "how to make content discoverable, interactive, and convertible into new services." The platform is no longer just a container, but begins to intervene in content distribution, community relations, and commercial value.

📝 Curator’s Note: When a website accumulates enough life details, it is no longer just providing pages, but providing an infrastructure for "how Taiwanese people search for life."

## From Traffic Platform to Creator Ecosystem

PIXNET’s commercialization is not just about putting ads on pages, but gradually transforming "content has viewers" into a whole ecosystem of "whether creators can continue producing, whether brands can find suitable targets, and whether the platform can bear storage and delivery costs." The 2012 MIB ad revenue sharing program made creator revenue a part of the platform’s product. A 2016 report described PIXinsight as a system organizing article and community behavior data, assisting ad and content collaborations. These are platform or corporate statements from specific periods, not guarantees of today’s revenue system. [^12]

The 2018 "New PIXNET" placed blogs, the Interest Wall, and BangBang within the same product architecture. Reports at the time stated the Interest Wall provided 80 interest categories and recorded that the platform had over 5,000 BangBangs; these numbers only represent data at the time of the 2018 launch. [^13]

The platform once expanded creator evaluation to off-site blogs. A 2018 report on the Personal Media Alliance described PIXinsight analyzing creators across dimensions such as search power, popularity, diffusion power, mobilization power, and interaction power, mentioning that nearly 300 independent blogs had joined the program at the time. [^15] For creators, this provided exposure and collaboration; for the platform, it was incorporating personal writing into a quantifiable, distributable, and tradable content ecosystem.

## A Platform Simultaneously Playing Four Roles

| Role                      | Function Visible to Users                                | Responsibility the Platform Must Bear                                    |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Personal Publishing Tool  | Post, edit, illustrate, categorize, and update long-term | Content must be storable, modifiable, and traceable                      |
| Search Database           | Find old articles and life experiences via keywords      | Stable links, clear indexing, content does not disappear arbitrarily     |
| Interest Community        | Comments, sharing, following, and topic aggregation      | Notifications, interaction, violation handling, and community governance |
| Commercial Infrastructure | Ads, collaborations, traffic, and creator income         | Transparent revenue, system stability, and communicable redesign risks   |

The concept of BangBang allows users to browse and interact autonomously based on interest categories, highlights, star ratings, discussion volume, and date. [^14] This design reduces the monopoly feel of a single recommendation mechanism on large platforms, but does not mean governance problems disappear: spam, plagiarism, malicious attacks, commercial disclosures, illegal content, and ranking power still need to be addressed.

The platform must therefore simultaneously explain freedom and limits: Who can post? What content will be removed? Who manages the community? Are ranking criteria understandable? Can content be appealed after removal? These are not just product interface issues, but governance contracts formed when users hand over their daily lives to the platform.

## Taiwanese Life in Search Results

PIXNET’s most underestimated function is that it gives many experiences not covered by traditional media a searchable form. Restaurant reviews, parenting tips, accommodation evaluations, appliance comparisons, and local travel do not need to be recognized as major news first to qualify for existence.

The value of this content lies not in every article being authoritative, but in the fact that after massive personal experiences stack up, they form a map of life perception. Someone records a store’s queueing method, someone else supplements with rainy-day routes, someone writes what the same scenic spot means for families with children. Readers are not just looking for answers, but comparing whether other people’s lives are similar to their own situations.

In a 2021 interview, Liu Hao-en described his role at PIXNET as a "lighthouse" bringing external needs back to the company, emphasizing that Taiwanese amateurs can also contribute works. [^2] This highlights the difference between PIXNET and pure media platforms: its content ecosystem is not supported solely by a few professional authors, but relies on a large number of users without fixed titles.

However, life experience is not equivalent to facts themselves. Blogger reviews may be influenced by stance, cooperative relationships, or memory bias; search rankings do not equal content quality. PIXNET provides a field where experience is seen, not a place to stamp each experience with a seal of truth. Readers should still identify authors, dates, and cooperative relationships, and cross-verify with other sources.

📝 Curator’s Note: The public nature of blogs lies not in everyone becoming an expert, but in the fact that everyone’s experience may become another person’s starting point.

## Why Feature Sunset Feels Like Memory Being Moved

In 2024, PIXNET announced that the "New Type Article" function would terminate on June 28. The announcement specifically clarified that this does not apply to general blog articles, which remain usable, and advised users to save data in advance. [^4]

From a product management perspective, this is a feature sunset; from a user’s perspective, it feels like a room in the house suddenly being sealed off. The content may still be in the account, but the original layout, image arrangement, comment context, and link relationships may not be preserved in the same way.

Digital preservation includes at least four levels: first, files such as text, photos, and videos; second, structure such as categories, tags, comments, internal links, and article versions; third, visibility such as URLs, search indexing, and front-end presentation; fourth, portability such as backups, exports, and cross-system reading. Interruption at any level turns "the article is still there" into an incomplete answer.

For users, important articles can retain original text and image files, note publication dates, original URLs, and categories, and regularly check external links. This is not shifting platform responsibility to individuals, but acknowledging that digital memory requires multiple copies to reduce losses caused by single-platform failures.

## 2025–2026: Redesign Pushes Platform Contradictions to the Forefront

In December 2025, PIXNET’s open letter explained that full system restructuring and cloud migration caused member inconvenience, listing old architecture, insufficient core module support, and security risks as reasons for reconstruction. [^6] A concurrent announcement mentioned that comment and popularity data backfilling, photo album display, front-end styles, back-end management, and website indexing were being repaired sequentially. Another announcement explained login anomalies and ad system issues. [^5] [^8]

On December 19, the official announcement stated that old article content and symbols had been restored, explaining that for old articles before specific dates, a "retain original links, synchronize latest data" repair method was adopted. Articles edited during the anomaly period simultaneously retain the original repaired version and a new backup version. [^10] This reveals that the hardest thing for the platform to fix is not a single file, but "which version counts as the original article."

In February 2026, the official list included items still being adjusted or tested: editors, system photo albums, popular articles, latest comments, popularity statistics, custom CSS, image hosting performance, and article export. [^7] A May announcement previewed member database optimization and system maintenance, suspending login and back-end services during maintenance, but front-end articles remain browsable. [^11]

As of the verification date of this article, these announcements prove the platform is still maintaining, repairing, and restructuring; they do not allow the conclusion that all functions are permanently stable, nor should official repair progress be treated as external independent verification. For readers, the safest understanding is: PIXNET is still operating, but its technology and product forms continue to change.

📝 Curator’s Note: For the platform, redesign is engineering; for users, redesign is handing over ten years of their own writing to an unfamiliar system for recognition.

## Conclusion: Preservation is Not Stasis, But Being Carried Away

PIXNET’s history has two speeds. The company’s speed, from campus photo albums, commercialization, and community services to cloud architecture; the user’s speed, which is an article written in half an hour, potentially solving a small problem for strangers ten years later.

The two speeds cannot always align. The platform cannot refuse to handle old systems and security risks simply because old interfaces carry memory; users should not be required to bear all preservation responsibility alone because the platform needs updates. Mature digital culture should treat "how to update" and "how to preserve" as the same problem.

Therefore, what PIXNET is worth remembering is not a certain traffic ranking, nor whether a certain function was successful, but that it once allowed Taiwanese lives to accumulate in a low-threshold way. Starting from a photo on the NJUST campus, many things not appearing in chronologies were preserved in search engines and readers' memories.

PIXNET may not always maintain the same appearance, but as long as it understands that what users hand over is not just content, but a small, reviewable piece of life, then every product decision is not just a technical decision, but also a cultural responsibility.
