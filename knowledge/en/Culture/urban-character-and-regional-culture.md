# City Character and Regional Culture

## 30-Second Overview

Although Taiwan is small in size, each city has developed distinct local characteristics and cultural personalities. Taipei is the political and economic center and international gateway, Taichung is livable, Kaohsiung is transforming from industry, Tainan retains its historic charm, Taoyuan is an aviation hub, and Hsinchu is a tech center. This urban diversity reflects the geographical conditions, historical development, and industrial characteristics of different regions, forming distinct lifestyles and cultural identities among people.

In 2010, Taiwan upgraded New Taipei City, Taichung, and Tainan to city status, and merged Kaohsiung County and city into a single city, forming the "Five Great Cities." After Taoyuan was upgraded in 2014, the "Six Great Cities" were established, with approximately 70% of the population concentrated within the Six Great Cities' jurisdiction[^1], significantly impacting resource allocation and political ecology.

Beyond the Six Great Cities, Hsinchu Science Park and the East Coast tourism axis are also important components of Taiwan's urban landscape.

## Why It Matters

Understanding Taiwan's city characteristics is understanding the spatial structure and cultural geography of Taiwanese society. Each city's character is shaped by natural environment, historical opportunities, and humanistic choices. This regional diversity allows Taiwan to maintain unique internal diversity within a small area, forming the concrete foundation of local identity and cultural pride among Taiwanese people.

Taiwan's cities each play their roles in political positioning (capital Taipei), industrial functions (tech Hsinchu, manufacturing Taoyuan and Kaohsiung), and cultural accumulation (historic Tainan), forming a network that is geographically compact but functionally dispersed.

For foreign visitors, understanding different city characteristics helps plan more in-depth travel routes and experience the multi-dimensional face of Taiwanese society[^3][^4].

## Northern Taiwan: International Metropolitan Area

Northern Taiwan includes Taipei, New Taipei, Taoyuan, Keelung, and Hsinchu, with a combined population of approximately 10 million, accounting for over 40% of the total population[^1], making it the most densely populated urban area in Taiwan. Taipei's political centrality, Taoyuan's international airport, and Hsinchu's science park make this region the main window for Taiwan's external connections.

Taiwan's most important financial, technology, and media industries are concentrated along this corridor.

### Taipei City: Political and Cultural Center

**Keywords:** International metropolis, political center, cultural creativity, nightlife
**Population:** Approximately 2.42 million (2026, Taiwan's political and cultural center. Population ranks fourth among the Six Great Cities, below New Taipei, Taichung, and Kaohsiung)[^1]

As the capital of the Republic of China, Taipei is the political, economic, cultural, and media center of Taiwan. This city preserves traditional cultural foundations while embracing internationalization and modernization with an open attitude.

**City Character:**

- **Fast-paced life:** The most internationalized city in Taiwan, with a faster pace of life
- **Cultural diversity:** High density of museums, theaters, and galleries, with rich cultural activities
- **Vibrant nightlife:** Night markets, bars, 24-hour bookstores and cafes
- **Convenient transportation:** The MRT network is the most dense in Taiwan, with six main lines plus the airport MRT

Xinyi District is the modern financial and commercial core, and Taipei 101 (the world's tallest building when completed in 2004) is a landmark. Ximending has been an entertainment district since the Japanese colonial era, known for gathering young trend culture, earning the reputation of "Taipei's original street." Shilin Night Market, Dihua Street, and Yongkang Street each represent different aspects of tourist night markets, traditional festival goods, and cultural creative food.

**Cultural Characteristics:**
Taipei residents have strong international vision, high acceptance of new things, strong purchasing power, but also relatively high living pressure. Taipei is the source of Taiwan's popular culture, from music, fashion to food trends, often spreading from Taipei to the rest of Taiwan.

### New Taipei City: Diverse Satellite City

**Keywords:** Surrounding Taipei, diversified development, mountains and seas combined
**Population:** Approximately 4.04 million (the most populous city in Taiwan)[^1]

New Taipei City surrounds Taipei, including mountainous areas, coastal areas, industrial zones, and residential areas, making it an important part of the Taipei metropolitan area.

New Taipei is vast with diverse districts. The historic streets of Tamsui and the fishing port are the most popular weekend tourist spots in northern Taiwan. Jiufen and Jinguashi, mountain towns left behind by mining during the Japanese colonial era, are often associated with foreign travelers and the scenes from "Spirited Away." Yingge is famous for ceramic crafts, Sanxia Old Street is known for its intricate temple carvings, and Wulai is a mountainous area with Atayal settlements and hot springs.

### Taoyuan City: Aviation Hub

**Keywords:** International airport, developed industry, Hakka culture, diverse ethnic groups
**Population:** Approximately 2.36 million[^1]

Taoyuan became Taiwan's international gateway due to Taoyuan International Airport, with dense industrial zones and many multinational companies choosing to establish their Taiwan headquarters here.

**City Characteristics:**

- **High degree of internationalization:** Higher proportions of foreign workers and new immigrants
- **Industrial center:** Zhongli Industrial Zone, Guanyin Industrial Zone
- **Hakka culture:** Longtan and Yangmei retain strong Hakka characteristics
- **Pond culture:** Known as the "land of a thousand ponds," with unique water scenery

### Hsinchu City and County: Tech Center

**Keywords:** Science park, high-tech industry, wind city, Hakka culture
**Population:** Approximately 1.05 million (combined city and county)[^1]

Hsinchu rose to prominence as Taiwan's high-tech industry core after the establishment of the Hsinchu Science Park in 1980, gathering semiconductor leaders like TSMC and MediaTek, earning the reputation of "Taiwan's Silicon Valley" from foreign media.

**City Characteristics:**

- **High-income population:** High proportion of tech industry employees with strong purchasing power
- **International community:** Many foreign professionals
- **Wind city culture:** Strong northeast monsoon, developing unique wind culture
- **Education center:** Top universities like National Tsing Hua University and National Yang Ming Chiao Tung University[^2]

**Representative Areas:**

- **Hsinchu Science Park:** Core of Taiwan's semiconductor and IC design industries
- **Hsinchu City God Temple:** A thriving religious center with famous street food around it
- **Bupi Old Street:** Historical block of Qing Dynasty Hakka settlements preserved to this day
- **Nuanzhen Old Street:** Mountain town known for Hakka wild ginger powder

## Central Taiwan: Livability Circle

Taichung is the core city in central Taiwan. In July 2017, its population first surpassed Kaohsiung (2,778,182 people, 309 more than Kaohsiung), becoming the second-largest city in Taiwan[^6]. The Taichung Basin has mild climate, without scorching summers or severe winters, and has long ranked among the top in "Taiwan's Most Livability" polls.

### Taichung City: Livable City

**Keywords:** Pleasant climate, cultural creativity, food paradise, modern construction
**Population:** Approximately 2.87 million[^1]

Taichung is located in central Taiwan with a mild climate. In recent years, due to improvements in municipal construction and quality of life, many people consider it the most livable city in Taiwan.

**City Character:**

- **Leisurely pace:** Compared to Taipei's fast pace, Taichung residents live more leisurely
- **Food culture:** Birthplace of Taiwanese cuisine innovation, with rich snack culture
- **Cultural creativity:** Cultural creative spots like Judicial New Village and Fantasia Micro Creative Cluster
- **Modern construction:** MRT Green Line officially opened in April 2021, connecting Nantun, city center, and Wu-Ji High-Speed Rail Station[^7]

**Representative Areas:**

- **Jingmei Cultural and Creative Park:** Urban space combining shopping, culture, and greenery
- **Fengjia Night Market:** The largest night market in Taiwan, birthplace of innovative snacks
- **Zhonghua Street:** Student commercial district, gathering of youth culture
- **Taichung Cultural and Creative Arts Center:** Cultural space transformed from Japanese-style architecture

**Lifestyle Characteristics:**
Taichung residents value quality of life, with developed afternoon tea culture and high demands for food. Taichung is also an important birthplace of Taiwanese handcrafted drinks (such as bubble tea), with active innovative dining culture.

## Southern Taiwan: Historical and Cultural Circle

Southern Taiwan, centered around Tainan, Kaohsiung, and Pingtung, is the earliest developed region in Taiwan and the area with the most complete preservation of traditional culture. Tainan's Dutch Fort Zeelandia, the Ming-Zheng Palace, the Qing Dynasty government city streets, and Japanese colonial architecture coexist, forming the most concentrated historical and cultural landscape in Taiwan.

### Tainan City: Cultural Ancient City

**Keywords:** Historical relics, traditional culture, food paradise, slow living
**Population:** Approximately 1.85 million[^1]

Tainan is the oldest city in Taiwan: In 1624, the Dutch built Fort Zeelandia in Anping. Subsequently, through the Ming-Zheng and Qing dynasties, Tainan was the political center of Taiwan for over 200 years until the provincial government moved north in 1887 after the province was established. It still preserves the densest historical relic cluster in Taiwan and is famous for traditional snacks throughout Taiwan.

**City Character:**

- **Rich historical foundations:** Highest density of historical relics in Taiwan
- **Traditional culture preservation:** Temple culture and traditional crafts are active
- **Refined food culture:** Diverse snacks with traditional flavors
- **Slow life pace:** Values life quality, not rushing

**Cultural Characteristics:**

- **Religious culture:** Numerous temples with frequent religious activities
- **Government city snacks:** Classic snacks like coffin bread, Danzai noodles, and beef soup
- **Literary arts:** Strong literary creation atmosphere with rich artistic activities
- **Architectural culture:** Buildings from the Dutch, Ming-Zheng, and Japanese colonial periods coexist

**Representative Areas:**

- **Anping Old Fort:** Dutch colonial relics, starting point of Tainan's history
- **Chihkan Tower:** Ming-Zheng era architecture, landmark of Tainan
- **Confucius Temple:** First school in Taiwan, earliest Confucius Temple in Taiwan
- **Shennong Street:** Ancient and charming old street, especially beautiful at night with lights

### Kaohsiung City: Maritime Industrial City

**Keywords:** Port city, heavy industry, maritime culture, urban transformation
**Population:** Approximately 2.71 million[^1]

Kaohsiung is the largest port city in Taiwan, traditionally known for heavy industry. Since the 2000s, it has demolished old factories and improved the Love River, transforming into a maritime culture and tourism city.

**City Character:**

- **Maritime charm:** Rich port culture and maritime atmosphere
- **Industrial transformation:** From heavy industry to cultural creativity and tourism
- **Southern Taiwan's magnanimity:** Strong human feelings, straightforward character
- **International port city:** Close ties with international trade

**Transformation Development:**

- **Pier-2 Art Center:** Cultural and creative park transformed from industrial warehouses
- **Love River improvement:** From a polluted ditch to a romantic riverbank
- **Light rail system:** Taiwan's first light rail transportation system
- **Maritime culture:** Maritime Museum, maritime schools, and other maritime education

## Eastern Taiwan: Natural Tourism Axis

Eastern Taiwan, centered around Hualien and Taitung, has a combined population of approximately 520,000[^1], yet it boasts Taiwan's most spectacular natural landscapes including Taroko Gorge, the East China Sea Rift Valley, and the Coastal Mountain Range. Among Taiwan's 16 indigenous ethnic groups[^8], the Amis, Paiwan, Taroko, Saisiyat, Atayal, Bunun, Paiwan, Rukai, and Tsou all have major settlements in eastern Taiwan, making this region the richest repository of indigenous cultures in Taiwan.

### Hualien County: Mountain and Sea Symphony

**Keywords:** Taroko, indigenous culture, natural scenery, slow life
**Population:** Approximately 310,000[^1]

Hualien faces the Pacific Ocean and is backed by the Central Mountain Range. Taroko Gorge and Qingshui Cliffs form Taiwan's most spectacular natural scenery. The strong earthquake on April 3, 2024 severely damaged Taroko, with the park being restored in phases, and some recreational areas reopening on July 1, 2025, with full restoration estimated by 2031[^9].

**Geographical Features:**

- **Taroko National Park:** World-class canyon scenery
- **East China Sea coast:** Qingshui Cliffs, Qixingtan Coast
- **Indigenous culture:** Rich Amis and Taroko cultures
- **Organic agriculture:** Pollution-free environment with high-quality organic products

### Taitung County: Taiwan's Backyard

**Keywords:** Slow life, indigenous culture, hot air balloons, natural purity
**Population:** Approximately 208,000[^1]

Taitung is known as "Taiwan's backyard," preserving relatively pristine natural environments and leisurely lifestyles.

**Cultural Characteristics:**

- **Diverse indigenous cultures:** Amis, Paiwan, Paiwan, Rukai, Bunun, Tsou, and others coexist
- **Slow living culture:** Slow pace of life, emphasizing harmony with nature
- **Tourism agriculture:** Specialties like sugar apples and passion fruits
- **International Hot Air Balloon Festival:** Attracts large numbers of tourists every year

## Outlying Islands: Unique Charm

Taiwan's outlying islands are scattered across the Taiwan Strait and the eastern Pacific, each with unique geological and cultural backgrounds. Penghu is a basalt volcanic island, Kinmen and Matsu were military frontline islands during the Cold War, Orchid Island is the traditional territory of the Tsou (Yami) people, and Little Liuqiu is a coral reef uplift island.

Kinmen opened to tourism after lifting martial law in 1992, known for military relics, Minnan settlements, and sorghum liquor. Penghu attracts over a million tourists every year, with the summer fireworks festival being one of Taiwan's most famous outlying island celebrations.

Orchid Island's Flying Fish Festival culture and traditional canoe craftsmanship are precious representations of Taiwan's indigenous maritime civilization. In recent years, eco-tourism has replaced past nuclear waste storage controversies, becoming a case study for sustainable development of outlying islands.

### Penghu County: Island Scenery

**Features:** Basalt geology, seafood culture, migratory bird habitat

### Kinmen County: Battlefield Culture

**Features:** Military relics, Minnan architecture, sorghum liquor culture

### Lianjiang County (Matsu): Military Frontline

**Features:** Battlefield scenery, Minnan culture, blue tear wonder

## Regional Development Differences and Characteristics

The cultural differences between northern and southern Taiwan are significant, closely related to geographical conditions and historical development paths. This difference has formed distinct lifestyles and is the concrete reason why Taiwan maintains cultural diversity within a small area.

### North-South Cultural Differences

The northern Taipei metropolitan area has a fast pace and high degree of internationalization, with the highest proportion of immigrants in Taiwan, and political media culture radiating to the whole country, but urban density also brings higher living pressure. The central region of Taichung, Changhua, and Nantou has a moderate pace, and Taichung has often ranked among the top livable cities after the MRT opened in 2021; southern cities like Tainan and Kaohsiung have better preservation of traditional culture, with the highest temple density in Taiwan and closer human relationships. The eastern region of Hualien and Taitung has a sparse population (combined under 600,000), yet preserves Taiwan's most complete indigenous cultures and organic agricultural ecology.

### Urban-Rural Gap

The average salary in Taiwan's Six Great Cities is higher than in non-Six Great Cities counties and cities, with public construction and medical resources concentrated in urban areas, forming a long-term trend of young people flowing to the Six Great Cities. The outflow rate of young people in the East Coast area is particularly prominent, with population natural decrease and migration occurring simultaneously, becoming a challenge for balanced regional development.

Each city has clear industrial divisions: Taipei focuses on finance, business, and media culture; Taoyuan, Taichung, and Kaohsiung are manufacturing centers; Hsinchu Science Park, Taichung Science Park, and Tainan Science Park support the semiconductor and precision machinery industries; Tainan and the East Coast are strong in historical tourism and organic agriculture; Changhua, Yunlin, Chiayi, and Pingtung are the bases for Taiwan's food and agricultural products.

## Local Identity and Urban Competition

Taiwan's cities strengthen local identity through differentiated marketing, forming a healthy competitive and cooperative relationship. Taipei positions itself as an international metropolis and design capital, Taichung emphasizes livability quality, Tainan attracts tourists with its cultural ancient city and food paradise, Kaohsiung promotes port area transformation with the image of a maritime capital, and Hsinchu attracts tech talent with its science city and wind city reputation.

### Urban Marketing and Branding

Each city's brand has clear historical background: Tainan's "Cultural Ancient City" stems from nearly 300 years of historical accumulation from the Dutch period to the Qing Dynasty[^5]; Kaohsiung's maritime capital positioning was gradually established after the Pier-2 Art Center opened in 2002[^10] and the circular light rail construction in 2013[^11]; Hsinchu Science City started from the 1980 science park, attracting over 600 tech companies in 40 years.

### Local Identity and Dialect Culture

Residents of different cities have strong identification with their cities, often expressed through food, dialects, and lifestyles. For example, Tainan people's pride in snacks, Kaohsiung people's maritime character, and Taichung people's life aesthetics.

### Healthy Competition Between Cities

Kaohsiung's Pier-2 Art Center, which started from three port warehouses in 2002[^10], Taipei's Songshan Cultural and Creative Park opened in 2011, and Tainan's Ten Drum Cultural Creative Park are all cases of transforming industrial spaces into cultural venues. The practice of cities learning from each other has accelerated the overall level of old space regeneration in Taiwan.

## Future Development Trends

In 2007, the high-speed rail was fully opened, shortening the time from Taipei to Kaohsiung to 96 minutes and officially forming the "one-day living circle" in the western corridor, reshaping the competitive and division patterns of each city. Urban renewal, smart city construction, and regional integration have become the three major development axes for Taiwan's cities in the 2020s.

### Urban Renewal and Regeneration

Many cities have transformed old industrial zones into cultural and creative spaces, and historical buildings have been activated into cultural facilities. Taipei's Songshan Cultural and Creative Park (opened in 2011), Kaohsiung's Pier-2 Art Center, and Tainan's Ten Drum Cultural Creative Park are typical cases, also driving surrounding real estate and commercial activities.

### Smart City Development

Taiwan's cities are promoting smart city construction, using IoT, big data, and AI to improve urban governance. Taoyuan won the Smart City of the Year from the Global Smart City Forum (ICF) in 2019[^12], Taichung's smart traffic signal system covers major arterial roads, and Kaohsiung's A-Wan 5G AIoT Innovation Park is Taiwan's largest smart city experimental field.

### Regional Cooperation and Integration

The high-speed rail one-day living circle has led to the emergence of cross-domain commuting patterns such as "working in Greater Taipei, living in Tainan." With the progress of Tainan's MRT construction, academia predicts that by the 2030s, Taiwan's western region may form three connected metropolitan areas of northern, central, and southern, changing population distribution and industrial configuration patterns.

## Suggestions for Visitors to Taiwan

Taiwan is small in size but with significant city characteristics, it is recommended to choose routes based on travel purposes. First-time visitors to Taiwan are advised to take Taipei as a base (2-3 days), then take the high-speed rail south to Tainan or Taichung (1-2 days), which can experience the contrast between northern and southern city characters. In-depth cultural tourism can take Tainan's ancient city and Lukang Old Street as the main axis; natural scenery prefers Hualien's Taroko and Taitung's Chishang; modern urban charm is best represented by Taipei's Xinyi District combined with Kaohsiung's Pier-2 Art Center.

In terms of transportation, the high-speed rail connects the Six Great Cities in the west most quickly, and the Taiwan Railway Administration's island ring line can reach the east and south. Bus routes between attractions are economical and practical. Taipei's MRT network is the most dense, and Kaohsiung, Taoyuan, and Taichung also have MRT or light rail. After the airport MRT opened in 2017, the direct train from Taipei Station to Taoyuan Airport takes about 35 minutes.

Accommodation choices vary with city styles: Taipei has both boutique hotels and youth hostels; Tainan's cultural guesthouses transformed from historical courtyard houses are distinctive; the East Coast's seaside resorts allow tourists to experience the natural environment of the backyard up close.

## Extended Thinking

The formation of Taiwan's city characteristics reflects the complex interaction of geographical environment, historical opportunities, policy choices, and humanistic characteristics. In the era of globalization, how to enhance international competitiveness while maintaining local characteristics is a common challenge faced by all cities in Taiwan.

Since the Five Great Cities reform in 2010 and the establishment of the Six Great Cities in 2014, urban governance capabilities have significantly improved, and decisions made by each mayor on MRT construction, cultural policies, and tourism promotion have directly affected the cities' population attraction and investment environment.

This urban diversity constitutes Taiwan's most unique humanistic asset, allowing people on the same island to choose completely different lifestyles and cultural environments. Understanding the differences in city characteristics is the starting point for understanding the complexity and inclusiveness of Taiwanese society.
