# Agricultural Technology and Precision Agriculture

## 30-Second Overview

With less than 40,000 square kilometers of land, Taiwan has created an agricultural miracle that amazes the world. Starting from "the father of Penglai rice" (磯永吉), Taiwanese agriculture has achieved long-term innovation, developing high-value products such as orchids, pineapple-jackfruit, and high-mountain tea, which hold a key position in international markets. Combining IoT and AI technologies, Taiwan is moving toward the era of "Smart Agriculture 4.0."

Keywords: Precision Agriculture, Smart Agriculture, Orchid Kingdom, Pineapple-jackfruit, Agricultural Technology, Cultivar Improvement

## Why It Is Important

In this island nation with limited area, Taiwan had to replace "extensive farming" with "precision," and "manual labor" with "technology." Agriculture is no longer just a primary industry; it has become a sixth-level industry combining technology, cultural creativity (文創), and tourism. The success of Taiwanese precision agriculture proves that even small countries can find a unique position in global agricultural competition.

For Taiwan, precision agriculture represents the maximization of land efficiency (creating maximum value from limited land) and a paradigm of technological innovation (systematic technological advancement from breeding to cultivation).

- **International Competitive Advantage**: Establishing irreplaceable status for specific agricultural products
- **Sustainable Development Model**: An agricultural development path that is environmentally friendly, efficient, and high-value

## The History of Agricultural Transformation in Taiwan

### Traditional Farming Era (1950–1980)

In the early post-war period, Taiwanese agriculture focused on food self-sufficiency. The "land to the tiller" policy released rural productivity, combined with the promotion of high-yield rice varieties (the Green Revolution) and heavy use of chemical fertilizers.

- **Foreign Exchange Earnings**: Agricultural products such as rice, sugar, and tea were exported to earn foreign exchange.

This stage emphasized increasing "quantity" but lacked consideration for "quality."

### The Start of Precision Agriculture (1980–2000)

In the 1980s, Taiwan began promoting a "precision agriculture" policy. Agricultural research institutes cultivated high-quality new varieties, and facility farming such as greenhouses and net houses developed rapidly.

- **Quality Improvement**: Shifting from pursuing yield to pursuing quality.
- **Brand Building**: Brands like "Taiwan Orchids" and "Taiwan Tea" established reputations in major export markets.

The critical turning point was the realization that Taiwan could not compete with low-priced agricultural products and had to pursue a high-value strategy.

### The Rise of Technological Agriculture (2000–2010)

After joining the WTO, Taiwanese agriculture faced international competitive pressure. Biotechnology, such as molecular breeding and tissue culture, was widely applied, and GPS and GIS technologies were introduced into field management.

- **Cold Chain Logistics**: Developing preservation technology to extend the shelf life of agricultural products.
- **Organic Farming**: Responding to consumer demand for food safety.

### The Smart Agriculture Era (2010–Present)

With the maturation of IoT and AI technologies, Taiwan promoted "Smart Agriculture 4.0." Sensors collect environmental data, AI analyzes optimal cultivation conditions, and robots and drones reduce labor requirements.

- **Blockchain Traceability**: Establishing a food safety tracking system from planting to consumption.
- **Circular Agriculture**: Resource utilization of waste, establishing a sustainable production model.

## Success Cases in Precision Agriculture

### The Orchid Kingdom: Taiwan's Orchid Industry

Taiwan holds the reputation of an "Orchid Kingdom" and is one of the world's most important producers and exporters of orchids:

**Industry Scale**: In 2023, total orchid exports reached $197.68 million (approx. NT$6 billion), with Phalaenopsis accounting for $159.38 million, or about 80%. The top five markets are sequentially the United States, Japan, Vietnam, the Netherlands, and Canada [^1]. Taiwan accounts for approximately 30% of the global Phalaenopsis market; the Netherlands surpassed Taiwan to become number one around 2014 with a share of about 5% [^8].

**Technological Advantages**: Tissue culture technology supports mass propagation of high-quality seedlings, and hybrid breeding creates new varieties with independent intellectual property rights. Precise environmental control (temperature, humidity, light) combined with standardized production processes ensures quality consistency.

**International Competitiveness**: Taiwan's market share in the US import market for Phalaenopsis rose from 48% in 2005 to 68% in 2010 [^9]. Vietnam is the third-largest export market in 2023, and Taiwanese enterprises have established factories there [^1].

**Key Success Factors**
The Tainan Agricultural Research Center under the Ministry of Agriculture has long undertaken orchid variety research and industry guidance [^2], covering the entire industrial chain from collecting and preserving native orchids to hybrid breeding, tissue culture, and cultivation techniques. In terms of overseas sales channels, the Taiwan Orchid Biotechnology Park in Houbi, Tainan, is a major hub for Phalaenopsis exports. Seedlings are transported via air cold chains, which also drives the Taiwanese air cargo system and flower cold storage.

### Pineapple-jackfruit: A New Star in Taiwanese Agriculture

Pineapple-jackfruit is not a variety bred in Taiwan, but it serves as an example of how Taiwanese cultivation technology transformed an exotic variety into an industry:

**Variety Characteristics**: Pineapple-jackfruit is a hybrid species of _Cucumis melo_ and _Cucumis anguria_. The main variety cultivated in Taiwan, Gefner, was first introduced for trial from Israel in 1965. It only formed an industry starting in 1997 after the Taitung Agricultural Research Center developed winter fruit through pruning techniques [^4]. The flesh is dense and sweet (about 20–25 degrees), giving it a pineapple-like fragrance, making it less prone to cracking and easier to transport.

**Industry Data**: The main production area is Taitung County, accounting for 99% of the total cultivation area. The cultivated area decreased from 2,951 hectares in 2021 to 2,454 hectares in 2025, with an estimated output of about 11,000 tons in 2025. Before the ban in 2021, 95% of exports were concentrated in China [^4].

**Technological Breakthroughs**: Grafting technology allows for a harvest in the year following grafting; ripening regulation technology extends the harvesting period to December through April of the next year; standardized grading and packaging ensure export quality, and post-harvest storage technology extends shelf life.

**Challenges and Opportunities**
China's comprehensive ban on Taiwanese pineapple-jackfruit in September 2021 led to special import permits being granted only to 25 orchards and 3 packing houses in 2023. In 2024, the tariff was increased from zero to 29%. Since 2021, the Ministry of Agriculture has provided incentives ranging from NT$150,000 to NT$200,000 per hectare for conversion, resulting in over 200 hectares being converted and encouraging processing. In 2025, processed fruit exceeded 600 tons, with domestic sales exceeding 5% for the first time [^4]. This forced transformation has made the pineapple-jackfruit industry directly confront the structural problem of reliance on a single market.

### High-Mountain Oolong Tea: A Miracle Created by Geography

High-mountain tea in Taiwan is a typical representative of precision agriculture:

**Geographical Advantages**: The high-mountain environment above 1,000 meters, large day-night temperature differences (favorable for nutrient accumulation in the leaves), natural shading from mist and clouds, and good soil drainage—these four conditions jointly create the unique quality of high-mountain Oolong tea.

**Quality Characteristics**: The liquor is fragrant and has a sweet aftertaste, with rich layers; the leaves are thick and durable for brewing. Each producing area's mountains have distinct character and have won numerous major awards in international tea competitions.

**Famous Producing Areas**: Alishan Tea Area (1,000–1,700 meters, famous for Pearl Dew tea) and Lishan Tea Area (above 2,000 meters, Taiwan's highest-altitude tea area) are two flagship areas; Shalin Stream Tea Area is perpetually misty, yielding sweet tea; Dayungling Tea Area at 2,200–2,600 meters has scarce production but excellent quality, often being in short supply during the purchasing season.

## Application of Smart Agriculture Technology

### Precision Agriculture Technology

The three core technological modules of precision agriculture support each other: environmental monitoring systems instantly capture soil temperature, moisture, pH value, EC value, and meteorological data, providing early warnings for pests and diseases; automated irrigation systems adjust water volume based on soil moisture, and integrated water-fertilizer systems precisely fertilize, improving water efficiency by 30–50% and reducing labor costs by 60%; the production traceability system records all information from planting to harvesting using blockchain technology, allowing consumers to check via QR code.

### AI Artificial Intelligence Applications

Major application scenarios for AI in agriculture include: drones equipped with multispectral cameras monitor crop growth; AI analyzes plant diseases with an accuracy exceeding 95%, improving automated quality grading; in terms of predictive analysis, AI combines historical meteorological data to forecast optimal cultivation times, market prices, and the probability of pest outbreaks; smart decision support systems integrate sensor data to provide cultivation advice and regulate greenhouse environments, reducing overall production costs by 20–30%.

### Agricultural Machinery Automation and Drone Spraying

Drone spraying only gained legal status in Taiwan after the Civil Aeronautics Act included a special chapter on remote-controlled drones in March 2020. Operators must hold both professional operation certificates from the Civil Aeronautics Administration and registration as pesticide application technicians from the Food and Drug Administration. In July 2020, only 227 people across Taiwan held both certifications, and only 5 spraying companies passed inspection [^5]. Subsequently, the Ministry of Agriculture included agricultural spraying drones in the "Labor-Saving, High-Efficiency, and Carbon Sequestration Agricultural Machinery Subsidy," requiring legal sprayers to display flags for identification starting in 2025 [^10]. Previously, two people might spend a day spraying one hectare; now, one person can finish half a day with a spraying machine and work in hilly or remote farmland, filling the labor shortage. Fruit and vegetable harvesting robots are still in the field verification stage.

## Government Policies and Support

### Smart Agriculture 4.0 Plan

The Council of Agriculture proposed the "Smart Agriculture 4.0" promotion plan in 2016, implemented from 2017 to 2020:

**Plan Objectives**: Ten leading industries—including orchids, seedlings, mushrooms, rice crops, agricultural facilities, aquaculture, poultry (waterfowl), traceable products, raw milk, and marine fisheries—were selected. The goal was to reduce labor demand and improve production efficiency through smart production and digital services, while establishing three technical promotion groups: smart production and human-machine aids, agricultural technology expert systems, and digital services and traceability data exchange [^3].

**Key Items**: The plan started with smart production (sensors, automated equipment) and digital services (agricultural cloud platforms), extending to agricultural finance (big data improving financing) and cross-domain cooperation (integration of ICT industry and agriculture).

### New Agriculture Innovation Promotion Plan

The Executive Yuan promoted the "New Agriculture Innovation Promotion Plan" (2017–2020), focusing on three main pillars: establishing a new agricultural paradigm, building an agricultural security system, and improving agricultural marketing capabilities, with ten key policies underneath. The 2.0 version from 2021 to 2024 focused on three main pillars: enhancing the farmer welfare system, improving the basic environment, and increasing industry competitiveness [^11].

### Organic Agriculture Promotion Act

The _Organic Agriculture Promotion Act_ was promulgated in May 2018 and enforced in May 2019. It incorporated friendly farming into guidance and established organic agriculture promotion zones, providing certification subsidies and marketing promotion. The target of 15,000 hectares for organic and friendly farming set by the New Agriculture Plan was achieved with 15,754 hectares by the end of 2020 [^11].

## Challenges and Future Development

### Major Challenges

Taiwan's precision agriculture faces four structural challenges. Climate: The frequency of extreme weather events is increasing, and pest patterns are changing, requiring climate-resilient varieties and disaster warning systems. Labor: According to the 2020 survey on agriculture, forestry, and fishery, the average age of farm and livestock operators was 64.4 years, an increase of 0.9 years from five years prior [^12], and the proportion of youth in farming is relatively low, leading to a gap in technological succession. Market: Competition from low-priced Southeast Asia is intensifying, protectionism is rising, and differentiated advantages need strengthening. Environment: Carbon reduction pressure requires agriculture to reduce the use of chemical fertilizers and pesticides, promoting circular agricultural models.

### Future Development Directions

The next step for precision agriculture is the popularization of sensor technology, iteration of AI analysis, and some fields achieving unmanned operation. Biotechnology applications focus on gene-editing breeding, biological pest control as a substitute for pesticides, soil improvement using probiotics, and commercializing plant factories. The circular economy direction includes resource utilization of agricultural waste, alternative proteins like insect protein, and agriculture carbon credit trading. International cooperation focuses on exporting agricultural technology to Southeast Asia and Africa, developing agricultural technology services, and establishing overseas agricultural production bases.

## International Influence and Future Vision

The international influence of Taiwanese precision agriculture is primarily in the form of technological export: orchid enterprises establish factories in Vietnam, and agricultural technical groups are dispatched to friendly countries annually. In terms of branding, "Taiwan Orchids" has established a clear price premium in high-end markets in the United States, while "Taiwan Tea" has also established a distinct quality premium in high-end consumer markets in Japan and Southeast Asia.

The transformation path of Taiwanese agriculture—from Penglai rice breeding in the 1920s to AI image recognition for pest control today—is essentially a systematic raising of technological barriers. The fact that Phalaenopsis accounts for nearly 70% of the US import market, and that 99% of the industry centered on Taitung relies on grafting techniques from Israel, illustrates the concrete meaning of "precision" in the Taiwanese agricultural context: it is a market position with a moat, not merely a quality claim.
