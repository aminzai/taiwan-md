# Agricultural Technology and Refined Agriculture

## 30-Second Overview

Taiwan has created an agricultural miracle that amazes the world with less than 40,000 square kilometers of land. Starting with So Eikichi (磯永吉), known as the "Father of Penglai Rice," Taiwan's agriculture has long been characterized by continuous innovation, developing refined agricultural products such as orchids, pineapple guava (cherimoya), and high-mountain tea, securing key positions in international markets. By integrating new technologies such as the Internet of Things (IoT) and AI, Taiwan is moving toward the era of "Smart Agriculture 4.0."

Keywords: Refined Agriculture, Smart Agriculture, Orchid Kingdom, Pineapple Guava, Agricultural Technology, Variety Improvement

## Why It Matters

In an island nation with limited land area, Taiwan must replace "extensive" farming with "refined" farming, and "labor" with "technology." Agriculture is no longer just a primary industry; it is a sixth-level industry that integrates technology, cultural creativity, and tourism. Taiwan's success in refined agriculture proves that small countries can find unique positions in global agricultural competition.

For Taiwan, refined agriculture represents the maximization of land efficiency (creating maximum value from limited land) and a paradigm of technological innovation (systematic technologicalization from breeding to cultivation).

- **International Competitive Advantage**: Establishing irreplaceable positions in specific agricultural products.
- **Sustainable Development Model**: An agricultural development path that is environmentally friendly, highly efficient, and high-value.

## Taiwan's Agricultural Transformation Journey

### Traditional Agriculture Era (1950–1980)

In the early post-war period, Taiwan's agriculture aimed for food self-sufficiency. The "Land-to-the-Tiller" policy released rural productive forces, combined with the promotion of high-yield rice varieties (Green Revolution) and the heavy use of chemical fertilizers.

- **Foreign Exchange Earnings**: Exporting agricultural products such as rice, sugar, and tea to earn foreign exchange.

This stage focused on increasing "quantity" but lacked consideration for "quality."

### The Rise of Refined Agriculture (1980–2000)

In the 1980s, Taiwan began promoting "Refined Agriculture" policies. The Taiwan Agricultural Research Institute cultivated high-quality new varieties, and facility agriculture such as greenhouses and net houses developed rapidly.

- **Quality Improvement**: Shifting from pursuing yield to pursuing quality.
- **Brand Building**: Brands such as "Taiwan Orchids" and "Taiwan Tea" had already established reputations in major export markets.

The key turning point was the realization that Taiwan could not compete in low-priced agricultural products and had to follow a high-value route.

### The Rise of Tech-Driven Agriculture (2000–2010)

After joining the WTO, Taiwan's agriculture faced international competitive pressure. Biological technologies such as molecular breeding and tissue culture were widely applied, and GPS and GIS technologies were introduced into farm management.

- **Cold Chain Logistics**: Developing preservation technologies to extend the shelf life of agricultural products.
- **Organic Agriculture**: Responding to consumer demand for food safety.

### The Era of Smart Agriculture (2010–Present)

With the maturity of IoT and AI technologies, Taiwan promoted "Smart Agriculture 4.0." Sensors collect environmental data, AI analyzes optimal cultivation conditions, and robots and drones reduce labor needs.

- **Blockchain Traceability**: Establishing a food safety tracking system from planting to consumption.
- **Circular Agriculture**: Resource utilization of waste to establish sustainable production models.

## Success Stories in Refined Agriculture

### The Orchid Kingdom: Taiwan's Orchid Industry

Taiwan enjoys the reputation of the "Orchid Kingdom" and is one of the most important producers and exporters of orchids in the world:

**Industry Scale**: The annual output value is approximately NT$6 billion. In 2023, the total value of orchid exports was nearly US$200 million (US$197.68 million) [^1]. Taiwan's butterfly orchid seedlings hold about one-third of the global market share [^1], with approximately 10,000 practitioners.

**Technological Advantages**: Tissue culture technology supports the mass propagation of high-quality seedlings. Hybrid breeding creates new varieties with independent intellectual property rights. Precision environmental control (temperature, humidity, light) combined with standardized production processes ensures consistent quality.

**International Competitiveness**: Major export markets include the United States, Japan, the European Union, and South Korea. Taiwan holds over 70% market share in the US high-end orchid (potted plants/seedlings) market. Taiwan has also established production bases in China and Vietnam to export technology.

**Key to Success**
The orchid research team at the Tainan District Agricultural Research and Extension Station (TNDARES) spent 30 years building the orchid industry system [^2]. From the collection and preservation of native orchids to hybrid breeding, tissue culture, and cultivation techniques, it covers the entire industry chain from breeding to export. In terms of export channels, Houbi in Tainan and Tianwei in Changhua are the main distribution centers for butterfly orchid seedlings. US floral retail channels (supermarkets such as Costco, Trader Joe's, and Whole Foods) are the largest mass markets. The Aalsmeer Flower Auction in the Netherlands and the Cultivate exhibition in the US are main stages for expanding into high-end wholesalers in Europe and America. The Japanese breeding licensing model allows Taiwan orchid companies to continue benefiting from variety intellectual property revenue. Orchid seedlings are transported via air cold chain, which has a significant driving effect on Taiwan's air cargo system and floral cold storage.

### Pineapple Guava: Taiwan's New Agricultural Star

Pineapple guava (cherimoya) is a masterpiece of Taiwan's agricultural technology, showcasing the results of variety improvement and refined cultivation:

**Variety Characteristics**: Combining Peruvian cherimoya with native Taiwan cherimoya, the flesh is dense and extremely sweet (approximately 20–25 degrees Brix), with a pineapple-like fragrance, hence the name. It is resistant to fruit cracking, durable in transport, and aesthetically pleasing.

**Industry Data**: The main production area is Taitung County (accounting for over 90% of the total in Taiwan), with an annual output of approximately 30,000 metric tons. Before the 2021 China ban, the proportion of exports to China was high (citations vary); after the ban, the export structure changed significantly [^4]. Farmers' income can reach NT$1–1.5 million per hectare annually.

**Technological Breakthroughs**: Grafting technology allows pineapple guava branches to bear fruit the year after grafting. Production adjustment techniques extend the harvest period from December to April of the following year. Grading and packaging standards ensure export quality, and post-harvest storage and transport technologies extend shelf life.

**Challenges and Opportunities**
After China suspended imports in 2021, Taiwan developed alternative markets in Japan, Hong Kong, and Southeast Asia. This forced transformation has led the pineapple guava industry to seriously address the structural issue of reducing dependence on a single market.

### High-Mountain Oolong Tea: A Miracle Created by Geography

Taiwan high-mountain tea is a typical representative of refined agriculture:

**Geographical Advantages**: High-mountain environments above 1,000 meters, large temperature differences between day and night (favorable for nutrient accumulation in tea leaves), natural shading by clouds and mist, and well-drained soil. These four conditions together create the unique quality of high-mountain oolong tea.

**Quality Characteristics**: The tea soup has a clear fragrance and sweet aftertaste with rich layers. The leaves are thick and durable for multiple infusions. Each production area has distinct mountain characteristics, and it has won multiple grand prizes in international tea competitions.

**Famous Production Areas**: The Ali Mountain Tea Area (altitude 1,000–1,700 meters, famous for Zhulu Tea) and the Lishan Tea Area (altitude above 2,000 meters, Taiwan's highest altitude tea area) are the two flagship production areas. The Shanlinxi Tea Area is surrounded by clouds and mist year-round, with sweet and beautiful tea quality. The Dayu Ridge Tea Area (altitude 2,200–2,600 meters) has scarce output but excellent quality, often experiencing supply shortages during the purchasing season.

## Applications of Smart Agriculture Technologies

### Precision Agriculture Technologies

The three core technology modules of precision agriculture support each other: Environmental monitoring systems instantly grasp soil temperature and humidity, pH, EC values, and meteorological data, providing early warnings for pests and diseases; automated irrigation systems adjust water volume based on soil moisture, while integrated water and fertilizer systems fertilize precisely, improving water-saving efficiency by 30–50% and reducing labor costs by 60%; production traceability systems use blockchain technology to record full-process information from planting to harvest, allowing consumers to scan QR codes for queries.

### AI Artificial Intelligence Applications

Main application scenarios of AI in agriculture include: Drones equipped with multispectral cameras monitor crop growth, with AI analysis achieving plant disease accuracy of over 95%, improving efficiency through automated quality grading; in predictive analysis, AI combines historical meteorological data to predict optimal cultivation timing, market prices, and pest occurrence probabilities; smart decision support systems integrate sensor data to provide cultivation advice and adjust greenhouse environments, reducing overall production costs by 20–30%.

### Agricultural Machinery Automation and Drone Plant Protection

Taiwan's agricultural drone industry has grown rapidly in recent years. In 2023, the operational area of agricultural drones in Taiwan exceeded 1.5 million hectares, with registered units exceeding 3,000, making it one of the regions with the highest density of agricultural drones in the world [^5]. Compared to traditional ground spraying, drone plant protection can reduce pesticide use per hectare by over 30% and can operate in mountainous and remote farmlands, filling labor shortages. The Ministry of Agriculture provides purchase subsidies for agricultural machinery such as rice harvesters, rice transplanters, and drones, helping farmers reduce the initial costs of mechanization transformation. Although fruit and vegetable harvesting robots are still in the field verification stage, multiple startups have demonstrated mass-producible prototypes for crops such as strawberries and cucumbers, expected to enter commercialization in 2026–2028.

## Government Policies and Support

### Smart Agriculture 4.0 Plan

The Executive Yuan launched the "Smart Agriculture 4.0" plan in 2017:

**Plan Objectives**: Establish 10 smart agriculture demonstration sites, cultivate 1,000 smart agriculture talents, and increase agricultural output value by 20% [^3].

**Key Projects**: The plan starts from smart production (sensors, automated equipment) and digital services (agricultural cloud platforms), extending to agricultural finance (big data improving financing) and cross-domain cooperation (ICT industry integrating with agriculture).

### New Agricultural Innovation Promotion Plan

The New Agricultural Innovation Promotion Plan supports industrial upgrading along three axes: Variety improvement (Agricultural Biotechnology and Research Center, seed industry R&D subsidies, plant variety rights protection, international breeding cooperation); Facility agriculture (subsidies for smart greenhouses and plant factories, promotion of environmental control facilities, development of vertical farms); Agricultural value-added (upgrading primary agricultural products to secondary and tertiary products, promoting the concept of "sixth-level industrialization" and brand marketing).

### Organic Agriculture Promotion Act

The Organic Agriculture Promotion Act, enacted in 2018, established organic agriculture zones, provided verification subsidies, and promoted marketing, with the goal of reaching 15,000 hectares of organic cultivation area.

## Challenges and Future Development

### Main Challenges

Taiwan's refined agriculture faces four structural challenges. Climate: Increasing frequency of extreme weather and changing pest patterns require stress-resistant varieties and disaster warning systems. Labor: The average age of the agricultural workforce is about 63, with a low proportion of young farmers, facing a break in technology transmission. Market: Intensifying low-price competition from Southeast Asia and rising trade protectionism require strengthening differentiated advantages. Environment: Carbon reduction pressure requires agriculture to reduce the use of chemical fertilizers and pesticides and develop circular agriculture models.

### Future Development Directions

The next step for precision agriculture is the popularization of sensor technology, AI analysis iteration, and unmanned operation in some fields. Biotechnology applications focus on gene-edited breeding, biological control pesticides replacing chemical ones, probiotic soil improvement, and commercialization of plant factories. The circular economy direction includes agricultural waste resource utilization, alternative proteins such as insect protein, and agricultural carbon credit trading. International cooperation focuses on exporting agricultural technology to Southeast Asia and Africa, developing agricultural technology service industries, and establishing overseas agricultural production bases.

## International Impact and Future Vision

Taiwan's international impact in refined agriculture is primarily in the form of technology export: The orchid industry technology has been exported to China and Vietnam, and agricultural experts are stationed in friendly countries and participate in UN Sustainable Development Goals projects. In terms of branding, "Taiwan Orchids" have established a clear quality premium in the US high-end market, and "Taiwan Tea" has done so in the high-end consumer markets of Japan and Southeast Asia.

Taiwan's agricultural transformation path—from Penglai rice breeding in the 1920s to AI image recognition for pests and diseases today—is essentially a systematic increase in technological barriers. Orchid seedlings account for 70% of the US high-end market, and pineapple guava generates an annual income of millions of New Taiwan dollars per hectare. These two figures illustrate the specific meaning of "refined" in Taiwan's agricultural context: a market position with a moat, rather than a simple quality claim.
