# Taiwan's Circular Economy and Resource Recycling

## 30-Second Overview

Taiwan began promoting resource recycling systems in the 1990s and developed a circular economy model with international visibility. Through the 3R principle of "reduce, reuse, recycle," Taiwan's general waste recycling rate reached 58.55% in 2024 (Ministry of Environment statistics, calculated as recyclable and kitchen waste collection volume divided by general waste generation) [^4]. In August 2023, the Environmental Protection Administration was upgraded to the Ministry of Environment and established the "Resource Circulation Administration," making circular economy a national priority policy [^3].

**Keywords**: circular economy, 3R principle, resource recycling, garbage sorting, waste management, sustainable development

## Why It Matters

Taiwan's circular economy development holds global demonstration significance. The geographical conditions of limited land and scarce natural resources have forced Taiwan to take waste management to the extreme — this not only directly improves living environment and public health but also spurs emerging green industries. Advanced resource circulation technology has become Taiwan's soft power export, while the operation of the entire system is also changing people's consumption patterns and promoting the formation of sustainable living culture.

## History of Taiwan's Circular Economy Development

### Stage 1: Establishment of Resource Recycling System (1990-2005)

**Policy Origins**: After the lifting of martial law in 1987, environmental awareness awakened, and the "garbage wars" became an opportunity for policy reform. In 1988, amendments to the "Waste Disposal Act" introduced the Extended Producer Responsibility system; in 1997, the "Four-in-One Resource Recycling Program" was launched; in 2002, the "Resource Recycling and Reuse Act" was promulgated and implemented [^1][^3].

**Important Milestones**:

- **2000**: Taipei City pioneered the "pay-as-you-throw" garbage fee system (starting July 1) [^9]
- **2002**: Restrictions on shopping plastic bags and plastic tableware were implemented in stages (starting July 1) [^10]

**System Features**: Extended Producer Responsibility is the core of the system, managed through the Resource Recycling Fund, with community residents, recyclers, local governments, and the recycling fund each playing their roles [^8].

### Stage 2: System Optimization and Technology Enhancement (2005-2015)

The core of this stage was the simultaneous deepening of technology and systems. Waste incineration technology improved, automated sorting systems were introduced, and hazardous waste treatment technology matured. At the same time, green procurement systems, eco-label certification systems, and strengthened industrial waste management were also promoted. By 2015, the resource recycling rate stabilized above 50%, the garbage collection and transportation system became increasingly perfect, and the recycling industry chain took shape during this period.

### Stage 3: Circular Economy Transformation (2015-Present)

**Policy Upgrade**:

- **2018**: The Executive Yuan passed the "Circular Economy Promotion Plan" (December 20), with "circular industrialization" and "industrial circulation" as the main axes [^2]
- **2022**: The Environmental Protection Administration approved the "New Generation of Circular Economy — Resource Circulation Promotion Plan (2011-2023)" [^4], targeting biomass, organic chemicals, metals, and inorganic recycled materials
- **2023**: The Environmental Protection Administration was upgraded to the Ministry of Environment, establishing the Resource Circulation Administration [^3]
- **2023 onwards**: The "Reduction, Recycling, and Resource Circulation Promotion Plan (2012-2026)" continued to be promoted, focusing on three things [^6]:

1. Amending the "Resource Circulation Promotion Act" and the "Waste Disposal Act" — the two laws on resource circulation
2. Inventorying Taiwan's key and strategic resources, promoting domestic circulation
3. Creating the "Green Taiwan" brand: establishing recycled material management mechanisms, introducing digital product tracking

**Target Setting**: The Ministry of Environment has set the long-term goal of building a circular society that maximizes resource circulation and minimizes waste generation. Specific annual indicators are set in the administrative plan each year. For example, in 2024, the target for general waste recycling rate was 55.5%, and the target for slag circulation rate was 73%; the actual results were 58.55% and 83% respectively [^4].

## Resource Recycling System Architecture

### Legal Foundation and Policy Framework

Taiwan's resource recycling legal foundation is jointly constituted by the "Resource Recycling and Reuse Act," the "Waste Disposal Act," and the Executive Yuan's "Circular Economy Promotion Plan." Implementation follows the 3R principle: Reduce (source reduction of waste), Reuse (extending product life cycle), and Recycle (converting waste into renewable resources).

### Four-in-One Recycling System

The Four-in-One system allows the public, recyclers, recycling funds, and government agencies to each play their roles: the public sorts garbage at the source and cooperates with scheduled collection; recyclers are responsible for waste collection, sorting, and recycled material quality control; recycling funds collect recycling and processing fees and distribute incentives to maintain system operation; government agencies are responsible for policy formulation, supervision and inspection, and publicity and education.

### Garbage Sorting System

**Basic Categories**:

- **General Garbage**: Non-recyclable mixed waste
- **Recyclable Resources**: Materials that can be reused
- **Kitchen Waste**: Organic waste that can be made into compost

**Recyclable Resources Subcategories**: Paper (cardboard, newspapers, magazines) and plastic (PET bottles, plastic bags, containers) are the two largest categories, followed by:

- **Metal**: Iron cans, aluminum cans, etc.
- **Glass**: Glass bottles, glass containers
- **Others**: Batteries, light bulbs, electronics, etc.

## Waste Treatment Technology and Facilities

### Incineration Treatment System

There are 28 incineration facilities across the country (including 24 large-scale garbage incineration plants), using high-temperature incineration combined with pollution prevention equipment. The designed processing capacity totals about 24,000 tons per day. In 2024, the actual annual processing was 6.81 million tons of garbage, generating about 3.4 billion kWh of electricity, achieving both waste incineration and thermal energy recovery. Most large-scale incineration plants have been operating for over 20 years, and the Ministry of Environment launched the upgrade and renovation project for 16 incineration plants starting at the end of 2024 [^4].

**Processing Process**: Garbage is received and temporarily stored before entering high-temperature incineration, flue gas passes through pollution control equipment, bottom ash is reused after treatment, and thermal energy is converted into electricity — five links form a zero-waste circular system.

### Landfill Management

Landfills are equipped with anti-seepage systems, leachate treatment facilities, and biogas collection and utilization devices, with a three-layer environmental monitoring network for groundwater, air quality, and soil pollution, ensuring long-term environmental safety.

### Kitchen Waste Treatment Technology

Kitchen waste is processed through composting (biological decomposition, temperature-controlled fermentation, finished product quality inspection) to produce organic fertilizers, or through anaerobic digestion processes to recover biogas and produce liquid fertilizers, achieving energy circulation utilization of organic waste.

## Circular Economy Industry Development

### Waste Regeneration Industry

Taiwan's waste regeneration industry has formed three main forces: plastic regeneration (PET bottle reprocessing fibers, waste plastic injection molding), metal recycling (waste iron and steel reprocessing, non-ferrous metal smelting), and paper regeneration (waste paper pulp manufacturing, regenerated paper products).

### Innovative Circulation Technology

In terms of innovative technology, three paths are being promoted simultaneously: biomass materials (biodegradable plastics, agricultural waste utilization, biofuels), urban mining (electronic waste precious metal recovery, rare earth element extraction), and green chemistry (non-toxic solvents, green processes), representing Taiwan's circular economy moving toward higher technological content [^7].

### Industrial Chain Symbiosis

Industrial park ecologyization transforms individual companies' waste into raw materials for neighboring companies through waste exchange utilization, energy ladder utilization, and water resource circulation. This is coordinated with industrial waste matching platforms, recycled material certification systems, and green supply chain management to form a symbiotic network at the industrial park level.

## Government Policies and Promotion Measures

### National-Level Promotion Plans

**From "Resource Recycling and Reuse Promotion Plan" to "Reduction, Recycling, and Resource Circulation Promotion Plan"**: The Executive Yuan's "Resource Recycling and Reuse Promotion Plan (2008-2010)" [^11] was divided into four key areas based on the product life cycle: production, consumption, recycling, and circulation. It included promoting easily recyclable product design, facilitating industrial chain symbiosis, creating green consumption patterns, and promoting government green procurement. Starting in 2023, the "Reduction, Recycling, and Resource Circulation Promotion Plan (2012-2026)" [^6] focused on three things:

1. Amending the "Resource Circulation Promotion Act" and the "Waste Disposal Act" — the two laws on resource circulation
2. Inventorying Taiwan's key and strategic resources, promoting domestic circulation
3. Creating the "Green Taiwan" brand: establishing recycled material management mechanisms, introducing digital product tracking

**Verifiable Results**: In 2024, the announced recyclable waste and container recycling volume was 1.54 million tons, the general waste recycling rate was 58.55%, and the government's green procurement total amount exceeded NT$89.1 billion [^4][^11].

### Legal and Regulatory System Innovation

Extended Producer Responsibility makes manufacturers responsible for recycling throughout the entire product life cycle, internalizing recycling costs and promoting design-stage recycling considerations. The green procurement system leads the government to purchase eco-friendly products, combined with private enterprise incentives and eco-label promotion to create market pulling force. Plastic restriction policies control disposable plastic products at the source, promoting reusable alternatives and implementing paid plastic bag usage.

### Technology Research and Development Support

The government promotes circular economy technology research and development, demonstration site construction, and talent cultivation through industry-academia-research cooperation; externally, it participates in international circular economy organizations, invests in technical standards formulation, and shares Taiwan's best practices.

## Public Participation and Environmental Awareness

### Daily Life Practices

Taiwan's public environmental protection daily practices cover three aspects: garbage sorting culture (detailed classification habits, cleaning recyclables, scheduled collection cooperation) has been deeply embedded in daily life; frugality and cherishing resources drive item repair and reuse, the rise of second-hand trading platforms, and participation in the sharing economy; environmental consumption choices are reflected in prioritizing eco-friendly products, reducing over-packaged goods, and supporting local production.

### Education and Promotion Results

On the school side, environmental education is integrated into teaching, campus resource recycling is promoted, and environmental protection talents are cultivated; at the community level, neighborhood recycling stations are set up, cleanup activities are organized, and environmental protection volunteers are formed. Media promotion makes environmental protection concepts popular, driving policy support rates up, and the overall behavioral change results have been concretely demonstrated.

## International Status and Achievements

### World-Class Performance Indicators

Taiwan's general waste recycling rate was 58.55% in 2024 [^4] (business waste has a separate statistical caliber and cannot be added together) [^5], and the waste management level is on par with advanced European countries. In terms of technology export, waste incineration technology, sorting equipment manufacturing, and management system consulting have formed concrete soft power exports. ISO 14001 environmental management system certification, circular economy best practice cases, and United Nations Sustainable Development Goals implementation are Taiwan's specific coordinates in the international circular economy arena.

### Regional Cooperation and Influence

Taiwan exchanges circular economy experiences through platforms such as APEC, exporting technology to Southeast Asia. International academic conferences, technical exchange visits, and system design consulting have made Taiwan's circular economy model a replicable reference model in the Asia-Pacific region.

## Challenges and Future Development

### Current Challenges

Technical challenges are concentrated in composite material separation, microplastic pollution treatment, and electronic waste complexity; economic challenges include rising recycling costs, recycled material quality competitiveness, and international raw material price fluctuations. Social aspects include public participation fatigue, inconsistent business cooperation willingness, and insufficient cross-departmental coordination and integration, which are the main obstacles to system deepening.

### Future Development Directions

Technological innovation focuses on AI intelligent sorting, chemical recycling technology, and digital tracking management; at the system level, it promotes legal system perfection, incentive mechanism adjustment, and regulatory strengthening; at the international level, it focuses on joining the global circular economy network, cross-border supply chain management, and carbon trading mechanism integration.

### 2030 Vision Goals

The Ministry of Environment treats circular economy as an industry to develop: as of April 2026, Taiwan's circular economy industry annual output value had reached approximately NT$168.8 billion, with over 100,000 employees, and the government preferentially adopts products that comply with circular economy principles through green procurement [^12]. Qualitative goals are to build a complete circular society, cultivate circular economy talents, and become the Asia-Pacific hub of circular economy.

## Conclusion

The development of Taiwan's circular economy is a transformation from "garbage wars" to "circular paradigm." Over more than 30 years, through government policy guidance, corporate technological innovation, and extensive public participation, Taiwan has built a world-class resource circulation system, not only solving the waste problem but also creating new economic value.

Taiwan has gone from "garbage wars" to the establishment of the Resource Circulation Administration, and over 30 years of policy accumulation has raised the general waste recycling rate from single digits to nearly 60%. The core lesson of this process is: system incentives (Four-in-One recycling) are more effective than promotion; Extended Producer Responsibility allows companies to internalize recycling costs at the design stage; and waste incineration power generation turns the "last mile" into an energy asset. From the annual output value of NT$168.8 billion to the Ministry of Environment's vision of a "trillion-yuan industry" [^12], the test is whether Taiwan can transform 30 years of technological accumulation into an exportable industrial model.
