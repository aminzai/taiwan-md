> **30 Second Overview:** Taiwan's local apparel brand, NET (Zhu Fu Clothing), originated from the "Housewife Market" of the 1960s. Led by founder Huang Teng-lung, it has successfully survived even after international giants like Uniqlo and Zara entered the market. What is most recognizable about NET is not its design sensibility, but its quiet yet profound social responsibility—donating clothes to underprivileged children for 15 consecutive years. However, this usually low-key enterprise recently found itself in a rare legal battle with the government over property rights at Keelung East Coast Plaza, showcasing its brand's "gentle yet resilient" core.

## From Yunlin Farmer to Ready-to-Wear Kingdom: Huang Teng-lung's Courageous Transformation

In 1991, the first store bearing the "NET" sign officially opened on Nanjing East Road in Taipei. Yet, the roots of this brand trace back even further to Keelung and a son from a farming family in Dongshi Township, Yunlin.

The founder, Huang Teng-lung (original name: Huang Wen-ting), came from an ordinary farming family in Dongshi Township, Yunlin. After graduating high school and being discharged from military service, he worked at a customs brokerage firm before joining a trading company for seven years, where he managed the ready-to-wear export division to the United States. This experience gave him a deep understanding of the global apparel supply chain. In the late 1960s, he established the "Housewife Market" in Keelung, initially focusing on selling overstock from overseas shipments. This business model was immensely popular at the time because consumers could acquire high-quality clothing that had been exported to Europe and America at low prices. [^19] [^20]

However, Huang Teng-lung realized that relying solely on overstock was not a sustainable long-term strategy. As Taiwan's industry underwent transformation, the supply of export ready-to-wear became unstable. In 1991, he resolutely decided to create his own brand, "NET," and in 1993, completely rebranded all "Housewife Market" stores. This was more than a name change; it was a perilous leap from being an "agent" to becoming a "brand owner."

📝 **Curator's Note:** NET's success is not accidental; it is a microcosm of Taiwan's shift from a "OEM mindset" to a "branding mindset." It retained the commitment to quality from the export era while injecting the soul of local operation.

## "Reverse Showrooming" and Spatial Aesthetics Under International Giants

After 2010, international fast-fashion brands such as Uniqlo, Zara, and H&M flooded Taiwan. Many local brands, like Hang Ten and Giordano, were forced out of prime commercial areas. However, NET adopted a completely different "reverse showrooming" strategy, coupled with a unique insistence on spatial design.

### Yongfu Building Flagship: Guardian of an East District Landmark

The most famous example is from 2019. When the long-established restaurant in the East District, "Yongfu Building," closed due to high rent, NET took over the site, constructing a three-story, over 770-ping flagship store for **NT$3.2 million per month** (a total lease cost of about NT$480 million). [^9] [^10] This strategy of "developing towards grander, more prominent locations" allowed NET to maintain revenue exceeding **NT$8 billion** in 2024 amidst international competition, solidifying its position as a leader among local apparel brands. [^11] [^12]

### Tainan Minsheng Store: The Gentle Rebirth of Old Architecture

In addition to the East District flagship store, NET's store on Tainan Minsheng Road is highly representative. This location was an old building with historical significance. When NET moved in, it preserved the original architectural outline, weaving together modernity and nostalgia so that the commercial space became part of the urban landscape. This commitment to the "street-level shop" is not merely about square footage efficiency; it is about establishing a daily connection with local residents. [^21]

### Sanzhong Taipei Bridge Store: Ambition for Taiwan's Largest Flagship

At the end of 2025, NET opened its largest flagship store in all of Taiwan near the Sanzhong Taipei Bridge Station—a nine-story building spanning over a thousand ping. [^22] This store is more than just a sales space; it functions as a brand exhibition center. The interior circulation paths are spacious, and the fitting room design leans toward modern technology, attempting to overturn the stereotype of "parents shopping." [^23]

## Commoner Memory: Red Tag 50% Off and "The First Suit"

In the daily lives of Taiwanese people, NET is more than just a store; it is a space filled with collective memory.

### Red Tag 50% Off: A Treasure Hunt Across All Stores

For many budget-conscious consumers, what makes NET fascinating are its regular discount events. During seasonal changes, items marked with "Red Tags" or "Yellow Tags" appear, and astonishing offers like "3 items for the price of 2 at 50% off" are introduced. [^27] On designated discount update Thursdays, stores are often packed with people engaged in a "treasure hunt"—the process of finding high cost-performance (CP) value items amidst vast racks has become a unique aspect of Taiwanese popular consumer culture. [^28]

### The First Suit: A Rite of Passage for Young Adults

Another irreplaceable role of NET is that it is the preferred destination for many students and young professionals purchasing their "first formal wear." [^29] Whether for university entrance interviews or starting a first job, NET's affordable and standard-fitting suits and shirts have accompanied countless Taiwanese youth through critical moments in their lives. For many newcomers with limited budgets, what NET provides is not luxury, but a sense of "dignified security." [^30]

📝 **Curator's Note:** A good brand does not need to be unattainable. Through its "Red Tag" sales and the "Suit Section," NET precisely caters to those in Taiwanese society who need "affordable dignity."

## Brand Competition: NET vs Uniqlo vs GU

In Taiwan's affordable apparel market, NET is frequently compared against Japanese brands Uniqlo and GU.

| Brand      | Core Competitiveness                               | Product Characteristics                                                                                                      |
| :--------- | :------------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **NET**    | Extreme Cost-Performance (CP), Localized Operation | Extremely wide product line (formal wear, casual wear, children's clothing, accessories); fits the Taiwanese physique. [^25] |
| **Uniqlo** | Material Research (Heattech, Airism)               | Focuses on functional fabrics and basics; recently strengthened designer collaborations (e.g., U Series, C Series). [^31]    |
| **GU**     | Trendiness, Youth Appeal, Low Price Point          | Styles are bolder and trendier; quality is slightly lower than Uniqlo but more popular with teenagers. [^32]                 |

NET's advantage lies in its supply chain flexibility and deep understanding of the Taiwanese market. When international brands adjust prices or styles based on global strategies, NET remains the one that "best understands the Taiwanese wardrobe." [^33]

## Warmth After Donations: More Than Just Giving Money—It is About Dignity

Since 2009, founder Huang Teng-lung has provided millions of vouchers to the Children's Foundation for the needy and held a special "store donation hour" before Chinese New Year to serve children from underprivileged families. [^2] [^13] This action has continued for over 15 years, uninterrupted even during the pandemic. The significance of this donation is to allow these children, who might otherwise only wear second-hand clothes, to walk into a bright store like ordinary shoppers and choose styles and sizes they like. This is not merely an infusion of goods; it is a gentle safeguarding of the recipients' human dignity.

---

## Keelung East Coast Plaza: The Gentle Giant's Counterattack

NET, which had long been known for its quiet philanthropy, made headlines in early 2024—in a highly charged manner—by confronting the Keelung municipal government. The core of the dispute was the property rights of "Keelung East Coast Plaza." NET asserted that floors 2 through 4 were built with nearly **NT$300 million** of its own investment and that it held ownership. However, after the city government re-tendered the space, they forcibly took over, sparking a legal battle over property rights, contracts, and jurisdictional boundaries. [^3] [^14] This dispute allowed many Taiwanese people to see the resilience of this local enterprise when facing injustice, and it was the first time "Zhu Fu Clothing" entered the public eye with such a strong stance. [^15] [^16]

## Why Do We Need NET?

In today's increasingly globalized fashion industry, NET is an anomaly. It does not chase runway trends, nor does it hire expensive international endorsers. It is more like an old friend quietly situated on a street corner, offering stable quality and reasonable prices. When international brands leave, NET remains; when vulnerable children need new clothes, NET opens its doors.

NET is not just a clothing company; it is a vessel for the resilience and warmth of Taiwanese society. In the torrent of fast fashion, it has proven over thirty years: **The value of an enterprise lies not in how much money it makes, but in what it leaves behind for this land.**
