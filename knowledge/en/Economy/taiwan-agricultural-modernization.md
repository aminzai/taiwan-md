# The Modernization of Taiwan's Agriculture

## 30-Second Overview

In just a few decades, Taiwan's agriculture has shifted from labor-intensive traditional farming to technology-intensive, high-value-added modern premium agriculture. This transformation encompasses the scientific advancement of production techniques, the corporatization of management methods, and the premium branding of products. From the promotion of organic farming and the implementation of rural regeneration plans to the rise of agritourism, Taiwan's agriculture has developed a distinctive "Taiwan model" that not only maintains agricultural competitiveness but also creates a sustainable development paradigm integrating agricultural, ecological, and cultural values.

**Keywords:** premium agriculture, organic farming, rural regeneration, agritourism, smart agriculture, sustainable development

## Why It Matters

### The Premiumization Miracle of Smallholder Farming

With an average cultivated area of less than one hectare per farm household, Taiwan belongs to the category of smallholder farming economies worldwide. Yet, Taiwan's agriculture manages to generate high productivity and quality within limited land resources. This "small but refined" development model offers direct reference value for countries and regions around the world facing similar conditions.

### A Model of Multifunctional Agriculture

Modern Taiwan agriculture has transcended the sole function of food production, developing multiple roles including ecological conservation, landscape maintenance, cultural preservation, and leisure tourism. This multifunctional agriculture has driven rural population return, activated land use, and provided urban residents with places to reconnect with nature and experience rural culture.

### A Forward-Looking Practice of Sustainable Development

Facing climate change and environmental challenges, Taiwan's agriculture has shifted toward organic and ecological sustainable development models. From policy support to market acceptance, Taiwan's experience in promoting sustainable agriculture provides concrete lessons for global agricultural development.

## Foundations and Challenges of Traditional Agriculture

### Agricultural Foundations During the Japanese Colonial Period

The foundations of Taiwan's modern agriculture can be traced back to the Japanese colonial period. The Japanese colonial government invested heavily in improving agricultural infrastructure in order to develop Taiwan into Japan's granary.

**Hydraulic Engineering Construction:** Major hydraulic projects such as the Chianan Plain Water Diversion and the Taoyuan Plain Water Diversion solved the drought problems in southern Taiwan, freeing agricultural production from dependence on natural rainfall. These irrigation systems remain the backbone infrastructure of Taiwan's agriculture to this day.

**Crop Improvement and Technology Introduction:** In the 1920s, during the Japanese colonial period, the introduction of Ponge rice and the improvement of sugarcane varieties, along with the establishment of agricultural experiment stations, laid the foundation for later agricultural technological development.

**Establishment of Agricultural Cooperatives:** The establishment of agricultural cooperatives provided farmers with technical guidance, funding support, and product sales services. This system has been retained to this day. According to the Agricultural Cooperatives Act, Taiwan's agricultural cooperatives are divided into township (town, city, district) agricultural cooperatives, county (city) and municipal agricultural cooperatives, and the National Agricultural Cooperative, three levels, with a total of 302 agricultural cooperatives at all levels. The Council of Agricultural Sanzhi (Republic of China Agricultural Cooperative) was established in 2013[^4]. Agricultural cooperatives are responsible for agricultural extension education, joint purchasing and sales, rural finance, and agricultural insurance, among other functions. They are the main channel for disseminating agricultural knowledge to rural areas and an important pillar for farmers' collective bargaining and market access.

### Land Reform in the Early Post-War Period

From 1949 to 1953, land reform created favorable conditions for the modernization of Taiwan's agriculture.

**Land to the Tillers:** Land reform granted farmers land ownership, directly enhancing their production motivation.

**Increased Farmer Incomes:** Farmers who owned land saw significant income increases, providing capital for agricultural investment and crop improvement.

**Formation of Rural Consumer Markets:** Increased farmer incomes promoted rural consumption, providing markets for industrial products.

### Impact of Industrialization on Agriculture

From the 1960s to the 1970s, as Taiwan began its industrialization process, agriculture faced unprecedented challenges.

**Rural Labor Outflow:** Industrial development attracted a large number of rural youth to factories, causing rural labor shortages and population aging.

**Non-Agricultural Use of Farmland:** The expansion of industrial zones occupied large amounts of high-quality farmland, reducing the area of agricultural land year by year.

**Shift in Comparative Advantages:** The rapid development of industry and services caused the proportion of agriculture in the national economy to decline rapidly, putting pressure on farmers to transition out of agriculture.

## The Development of Premium Agriculture

### Policy Shift and Strategic Planning

Starting in the 1980s, agricultural authorities began to adopt "premium agriculture" as a development strategy, aiming to create maximum economic value within limited agricultural resources. In May 2009, the Executive Yuan passed the "Premium Agriculture Health and Excellence Plan," listing it as one of the six emerging industries[^2].

**Definition of Premium Agriculture:** Premium agriculture refers to the use of advanced agricultural technology for high-density, high-value-added agricultural production, emphasizing the characteristics of "labor-intensive, technology-intensive, capital-intensive."

**Policy Objectives:** The "Premium Agriculture Health and Excellence Plan" takes healthy agriculture (deepening brand verification), excellent agriculture (leading technology R&D), and leisure agriculture (reshaping rural charm) as its three main axes[^2], with supporting measures to attract young people into agriculture and revitalize rural development. The government promotes transformation through technology R&D, talent cultivation, funding subsidies, and market expansion.

### Rise of Technological Agriculture

**Agricultural Technology R&D:** The government invested in agricultural technology R&D and established an agricultural experiment and research system. From breeding, cultivation, pest and disease control to post-harvest processing, each link is supported by technology.

**Development of Facility Agriculture:** The rapid development of facility agriculture such as greenhouses, net houses, and environmental control systems has enabled agricultural production to be unaffected by weather conditions, improving the stability of yield and quality.

**Application of Biotechnology:** The application of biotechnology such as tissue culture, genetic engineering, and molecular marker-assisted breeding has greatly improved the efficiency and precision of crop improvement.

**Smart Agriculture Initiatives:** Technologies such as the Internet of Things, big data, and artificial intelligence have begun to be applied to agricultural production, realizing precision management and automated operations.

### Establishment of Brand Agriculture

**Product Differentiation:** Taiwan's agriculture has shifted from mass production to premium branding, producing high-quality agricultural products through crop improvement and refined management.

**Brand Management:** Brand marks such as "Good Taiwanese Farm" and "Traceable Agricultural Products" have been established, enhancing the market recognition and added value of Taiwan's agricultural products[^7].

**International Marketing:** Taiwan's high-quality agricultural products such as fruits, flowers, and tea have established a good reputation in international markets, with the proportion of brand exports steadily increasing.

## Promotion and Development of Organic Agriculture

### Evolution of Organic Agriculture Policies

The development of Taiwan's organic agriculture has evolved from grassroots spontaneous action to government-led initiatives.

**Early Development (1980s-1990s):** With rising awareness of environmental protection and health, some farmers spontaneously adopted organic farming methods.

**Institutional Establishment (2000s-2010s):** The government began to establish an organic agriculture certification system and formulated the Agricultural Products Production and Verification Management Act, providing legal guarantees for the development of organic agriculture.

**Institutional Promotion (2018-Present):** The Organic Agriculture Promotion Act, promulgated in May 2018 and implemented in May 2019, marked the entry of Taiwan's organic agriculture into a stage of legal promotion, and included friendly farming practices that follow organic definitions but have not yet been certified under guidance[^9].

### Current Status and Achievements of Organic Agriculture

**Growth in Cultivated Area:** When the certification system was launched in 2007, the area under organic certification accounted for only 0.24% of the nation's cultivated land. By the end of 2025, it reached 21,871 hectares (2.81% of cultivated land), plus 6,735 hectares of friendly farming (0.86%), with the two combined accounting for 3.67% of cultivated land[^9]. As of August 2026, there were 6,600 organic certified farmers, covering 23,631 hectares, with vegetables and rice being the largest two categories[^1].

**Product Diversification:** From the initial focus on vegetables, it has expanded to rice, fruits, tea, and livestock products.

**Increased Market Acceptance:** The market acceptance of organic agricultural products has significantly improved, driven by consumer demand for food safety and environmental friendliness, which has driven the expansion of the organic market.

**International Certification Alignment:** Taiwan's organic agricultural products have obtained international certifications and can be exported to markets such as Japan, the United States, and the European Union.

### Friendly Environmental Farming

In addition to organic agriculture, Taiwan has also promoted friendly environmental farming, providing support for farmers who have not yet obtained organic certification but produce using environmentally friendly methods.

**Policy Inclusiveness:** Friendly environmental farming policies recognize that agricultural transformation requires time and provide farmers with flexible choices.

**Ecological Compensation System:** The government provides ecological compensation to farmers adopting friendly environmental farming, encouraging the reduction of pesticide and chemical fertilizer use[^11].

**Biodiversity Protection:** Friendly environmental farming helps protect farmland ecosystems and maintain biodiversity.

## Rural Regeneration Plans

### Background and Objectives of the Plan

The rural regeneration plan, implemented starting in 2010, is an important policy of the Taiwan government to promote rural development.

**Policy Background:** Facing problems such as rural population outflow, aging infrastructure, and industrial decline, the government proposed the rural regeneration plan, hoping to promote rural revitalization through a "bottom-up" approach.

**Core Objectives:**

- Improve rural infrastructure and public facilities
- Promote rural industrial development
- Inherit rural culture
- Improve rural quality of life
- Attract young people to return home

**Budget Investment:** Article 7 of the Rural Regeneration Act stipulates the establishment of a rural regeneration fund of NT$150 billion, to be allocated in installments over ten years after the Act's implementation, and implemented in phases by the Rural Development and Soil and Water Conservation Commission[^5].

### Implementation Strategies and Methods

**Community-Led:** Emphasizing the subjectivity of community residents, communities propose development plans and the government provides resource support.

**Integrated Planning:** In addition to hardware construction, it also covers cultural preservation, industrial development, and human resources cultivation in the software dimension.

**Tailored Approaches:** According to the characteristics and conditions of each region, different rural regeneration models are developed.

**Sustainable Development:** Emphasizing environmental protection and ecological maintenance, promoting green energy and circular economy.

### Successful Cases and Models

**Xiehe Community in Sinsheng:** Taking the mushroom industry as the core, combined with flower sea tourism, successfully transformed into a leisure agriculture area.

**Xingyi in Nantou:** Through the revival of the pear industry and cultural preservation, it found new momentum for rural development.

**Shenggou Village in Ilan:** Combining organic agriculture and agricultural experiences, it developed an educational garden model.

**Fengbin in Hualien:** The indigenous community combined coastal resources to develop ecological tourism and indigenous cultural experiences.

## The Rapid Development of Agritourism

### Background of the Rise of Agritourism

Improved living standards have driven changes in lifestyle demands, and the need for urban residents to reconnect with nature and experience rural life has become increasingly apparent, creating market space for agritourism.

**Reflection on Urbanization:** The stress and alienation of urban life have made people yearn to return to nature and seek a pure rural experience.

**Changes in Educational Needs:** Parents hope their children can come into contact with nature and understand agriculture, and agritourism provides a good educational platform.

**Rise of Holiday Economy:** The implementation of the weekend rest system has created time conditions for short-distance travel and leisure activities.

### Development Models of Agritourism

**Tourist Farms:** Providing agricultural product picking experiences, such as strawberry fields, orange orchards, lotus ponds, etc., allowing visitors to pick agricultural products by hand.

**Agricultural Experiences:** Designing various agricultural experience activities, such as transplanting rice seedlings, harvesting, making tea, and pickling, allowing urban people to experience the life of farmers.

**Agricultural Education:** Combining environmental education and food and agriculture education, allowing students and the public to understand the process of agricultural production and the source of food.

**Rural Homestays:** Providing accommodation services, allowing visitors to deeply experience rural life and enjoy the tranquility and beauty of the countryside.

**Agricultural Product Processing Experiences:** Developing agricultural product DIY activities, such as making sauce, baking cakes, and making soap.

### Successful Models and Features

**Qingjing Farm:** Combining animal husbandry, horticulture, and tourism, it has become one of the most famous leisure farms in Taiwan[^6].

**Fei Niu Ranch:** Based on cattle breeding, it has developed a leisure agriculture experience chain covering ranch experiences, dairy product DIY, and ecological education.

**Dongshan River in Ilan:** Combining rice culture and water town charm, it has developed a unique rural tourism model.

**Chihshang in Taitung:** Based on high-quality rice, combined with pastoral scenery and slow living culture, it attracts a large number of visitors.

## Development of Smart Agriculture

### Agricultural Technology 4.0

Taiwan is actively promoting the development of smart agriculture, applying technologies such as the Internet of Things, big data, and artificial intelligence to improve agricultural production efficiency and quality.

**Sensor Technology:** The application of sensors for soil, weather, and crop growth enables precise monitoring of the agricultural production environment.

**Automated Equipment:** The application of automated equipment such as automatic irrigation, automatic fertilization, and automatic harvesting reduces labor demand and improves production efficiency.

**Big Data Analysis:** Collecting and analyzing agricultural production data to provide scientific decision-making support for farmers.

**AI Applications:** Using artificial intelligence for pest and disease identification, yield prediction, and quality grading.

### Practice of Precision Agriculture

**Precision Fertilization:** Based on soil test results, precisely adjusting fertilizer formulas to reduce waste and lower environmental burden.

**Precision Medication:** Using pest monitoring systems to apply medication precisely, reducing pesticide usage.

**Precision Irrigation:** Based on crop water requirements and soil moisture content, precisely controlling irrigation to conserve water resources.

**Precision Harvesting:** Using maturity testing technology to harvest at the optimal time, ensuring product quality.

### Achievements of Smart Agriculture

**Yield Improvement:** The application of smart agriculture technology can increase crop yields (Ministry of Agriculture Smart Agriculture Project Report)[^10].

**Quality Improvement:** Precise management makes the quality of agricultural products more stable, reducing differences between batches.

**Cost Reduction:** Automation and precision management reduce labor costs and material costs.

**Environmental Friendliness:** Reducing the use of pesticides and chemical fertilizers, lowering negative impacts on the environment.

## Integration of the Agricultural Industry Chain

### Development of the Six-Level Industry

Taiwan's agriculture is promoting the development of the six-level industry, integrating primary production, secondary processing, and tertiary services to create higher added value.

**Agricultural Product Processing Industry:** Developing refined processing technology to produce high-value-added agricultural processed products, extending the preservation period of agricultural products, and increasing product diversity.

**Agricultural Service Industry:** Including agricultural technology services, agricultural machinery services, agricultural finance, and agricultural insurance, providing comprehensive support for agricultural production.

**Agricultural Tourism Industry:** Combining agricultural production and tourism, developing agricultural experiences, rural homestays, and agricultural product souvenirs.

### Traceability System for Agricultural Products

**Food Safety Traceability:** Establishing a complete record system from production to sales, ensuring traceability of food safety.

**Quality Assurance:** Through standardized production and certification systems, improving the quality of agricultural products and consumer confidence.

**Brand Value Enhancement:** The traceability mark has become a guarantee of high-quality agricultural products, enhancing product competitiveness.

### E-Commerce and Direct Sales

**Online Sales Platforms:** Farmers sell agricultural products directly through online platforms, reducing intermediate links and improving income.

**Community-Supported Agriculture:** Consumers directly order agricultural products from farmers, establishing stable production and sales relationships.

**Farmers' Markets:** Regularly holding farmers' markets, allowing farmers to directly face consumers and build trust relationships.

## Challenges and Responses

### Impact of Climate Change

**Extreme Weather:** The increasing frequency of extreme weather such as typhoons, droughts, and heavy rains has impacted agricultural production.

**Pest Changes:** Climate change has led to the emergence of new pests, requiring adjustments to traditional prevention methods.

**Crop Adaptability:** Traditional varieties may not be suitable for new climate conditions, requiring the breeding of new varieties.

**Response Strategies:**

- Develop stress-resistant varieties
- Build disaster prevention facilities
- Adjust planting systems
- Establish early warning systems

### Labor Shortage

**Population Aging:** Rural populations are aging severely, with young people unwilling to work in agriculture.

**Seasonal Labor Demand:** Some crops require a large amount of labor at specific seasons, but it is difficult to recruit.

**Shortage of Technical Talent:** Modern agriculture needs more technical talent, but the training system still needs to be strengthened.

**Response Strategies:**

- Promote agricultural mechanization
- Introduce foreign labor
- Strengthen vocational training
- Improve agricultural wages

### International Competitive Pressure

**Agricultural Product Trade Liberalization:** WTO and various regional trade agreements have increased international competitive pressure.

**Cost Competition:** Compared to low-cost regions such as Southeast Asia, Taiwan's agriculture faces cost disadvantages.

**Quality Competition:** It must win in quality and characteristics, taking the high-value-added route.

**Response Strategies:**

- Strengthen brand marketing
- Develop high-value-added products
- Establish regional characteristics
- Expand niche markets

## Future Outlook of Sustainable Development

### Promotion of Circular Agriculture

**Resource Recycling Utilization:** Recycling agricultural waste, such as using rice straw to make boards and fruit pomace to make organic fertilizers.

**Energy Circulation:** Utilizing agricultural waste to generate green energy, such as biogas power generation and solar panel installation.

**Water Resource Circulation:** Developing water-saving irrigation technology and recycling rainwater and treated water.

### Biodiversity Protection

**Field Ecology:** Retaining biological habitats in farmland to maintain farmland biodiversity.

**Genetic Resource Preservation:** Preserving traditional varieties and wild species to maintain agricultural genetic resources.

**Environment-Friendly Farming Methods:** Promoting farming methods that do not use pesticides and chemical fertilizers, protecting soil and water sources.

### Carbon-Neutral Agriculture

**Carbon Reduction Measures:** Reducing greenhouse gas emissions in the agricultural production process.

**Carbon Sequestration Farming Methods:** Through soil management and crop planting, increasing soil carbon storage.

**Carbon Trading Mechanisms:** Establishing an agricultural carbon trading system to provide economic incentives for carbon-reducing farmers.

### Digital Agriculture Ecosystem

**Platform Integration:** Establishing an integrated digital agriculture platform connecting production, processing, and sales.

**Data Sharing:** Establishing an agricultural big data platform to promote data sharing and application.

**Innovative Services:** Developing new agricultural service models based on digital technology.

The driving force behind the modernization of Taiwan's agriculture has always been external pressure: rural labor outflow has forced mechanization, WTO competition has forced branding, and food safety crises have forced organicization. Each pressure has found a policy outlet. The next challenge is the variety renewal under climate change and the land contradictions arising from the co-development of agriculture and electricity—the solution still lies in institutional innovation first.
