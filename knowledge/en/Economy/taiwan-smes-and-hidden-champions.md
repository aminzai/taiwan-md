# Taiwan's SMEs and Hidden Champions

## 30-Second Overview

**Why should the world care about Taiwan' $\text{s}$ SMEs?**

The true backbone of Taiwan's economy is not just its "national guardian" semiconductor giants, but 1.71 million seemingly ordinary SMEs that create 79% of all jobs. Within this group lie "hidden champions" controlling global markets beyond your imagination: precision screws from Lukang, Changhua, account for 30% of BMW's supply chain; the screw kingdom of Gangshan, Kaohsiung, accounts for 80% of Taiwan's exports; and the saxophone industry cluster in Houli impresses musicians worldwide. From the garage-based "black hand" (handyman) masters of the 1960s to today's status as having the "highest density of hidden champions in the world" according to German management experts, Taiwan's SMEs have proven over 60 years that being small and specialized can conquer the world.

---

## Global Hegemons in Screw Factories

In an inconspicuous corrugated iron factory in Lukang, Changhua, tens of thousands of precision screws are manufactured every day, shipped directly to the assembly lines of BMW and Mercedes-Benz in Germany. This enterprise, with fewer than 300 employees, holds a 30% share of the global automotive precision screw market. The owner is a 50-year-old _heishou shifu_ (black hand master/handyman), wearing work clothes stained with machine oil, yet his clients span top-tier European and American automotive brands.

This is not an isolated case; it is a true portrait of the Taiwanese economy. While the media focuses on "national guardians" like TSMC and Hon Hai (Foxconn), the real strength sustaining Taiwan's economy lies in these hardworking "hidden champions."

> **The definition of "Hidden Champions" by German management expert Hermann Simon: Companies that are among the top three globally in a specific niche, have revenues under $4 billion, and possess relatively low public brand recognition. Taiwan possesses over 105 such hidden champions, the highest density in the world.**

## Economic Strength Behind the Numbers

### SMEs are the Taiwanese Economy

**Key Data for Taiwan's SMEs in 2024:**

- **Number of Enterprises**: 1.716 million, accounting for 98.87% of all enterprises
- **Employment**: 9.194 million people, accounting for 79.29% of national employment
- **Sales Revenue**: NT$31.1 trillion, an annual increase of 8.05%
- **Export Contribution**: Although only 10.28% of total exports, they dominate specific global sectors

* (Source: Small and Medium Enterprise Administration, Ministry of Economic Affairs, 2025 SME White Paper) \*

The stories behind these numbers are even more striking: among the 1.71 million SMEs, 58.22% have operated for over 8 years, and 51.79% are sole-proprietorship family businesses. They are not "short-lived startups," but mature enterprises focused on long-term survival and stable operations.

### The Global Map of Hidden Champions

Taiwan's hidden champions are spread across the globe:

**🔩 Kingdoms of Precision Manufacturing**

- **Screws and Nuts**: Ranked among the top three globally for exports; 2019 export value reached NT$144 billion (10.8% global market share)
- **Gangshan, Kaohsiung**: The screws produced here account for 80% of Taiwan's total screw exports, earning it the title "the world's screw cave"

**🏭 Professional OEM Empires**

- **Faucets**: The Dingfanpo cluster in Changhua holds a 60% global market share
- **Wetsuits**: Xue Changxing holds a 65% global marketting share
- **Bicycle Chains**: KMC produces enough chain annually to wrap around the Earth's equator five times

**🎷 Cultural and Creative Specialties**

- **Saxophones**: The Houli industry cluster offers quality comparable to European and American luxury goods
- **Yachts**: Donggo Yachts is the world's fourth-largest manufacturer, ranked first in the US market

* (Sources: AddMaker, Economic Daily News Industry Survey, 2022 Commercial Times Hidden Champion Research) \*

## From Handyman Workshops to Precision Manufacturing: A 60-Year Evolution

### The First Wave: The Golden Age of Entrepreneurship (1960–1980)

**Context**: The post-war baby boom generation entered the workforce just as global manufacturing began shifting toward Asia.

**Typical Entrepreneurial Models**:

- Masters training apprentices, with skills passed down orally
- Family businesses where wives managed accounts and children assisted in assembly
- Taking foreign OEM orders and learning through imitation
- High volume, low margin; competing through diligence and cost advantages

**Representative Enterprises**:

- **Cheng Shin (CST)** (1967): Started with bicycle tires; now the world's ninth-largest tire manufacturer
- **Dayi Transportation** (1969): Evolved from auto parts OEM to a global supplier for Nissan
- **Feng Tay** (1971): Evolved from traditional footwear to being Nike's largest manufacturer

### The Second Wave: Technological Upgrading and Transformation (1980–2000)

Facing cost competition from latecomers like South Korea and China, Taiwan's SMEs began a critical transformation:

**Transformation Strategies**:

- Investing in automated equipment to improve production efficiency
- Evolving from pure OEM (Original Equipment Manufacturer) to ODM (Original Design Manufacturer)
- Deepening presence in niche markets to become specialists in specific fields
- Establishing overseas production bases to maintain cost advantages

**Success Stories**:

- **Giant Manufacturing**: Transitioned from OEM to branding, becoming the world's largest bicycle brand
- **Merida**: Specializes in the high-end bicycle market, standing alongside Giant as one of Taiwan's "bicycle twins"

### The Third Wave: Global Supply Chain Integration (2000–2020)

**New Challenges**: The rise of "Made in China," intensifying global competition, and rising technical barriers.

**Response Strategies**:

- Focusing on the two ends of the "smile curve": R&D/Design + Brand Marketing
- Becoming indispensable key component suppliers for multinational corporations
- Building technological moats by mastering proprietary processes

**Outstanding Performance**:

- **KMC Chain**: Holds 73% of the global high-end bicycle chain market
- **TXC**: A major supplier of quartz oscillators to international giants like Apple and Samsung

## Three Success Codes of Hidden Champions

### Code 1: Focus on Niches, Achieve Excellence

The common trait among Taiwan's hidden champions is being "narrow and deep":

- **Johnson Health Tech**: Specializes in fitness equipment; third largest globally, first in Asia
- **KMC**: Only makes bicycle chains, but holds a 7．3% global market share
- **Eclat Textile**: Focuses on the curtain market, becoming one of the world's top three manufacturers

> "Uniqueness is a prerequisite for market leadership." — Wu Hsin-chuan, Vice President of KMC

### Code 2: Technology First, Continuous Innovation

These companies share one commonality: investing 8–12% of revenue into R&D.

**Representative Cases**:

- **Chyi Mei (Aten)**: Holds a 13% global market share in KVM switches; R&D expenses account for 10% of revenue, with 427 global patents
- **Chroma Technology**: A leader in precision testing instruments; received government subsidies to assist in developing SoC chip testing systems
- **Pixart**: An expert in CMOS image sensors, acquiring over 850 patents annually

### Code 3: Family Governance, Long-term Management

**Characteristics of Family Businesses**:

- 51.79% of SMEs operate as sole proprietorships
- Rapid decision-making allows for quick responses to market changes
- Emphasis on long-term relationships and building deep partnerships with clients
- The Taiwanese business culture of "doing business with integrity" (doing business is about character)

## Irreplaceability in the Global Supply Chain

### Why are Taiwan's SMEs difficult to replace?

**Manufacturing Precision**:

- KMC bicycle chains are 5–10% lighter than Japanese or European products, yet have double the lifespan
- Taiwan's screws reach aerospace-grade standards, serving as designated suppliers for BMW and Mercedes-Benz

**Flexibility Advantages**:

- Capability for small-batch, highly diversified production
- Rapid response to changing customer requirements
- Establishing long-term partnerships with international giants

**Cluster Effects**:

- Gangshan, Kaohsiung Screw Cluster: Complete upstream and downstream integration
- Dingfanpo, Changhua Faucet Cluster: Holds 60% of the global market share
- Houli Saxophone Cluster: A full "one-stop" chain from components to finished products

### New Challenges in Digital Transformation

**Current Challenges**:

- Shortage of young labor; an aging workforce
- Insufficient digital transformation capabilities
- Succession issues (74% are family businesses)

**Government Support**: The Ministry of Economic Affairs' "Mittelstand (SME) Awards" launched in 201 $\text{t}$ to assist with 42 transformation measures, investing NT$516 million and creating 12,775 jobs.

## Future Outlook: From Hidden to Visible

### The New Generation of Hidden Champions

**Highlights from the 2015 Mittelstand Award Winners**:

- **GSS (Global Software Service)**: Ranked first in market share for HR management systems in the banking industry
- **Pixart**: A rising star in the IoT sensor chip field, with an average of over 8 patents per employee
- **Singtex**: Produces eco-friendly functional fabric made from coffee grounds; has won international invention awards such as the US INPEX and Germany's iENA

### The Fourth Wave: ESG and Digitalization

Looking toward 2030, Taiwan's SMEs are facing new challenges:

- **ESG Transformation**: Environmentally friendly processes and the circular economy
- **Digital Transformation**: AI integration and smart manufacturing
- **Brand Internationalization**: Moving from OEM to OBM (Original Brand Manufacturer)

## The Truth of the Taiwan Miracle

When we discuss the "Taiwan Miracle," the focus often falls on tech giants like TSMC and Hon Hai. But the true miracle lies in how an island, through 1.71 million SMEs, has woven the world's most tightly integrated manufacturing network.

From the precision screws of Lukang, Changchanghua, to the saxophones of Houli; from the screw kingdom of Gangshan, Kaohsiung, to the hand tool clusters of Taoyuan—these seemingly ordinary SMEs have occupied an unshakeable position on the global economic map through professionalism, persistence, and innovation.

They prove one principle: in this era dominated by mega-corporations, being small and specialized can still conquer the world.
