> **30-second overview**: Taiwan's five major science parks (Nangang Software Park, Neihu Technology Park, Hsinchu Science Park, Central Taiwan Science Park, Southern Taiwan Science Park) all display a common commercial district pattern. With tens of thousands of employees, they all appear collectively during a 90-minute lunch window and vanish by 6:30 PM. Restaurants along Sanzhong Road, Park Boulevard, and Section 2 of Zhongxiao East Road in Nangang have quietly cut dinner service in favor of lunch-only operations. After the opening of Nangang LaLaport on March 20, 2025, even this "lunch-only" defensive strategy faced direct competition from the mall's food street.

Taiwan's five major science parks (Nangang Software Park, Neihu Technology Park, Hsinchu Science Park, Central Taiwan Science Park, Southern Taiwan Science Park) share a counterintuitive common trait in their surrounding dining industry: the more successful the park, the less like a real commercial district its surroundings become. With tens of thousands of employees, they all appear collectively during a 90-minute lunch window and vanish by 6:30 PM, either heading home or back to Taoyuan or Hsinchu city centers. Sanzhong Road, Rueiguang Road in Neihu, and Jinshan Street in Hsinchu all display the same phenomenon. Restaurants once served both lunch and dinner, but gradually shifted to **lunch-only**. The reason is simple: the customer base itself has no evening demand.

**Counterintuitive core statement**: The commercial district pattern created by science parks can be summarized as "90-minute lunch rush, then vanishing after 6:30 PM." Restaurants choosing to cut dinner service and operate lunch-only is often a rational calculation responding to commuting rhythms.

## Why This Phenomenon Is Worth Recording

Taiwan's tech corridors (Nangang Software Park, Neihu Technology Park, Hsinchu Science Park, Central Taiwan Science Park, Southern Taiwan Science Park) are key engines of Taiwan's GDP. Neihu Technology Park had already registered 5,750 companies by 2017[^1]. Nangang Software Park had 418 enterprises, 24,443 employees, and annual revenue of NT$580.4 billion by 2015[^2].

Normally, such a dense concentration of workers should support a thriving surrounding dining scene. But walking the streets around these parks reveals a uniquely Taiwanese tech industry landscape: **lines stretching from noon to 1:30 PM, then almost empty after 2 PM with only delivery drivers around, and completely deserted after 6 PM with many restaurants not even serving dinner**.

Sanzhong Road, Park Boulevard, and Section 2 of Zhongxiao East Road in Nangang Software Park are the most typical representatives of this pattern. Many restaurants on these streets once operated "both lunch and dinner," but in recent years have gradually shifted to **lunch-only**. After lunch ends (around 2 PM), they pull down the iron gates and don't reopen in the evening.

This pattern is observable across all five parks. It has already become a **structural feature** of science park commercial districts, not an individual business issue on any single street or at any single store.

## Data: Single-Peak Population Shaped by Commuting Rhythms

Taipei's Department of Information Technology analyzed Neihu Technology Park's commuting patterns using MRT smart card and mobile phone signal data[^3], revealing the most critical fact about the park's commercial district: **Neihu's daytime "mobile population" far exceeds its registered population**. This means Neihu is a destination for massive influxes of commuters during work hours. After work, these people collectively flow back to other districts in Taipei, New Taipei, or even Taoyuan.

Looking specifically at Neihu's commuting routes. The MRT Wenhu Line's Gangqian, Xihu, and Donghu stations see peak outbound crowds from 8 AM to 9 AM, and reverse inbound crowds from 5:30 PM to 6:30 PM. The same double-peaked curve appears at the Banan Line's Kunyang and Houshanpi stations (toward Nangang), the TRA Nangang Station, and the MRT Banan Line's Nangang Software Park Station (BR23). **This curve is the direct counterpart of the park commercial district's revenue curve**: crowds come in for lunch, crowds leave and businesses close.

Nangang Software Park's population structure is even more extreme. The streets along Huandong Boulevard and the railway tracks where Sanzhong Road, Park Boulevard, and Section 2 of Zhongxiao East Road are located are mostly office towers and logistics sites, with **sparse surrounding residential population**[^4]. When over 20,000 employees leave the park around 6:30 PM, the remaining permanent residents cannot support evening dining demand. The same sparse population issue appears in Neihu's Rueiguang Road and Xihu areas, though the presence of Breeze and Da Strait Life Circle provides some evening foot traffic supplement, the distance of over a kilometer from the park core weakens the effect.

Hsinchu Science Park shows a similar but slightly different version. After Hsinchu Park employees disperse, they collectively flow toward **Jinshan Street, Guanxin Village, Guanxin Road, and Guangfu Road**[^5][^6]. These areas are engineer residential settlements, so **streets more than a kilometer from the park** actually have thriving dinner scenes. But **stores right next to the park fence** face the same fate as Nangang's Sanzhang Road.

## Case Study: Nangang Software Park's Sanzhong Road, Park Boulevard, and Section 2 of Zhongxiao East Road

Sanzhong Road, Park Boulevard, and Section 2 of Zhongxiao East Road form Nangang Software Park's main circulation routes, each bearing different passenger flow roles. **Sanzhong Road** runs along the park's main axis, with large office buildings like MediaTek, Ralink, and Qunchuang on both sides. **Park Boulevard** is the park's northern boundary, adjacent to the MRT Nangang Software Park Station (BR23), serving as the main route for employee walking commutes; **Section 2 of Zhongxiao East Road** is the eastern boundary, adjacent to the China Trust Financial Center and Nangang Exhibition Hall, primarily serving business and exhibition traffic[^7].

Geographically, these three streets are constrained by similar structures: connected to Huandong Boulevard elevated expressway to the north, adjacent to the railway and Nangang Railway Station to the east, and lacking deep residential hinterland. The customer base for restaurants on these streets is almost 100% park workers:

- **Noon**: 12:00 to 1:30 PM is the 90-minute window accounting for over 70% of daily revenue
- **Afternoon**: After 2 PM, almost no walk-in customers. No tourists, no residents, no students
- **Evening**: 6 PM to 7:30 PM is when park employees disperse, most heading directly home via the Banan or Wenhu lines, with a few eating dinner along Park Boulevard before leaving

For restaurant owners, **continuing dinner service requires bearing labor, utilities, and ingredient waste costs, but the low customer volume cannot support these fixed costs**. The rational choice is to cut dinner service.

Walking Sanzhong Road and Park Boulevard, you can see three different paths of restaurant segmentation, corresponding to different cost-bearing capacities:

1. **Pure lunch-only stores**: NT$100 set meals, buffet, noodle dishes. Open 11:30 AM, close at 2 PM, operating only 2.5 hours per day. These stores usually have small spaces and 2-3 staff, relying on weekday lunch to sustain weekly revenue.
2. **Extended breakfast type**: Pushing business hours forward to breakfast time (7 AM to 10 AM), tapping into employee breakfast demand before entering the office, then extending to lunch. These stores expand operating hours to 7 hours but still avoid the evening.
3. **Chain brand entry type**: McDonald's, Starbucks, and other large chain brands still maintain dinner service, but customer flow is clearly lower than lunch. Their operating hours are uniformly regulated by headquarters and cannot be flexibly adjusted like independent small stores.

All three paths point to the same fact: each store finds its own operating rhythm to coexist with the park's single-peak population flow based on its cost structure.

## Why This Is Particularly Visible in Taiwan

The single-peak phenomenon around science park commercial districts is not unique to Taiwan, but **the Taiwan version has four amplifying factors**:

### 1. BOT and Zoning Controls Create Pure Functional Spaces

Taipei listed Nangang as the eastern node of the "Taipei Tech Corridor"[^8], with park land released via BOT to developers and the entire area planned as **single-function (tech office)**. This forces residential, retail, and nightlife functions to rely on "spilling over" to surrounding areas. But due to infrastructure cuts from Huandong Boulevard and railway elevated tracks, spillover routes are quite limited.

### 2. Engineer Commuting Distance and Cost Sensitivity

Compared to New York's financial district or Tokyo's Roppongi, Taiwan's park engineers mostly **don't live near the parks**. Nangang Software Park employees typically live in Neihu, Xizhi, and New Taipei's eastern side; Hsinchu Science Park employees live in Hsinchu city center or Zhubei. After work, the incentive to eat dinner nearby is low, instead tending to **return to "family-style commercial districts" near their homes** for consumption.

### 3. Changing Overtime Culture

The old myth assumed engineers were "always working overtime," but actual data shows **most park companies see large-scale dispersal between 5:30 PM and 6 PM**[^9]. This shift significantly impacts surrounding restaurants. Employees no longer have a rigid need to stay for dinner, leaving on time collectively and emptying the commercial district after 7 PM.

### 4. Ingredient Costs and Rent Pressure

As parks expand, surrounding storefront rents rise. Sanzhong Road and Park Boulevard rents have reached high levels, and since 2024, dining ingredient costs (oil, meat, eggs) have increased. **If dinner customer volume doesn't reach 60% of lunch, it directly causes losses**. Under this cost structure, "cutting dinner to preserve lunch profits" becomes the most rational choice.

## Cross-Park Comparison

Looking at all five parks together makes the common structure and differences of the single-peak phenomenon clearer. The table below summarizes each park's employee size, main dining streets, and dinner ecology:

| Park                         | Employee Size (Recent) | Main Dining Streets                                                                                                            | Dinner Ecology                                                                                                                                           |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Nangang Software Park        | ~24,000[^2]            | Sanzhong Road, Park Boulevard, Section 2 of Zhongxiao East Road                                                                | Significantly reduced, most stores lunch-only                                                                                                            |
| Neihu Technology Park        | Over 100,000[^1]       | Rueiguang Road, Zhouzi Street, Jiangnan Street, Xingai Road                                                                    | Partially maintained, relying on Da Strait Life Circle                                                                                                   |
| Hsinchu Science Park         | ~170,000               | Jinshan Street, Guanxin Road, Guangfu Road                                                                                     | Active. Jinshan Street is an engineer "new village" settlement[^5], Guanxin Village is one of the highest-income villages in Taiwan[^6]                  |
| Central Taiwan Science Park  | Tens of thousands      | Inside park: Park First/Second/Third Road, Central Science Park Road; outside: Section 3 of Xitun Road, Miu Pin Square, J-Mall | Inside park relies on Central Science Park Food Court; Section 3 of Xitun Road relies on a dual customer base of Donghai University and Fukang community |
| Southern Taiwan Science Park | Tens of thousands      | Inside park: Park17 Mall; outside: Shanchuan Old Street, Qing'an Temple commercial district                                    | Inside park relies on employee restaurants and Park17; outside relies on Shanchuan's existing population rather than park employees[^10]                 |

From the table, a clear pattern emerges: **whether the surrounding commercial district's dinner can survive almost entirely depends on whether there's an engineer residential settlement within one kilometer of the park**. Hsinchu Science Park's Jinshan Street thrives because of complete residential hinterland, similar to a regular urban commercial district; Nangang Software Park's Sanzhong Road struggles because of lack of housing, surviving only on lunch; Central Science Park's Section 3 of Xitun Road supplements with Donghai University students; Southern Science Park relies on Shanchuan's existing residents.

This difference is also reflected in real estate valuations. Streets adjacent to parks but lacking residential hinterland have lower storefront rents and higher turnover rates among dining operators; streets with residential hinterland support are closer to normal urban levels, with more stable rents and business.

## Where Do Employees Actually Go After Work?

Understanding why single-peak commercial districts form requires seeing where employees actually go after dispersing. Taking Nangang Software Park as an example, employees split into four main destinations after work:

- **Return home**: Banan Line toward Kunyang, Houshanpi, Zhongxiao Fuxing, connecting to Neihu, Xinyi, and Wenshan residential areas; Wenhu Line's Gangqian, Xihu, Donghu connect to Neihu and Xizhi residential clusters. This group accounts for about 60% of the dispersal crowd.
- **Transfer to city center gatherings**: Banan Line westward to Zhongxiao Dunhua, Zhongxiao Fuxing, and Taipei City Hall Station; Wenhu Line southward to Da'an and Technology Building Station. Dining, exercise, and shopping destinations are not near the park, but in mature commercial districts within Taipei City.

The Banan Line westward branch is mainly weekday gathering scenes. Friday evening "drink gatherings," "ball games," and "post-work cat-catching" commonly occur in Da'an, Xinyi, and Taipei City Hall Station. These activities happen more than ten kilometers from the park, completely unrelated to the park's surrounding commercial district.

- **Return to Taoyuan or Hsinchu**: The THSR Nangang Station directly connects to Taoyuan, Hsinchu, and Taichung. Friday evenings see large numbers of engineers with luggage at THSR Nangang Station, accounting for about 5% to 10% of the dispersal crowd.
- **Stay near the park**: A small number of employees eat dinner, exercise, and buy daily necessities near the park. But this proportion is usually below 15%, and scattered across Sanzhong Road, Section 2 of Zhongxiao East Road, MRT station shops, convenience stores, and small shops, with **each individual store receiving minimal traffic**.

This distribution structure is the fundamental reason Sanzhong Road restaurants cut dinner service. Even if the entire park has 20,000 people dispersing, only a few hundred end up on one street, and further divided among stores, **individual store dinner customer volume is extremely low**.

Converting these four distributions into simple arithmetic: of 20,000 employees, subtracting 60% who go directly home, 15% who transfer to the city center, and 7% who return to Taoyuan or Hsinchu, the remaining 18% who stay near the park is about 3,600 people. Distributing these 3,600 people across Sanzhong Road, Park Boulevard, Section 2 of Zhongxiao East Road, Section 2 of Zhongxiao East Road, MRT station shops, convenience stores, and small shops, each store's potential dinner customer flow might only be a few dozen people. For individual store profit margins, this number is far below the break-even threshold.

Worth noting is that weekends tell a completely different story. On Saturdays and Sundays, Nangang Software Park is almost empty, and most lunch-only stores on Sanzhong Road and Park Boulevard are closed on weekends. The few that open only operate until the afternoon, with basically no weekend dinner customer source. This "weekend ghost city" effect again confirms: the park's commercial district is entirely dependent on weekday lunch crowds.

## Urban Planning Insights

This phenomenon reveals an often-overlooked policy observation: **building science parks does not automatically drive complete commercial districts, but instead creates single-function commercial streets**. To make the park's surroundings truly part of a 24-hour city, three things need to be done from the planning level:

1. **Mixed-use zoning**: Allow residential, retail, and cultural functions to coexist within the park and adjacent streets, rather than pure office use.
2. **Shuttle system optimization**: Shorten employee commuting time, making them more willing to consume nearby.
3. **Government public space support**: Incorporate parks, sports facilities, and community activity spaces to create "reasons to stay after work."

Nangang and Neihu's recent reconstruction plans have begun trying mixed-use approaches, such as the mixed residential-commercial buildings around Nangang Railway Station and the life circle design combining Neihu's Yangshan Street with Breeze. These changes are slowly filling the park's missing residential and nighttime functions.

But **streets like Sanzhong Road that are already established as pure office corridors, may continue the trend of restaurants cutting dinner service unless planning functions change**. If the physical space lacks residential areas, students, and nighttime activities, the dining industry has no nighttime customer source to serve.

## Future Variable: Structural Impact from LaLaport

The surrounding commercial district of Nangang Software Park is now facing the biggest structural impact since its opening: **Mitsui Fudosan's Nangang LaLaport officially opened on March 20, 2025**[^11], with a total area of 47,000 pings and nearly 300 brands, with dining accounting for over 30%. LaLaport specially planned food streets on B1 and 5F "to solve the dining problems of office workers and exhibition visitors"[^12].

LaLaport is located within the Nangang Software and Trade Park, just one street away from Sanzhong Road and Park Boulevard restaurants. The impact on single-peak commercial districts is direct and structural:

- **Lunch customer diversion**: Park employees' lunch choices shift from "walking out of the park to find restaurants on Sanzhong Road" to "crossing the street to eat at LaLaport's food street." Traditional small stores relying on lunch are the first to be impacted.
- **Reverse dinner opportunity**: LaLaport also creates "reasons to stay after work." Employees have the opportunity to stop by for shopping, movies, and dinner. For differentiated, mid-range restaurants that can match LaLaport's flow, this is a new opportunity, but the premise is they must survive until LaLaport triggers the crowd.
- **Rent re-evaluation**: Mall opening has already pushed up the overall commercial real estate valuation in Nangang, and correspondingly raised expectations for surrounding storefront rents. For lunch-only stores with already thin margins, rent increases may bring the final fatal cost pressure.

The essence of this impact is the competition between "centralized food streets vs. scattered street-side small stores." LaLaport uses mall scale, brand appeal, air conditioning, and parking convenience to gather lunch crowds from scattered street-side stores into a centralized food street in one go. Traditional small stores' only counterplay space is price (NT$100 set meals vs. NT$180+ at food streets) and local regular customer relationships. Price advantages are increasingly difficult to maintain with rising ingredient costs, and regular customer relationships weaken with increasing employee turnover.

Beyond LaLaport, **before 2029, Nangang International Mall, Nangang Core Mall, Taiwan Power Company Nangang AR1 Global Shopping Center, and Nangang World Pearl will open one after another**[^13], transforming Nangang from "single-peak science park" to something like the Xinyi Planning District's "dual engine of commercial district and office." The operating logic of traditional small stores on Sanzhong Road and Park Boulevard will be completely rewritten. Stores that find differentiated positioning have a chance to continue, while those that don't will be replaced by mall food streets.

Other long-term variables include:

- **Remote work normalization**: If hybrid work becomes mainstream, daytime park population decreases, and the 90-minute lunch crowd also shrinks. LaLaport's impact and remote work effects may compound.
- **Delivery platform changing spatial demands**: Restaurants can increasingly operate in "locations somewhat distant from the crowd but with cheaper rent," changing the traditional commercial district logic that relies entirely on walking traffic.
- **AI office environments**: Companies like MediaTek, Qunchuang, and Ralink in Nangang Software Park are expanding AI and semiconductor R&D departments. If AI engineers work longer hours, the evening commercial district might have a chance to warm up, but there's currently no data to confirm this hypothesis.

For Sanzhong Road and Park Boulevard restaurant owners, **the next 3 to 5 years are a critical period to test whether the "lunch-only" defensive strategy can still hold**. LaLaport's food street has placed the greatest challenge to this strategy right at their doorstep.
