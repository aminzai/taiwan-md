> **30-Second Overview:** Starting from two bamboo-leaf rice balls on the platform during the late Japanese colonial period, the Chishang bento used eighty years to turn the name of an eastern Taiwan station into the highest standard for rice cuisine across the island. It is a sophisticated "humidity management system"—the thin wooden box absorbs moisture, preserving the chewy texture of Kaohsiung No. 139 "Ugly Beauty" rice. Under the shadow of industrial pollution in the west and the 1982 cadmium rice incident, Chishang rice, relying on the pure water sources of the eastern valley, completed the historical transfer of Taiwan's premium rice hegemony from the "Zhuoshui River rice."

During the Showa era, the Hualien-Taitung line steam trains took a full eight hours to travel from Hualien to Taitung. As the train slowly pulled into Chishang Station, the cry of "rice package~ rice package~" on the platform was one of the few comforts for travelers at the time. The earliest Chishang rice package had no wooden box; it consisted of just two rice balls wrapped in bamboo leaves, served with braised pork, yellow radish, and lean meat jerky, designed specifically to fill the empty stomachs of those on a long journey [^1].

There are two versions of the founding year: Wutao's official website counts from 1939 (Showa 14) [^4], while the National Cultural Memory Bank of the Ministry of Culture records it as Showa 15 (1940) [^2]. A one-year difference, but referring to the same couple, the same platform.

## Eight Hours of Hunger: The Origin on the Platform

The origin of the Chishang bento is a story about "empathy for travelers." At the time, Chishang Station was an important relay point on the Hualien-Taitung line. The couple Li Yue-dian and his wife operated the "Chishang Rice Package Store" (now the original old shop of Wutao Chishang Rice Package) in front of the train station [^2]. Because the train traveled slowly and meals were not served on board, the Li family began making and selling rice packages. It was the daughter-in-law, Li Chen-yun, who later took over the family business, who truly improved the rice balls into a full bento box—she calculated the travel time, knowing that eight hours of hunger required more than just two rice balls.

To ensure the rice package remained delicious in an era without thermal insulation equipment, Li Chen-yun left a quote that the family has repeated many times:

> "The rice package is made for travelers; it must be made with care." [^3]

This sentence became the spiritual core of the Chishang bento. From the initial bamboo-leaf rice balls to the later switch to wooden boxes to increase the variety of dishes, the evolution of the Chishang bento has always revolved around the balance of "convenience" and "deliciousness." By 1962, the Chishang bento officially entered the wooden box era, which has become its most recognizable symbol [^4].

📝 **Curator's Note:** The Chishang bento was born from a very practical need: people need to eat during an eight-hour train ride. This "rigid demand" attribute means that more than eighty years later, it remains the most stable presence in the daily lives of Taiwanese people. A netizen recorded that the Wutao fried pork chop bento price rose from 95 NTD in 2019 to 115 NTD in 2025 [^11]. An increase of 20% over six years, it remains the hot meal that many working-class people cannot do without.

## Shift in Rice Power: From Zhuoshui River to Hualien-Taitung Valley

In Taiwan's culinary history, the coordinates for "premium rice" once underwent a drastic shift. In early Taiwan, the most prestigious rice-producing area was the Zhuoshui River basin at the border of Changhua and Yunlin. The so-called "Xiluo Rice" was once the tribute rice presented to the Emperor during the Japanese colonial period [^14]. The black mud of the Zhuoshui River, rich in minerals, nurtured Taiwan's first generation of rice glory.

However, the turning point appeared in the 1980s. With the acceleration of industrialization in the west, the severe "cadmium rice incident" broke out in Changhua in 1982, casting a shadow of industrial wastewater pollution over the western granary [^15]. Consumers began to turn their gaze to the "last pure land"—the east. Chishang Township is located at the highest point of the Hualien-Taitung Valley, with water sources coming from pure springs in the Central Mountain Range and the Coastal Range, free from heavy industrial pollution.

The Chishang bento played a key "mobile billboard" role at this time. Every traveler passing through Chishang completed a sensory vote for eastern Taiwan's premium rice on the platform. Benefiting from the microclimate regulation of Dapo Pond and the temperature difference between day and night in the valley, the rice maturation period was long, allowing for sufficient accumulation of nutrients [^17]. By the 1990s, Chishang rice officially replaced Zhuoshui River rice as the synonym for Taiwan's premium rice [^16].

## The Secret of the Wooden Box: Humidity Management and Ugly Beauty

Walk into any bento shop with a "Chishang" sign, and the thin wooden box made of slats is standard equipment. This is a precise "humidity management technology." The wooden box is made from _Albizia julibrissin_ (Silk Tree) or _Albizia chinensis_ (Malacca Albizia), whose wood pores can absorb excess moisture from the rice, preventing the grains from becoming soggy [^5].

- **Kaohsiung No. 139 (Ugly Beauty)**: The main rice variety used for Chishang bento. Although this rice has many white cores and looks unappealing, its texture is extremely chewy and durable; it remains delicious even when cold, hence the nickname "Ugly Beauty" [^12].
- **Bourong (Deep-fried Pork) and Pickled Ginger**: In an era without refrigerators, deep-fried Bourong and highly pickled ginger slices effectively prevented food spoilage [^13]. Interestingly, the signature dish of the early Chishang bento was actually the "freshwater shrimp" abundant in Dapo Pond, which later evolved into the common side dish combination seen today [^18].

## From Fake Rice Storm to Origin Certification: Guarding Dignity

In 1996, the wave of counterfeits reached its peak, with the market flooded with bento boxes using inferior rice to pass off as "Chishang Rice" [^7]. Facing the crisis, the Chishang Township Office promoted Taiwan's first "Geographical Indication Certification Mark" in 2005. This certification required the rice grain weight to meet the national CNS standard, and the taste value must be above 65 points [^8].

📝 **Curator's Note:** The common saying often believes that "Chishang Rice is famous because of its good soil," but this reverses the cause and effect. The success of Chishang Rice lies more in its "structural defense." The 1996 crisis forced the people of Chishang to establish a self-discipline system stricter than national standards, turning "Chishang" from a geographical noun into a brand with legal boundaries.

## The Three Giants and the Chain Empire: When Nostalgia Becomes an Industry

Today, around the Chishang Train Station, there are three representative old shops:

- **Wutao Chishang Rice Package**: The old shop introduced an external management team in 1999 and was renamed "Wutao," subsequently embarking on a path of nationwide chain franchising [^2]. Wutao also converted retired Taiwan Railways Administration carriages into a "Cultural Story Hall," transforming rice cuisine culture from simple food into an educational curatorial space [^19].
- **Quan Mei Hsing**: Long-term contractor of the hawking business on the Chishang Train Station platform, it is the source of the image of many old travelers remembering the figure hawking with a basket [^9].
- **Jiaxiang Chishang Rice Package**: Known for the pure quality of its rice, deeply loved by locals.

Although the sound of platform hawking has gradually disappeared due to railway electrification, the Chishang bento has expanded from a small station in Taitung into a collective memory for all of Taiwan.
