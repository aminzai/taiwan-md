# Dan Dan Burger: A Bowl of Mian Xian Geng and a Fast-Food Brand That Never Went North

![Sunset over Kaohsiung’s city center and harbor](/article-images/food/kaohsiung-city-sunset.webp)
_Sunset over Kaohsiung’s city center and harbor. This is a contextual image of the city, not a photo of a Dan Dan Burger storefront or meal. Photo: Heeheemalu / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0._

> **30-Second Overview:** In 1984, Dan Dan Burger opened its first store in the Qianjin District of Kaohsiung. The Qixian Store is located at 224 Qixian 2nd Road, Qianjin District, Kaohsiung. Brand pages and numerous media reports identify Kaohsiung, Tainan, and Pingtung as the brand’s long-term core operating areas. Its menu features burgers, fried chicken, and fries alongside mian xian geng, lean pork porridge, and rice cakes. Public records provide store counts and prices for various years, but no officially published document from Dan Dan Burger confirms the total number of stores across Taiwan in 2026 or explains the reasons for not expanding north. This article clarifies the known origins, menu items, and geographic scope, while leaving the unanswered questions where they belong. [^1] [^2] [^3] [^5] [^8]

In 1984, the first Dan Dan Burger store appeared on Qixian 2nd Road in the Qianjin District of Kaohsiung. In the same year, McDonald’s entered the Taiwanese market. This temporal parallelism easily places Dan Dan in a comparative table of Taiwan’s fast-food history. More interestingly, Dan Dan did not simply replicate the Western menu of burgers, fried chicken, and fries. It placed a bowl of Taiwanese-style noodles within the same set-meal framework, establishing its own dining path from the very beginning. [^1] [^3]

The address of the Qixian Store can be cross-verified through three types of pages. The Dan Dan Burger brand page lists Qixian 2nd Road in Qianjin District, Kaohsiung. The official branch data page lists the complete address of the Qixian Store. The Qixian Store’s own menu page also lists 224 Qixian 2nd Road, Qianjin District, Kaohsiung. These pages anchor the story to a specific location. However, they cannot individually answer questions about the founders’ full names, the headquarters’ organizational structure, or the current total number of stores across Taiwan. [^1] [^2] [^10]

> What is most worth writing about Dan Dan Burger is how it brings burgers and Taiwanese snacks together on the same menu.

## Clarifying the Year

The currently cross-supported founding year is 1984. Dan Dan Burger’s own brand page states “1984” and the 73rd year of the Republic of China (ROC). _United Daily News_’ _Bao Time_, NOWnews, _The Manager_, and the 2026 _News Horizon Times_ all place the first store in 1984. [^1] [^3] [^4] [^5] [^8]

The information card at the bottom of the _The Manager_ article additionally lists “Brand Founding Year: 1974.” This year contradicts the article’s body text, the Dan Dan brand page, and other reports. Since the article’s body text and multiple independent pages point to 1984, this article adopts 1984 and treats 1974 as an unresolved internal data contradiction on that page, without quietly erasing it. [^1] [^4]

Sources found so far refer to the founders only as “the Xie couple” or “a surnamed Xie couple,” without providing full names that can be verified against official pages. Therefore, this article does not supplement the founders’ names nor derive a seemingly complete family story from online nicknames or retellings. What can be stably confirmed is the address on Qixian 2nd Road, the 1984 starting point, and the subsequent southern expansion scope. [^4] [^5] [^7]

## Two Speeds on One Menu

Fast-food ordering relies on clarity. Sets have numbers, meals have combinations, and customers can quickly decide what to eat at the counter. Dan Dan’s uniqueness lies in its combination content. The brand’s self-description lists Cantonese congee, chicken geng (thick soup), mian xian hu (noodle paste), and rice cakes. News reports list mian xian geng, lean pork porridge, and rice cakes alongside burgers, fried chicken, and fries. [^1] [^3] [^4] [^8]

This structure creates two familiar directions in one meal. Burgers and fried chicken connect to the Western-style fast food that became increasingly familiar in Taiwan during the 1980s. Mian xian geng, congee, and rice cakes connect to memories of Taiwanese snacks for breakfast, snacks, and main meals. The “East-meets-West” merger here has a specific menu position. The options listed on the brand page and in reports give this term verifiable content. [^1] [^3] [^4]

A 2008 _United Daily News_ report described Dan Dan as a fast-food restaurant combining Chinese and Western elements into set meals, listing mian xian hu, Cantonese congee, crispy rice cakes, crispy chicken leg burgers, grilled chicken burgers, and pork croquettes. This report also recorded the store manager’s comments on the menu at the time. Since the original newspaper content was reposted and archived by _Bao Time_, this article only uses verifiable menu items and store counts, treating the store manager’s quotes in the repost as not constituting current brand statements. [^3]

## 38 Stores, 43 Stores: Distinct by Year

Dan Dan Burger’s store count data is easily miswritten because each number has its own date. A 2008 _United Daily News_ report stated there were 38 branches in Tainan, Kaohsiung, and Pingtung. A 2019 _foodNEXT_ report, citing the _2019 Taiwan Chain Store Yearbook_, stated there were 43 stores across Taiwan in early 2019. Both numbers can be cited, but neither can be unconditionally turned into “there are now 43 stores” or “an increase of 5 stores over eleven years.” [^3] [^4]

The official branch data page from March 2021 lists store addresses in Tainan, Kaohsiung, and Pingtung. The page lists the Kaohsiung Qixian Store under two different administrative district headings, repeating the same address. If duplicates are removed, the page yields 41 unique addresses. This is a historical page count derived from that page’s list, not the brand’s published store count, nor a real-time 2026 total. [^2]

The value of the official branch page lies in breaking down “the South” into individual addresses. The Tainan page lists stores in Rende, Jiali, Chenggong, Annan, Anping, Xinhua, Xinsi, Chongde, Yongkang, Dawan, and Madou. The Kaohsiung page ranges from Qianjin’s Qixian 1st Road to Gushan, Fengshan, Gangshan, Zuoying, Nanzhi, Qiaotou, and Yanchao. The Pingtung page lists Shengli, Guangdong, and Zhonghua stores in Pingtung City. This list presents how the brand was distributed in 2021, not the current operating status of every store. [^2]

Prices also have the same temporal issue. A 2019 report stated that 14 combination set meals ranged from 79 to 99 NTD. A 2022 ETtoday report noted price adjustments for some sets, the discontinuation of two items, and a price drop for one set. Menus change over time; historical prices only explain the consumption context of that time and cannot endorse today’s counter prices. [^4] [^6]

### Read the Year Before the Number

Placing historical data back into its date allows readers to see the scale of the brand’s changes. The 38 branches described in 2008 answer the scale recorded by _United Daily News_ that year. The 43 stores in early 2019 answer the statistics from the _2019 Taiwan Chain Store Yearbook_ at that time point. The 2021 official address page answers how the store list was compiled. The 2022 price adjustment report answers a specific menu update. Each set of data is useful, and they cannot replace each other. [^2] [^3] [^4] [^6]

The difference in store counts also reminds us that brand research cannot just look for the most convenient number. Placing the 2008 data, 2019 data, and 2021 address page side by side reveals nodes of data updates, not a growth curve that can be directly extrapolated. The official page lists addresses, the yearbook lists statistics, and news articles leave behind contemporary interviews and narratives. They must be read together so that time is not flattened into a single present tense. [^2] [^3] [^4]

### Menu Items and Prices Also Update

When applying this date principle to the menu, the items listed on the brand page prove which options were available on that page at that time. The sets listed in the 2008 report prove the combinations seen by reporters that year. The 2022 discontinuations and price adjustments prove that the menu is not permanently fixed. This article does not create a “Dan Dan Current Complete Menu” because the data found so far does not provide a dated, 2026 official menu covering all branches. [^1] [^3] [^6]

It is not the same dish as Dan Dan’s mian xian geng, nor does it prove which type of noodle Dan Dan uses. It only reminds readers that Taiwanese noodles can enter different scenes: household shopping, street snacks, and fast-food sets. This article still does not treat this photo as a Dan Dan meal photo. ## How Store Data Shapes Brand Impression

The address list on the official branch page offers another research reminder. Readers seeing dense store names and place names easily read the list as a complete current status. But the page itself is March 2021 data, and the list shows the same Qixian address repeated under different administrative district headings. This means the page provides traceable historical information but must be read with dates and duplicate items. The Qixian Store’s own menu page narrows the focus to a single store. It lists the address and phone number, as well as recruitment information for the Qixian Store at that time. This page is suitable for cross-verifying the street address but not for inferring the brand’s entire franchise policy, salary system, or operating hours for all branches. The purpose of a source must match the scope of the page. [^2] [^10]

From the brand page to media reports, Dan Dan’s image has different sizes across different pages. Official pages handle meals and stores. Historical reports handle founding year, store counts, and prices of a certain era. News articles handle external questions and online discussions. Analytical articles propose possible explanations regarding direct operation, costs, and supply chains. These texts can jointly outline the problem but cannot compress all narratives into a single official statement. [^1] [^2] [^3] [^4] [^5] [^7] [^9]

Therefore, this article deliberately avoids listing a “Must-Eat Ranking.” News and travel articles have indeed listed recommended items, but those were recommendations by article authors or within reporting contexts. No public sales statistics for all Dan Dan stores have been found, nor any taste survey representative of all customers. Lean pork mian xian geng, crispy fried chicken, crispy chicken leg burgers, and lean pork porridge can be items that appear repeatedly in sources, but they cannot be rewritten as a sequence voted by customers across Taiwan. [^1] [^3] [^5] [^8]

## Where Is the Public Evidence for “Only Opening in the South”?

“Only opening in the South” is not a claim found only in social media comments. The official branch data page concentrates stores in Tainan, Kaohsiung, and Pingtung. 2024 _Bao Time_ and NOWnews also describe the brand as “Southern-only.” The 2026 _News Horizon Times_ article still lists Kaohsiung, Tainan, and Pingtung as Dan Dan’s main operating areas. These sources jointly support a limited conclusion: Dan Dan has operated long-term in southern Taiwan, and has not been recorded in these sources as having expanded significantly across regions. [^2] [^3] [^5] [^8]

“Why not expand north?” is another question. NOWnews and Sanli News Network have both compiled statements from netizens and Facebook fan pages, involving management considerations, franchise conditions, and family relationships. Foundi’s analytical article also discusses direct operation, costs, supply chains, and regional markets, but simultaneously admits that claims about family members or senior employees having exclusive franchise opportunities lack official verification. These articles are suitable for observing how the outside world understands Dan Dan but cannot be used to announce internal brand policies. [^5] [^7] [^9]

There is another important difference among public sources. Dan Dan’s brand page provides menu and branch information. News articles organize brand history, prices, and netizen discussions. Analytical articles propose possible business explanations. The three types of sources can each answer different questions. Stacking them into “Dan Dan doesn’t go north just because of rent” exceeds the scope of the original sources. [^1] [^2] [^5] [^7] [^9]

As of the data compiled in this article, no official public document from Dan Dan Burger has been found that directly answers whether the reason for not going north is rent, wages, logistics, taste, or franchise systems. Nor has a verifiable, 2026-dated total list of stores across Taiwan been found. The most honest approach is to treat the “Southern-only” limitation as a geographic phenomenon supported by multiple sources, and to leave the underlying reasons as questions not yet officially explained by the brand. [^1] [^2] [^5] [^7] [^9]

## Southern on the Map, Southern on the Table

224 Qixian 2nd Road, Qianjin District, Kaohsiung is a starting point that can be navigated, verified by store pages, and repeatedly confirmed by reports. From here, the official branch page pushes the brand’s spatial scope to Tainan, Kaohsiung, and Pingtung. This geographic shape does not need exaggerated adjectives to stand. It is already a clear brand boundary. [^1] [^2] [^3] [^10]

For readers in central and northern Taiwan, the “Southern-only” limitation places a meal within a journey. NOWnews writes that out-of-town visitors arrange meals when traveling to the south. _The Manager_ also records that Dan Dan attracts people from central and northern Taiwan to travel south due to its East-meets-West menu. These are media observations and reports, not representing the feelings of every traveler or every southern resident. [^4] [^5]

_News Horizon Times_ writes that the Qixian Store is a destination for out-of-town visitors and reminds readers that some branches have fixed rest days, advising them to check operating information before departure. Such travel advice can help readers understand the “special trip south” route, but the article does not provide verifiable tourist statistics. Therefore, this article does not write “pilgrimage” as a universal behavior, nor does it declare the Qixian Store as an attraction everyone will visit. [^8]

This approach may seem conservative, but it is closer to Dan Dan’s existing public data. What can be confirmed is the address, region, menu items, historical store counts, and price changes. What cannot be confirmed is the current store count, the official reason for not going north, the complete rules of franchise conditions, and which items are still supplied in all branches. Each item should remain within its own scope of evidence. [^1] [^2] [^4] [^5] [^6] [^7] [^8] [^9] [^10]

Another free-license photo features Taiwanese beef noodles in Taipei. It is also not a Dan Dan Burger meal and cannot explain the ingredients, taste, or preparation of the lean pork mian xian geng. It is placed here to keep “noodles” within the visible range of Taiwan’s daily diet, while clearly telling readers that the photo provides no evidence for Dan Dan itself. [^1] [^3]

## Not Adding an Answer for the Brand

Dan Dan Burger’s public outline is actually quite specific. It started in Qianjin District, Kaohsiung in 1984. The Qixian Store is located at 224 Qixian 2nd Road. It has long been mainly distributed in Kaohsiung, Tainan, and Pingtung. Its menu places burgers, fried chicken, and fries alongside mian xian geng, lean pork porridge, and rice cakes in the same fast-food framework. Store counts and prices vary by data year. [^1] [^2] [^3] [^4] [^5] [^6] [^8]

These facts are sufficient to answer “What is Dan Dan?” It is a local fast-food brand that started in Kaohsiung, uses the South as its main scope, and identifies itself with an East-meets-West menu. This summary is an editorial judgment compiled from the aforementioned sources, not a Dan Dan official slogan. [^1] [^2] [^3] [^4] [^8]

As for “why it didn’t go north,” current data only allows us to say: the outside world has long been asking, media have repeatedly compiled netizen and fan page speculations, and Foundi also reminded that part of this lacks official verification. Taking one more step forward would turn speculation into fact. This article stops where the evidence can reach. [^5] [^7] [^9]

The significance of a burger paired with mian xian geng does not need to be amplified by a fabricated founder story or an unverified business insider secret. When the brand keeps its stores in a few counties and cities, readers must place the food back into the streets of those counties and cities to understand it. Kaohsiung, Tainan, and Pingtung constitute the geographic conditions under which Dan Dan’s menu can be recognized, giving this meal a clear local position. [^1] [^2] [^3] [^5] [^8]
