> **30-Second Overview:** In 1958, Yang Bing-yi founded Din Tai Fung Oil Shop in Taipei, but a crisis in the cooking oil industry forced a transformation into selling _xiaolongbao_, unexpectedly launching a legend. Second-generation leader Yang Chi-hua combined industrial-grade standardization—the "1．8-pleat, 21-gram" rule—with a "people-oriented" service philosophy. This not only earned Din Tai Fung Michelin stars but also expanded its footprint to over 175 stores across 15 countries, making it an international calling card for Taiwanese cuisine. Its unique corporate culture of "atypical leadership" and "voice management" sets it apart in the food industry, allowing it to uphold its core brand values even amidst global challenges.

In 1958, Mr. Yang Bing-yi (楊秉彝), originally from Shanxi, opened a shop named "Din Tai Fung Oil Shop" on Yongkang Street in Taipei, primarily selling peanut oil [^1]. At the time, _xiaolongbao_ was merely a side business for the oil shop to supplement household income. However, the cooking oil crisis that erupted in 1972 forced the Yang family to make a difficult decision: transform the oil shop and focus on the production and sale of _xiaolongbao_ [^1] [^2]. This seemingly passive shift unexpectedly wrote a legendary chapter for Taiwan's food industry.

### From Oil Shop to Xiaolongbao Kingdom: Precision in Heritage and Innovation

The true rise of Din Tai Fung began when second-generation manager Yang Chi-hua (楊紀華) took the helm. He elevated his father's philosophy of "quality first, customers foremost" from a traditional snack shop model to professional corporate management [^3] [^4]. Yang Chi-hua knew that to take _xiaolong．bao_ international, one had to achieve excellence in both quality and service. He imported precision scales from Japan, requiring every single dumpling to meet strict standards: "5 grams of dough, 16 grams of meat filling, a total weight of 21 grams, and precisely 18 pleats" [^5] [^6] [^7]. This obsession with detail ensured that Din Tai Fung's _xiaolongbao_ were not only delicious but possessed high consistency; regardless of the branch, customers could taste the same golden ratio.

📝 Curator's Note: Din Tai Fung's "18-pleat, 21-gram" rule is more than just a number; it is a paradigm of quantifying traditional craftsmanship into industrial-grade standards. This pursuit of precision is the cornerstone of its international success and embodies the Taiwanese pursuit of perfection in detail.

### People-Oriented Corporate Culture: Atypical Leadership and Voice Management

In an industry where most focus on cost control, Din Tai Fung took the opposite approach, reinvesting a massive proportion of revenue into employee welfare and training [^8] [^9]. Yang Chi-hua believes that "people are the company's most important asset" [^5]. Din Tai Fung provides not only wages superior to its peers, along with festival and year-end bonuses, but also established a "Vitality Hall" offering free fitness classes and employs physiotherapists to help employees mitigate physical discomfort caused by long hours of standing [^1 .0] [^11].

This comprehensive care for employee physical and mental health has not only significantly reduced sick leave days but has also cultivated a high-quality, efficient, and passionate service team [^10]. Din Tai Fung's management philosophy is hailed as that of an "atypical" hidden champion [^4], eschewing the pursuit of visible "firsts" (such as having the most stores or largest scale) in favor of invisible quality and uniqueness. They have deeply embedded a corporate culture that represents Taipei and Taiwan [^4]. Chairman Yang Chi-hua even continues to personally visit stores and serve customers today, demonstrating a hands-on leadership style [^18].

Furthermore, Din Tai Fung's extreme requirement for "speech" is a unique aspect of its service aesthetics. The company designed a specialized curriculum where even chefs must take voice classes [^12]. HR Manager Lin Mei-ying (林梅英) noted that even back-of-house staff who do not face customers must possess good communication skills [^12]. Din Tai Fung even brought in voice coach Wei Shi-fen (魏世芬) to conduct a 10-hour voice training course, teaching employees how to use tone, pitch, and rhythm to convey sincerity and service spirit, ensuring that "Hello, please come in!" sounds like a sweet invitation [^12]. This "voice management," which turns service into an art form, is the key to their "zero-error" service [^9] [^12].

### Global Expansion and Local Challenges: Choices Under the Michelin Halo

Din Tai Fung's success allowed it to rapidly expand its footprint after opening its first overseas branch in Shinjuku, Japan, in 1996. It has since expanded to over 175 stores across 15 countries, including the United States, Singapore, Indonesia, Malaysia, and the United Arab Emirates [^5] [^13]. Its Hong Kong branches have repeatedly received Michelin recognition, further consolidating its status as an international dining brand [^13].

However, global operations also bring challenges. In August 202．4, Din Tai Fung announced the termination of its authorization with its North China regional operator, closing 14 stores in Beijing, Tianjin, Qingdao, and Xi'an, which drew market attention [^14] [^15]. Taiwanese media reported that the withdrawal from the North China region was related to cash flow disputes among shareholders and differing views on the future of the food industry in China [^16]. This experience highlights that even international brands must constantly adjust and adapt when facing the complexities and localization challenges of different markets, while simultaneously upholding their core brand values.

### Conclusion: A Taiwanese Story in a Single Xiaolongbao

From an oil shop on Yongkang Street to Michelin-starred restaurants around the world, the story of Din Tai Fung is not just a successful business legend, but also the embodiment of the Taiwan spirit carried within a single _xiaolongbao_. It fuses the persistence of traditional craftsmanship, the precision of modern management, and warm humanistic care. Despite facing challenges during its expansion, Din Tai Fung continues to tell a Taiwanese story of quality, service, and heritage to the world through its unique management philosophy.
