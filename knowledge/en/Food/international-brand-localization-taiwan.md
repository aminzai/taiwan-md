> **30-Second Overview:** When international brands enter the Taiwan market, they bring not only globalized products but also deeply localized strategies that blend Taiwan's unique food culture and consumption habits into their offerings and services. From McDonald's corn soup and KFC's Portuguese egg tarts, to Pizza Hut's boundary-pushing signature pizzas, and 7-Eleven's braised snacks and OPEN rice boxes, even kaiten-zushi's fondness for salmon—these cases demonstrate brands' respect and flexibility toward the Taiwan market. Going further, Starbucks' themed stores, Costco's worldwide top-performing store, MOS Burger's rice culture, IKEA's dining miracle, and Carrefour's rebranding after acquisition all jointly illustrate how international brands achieve unique success formulas through cultural integration.

As international brands expand globally, they often face the challenge of balancing standardization with localization. In Taiwan, this challenge has given rise to many unique products and services, bringing these brands closer to Taiwan consumers' daily lives and unexpectedly creating many "Taiwan-exclusive" experiences that surprise foreigners. These localization strategies are not only commercial successes but also reflect Taiwan's rich and diverse food culture and consumption habits.

## McDonald's: Stomach-Warming Corn Soup and the Allure of Local Sweet Potatoes

The global fast-food leader McDonald's has demonstrated its localization flexibility in the Taiwan market. Among its most well-known offerings are its **Corn Soup** and **Golden Sweet Potato Fries**. The corn soup is unique to McDonald's worldwide, with its origins tracing back to 1978, before McDonald's officially entered Taiwan in 1984. A local Taiwanese fast-food shop named 「麥當樂」 had already been offering "Chicken Noodle Corn Soup"[^1][^6]. After McDonald's entered Taiwan, in order to cater to the Taiwanese habit of eating rice with soup, the brand specifically retained and optimized this product, making it a classic combo option for McDonald's Taiwan[^6].

> 📝 **Curator's Note:** The localization of corn soup is not merely a flavor adjustment but a deep understanding of Taiwanese dining habits. A bowl of hot soup carries the expectations and comfort of Taiwanese people's main meals.

Furthermore, McDonald's Taiwan has also introduced **Golden Sweet Potato Fries** made from Taiwan's local Taiwan Agricultural No. 64 red heart sweet potatoes[^1]. This product not only satisfies Taiwanese consumers' love for sweet potatoes but also showcases the brand's connection with local agriculture in ingredient sourcing. These localized products have successfully transformed McDonald's from a simple "American fast food" into a more "Taiwan-flavored" dining choice.

## KFC: Dominating with Portuguese Egg Tarts and Innovation in Paper-Wrapped Chicken

KFC's localization strategy in Taiwan is exemplified by the tremendous success of its **Portuguese egg tarts**. This dessert has even been jokingly referred to as an "egg tart shop delayed by fried chicken"[^7]. The introduction of Portuguese egg tarts originated from the egg tart craze that swept Taiwan in the 1990s. KFC introduced the recipe from Macau's Margaret's in 1998 and quickly made it a signature product[^7]. This strategy successfully expanded KFC's brand image in Taiwan from merely a fried chicken shop to a dining option where "desserts and main dishes coexist," with egg tarts becoming one of the primary reasons many consumers visit KFC.

In addition to egg tarts, KFC in Taiwan has also launched its **paper-wrapped chicken series**. This cooking method, which uses paper packaging to lock in meat juices, is relatively rare across KFC globally but aligns with Taiwanese consumers' preference for tender and juicy textures[^2]. Furthermore, products such as **sweet potato QQ balls** and **sweet potato fries** also utilize locally sourced Taiwanese sweet potatoes and incorporate elements of Taiwan's night market snacks, further deepening KFC's local connection in the Taiwanese market[^2].

## Pizza Hut: Challenging the Limits of Taste with Creative Pizzas

Pizza Hut's localization strategy in Taiwan is perhaps the most experimental and buzzworthy among international brands. From "Cilantro Century Egg and Pig's Blood Cake Pizza" to "Stinky Tofu Pizza" and "Bubble Tea Pizza," these creative products that challenge the limits of taste have repeatedly sparked heated discussions on social media[^3][^8]. For example, the cilantro century egg and pig's blood cake pizza launched in 2021 set a record of over 100,000 Google keyword searches in a single day on its launch day[^3].

Behind these seemingly "quirky" flavors is Pizza Hut's general manager Liang Chia-chun (from Hong Kong), who drives the "e-commerce mindset" and "WOW Pizza strategy"[^3]. Liang Chia-chun believes that in Taiwan, where food delivery platforms are thriving, Pizza Hut is essentially an e-commerce business, so "talkability," "traffic," and "transaction" have become the key 3T strategy[^3]. By continuously launching highly buzzworthy localized products, even if consumers don't necessarily purchase these special flavors, it can effectively enhance the brand's online presence and attention, thereby driving overall sales[^3]. This strategy not only breaks the established impression that Taiwanese people only eat pizza during festivals, but also successfully positions Pizza Hut as a brand full of innovation and vitality[^3].

## 7-ELEVEN: Practitioner of Lifestyle Proposals and the Allure of the OPEN Warrior

As a representative of Taiwan's convenience store culture, 7-ELEVEN's localization has long transcended the mere realm of products, embedding itself deeply into the daily lives of Taiwanese people. In addition to the well-known **tea leaf eggs** (with annual sales exceeding 100 million pieces), in recent years 7-ELEVEN has launched "Star-Rated Feast Braised Snacks," standardizing the culture of traditional night market braised snacks and offering consumers 24-hour access to convenient delights[^4][^9].

> 📝 **Curator's Note:** 7-ELEVEN's success lies not only in the diversity of its products, but in how it transforms the convenience store into a platform that provides "lifestyle proposals," satisfying Taiwanese people's dual needs for convenience and local cuisine.

Another successful case of localization is its exclusive mascot, the **OPEN Warrior**. The OPEN Warrior was jointly developed by Uni-President and Dentsu in 2005. It not only has a complete character setting and background story, but has also successfully integrated into Taiwan's popular culture through peripheral products and themed activities, even creating a business value of NT$1 billion in annual revenue for 7-ELEVEN[^4][^10][^11]. The success of the OPEN Warrior proves the enormous potential of brand mascots in the Taiwan market and their importance in building brand emotional connections.

## Conveyor Belt Sushi: The Taiwanese Passion for Salmon

In recent years, conveyor belt sushi brands such as Sushi Land and Kura Sushi have rapidly risen in the Taiwan market, showcasing interesting localization phenomena — namely, the abnormal passion of Taiwanese consumers for **salmon**. Compared to Japan’s home market, Taiwan’s conveyor belt sushi restaurants typically offer a far wider variety of salmon items and achieve significantly higher sales volumes for salmon compared to other fish[^5]. This trend even sparked a "Salmon Chaos" in 2021, when Sushi Land launched a renaming promotion that attracted over 300 people across Taiwan to legally change their names to "salmon" for free sushi[^5][^12].

The Taiwanese fondness for salmon can be attributed to its widespread perception as offering a high CP value and rich flavor, as well as the fact that when Japanese cuisine was first introduced to Taiwan, salmon was the most popular choice for raw fish dishes[^5]. Data shows that Cherbon, Taiwan’s largest salmon importer, brings in approximately 2,500 tons of salmon annually, accounting for about 20% of Taiwan’s total salmon import volume[^5][^13]. This passion for salmon has driven conveyor belt sushi brands to introduce an ever-expanding array of salmon-based products in the Taiwan market to satisfy consumer demand.

## Starbucks: The Aesthetics of Localization in the Third Space

Starbucks' localization strategy in Taiwan extends beyond beverages, delving deeply into its "third space" business philosophy. By renovating historic sites or integrating local landscapes, Starbucks has created distinctive stores such as the Bao'an Store in Dadaocheng's Ye Jin Tu historic residence, the former joint office building store in Huwei, Yunlin, and the Hualien Ideal Land Store, among others[^14][^15]. These unique stores not only serve coffee but also act as carriers of local culture and history, reducing the gap between the international brand and the local environment, and enhancing consumers' spatial experience[^15].

Additionally, Starbucks has actively launched seasonal gift boxes with Taiwanese characteristics, such as Mid-Autumn mooncakes, Dragon Boat Festival zongzi, and local ingredient products like the local tea beverage series and taro Mont Blanc[^14]. The introduction of these products aims to cater to Taiwanese consumers' emphasis on festive customs and their preference for local flavors, further deepening the connection between the brand and Taiwanese culture.

## Costco: Perfect Fusion of Global Store King and Localized Services

The American warehouse retailer Costco has achieved remarkable success in Taiwan, serving as a model for international brand localization. Costco's Taiwan stores, particularly the South Taichung location, have earned the title of "most profitable store worldwide," while the Neihu and Zhonghe branches consistently rank among the global top ten[^16][^17]. With over 4 million members in Taiwan, and an annual fee of NT$1,350, Costco's membership revenue alone reaches NT$5.4 billion, underscoring its business model where 80% of profits come from membership fees rather than product margins[^17].

Costco's localized services in Taiwan are also highly notable, including the rare installation of gas stations, with the South Taichung store leading the island in fuel volume[^18]. Additionally, while Costco primarily offers bulk goods, its Taiwan stores have expanded their selection of deli items and snacks tailored to local family structures and preferences[^17]. This strategy precisely targets Taiwanese consumers' pursuit of high cost-performance value and family shopping habits, leading to tremendous market success.

## MOS Burger: The Oriental Fast Food Representative of Rice Culture

Originating from Japan, MOS Burger has successfully developed its "oriental flavors" and "high-tech service" features in the Taiwan market. Its most representative product is the **rice burger**. In 1991, MOS Burger Taiwan launched the "MOS Barbecue Pearl Burger," using locally sourced rice as the main ingredient, successfully integrating rice culture into fast food products and making it a signature item of the brand[^19][^20].

MOS Burger has also actively promoted the use of local ingredients, such as being the first store in Taiwan to receive the "Taiwan Pork Certification Mark" in 2020, and emphasizing the use of products with the Taiwan Rice Certification Mark[^19]. Compared to other Western fast food brands, MOS Burger is closer to Taiwanese people's reliance on premium fast food and rice culture, successfully establishing a unique market position.

## IKEA: The Restaurant Delayed by Furniture and Creative Marketing

The Swedish furniture brand IKEA in Taiwan has become deeply associated with its image as a "restaurant delayed by furniture." In Taiwan, IKEA's food revenue accounts for as high as 12%, nearly three times the global average (4-5%) and ranking first worldwide[^21][^22]. Among these, the mere NT$10 **soft-serve ice cream** alone achieves annual sales exceeding one million units, with its sales volume even accounting for 30% of food revenue[^23]. This "leading chicken strategy" attracts foot traffic through affordable food, not only enhancing the customer shopping experience but also creating the psychological impression that "IKEA products are all very cheap"[^24].

IKEA's localized marketing in Taiwan is also full of creativity, such as its **BLÅHAJ** shark plush toy sparking a craze in Taiwan, and combining Taiwanese festivals to launch distinctive foods, such as wasabi soft-serve ice cream, soy milk soy sauce-flavored soft-serve ice cream, and other quirky flavors[^25]. These strategies have successfully transformed "browsing furniture" into "family dining outings," satisfying the Taiwanese people's high interest in dining.

## Carrefour: From International Brand to Deep Localization as "Prosperity Plaza"

The French hypermarket brand Carrefour's localization strategy in Taiwan has undergone significant transformation, moving from aggressive acquisitions to brand repositioning. In 2020, Carrefour acquired Wellcome and Jasons supermarkets for NT$3.2 billion, aiming to strengthen its footprint in the "community supermarket" segment to respond to intense competition in Taiwan's retail market[^26][^27]. Through these acquisitions, Carrefour rapidly expanded its store network and deepened its presence in Taiwanese communities, providing services more aligned with local resident needs.

Carrefour also actively promoted its "Influence Center" initiative, partnering closely with Taiwanese small-scale farmers to promote local ingredients and friendly eggs, demonstrating its support for local agriculture[^28]. However, with Uni-President completing the transaction on June 30, 2023, officially acquiring 100% ownership of Carrefour Taiwan, making it a purely Taiwan-incorporated company[^29][^30]. This change in ownership also led to a rebranding. Due to the expiration of the brand licensing agreement with the French Carrefour, Carrefour Taiwan decided to terminate the license on December 19, 2025, and will officially change its name starting **July 1, 2026**[^31][^32][^33].

The hypermarkets will be renamed **"Prosperity Plaza" (Prosperity Plaza)**, while the supermarkets will be renamed **"Uni-Prosperity" (Uni-Prosperity)**[^34][^35]. This renaming is not merely a change of signage; it symbolizes Carrefour's formal integration into the Uni-President retail ecosystem, creating deeper synergies with brands like 7-ELEVEN and RT-Mart, and represents a shift in brand image from "French brand" to "local brand," emphasizing the auspicious meanings of "Prosperity Plaza" and "Uni-Prosperity," which better align with Taiwanese consumers' cultural preferences[^36][^37].

## Conclusion

International brands' localization strategies in Taiwan represent a culinary transformation that fuses global vision with local insights. From McDonald's corn soup and KFC's Portuguese egg tarts, to Pizza Hut's creative pizzas, 7-Eleven's _lu wei_ (stewed snacks) and OPEN's offerings, and the salmon craze at kaiten-zushi, these cases are not merely commercial successes but vivid portrayals of Taiwan's distinctive food culture and consumer habits. Going further, Starbucks' specialty stores, Costco's status as the world's most profitable store, MOS Burger's rice-based cuisine, IKEA's dining miracle, and even Carrefour's local acquisitions and brand transformation, together illustrate how international brands in Taiwan craft unique success formulas through cultural integration and business innovation. These brands, through continuous innovation and adjustment, demonstrate that only by deeply understanding and embracing local culture can one stand out in a fiercely competitive market and forge deep emotional connections with consumers.
