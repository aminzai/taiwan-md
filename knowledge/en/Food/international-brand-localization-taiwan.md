> **30-Second Overview:** As international brands enter the Taiwanese market, they bring not only globalized goods but also integrate Taiwan's unique food culture and consumption habits into their products and services through in-depth localization strategies. From McDonald's corn soup and KFC Portuguese egg tarts to Pizza Hut's specialty pizzas that challenge taste buds, 7-ELEVEN's _lurou fan_ (braised snacks) and OPEN drinks, and even the preference for salmon at Sushiro, these cases demonstrate the brands' respect and flexibility toward the Taiwanese market. Furthermore, Starbucks' signature stores, Costco's status as a global store leader, Mos Burger's food culture, IKEA's dining miracles, and Carrefour's transformation after acquisitions and renaming collectively paint a picture of how international brands create unique success formulas through cultural integration.

In the process of global expansion, international brands often face the challenge of balancing standardization with localization. In Taiwan, this challenge has given rise to many unique products and services that not only bring these brands closer to the lives of Taiwanese consumers but have also unexpectedly created many "Taiwan-exclusive" experiences that surprise foreigners. These localization strategies are not just commercial successes; they also reflect Taiwan's rich and diverse food culture and consumption habits.

## McDonald's: The Charm of Warm Corn Soup and Local Sweet Potatoes

The global fast-food leader, McDonald's, has demonstrated its localization flexibility in the Taiwanese market. Among its most well-known offerings are **corn soup** and **golden sweet potato fries**. Corn soup is unique among global McDonald's; its origins can be traced back to a local fast-food restaurant in Taiwan named "McDonald's" (麥當樂) that offered "chicken corn soup" [^1][^6] before McDonald's officially entered Taiwan in 1984. After entering Taiwan, McDonald's specifically retained and optimized this product to cater to the Taiwanese dining habit of having a soup with a meal, making it a classic side dish at McDonald's in Taiwan [^6].

> 📝 **Curator's Note:** The localization of corn soup is not just an adjustment in flavor but also a reflection of a deep understanding of Taiwanese eating habits. A bowl of hot soup carries the Taiwanese people's expectation and comfort regarding a proper meal.

Furthermore, McDonald's in Taiwan has previously launched **golden sweet potato fries** [^1], made from local Taiwanese sweet potatoes (specifically 'Red Heart' variety 64). This product not only satisfies the Taiwanese consumers' fondness for sweet potatoes but also demonstrates the brand's connection to local agriculture through ingredient selection. These localized products have successfully transformed McDonald's from merely "American fast food" into a dining choice with a more distinct "Taiwanese flavor."

## KFC: The Reign of Egg Tarts and the Innovation of Paper-Wrapped Chicken

KFC's localization strategy in Taiwan is exemplified by the massive success of its **Portuguese egg tarts**. This dessert has even been jokingly referred to as the "egg tart shop overshadowed by fried chicken" [^7]. The introduction of Portuguese egg tarts originated from the craze for these pastries that swept through Taiwan in the 1990s. KFC introduced the recipe from Macalline Egg Tarts in Macau in 1998, and it quickly became a signature item [^7]. This strategy successfully expanded KFC's brand image in Taiwan from merely a fried chicken specialty store to a dining option that emphasizes "both desserts and main courses," with egg tarts becoming one of the primary reasons many consumers visit KFC.

In addition to egg tarts, KFC in Taiwan also introduced the **paper-wrapped chicken series**. This cooking method, which uses paper wrapping to lock in meat juices, is relatively rare among global KFCs but aligns with Taiwanese dietary habits that prefer tender and juicy textures [^2]. Furthermore, products such as **sweet potato QQ balls** and **sweet potato fries** also utilize local sweet potatoes from Taiwan and incorporate elements of night market snacks, further deepening KFC's local connection in the Taiwanese market [^2].

# McDonald's: Creative Pizzas Pushing Taste Buds to the Limit

McDonald's localization strategy in Taiwan is arguably one of the most experimental and talked-about among international brands. These creative products, such as "Cilantro Century Egg Pig Blood Cake Pizza," "Stinky Tofu Pizza," and "Bubble Tea Pizza"—which challenge consumers' taste buds—have repeatedly sparked heated discussions on social media[^3][^8]. For example, the Cilantro Century Egg Pig Blood Cake Pizza launched in 2021 set a record by exceeding 100,000 daily searches on Google on its launch day[^3].

Behind these seemingly "exotic" flavors is the "e-commerce mindset" and "WOW Pizza strategy" promoted by McDonald's General Manager Lai Chia-chuan (from Hong Kong)[^3]. Lai Chia-chuan believes that in a market like Taiwan, where food delivery platforms are prevalent, McDonald's essentially functions as e-commerce. Therefore, "Talkability," "Traffic," and "Transaction" have become the key 3T strategy[^3]. By continuously launching highly talked-about localized products, even if consumers do not necessarily purchase these special flavors, it effectively increases brand visibility and attention online, which in turn drives overall sales[^3]. This strategy has not only broken the preconceived notion among Taiwanese people that "pizza is only eaten during holidays" but has also successfully positioned McDonald's as an innovative and vibrant brand[^3].

## 7-ELEVEN: The Practitioner of Lifestyle Propositions and the Charm of OPEN Jiang

As a representative of Taiwan's convenience store culture, 7-ELEVEN's localization has long surpassed mere product offerings; it has deeply integrated into the daily lives of Taiwanese people. In addition to its well-known **tea eggs** (which sell over 100 million annually), in recent years, 7-ELEVEN has introduced "Star Feast Braised Snacks" (_xīngjí xiǎngyàn lǔwèi_), standardizing the traditional night market braised snack culture of Taiwan and providing consumers with convenient options available 24 hours a day[^4][^9].

> 📝 **Curator's Note:** The success of 7-ELEVEN lies not only in its product diversity but also in how it has established itself as a platform offering "lifestyle propositions," meeting the dual needs of Taiwanese people for convenience and local cuisine.

Another successful case of localization is its exclusive mascot, **OPEN Jiang**. Developed by Uni-President Convenience Store and Dentsu Japan in 2005, OPEN Jiang not only possesses a complete character setting and backstory but has also successfully integrated into Taiwan's popular culture through merchandise and themed events, even generating an annual commercial value of NT$1 billion for 7-ELEVEN[^4][^10][^11]. The success of OPEN Jiang proves the immense potential of brand mascots in the Taiwanese market and their importance in building emotional connections with consumers.

## Conveyor Belt Sushi: Taiwanese Obsession with Salmon

In recent years, conveyor belt sushi brands that have rapidly risen in the Taiwan market, such as Sushiro and Kura Sushi, have also demonstrated an interesting localization phenomenon: the abnormal fervor of Taiwanese consumers for **salmon**. Compared to Japan itself, the salmon items and sales at conveyor belt sushi restaurants in Taiwan are usually far higher than other fish species[^5]. This phenomenon even sparked the "Salmon War" in 2021, when Sushiro held a renaming promotion that attracted over 300 people across Taiwan who changed their ID names to "salmon" to enjoy free sushi[^5][^12].

The Taiwanese preference for salmon can be attributed to the common perception of its "high cost-performance ratio and rich texture," as well as the fact that when Japanese cuisine was first promoted in Taiwan, salmon was one of the most widely available sashimi options[^5]. Data shows that Chang Xian, a major salmon importer in Taiwan, imports about 2,500 tons of salmon annually, accounting for about 20% of the total imported amount in Taiwan[^5][^13]. This passion for salmon has prompted conveyor belt sushi brands to launch more diverse salmon products in the Taiwanese market to meet consumer demand.

## Starbucks: Local Aesthetics of the Third Place

Starbucks' localization strategy in Taiwan is not only reflected in its beverages but also deeply embedded in its concept of the "third place." Taiwanese Starbucks creates uniquely characteristic stores by renovating historical sites or integrating local landscapes, such as the store located in the Ye Jin Tu Ancient House in Dazhong, the former Contract Hall in Hubei, Yunlin, and the Ideal Land store in Hualien[^14][^15]. These distinctive stores not only serve coffee but also act as vessels for local culture and history, reducing the gap between international brands and the local environment and enhancing the consumer's spatial experience[^15].

Furthermore, Starbucks actively launches festive gift boxes with Taiwanese characteristics, such as Mid-Autumn Festival mooncakes and Dragon Boat Festival zongzi (rice dumplings), as well as products made from local ingredients, like local tea series and taro Mont Blanc[^14]. The introduction of these products aims to cater to Taiwanese consumers' emphasis on holiday customs and their preference for local flavors, further deepening the connection between the brand and Taiwanese culture.

## Costco: The Perfect Combination of Global Retail and Localized Service

The success of the American hypermarket Costco in Taiwan is a model for international brand localization. Costco stores in Taiwan, particularly the Nantou branch, have held the title of "Global Profit Leader," while the Neihu and Zhonghe branches have consistently ranked among the top ten globally [^16][^17]. With over 4 million members in Taiwan, membership fees alone account for NT$5.4 billion based on an annual fee of NT$1,350, which confirms their business model where 80% of profits come from membership fees rather than product gross margin [^17].

Costco's localized services in Taiwan are also significant; for example, they have gasoline stations, a feature rare globally, with the Nantou branch having the highest fuel sales in Taiwan [^18]. Furthermore, although Costco primarily offers large-sized items, Taiwanese stores have added more ready-made meals and snacks favored by locals to cater to local family structures [^17]. This strategy accurately targets the Taiwanese consumer psychology of "seeking high cost-performance" and "family grocery shopping," leading to great success in the Taiwanese market.

## Mos Burger: An Eastern Fast Food Representative of Rice Culture

Mos Burger, originating from Japan, has successfully promoted its characteristics of "Eastern flavors" and "high-tech service" in the Taiwanese market. The most representative product is the **rice burger**. In 1991, Taiwan's Mos Burger launched the "Mos Grilled Meat Pearl Burger," using local Taiwanese rice as a main ingredient to successfully integrate rice culture into fast food products, making it a signature item of the brand [^19][^20].

Mos Burger also actively promotes the use of local ingredients; for instance, in 2020, it received the first "Taiwan Pork Certification Mark" in Taiwan and emphasizes using Taiwanese rice-certified products [^19]. Compared to other American fast-food brands, Mos Burger is closer to the reliance of Taiwanese people on refined fast food and rice culture, successfully establishing a unique position in the market.

## IKEA: The Restaurant Held Back by Furniture and Creative Marketing

The Swedish furniture brand IKEA has established a strong image in Taiwan as the "restaurant held back by furniture." Food and beverage revenue at Taiwanese IKEA accounts for as much as 12%, nearly three times the global average (4-5%), ranking it number one globally[^21][^22]. The annual sales of just 10-dollar **ice cream** have surpassed one million units, accounting for 30% of food revenue[^23]. This "guide chicken strategy" attracts foot traffic through low-priced food, not only enhancing the customer's shopping experience but also creating a psychological impression among consumers that "IKEA products are all cheap"[^24].

IKEA's localized marketing in Taiwan is also full of creativity. For example, its **Shark (BLÅHAJ)** dolls have created a craze in Taiwan, and it has launched unique flavors combined with Taiwanese festivals, such as wasabi ice cream and soy sauce soybean milk ice cream—exotic tastes[^25]. These strategies successfully transformed "furniture shopping" into a "family dining outing," meeting the high interest of Taiwanese people in food and beverage.

## Carrefour: Local Deepening from an International Brand to "Wan Jia Fu"

The localization strategy of the French retailer Carrefour in Taiwan has undergone a major transformation, moving from aggressive acquisitions to brand transition. In 2020, Carrefour acquired Wellcome and Jasons supermarkets for NT$3.2 billion, aiming to strengthen its footprint in "community supermarkets" to cope with intense competition in the Taiwanese retail market[^26][^27]. Through these acquisitions, Carrefour was able to rapidly expand its store network and penetrate deeper into local communities in Taiwan, providing services closer to residents' needs.

Carrefour also actively promoted the "Influence Center" project, collaborating closely with small farmers in Taiwan to promote local ingredients and friendly eggs, demonstrating its support for local agriculture[^28]. However, following the completion of the transaction by Uni-President on June 30, 2023, which secured 100% ownership of Carrefour in Taiwan, making it a purely Taiwanese-owned company[^29][^30]. This change in equity also led to a brand name change. As the brand licensing agreement with the French Carrefour expired, Carrefour in Taiwan resolved to terminate the license on December 19, 2025, and officially rebranded starting **July 1, 2026**[^31][^32][^33].

The hypermarkets will be renamed **"Wan Jia Fu" (Prosperity Plaza)**, while the supermarkets will be renamed **"Le Jia Kang" (Uni-Prosperity)**[^34][^35]. This renaming is not just a change of signage; it symbolizes Carrefour's formal integration into the Uni retail system, creating deeper synergies with brands like 7-ELEVEN and Kangsimei. It also represents the brand image shifting from a "French brand" to a "local brand," emphasizing the auspicious connotations of "Wan Jia Fu" and "Le Jia Kang," which better aligns with the cultural preferences of Taiwanese consumers[^36][^37].

## Conclusion

The localization strategies of international brands in Taiwan are a transformation of taste that merges global vision with local insight. From McDonald's corn soup and KFC Portuguese egg tarts, to Pizza Hut's creative pizzas, 7-ELEVEN's braised snacks and _jiang_ (a type of food), and the salmon craze at Sushiro, these cases are not merely commercial successes but vivid reflections of Taiwan's unique culinary culture and consumption habits. Furthermore, Starbucks' specialty stores, Costco's status as a global store champion, Mos Burger's rice-based food culture, IKEA's dining miracles, and Carrefour's local acquisitions and brand transformation collectively paint a picture of how international brands create a unique success formula on Taiwanese soil through cultural integration and business innovation. Through continuous innovation and adjustment, these brands prove that only by deeply understanding and embracing local culture can they stand out in a fiercely competitive market and establish a deep emotional connection with consumers.
