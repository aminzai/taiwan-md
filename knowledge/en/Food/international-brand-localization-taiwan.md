**30-second overview:** International brands entering the Taiwan market bring not only globalized products but also integrate Taiwan's unique food culture and consumption habits into their products and services through deep localization strategies. From McDonald's corn chowder and KFC's Portuguese egg tarts, to Pizza Hut's palate-challenging specialty pizzas, and 7-ELEVEN's _luwei_ (braised delicacies) and OPEN Xiao Jiang, even conveyor-belt sushi's preference for salmon — these cases demonstrate brands' respect for and flexibility toward the Taiwan market. Furthermore, Starbucks' specialty stores, Costco's status as the world's top-performing store, Mos Burger's rice-based food culture, IKEA's dining miracle, and even Carrefour's transformation after its acquisition and renaming, together depict how international brands create unique formulas for success through cultural integration.

In the process of global expansion, international brands often face the challenge of balancing standardization and localization. In Taiwan, this challenge has given rise to many unique products and services that not only bring these brands closer to the lives of Taiwan consumers but also unexpectedly create many "Taiwan-exclusive" experiences that surprise foreigners. These localization strategies are not only commercial successes but also reflect Taiwan's rich and diverse food culture and consumption habits.

## McDonald's: The Comfort of Corn Chowder and the Appeal of Local Sweet Potatoes

Global fast-food giant McDonald's has demonstrated its flexibility in localization in the Taiwan market. The most well-known examples are the **corn chowder** and **golden sweet potato fries** on its menu. The corn chowder is unique among McDonald's worldwide; its origins trace back to 1978, before the U.S. McDonald's officially entered Taiwan (1984), when a local Taiwan fast-food shop called "McDonald's" (麥當樂) was already serving "chicken corn soup" (雞絨玉米湯)[^1][^6]. After McDonald's entered Taiwan, to cater to the Taiwanese habit of having soup with rice, it specially retained and refined this product, making it a classic side dish option at McDonald's Taiwan[^6].

> 📝 **Curator's Note:** The localization of corn chowder is not merely a flavor adjustment, but a reflection of a deep understanding of Taiwanese dining habits. A bowl of hot soup carries the Taiwanese people's expectations and comfort for a proper meal.

Additionally, McDonald's Taiwan once launched **golden sweet potato fries** made from Taiwan's locally grown Tainong 64 red-heart sweet potatoes[^1]. This product not only satisfied Taiwanese consumers' love for sweet potatoes but also demonstrated the brand's connection with local agriculture in its ingredient selection. These localized products successfully transformed McDonald's from simply "American fast food" into a dining choice with more "Taiwan flavor."

## KFC: Egg Tart Dominance and Paper-Wrapped Chicken Innovation

KFC's localization strategy in Taiwan is represented by the tremendous success of its **Portuguese-style egg tarts**. This dessert has even been jokingly called an "egg tart shop delayed by fried chicken"[^7]. The introduction of Portuguese-style egg tarts stemmed from the Portuguese egg tart craze that swept Taiwan in the 1990s; KFC introduced the recipe for Macau's Margaret's egg tarts in 1998, and it quickly became a signature product[^7]. This strategy successfully transformed KFC's brand image in Taiwan from a simple fried chicken specialty shop into a dining option that "equally emphasizes desserts and main dishes," with egg tarts even becoming one of the primary reasons many consumers visit KFC.

Besides egg tarts, KFC in Taiwan also launched the **paper-wrapped chicken series**; this cooking method of baking wrapped in paper to lock in juices is relatively rare among KFC outlets worldwide, yet it aligns with Taiwanese people's dietary preference for tender, juicy textures[^2]. Additionally, products such as **sweet potato QQ balls** and **sweet potato fries** likewise utilize local Taiwanese sweet potatoes and incorporate elements of Taiwanese night market snacks, further deepening KFC's local connection in the Taiwan market[^2].

## Pizza Hut: Creative Pizzas That Push the Limits of Taste

Pizza Hut Taiwan's localization strategy can be considered the most experimental and buzz-worthy among international brands. From the _cilantro century egg pig blood cake pizza_ to the _stinky tofu pizza_ and _bubble tea pizza_, these creative products that challenge consumers' taste buds have repeatedly sparked heated discussions on social media[^3][^8]. For example, the _cilantro century egg pig blood cake pizza_ launched in 2021 set a record of over 100,000 Google keyword searches in a single day on its launch date[^3].

Behind these seemingly "bizarre" flavors is the "e-commerce mindset" and "WOW Pizza strategy" promoted by Pizza Hut General Manager Liang Chia-chun (from Hong Kong)[^3]. Liang Chia-chun believes that in Taiwan, where food delivery platforms prevail, Pizza Hut is essentially an e-commerce business; therefore, "Talkability," "Traffic," and "Transaction" have become the key 3T strategy[^3]. By continuously launching highly buzz-worthy localized products, even if consumers don't necessarily purchase these special flavors, it can effectively boost the brand's online buzz and attention, thereby driving overall sales[^3]. This strategy not only broke the Taiwanese perception that "pizza is only for holidays," but also successfully shaped Pizza Hut into a brand full of innovation and vitality[^3].

## 7-ELEVEN: Practitioner of Life Proposals and the Charm of OPEN Xiao Jiang

As the representative of Taiwan's convenience store culture, 7-ELEVEN's localization has long transcended mere product categories, penetrating deeply into the daily lives of Taiwanese people. Beyond the well-known **tea eggs** (annual sales exceeding 100 million), in recent years 7-ELEVEN has also launched the "Star-Grade Braised Delicacies Feast," standardizing Taiwan's traditional night market braised delicacy culture and offering consumers the convenience of enjoying it 24 hours a day[^4][^9].

> 📝 **Curator's Note:** 7-ELEVEN's success lies not only in product diversity, but in how it has built the convenience store into a platform providing "life proposals," satisfying Taiwanese people's dual needs for convenience and local cuisine.

Another successful localization case is its exclusive brand mascot **OPEN Xiao Jiang**. OPEN Xiao Jiang was co-developed in 2005 by Uni-President Convenience Store and Japan's Dentsu; it not only possesses a complete character design and story background, but has also successfully integrated into Taiwan's pop culture through merchandise and themed events, even generating NT$1 billion in annual commercial value for 7-ELEVEN[^4][^10][^11]. OPEN Xiao Jiang's success proves the enormous potential of brand mascots in the Taiwan market and their importance in building emotional brand connections.

## Conveyor Belt Sushi: Taiwan's Salmon Obsession

Conveyor belt sushi brands that have rapidly risen in the Taiwan market in recent years, such as Sushiro and Kura Sushi, have also shown an interesting localization phenomenon: Taiwan consumers' abnormal obsession with **salmon**. Compared to Japan itself, the salmon items and sales volume at Taiwan's conveyor belt sushi restaurants are usually far higher than other fish species[^5]. This phenomenon even triggered the "Salmon Chaos" in 2021, when Sushiro held a name-change promotion that attracted over 300 people across Taiwan to change their legal names to "Salmon" on their ID cards in order to enjoy free sushi[^5][^12].

Taiwanese people's preference for salmon can be attributed to the widespread perception of its "high cost-performance ratio and rich, fatty texture," as well as salmon being the most popular sashimi choice when Japanese cuisine was first promoted in Taiwan[^5]. Data shows that Taiwan's largest salmon importer, Sushi Express, imports approximately 2,500 metric tons of salmon annually, accounting for about 20% of Taiwan's total imports[^5][^13]. This love for salmon has prompted conveyor belt sushi brands to launch more diverse salmon products in the Taiwan market to meet consumer demand.

## Starbucks: The Localised Aesthetics of the Third Place

Starbucks' localisation strategy in Taiwan is reflected not only in its beverages but also extends deeply into its "third place" business philosophy. Starbucks Taiwan has created uniquely characteristic stores by renovating historic sites or integrating with local landscapes, such as the Bao'an Store located in the Ye Jintu Historic Residence in Dadaocheng, the Former Huwei Township Office Store in Huwei, Yunlin, and the Hualien Ideal Land Store, etc.[^14][^15]. These specialty stores not only serve coffee but also become vessels of local culture and history, reducing the distance between an international brand and the local environment, and enhancing consumers' spatial experience[^15].

In addition, Starbucks has actively launched festive gift boxes with Taiwan characteristics, such as Mid-Autumn Festival mooncakes and Dragon Boat Festival zongzi, as well as products featuring local ingredients, such as the local tea beverage series and taro Mont Blanc[^14]. The launch of these products aims to cater to Taiwan consumers' emphasis on festive customs and preference for local flavours, further deepening the brand's connection with Taiwan culture.

## Costco: The Perfect Combination of World's Top-Performing Store and Localized Service

The American warehouse chain Costco's success in Taiwan stands as a model of international brand localization. Costco stores in Taiwan, especially the Nantun store, once claimed the title of "world's most profitable," while the Neihu and Zhonghe stores have long ranked among the global top ten[^16][^17]. Costco's membership in Taiwan exceeds 4 million; based on the annual fee of NT$1,350, membership fee revenue alone reaches NT$5.4 billion, verifying its business model where 80% of profit comes from membership fees rather than product gross margin[^17].

Costco's localized services in Taiwan are also remarkable, such as having gas stations rarely seen globally, among which the Beitun store's fuel dispensing volume ranks first in Taiwan[^18]. In addition, although Costco mainly sells bulk products, Taiwan stores have added more prepared foods and snack options favored by Taiwanese people, targeting local family structures[^17]. This strategy precisely hit Taiwanese consumers' psychology of "pursuing high cost-performance ratio" and "family shopping," enabling it to achieve tremendous success in the Taiwan market.

## MOS Burger: The Oriental Fast Food Representative of Rice Culture

Originating from Japan, MOS Burger has successfully expanded its "Oriental flavors" and "tech-savvy service" characteristics in the Taiwan market. Its most representative product is the **rice burger**. Taiwan MOS Burger launched the "MOS Yakiniku Pearl Burger" in 1991, using local Taiwanese rice as the main ingredient, successfully integrating rice culture into fast food products, making it a brand signature item[^19][^20].

MOS Burger also actively promotes the use of local ingredients, for example becoming the first in Taiwan to obtain the "Taiwan Pork Certification Mark" in 2020, and emphasizing the use of Taiwan Rice Mark products[^19]. Compared to other American fast food brands, MOS Burger is closer to Taiwanese people's reliance on refined fast food and rice culture, successfully establishing a unique positioning in the market.

## IKEA: The Restaurant Overshadowed by Furniture and Creative Marketing

The Swedish furniture brand IKEA in Taiwan has deeply embedded its image as a "restaurant overshadowed by furniture." Taiwan IKEA's food and beverage revenue ratio reaches as high as 12%, nearly 3 times the global average (4–5%), ranking first worldwide[^21][^22]. Among these, the **soft serve** priced at just NT$10 sees annual sales exceeding one million units, with its sales volume even accounting for 30% of food revenue[^23]. This "loss leader strategy" attracts foot traffic through low-priced food, not only enhancing customers' shopping experience but also creating the psychological impression that "IKEA products are all very cheap"[^24].

IKEA's localized marketing in Taiwan is also full of creativity; for example, its **shark (BLÅHAJ)** plush toy sparked a craze in Taiwan, and it launched specialty foods combining Taiwanese festivals, such as wasabi soft serve, soy milk soy sauce paste soft serve, and other novelty flavors[^25]. These strategies successfully transformed "furniture shopping" into "family dining outings," satisfying Taiwanese people's high interest in dining.

## Carrefour: From International Brand to "Wanjiafu" — Deep Local Cultivation

The French hypermarket brand Carrefour's localization strategy in Taiwan has undergone a major transformation from aggressive mergers and acquisitions to brand transformation. In 2020, Carrefour acquired Wellcome and Jasons Market Place for NT$3.2 billion, a move aimed at strengthening its presence in the "community supermarket" segment to respond to fierce competition in Taiwan's retail market[^26][^27]. Through the acquisition, Carrefour was able to rapidly expand its store network, penetrate deeper into Taiwan's communities, and provide services more closely aligned with residents' needs.

Carrefour also actively promoted the "Impact Center" program, deeply collaborating with Taiwan's smallholder farmers to promote local ingredients and cage-free eggs, demonstrating its support for local agriculture[^28]. However, with Uni-President Group completing the transaction on June 30, 2023, officially acquiring 100% equity of Carrefour Taiwan, making it a purely Taiwan-capital company[^29][^30]. This equity change also led to a brand name transformation. Due to the expiration of the brand licensing agreement with French Carrefour, Carrefour Taiwan resolved to terminate the authorization on December 19, 2025, and officially rename starting July 1, 2026[^31][^32][^33].

The hypermarkets will be renamed "Wanjiafu" (Prosperity Plaza), while the supermarkets will be renamed "Lejiakang" (Uni-Prosperity)[^34][^35]. This renaming is not merely a signage change; it symbolizes Carrefour's formal integration into the Uni-President retail system, creating deeper synergies with brands like 7-ELEVEN and Cosmed, and represents a shift in brand image from "French brand" to "local brand," emphasizing the auspicious meanings of "Wanjiafu" and "Lejiakang," which better align with Taiwanese consumers' cultural preferences[^36][^37].

## Conclusion

The localization strategies of international brands in Taiwan represent a culinary revolution that fuses global vision with local insights. From McDonald's corn chowder and KFC's Portuguese egg tarts, to Pizza Hut's creative pizzas, 7-ELEVEN's braised delicacies and OPEN Xiaojiang, as well as the conveyor belt sushi salmon frenzy, these cases are not only commercial successes but also vivid portrayals of Taiwan's unique food culture and consumption habits. Furthermore, Starbucks' specialty stores, Costco's status as the world's top-performing store, Mos Burger's rice-based food culture, IKEA's dining miracle, and even Carrefour's local mergers and brand transformation, together depict how international brands on this land of Taiwan have created unique formulas for success through cultural integration and business innovation. Through continuous innovation and adjustment, these brands have proven that only by deeply understanding and embracing local culture can one stand out in a fiercely competitive market and build deep emotional connections with consumers.
