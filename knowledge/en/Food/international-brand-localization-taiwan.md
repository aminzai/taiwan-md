> **30-Second Overview:** As international brands enter the Taiwan market, they bring not only globalized products but also integrate Taiwan's unique food culture and consumption habits into their products and services through in-depth localization strategies. From McDonald's corn soup and KFC's Portuguese egg tarts to Pizza Hut's specialty pizzas that challenge taste buds, 7-ELEVEN's _lurou_ (braised snacks) and OPEN brand items, or even the preference for salmon at Sushiro, these cases demonstrate the brands' respect and flexibility toward the Taiwanese market. Furthermore, Starbucks' unique stores, Costco's status as a global store champion, Mos Burger's food culture, IKEA's dining miracles, and Carrefour's transformation after acquisitions and rebranding collectively paint a picture of how international brands create a unique success formula through cultural integration.

During the global expansion of international brands, they often face the challenge of balancing standardization with localization. In Taiwan, this challenge has given rise to many unique products and services, not only making these brands closer to the lives of Taiwanese consumers but also unexpectedly creating many "Taiwan-exclusive" experiences that surprise foreigners. These localization strategies are not just commercial successes; they also reflect Taiwan's rich and diverse food culture and consumption habits.

## McDonald's: The Charm of Warming Corn Soup and Local Sweet Potato Fries

The global fast-food leader, McDonald's, has demonstrated its localization flexibility in the Taiwanese market. Among its most recognizable offerings are **corn soup** and **golden sweet potato fries**. Corn soup is unique among global McDonald's; its origins can be traced back to a local fast-food restaurant in Taiwan named "McDonald's" (麥當樂), which offered "chicken corn soup" [^1][^6] before McDonald's officially entered Taiwan in 1984. After entering Taiwan, McDonald's specifically retained and optimized this product to cater to the Taiwanese dining habit of having a soup with a meal, making it a classic side dish at McDonald's in Taiwan [^6].

> 📝 **Curator's Note:** The localization of corn soup is not just a taste adjustment but a reflection of a deep understanding of Taiwanese eating habits. A bowl of hot soup carries the Taiwanese expectation and comfort associated with a proper meal.

Furthermore, McDonald's in Taiwan has also released **golden sweet potato fries** [^1], which were made using local Tainan 64 red sweet potatoes. This product not only satisfies Taiwanese consumers' fondness for sweet potatoes but also demonstrates the brand's connection to local agriculture through ingredient selection. These localized products have successfully transformed McDonald's from merely "American fast food" into a dining option with a more distinct "Taiwanese flavor."

## KFC: The Reign of Portuguese Egg Tarts and the Innovation of Paper-Wrapped Chicken

KFC's localization strategy in Taiwan is exemplified by the massive success of its **Portuguese egg tarts**. This dessert has even been jokingly referred to as a "tart shop delayed by fried chicken" [^7]. The introduction of the Portuguese egg tart originated from the craze for these pastries that swept through Taiwan in the 1990s. KFC introduced the recipe for Macalline egg tarts from Macau in 1998, and it quickly became a signature product [^7]. This strategy successfully expanded KFC's brand image in Taiwan from merely a fried chicken specialty store to a dining option that emphasizes "both desserts and main courses," with the egg tart becoming one of the main reasons many consumers visit KFC.

In addition to egg tarts, KFC in Taiwan also launched the **paper-wrapped chicken series**. This cooking method, which uses paper wrapping to lock in meat juices, is relatively rare among global KFCs but aligns with Taiwanese dietary habits that prefer tender and juicy textures [^2]. Furthermore, products such as **sweet potato QQ balls** and **sweet potato fries** also utilize local sweet potatoes from Taiwan and incorporate elements of Taiwanese night market snacks, further deepening KFC's connection to the local market in Taiwan [^2].

## Pizza Hut: Creative Pizzas Challenging Taste Buds

The localization strategy of Pizza Hut in Taiwan is arguably the most experimental and talked-about among international brands. These creative products, such as "Cilantro Century Egg Pig's Blood Cake Pizza," "Stinky Tofu Pizza," and "Bubble Tea Pizza," which challenge consumer taste buds to their limits, have repeatedly sparked heated discussions on social media[^3][^8]. For example, the Cilantro Century Egg Pig's Blood Cake Pizza launched in 2021 recorded over 100,000 daily searches on Google on its launch day[^3].

Behind these seemingly "unusual" flavors is the "e-commerce mindset" and "WOW Pizza strategy" driven by CEO Leung Ka-chun (from Hong Kong) at Pizza Hut[^3]. Leung Ka-chun believes that in a market like Taiwan, where food delivery platforms are prevalent, Pizza Hut essentially functions as e-commerce. Therefore, "Talkability," "Traffic," and "Transaction" have become the key 3T strategy[^3]. By continuously launching highly topical localized products, even if consumers do not necessarily purchase these special flavors, it effectively increases brand visibility and attention online, thereby boosting overall sales[^3]. This strategy has not only broken the preconceived notion among Taiwanese people that pizza is only eaten during holidays but has also successfully positioned Pizza Hut as an innovative and vibrant brand[^3].

## 7-ELEVEN: The Practitioner of Lifestyle Proposals and the Charm of OPEN Chang

As a representative of Taiwan's convenience store culture, 7-ELEVEN's localization has long surpassed mere product offerings; it has deeply integrated into the daily lives of Taiwanese people. In addition to its well-known **tea eggs** (with annual sales exceeding 100 million), in recent years, 7-ELEVEN has introduced "Star Banquet Braised Food" (_xīngjí xiǎngyàn lǔwèi_), standardizing the traditional night market braised food culture of Taiwan and providing consumers with convenient options available 24 hours a day[^4][^9].

> 📝 **Curator's Note:** The success of 7-ELEVEN lies not only in its product diversity but also in how it has transformed the convenience store into a platform that offers "lifestyle proposals," meeting Taiwanese people's dual needs for convenience and local cuisine.

Another successful case of localization is its dedicated mascot, **OPEN Chang**. Developed by Uni-President Convenience Store and Dentsu Japan in 2005, OPEN Chang not only possesses a complete character setting and backstory but has also successfully integrated into Taiwan's popular culture through merchandise and themed events, even generating an annual commercial value of NT$1 billion for 7-ELEVEN[^4][^10][^11]. The success of OPEN Chang proves the immense potential of brand mascots in the Taiwanese market and their importance in building emotional connections with consumers.

## Conveyor Belt Sushi: Taiwanese Obsession with Salmon

Conveyor belt sushi brands that have rapidly risen in the Taiwan market, such as Sushiro and Kura Sushi, also demonstrate an interesting localization phenomenon: the abnormal fervor of Taiwanese consumers for **salmon**. Compared to Japan, salmon items and sales at conveyor belt sushi restaurants in Taiwan are usually far higher than other fish species[^5]. This phenomenon even sparked the "Salmon War" in 2021, when Sushiro held a renaming promotion that attracted over 300 people across Taiwan who changed their ID names to "salmon" to enjoy free sushi[^5][^12].

The preference of Taiwanese for salmon can be attributed to the common perception that it offers high "value for money" and has a rich texture, as well as because salmon was one of the most widely available raw fish options when Japanese cuisine was first promoted in Taiwan[^5]. Data shows that Changshun, Taiwan's largest importer of salmon, imports about 2,500 tons of salmon annually, accounting for about 20% of the total volume imported to Taiwan[^5][^13]. This passion for salmon has prompted conveyor belt sushi brands to introduce more diverse salmon products in the Taiwanese market to meet consumer demand.

## Starbucks: Local Aesthetics of the Third Place

Starbucks' localization strategy in Taiwan is reflected not only in its beverages but also deeply within its concept of the "third place." Taiwanese Starbucks has created unique stores by renovating historical sites or integrating local landscapes, such as the store located in the Ye Jin Tu Ancient House in Taodai, the former Contract Hall store in Hubei, Yunlin, and the Ideal Land store in Hualien[^14][^15]. These distinctive stores offer more than just coffee; they serve as vessels for local culture and history, reducing the gap between international brands and the local environment while enhancing the consumer's spatial experience[^15].

Furthermore, Starbucks actively launches festive gift boxes with Taiwanese characteristics, such as Mid-Autumn Festival mooncakes and Dragon Boat Festival zongzi, as well as products made from local ingredients, like local tea drink series and taro Mont Blanc[^14]. The introduction of these products aims to cater to Taiwanese consumers' emphasis on holiday customs and their preference for local flavors, further deepening the connection between the brand and Taiwanese culture.

## Costco: A Perfect Blend of Global Store Excellence and Localized Service

The success of the American hypermarket Costco in Taiwan is a prime example of international brand localization. Costco stores in Taiwan, particularly the Nantou branch, have held the title of "Global Profit Leader," while the Neihu and Zhonghe branches have consistently ranked among the top ten globally [^16][^17]. With over 4 million members in Taiwan, the membership fee alone accounts for NT$5.4 billion based on an annual fee of NT$1,350, which validates its business model where 80% of profits come from membership fees rather than product gross margin [^17].

Costco's localized services in Taiwan are also significant; for example, it features a gas station, which is rare globally, with the Nantou branch having the highest fuel sales volume in Taiwan [^18]. Furthermore, although Costco primarily offers large-sized products, Taiwanese stores have added more ready-made meals and snacks favored by local families to cater to the local household structure [^17]. This strategy accurately targets the consumer psychology of Taiwanese people who "seek high cost-performance value" and engage in "family grocery shopping," leading to great success in the Taiwanese market.

## MOS Burger: A Representative of Eastern Fast Food Culture

MOS Burger, originating from Japan, has successfully promoted its characteristics of "Eastern flavors" and "high-tech service" in the Taiwanese market. The most representative product is the **rice burger**. In 1991, MOS Burger launched the "MOS Grilled Meat Pearl Burger," using local Taiwanese rice as a main ingredient to successfully integrate rice culture into fast food products, making it a signature item for the brand [^19][^20].

MOS Burger also actively promotes the use of local ingredients. For instance, in 2020, it received the title of the first "Taiwan Pork Certification Mark" in Taiwan and emphasizes using Taiwanese rice-certified products [^19]. Compared to other American fast-food brands, MOS Burger is closer to the reliance of Taiwanese people on refined fast food and rice culture, successfully establishing a unique position in the market.

## IKEA: The Restaurant "Delayed by Furniture" and Creative Marketing

The Swedish furniture brand IKEA has established a strong image in Taiwan as the "restaurant delayed by furniture." Food service revenue at Taiwanese IKEA accounts for 12% of total sales, nearly three times the global average (4-5%), placing it first globally[^21][^22]. The annual sales of just NT$10 ice cream have surpassed one million units, accounting for 30% of food revenue[^23]. This "guide chicken strategy" attracts foot traffic through low-priced food, not only enhancing the customer's shopping experience but also creating a consumer perception that "all IKEA products are cheap"[^24].

IKEA's localization marketing in Taiwan is also full of creativity. For example, its **Shark (BLÅHAJ)** plush toy created a craze in Taiwan, and it has combined with Taiwanese festivals to launch unique flavors such as wasabi ice cream and soy sauce soy milk ice cream—exotic tastes[^25]. These strategies successfully transformed "furniture shopping" into a "family dining itinerary," meeting the high interest of Taiwanese people in food and beverage.

## Carrefour: Local Deepening from an International Brand to "Wan Jia Fu"

The localization strategy of the French retailer Carrefour in Taiwan has undergone significant changes, moving from aggressive acquisitions to brand transformation. In 2020, Carrefour acquired Wellcome and Jasons supermarkets for NT$3.2 billion, aiming to strengthen its presence in the "community supermarket" sector to cope with intense competition in the Taiwanese retail market[^26][^27]. Through these acquisitions, Carrefour was able to rapidly expand its store network and penetrate deeper into local communities, providing services that were closer to residents' needs.

Carrefour also actively promoted the "Influence Center" project, cooperating closely with small farmers in Taiwan to promote local ingredients and friendly eggs, demonstrating its support for local agriculture[^28]. However, after Uni-President completed the transaction on June 30, 2023, officially acquiring 100% of Taiwanese Carrefour shares, making it a purely domestic company[^29][^30]. This change in ownership also led to a change in brand names. Because the brand licensing agreement with the French Carrefour expired, Taiwanese Carrefour resolved to terminate the license on December 19, 2025, and officially rebrand starting **July 1, 2026**[^31][^32][^33].

The hypermarkets will be renamed **"Wan Jia Fu" (Prosperity Plaza)**, while the supermarkets will be renamed **"Le Jia Kang" (Uni-Prosperity)**[^34][^35]. This renaming is not just a change of signage; it symbolizes Carrefour's formal integration into the Uni retail system, creating deeper synergies with brands like 7-ELEVEN and Kangsimei. It also represents the brand image shifting from a "French brand" to a "local brand," emphasizing the auspicious meanings of "Wan Jia Fu" and "Le Jia Kang," which better aligns with the cultural preferences of Taiwanese consumers[^36][^37].

## Conclusion

The localization strategies of international brands in Taiwan represent a transformation of taste that merges global vision with local insight. From McDonald's corn soup and KFC Portuguese egg tarts to Pizza Hut's creative pizzas, 7-ELEVEN's _lurou_ (braised items), OPEN Gang's snacks, and the salmon craze at conveyor belt sushi, these cases are not merely commercial successes but vivid reflections of Taiwan's unique food culture and consumption habits. Furthermore, Starbucks' specialty stores, Costco's global king status, Mos Burger's rice-based culture, IKEA's dining miracles, and Carrefour's local acquisitions and brand transformation all collectively paint a picture of how international brands create a unique success formula on Taiwanese soil through cultural integration and business innovation. These brands prove, through continuous innovation and adjustment, that only by deeply understanding and embracing local culture can they stand out in a highly competitive market and establish a deep emotional connection with consumers.
