> **30-Second Overview:** As international brands enter the Taiwan market, they bring not only globalized goods but also integrate Taiwan's unique food culture and consumption habits into their products and services through in-depth localization strategies. From McDonald's corn soup and KFC Portuguese egg tarts to Pizza Hut's signature pizzas that challenge taste buds, 7-ELEVEN's _lurou fan_ (braised snacks) and OPEN rice cakes, and even the preference for salmon at Sushiro, these cases demonstrate the brands' respect and flexibility toward the Taiwanese market. Furthermore, Starbucks' specialty stores, Costco's status as a global store leader, Mos Burger's food culture, IKEA's dining miracles, and Carrefour's transformation after acquisitions and renaming collectively paint a picture of how international brands create unique success formulas through cultural integration.

As international brands expand globally, they often face the challenge of balancing standardization with localization. In Taiwan, this challenge has given rise to many unique products and services that not only bring these brands closer to the lives of Taiwanese consumers but have also unexpectedly created many "Taiwan-exclusive" experiences that surprise foreigners. These localization strategies are not just commercial successes; they also reflect Taiwan's rich and diverse food culture and consumption habits.

## McDonald's: The Charm of Warming Corn Soup and Local Sweet Potato Fries

The global fast-food leader, McDonald's, has demonstrated its localization flexibility in the Taiwan market. Among its most well-known offerings are **corn soup** and **golden sweet potato fries**. While corn soup is unique among global McDonald's, its origins can be traced back to a local fast-food establishment in Taiwan named "McDonald's" (麥當樂) that offered "chicken corn soup" [^1][^6] before McDonald's officially entered Taiwan in 1984. After McDonald's arrived in Taiwan, it specifically retained and optimized this product to cater to the Taiwanese dining habit of "eating with soup," making it a classic side dish at McDonald's in Taiwan [^6].

> 📝 **Curator's Note:** The localization of corn soup is not just an adjustment of flavor, but a reflection of a deep understanding of Taiwanese eating habits. A bowl of hot soup carries the Taiwanese expectation and comfort associated with a proper meal.

Furthermore, McDonald's in Taiwan has also launched **golden sweet potato fries** [^1], which were made using local Taiwanese sweet potatoes from variety No. 64 (Hóngxīn Dìguā). This product not only satisfied Taiwanese consumers' fondness for sweet potatoes but also demonstrated the brand's connection to local agriculture through ingredient selection. These localized products successfully transformed McDonald's from merely "American fast food" into a dining choice with a distinct "Taiwanese flavor."

## KFC: The Reign of Egg Tarts and the Innovation of Paper-Wrapped Chicken

KFC's localization strategy in Taiwan is exemplified by the massive success of its **Portuguese egg tarts**. This dessert has even been jokingly dubbed "the tart shop overshadowed by fried chicken" [^7]. The introduction of Portuguese egg tarts originated from the craze for them that swept through Taiwan in the 1990s. KFC introduced the recipe from Macalline Egg Tarts in Macau in 1998, and it quickly became a signature item [^7]. This strategy successfully expanded KFC's brand image in Taiwan from merely a fried chicken specialty store to a dining option that emphasizes "both desserts and main courses," with egg tarts becoming one of the main reasons many consumers visit KFC.

In addition to egg tarts, KFC in Taiwan also launched the **paper-wrapped chicken series**. This cooking method, which uses paper wrapping to lock in juices during baking, is relatively rare among global KFCs but aligns with Taiwanese dietary habits that prefer a tender and juicy texture [^2]. Furthermore, products such as **sweet potato QQ balls** and **sweet potato fries** also utilize local sweet potatoes from Taiwan and incorporate elements of Taiwanese night market snacks, further deepening KFC's connection to the local market in Taiwan [^2].

# Pizza Hut: Creative Pizzas Challenging Taste Buds

The localization strategy of Pizza Hut in Taiwan is arguably the most experimental and talked-about among international brands. These creative products, which challenge consumer taste buds—such as "Cilantro Century Egg Pig's Blood Cake Pizza," "Stinky Tofu Pizza," and "Bubble Tea Pizza"—have repeatedly sparked heated discussions on social media[^3][^8]. For example, the Cilantro Century Egg Pig's Blood Cake Pizza, launched in 2021, set a record by exceeding 100,000 daily searches on Google on its launch day[^3].

Behind these seemingly "exotic" flavors is the "e-commerce mindset" and "WOW Pizza strategy" promoted by CEO Leung Ka-chun (from Hong Kong) at Pizza Hut[^3]. Leung Ka-chun believes that in a market like Taiwan, where food delivery platforms are prevalent, Pizza Hut essentially functions as e-commerce. Therefore, "Talkability," "Traffic," and "Transaction" have become the key 3T strategy[^3]. By continuously launching highly talked-about localized products, even if consumers do not necessarily purchase these special flavors, it effectively increases brand visibility and attention online, which in turn drives overall sales[^3]. This strategy has not only broken the preconceived notion among Taiwanese people that "pizza is only eaten during holidays" but has also successfully positioned Pizza Hut as an innovative and vibrant brand[^3].

## 7-ELEVEN: The Practitioner of Lifestyle Propositions and the Charm of OPEN Jiang

As a representative of Taiwan's convenience store culture, 7-ELEVEN's localization has long surpassed mere product offerings; it has deeply penetrated the daily lives of Taiwanese people. In addition to its well-known **tea eggs** (selling over 100 million annually), in recent years, 7-ELEVEN has introduced "Star Feast Braised Snacks" (_xīngjí xiǎngyàn lǔwèi_), standardizing the traditional night market braised snack culture of Taiwan and providing consumers with convenient options available 24 hours a day[^4][^9].

> 📝 **Curator's Note:** The success of 7-ELEVEN is not only due to its product diversity but also how it has established itself as a platform that offers "lifestyle propositions," meeting the Taiwanese demand for both convenience and local cuisine.

Another successful case of localization is its dedicated mascot, **OPEN Jiang**. Developed in 2005 by Uni-President Convenience Store and Dentsu (Japan), OPEN Jiang not only possesses a complete character setting and backstory but has also successfully integrated into Taiwan's popular culture through merchandise and themed events, even generating an annual commercial value of NT$1 billion for 7-ELEVEN[^4][^10][^11]. The success of OPEN Jiang demonstrates the immense potential of brand mascots in the Taiwanese market and their importance in building emotional connections with consumers.

## Conveyor Belt Sushi: Taiwanese Obsession with Salmon

Conveyor belt sushi brands that have rapidly grown in the Taiwan market, such as Sushiro and Chang Xian, also demonstrate an interesting localization phenomenon: the abnormal enthusiasm of Taiwanese consumers for **salmon**. Compared to Japan, salmon items and sales at conveyor belt sushi restaurants in Taiwan are usually much higher than other fish species[^5]. This phenomenon even sparked the "Salmon War" in 2021, when Sushiro held a renaming promotion that attracted over 300 people across Taiwan who changed their ID names to "salmon" to enjoy free sushi[^5][^12].

The Taiwanese preference for salmon can be attributed to the common perception of its "high cost-performance ratio and rich texture," as well as the fact that, when Japanese cuisine was first promoted in Taiwan, salmon was one of the most widely available raw fish options[^5]. Data shows that Chang Xian, a major salmon importer in Taiwan, imports about 2,500 tons of salmon annually, accounting for about 20% of the total imports in Taiwan[^5][^13]. This love for salmon has prompted conveyor belt sushi brands to launch more diverse salmon products in the Taiwanese market to meet consumer demand.

## Starbucks: Local Aesthetics of the Third Place

Starbucks' localization strategy in Taiwan is not only reflected in its beverages but also deeply embedded in its concept of the "third place." Taiwanese Starbucks has created uniquely characteristic stores by renovating historical sites or integrating local landscapes, such as the store located in the Ye Jin Tu residence in Daodai, the former Contract Hall in Hubei, Yunlin, and the Ideal Land store in Hualien[^14][^15]. These distinctive stores not only serve coffee but also act as vessels for local culture and history, reducing the gap between international brands and the local environment while enhancing the consumer's spatial experience[^15].

Furthermore, Starbucks actively launches festive gift sets with Taiwanese characteristics, such as Mid-Autumn Festival mooncakes and Dragon Boat Festival zongzi, as well as products using local ingredients, like local tea series and taro Mont Blanc[^14]. The introduction of these products aims to cater to Taiwanese consumers' emphasis on holiday customs and their preference for local flavors, further deepening the connection between the brand and Taiwanese culture.

## Costco: A Perfect Combination of Global Store Excellence and Localized Service

The success of the American hypermarket Costco in Taiwan is a prime example of international brand localization. Costco stores in Taiwan, particularly the Nantian branch, have held the title of "Global Profit Leader," while the Neihu and Zhonghe branches have consistently ranked among the top ten globally [^16][^17]. With over 4 million members in Taiwan, the membership fee revenue alone amounts to NT$5.4 billion based on an annual fee of NT$1,350, which validates their business model where 80% of profits come from membership fees rather than product gross margin [^17].

Costco's localized services in Taiwan are also quite significant. For example, they have gasoline stations, a feature rarely seen globally, with the Nantian branch having the highest fuel sales volume in Taiwan [^18]. Furthermore, although Costco primarily offers large-sized items, Taiwanese stores have added more ready-to-eat meals and snacks favored by local families to cater to the local family structure [^17]. This strategy precisely targets the Taiwanese consumer psychology of "seeking high cost-performance" and "family grocery shopping," leading to great success in the Taiwanese market.

## MOS Burger: An Eastern Fast Food Representative of Rice Culture

MOS Burger, originating from Japan, has successfully promoted its characteristics of "Eastern flavors" and "tech-savvy service" in the Taiwanese market. The most representative product is the **rice burger**. In 1991, Taiwan's MOS Burger launched the "MOS Grilled Meat Pearl Burger," using local Taiwanese rice as a main ingredient to successfully integrate rice culture into fast food products, making it a signature item of the brand [^19][^20].

MOS Burger has also actively promoted the use of local ingredients. For instance, in 2020, it received the first "Taiwan Pork Certification Mark" in Taiwan and emphasized using Taiwanese-certified rice products [^19]. Compared to other American fast-food brands, MOS Burger is closer to the reliance of Taiwanese people on refined fast food and rice culture, successfully establishing a unique position in the market.

## IKEA: The Restaurant Delayed by Furniture and Creative Marketing

The Swedish furniture brand IKEA has established a strong image in Taiwan as the "restaurant delayed by furniture." Food and beverage revenue at IKEA in Taiwan accounts for up to 12%, nearly three times the global average (4-5%), placing it number one globally[^21][^22]. The annual sales of just 10-dollar **ice cream** have exceeded one million units, with its sales even accounting for 30% of food revenue[^23]. This "lead chicken strategy" attracts foot traffic through low-priced food, not only enhancing the customer's shopping experience but also creating a psychological impression among consumers that "IKEA products are all cheap"[^24].

IKEA's localized marketing in Taiwan is also full of creativity. For example, its **Shark (BLÅHAJ)** dolls became a sensation in Taiwan, and it launched unique flavors combined with Taiwanese festivals, such as wasabi ice cream and soy sauce soybean milk ice cream—exotic tastes[^25]. These strategies successfully transformed "shopping for furniture" into a "family dining itinerary," meeting the high interest of Taiwanese people in food and beverage.

## Carrefour: Local Deep Cultivation from an International Brand to "Wan Jia Fu"

The localization strategy of the French retailer Carrefour in Taiwan has undergone a major transformation, moving from aggressive acquisitions to brand transition. In 2020, Carrefour acquired Wellcome and Jasons supermarkets for NT$3.2 billion, aiming to strengthen its presence in the "community supermarket" sector to cope with intense competition in the Taiwanese retail market[^26][^27]. Through these acquisitions, Carrefour was able to rapidly expand its store network and delve deeper into local communities, providing services that are closer to residents' needs.

Carrefour also actively promoted the "Influence Center" project, collaborating closely with small farmers in Taiwan to promote local ingredients and friendly eggs, demonstrating its support for local agriculture[^28]. However, after Uni-President completed the transaction on June 30, 2023, officially acquiring 100% of Taiwan Carrefour's shares, making it a purely Taiwanese-owned company[^29][^30]. This change in ownership also led to a change in brand name. Due to the expiration of the brand licensing agreement with the French Carrefour, Taiwan Carrefour resolved to terminate the license on December 19, 2025, and officially rebranded starting **July 1, 2026**[^31][^32][^33].

The hypermarkets will be renamed **"Wan Jia Fu" (Prosperity Plaza)**, while the supermarkets will be renamed **"Uni-Prosperity"**[^34][^35]. This renaming is not just a change of signage; it symbolizes Carrefour's formal integration into the Uni retail system, creating deeper synergies with brands like 7-ELEVEN and Kangsimei. It also represents the brand image shifting from a "French brand" to a "local brand," emphasizing the auspicious connotations of "Wan Jia Fu" and "Uni-Prosperity," which better aligns with the cultural preferences of Taiwanese consumers[^36][^37].

## Conclusion

The localization strategies of international brands in Taiwan represent a transformation of taste that merges global vision with local insight. From McDonald's corn soup and KFC Portuguese egg tarts to Pizza Hut's creative pizzas, 7-ELEVEN's _lurou_ (braised food), OPEN Gang's offerings, and the salmon craze at Sushiro, these cases are not merely commercial successes but vivid reflections of Taiwan's unique culinary culture and consumption habits. Furthermore, Starbucks' specialty stores, Costco's global king status, Mos Burger's rice-based culture, IKEA's dining miracles, and Carrefour's local acquisitions and brand transformation all collectively paint a picture of how international brands create a unique success equation on Taiwanese soil through cultural integration and business innovation. Through continuous innovation and adjustment, these brands prove that only by deeply understanding and embracing local culture can they stand out in a highly competitive market and build deep emotional connections with consumers.
