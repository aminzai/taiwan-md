> **30-second overview:** International brands entering the Taiwan market not only bring globalized products but also integrate Taiwan's unique food culture and consumption habits into their offerings through deep localization strategies. From McDonald's corn soup and KFC's Portuguese egg tarts to Pizza Hut's adventurous pizzas, 7-Eleven's braised snacks and OPEN Junior's bento boxes, even Kura Sushi's salmon craze, these cases demonstrate the brands' respect and flexibility for the Taiwan market. Furthermore, Starbucks' specialty stores, Costco's global top store status, MOS Burger's rice culture, IKEA's food miracle, and Carrefour's acquisition and rebranding all together depict how international brands create unique success formulas through cultural integration.

International brands expanding globally often face the challenge of balancing standardization and localization. In Taiwan, this challenge has given rise to many unique products and services, not only bringing these brands closer to Taiwanese consumers' lives but also unexpectedly creating many surprising "Taiwan-exclusive" experiences. These localization strategies are not just business successes but also reflect Taiwan's rich and diverse food culture and consumption habits.

## McDonald's: The Charm of Warm Corn Soup and Local Sweet Potatoes

The global fast-food leader McDonald's has demonstrated its localization flexibility in the Taiwan market. Among its most well-known offerings are its **corn soup** and **golden sweet potato fries**. The corn soup is unique among McDonald's worldwide, tracing its origins back to 1978, before McDonald's officially entered Taiwan in 1984. A local Taiwanese fast-food shop named 「麥當樂」 already offered "chicken noodle corn soup"[^1][^6]. After McDonald's entered Taiwan, in order to cater to the Taiwanese habit of eating rice with soup, they specifically retained and optimized this product, making it a classic meal option for McDonald's Taiwan[^6].

> 📝 **Curator's Note:** The localization of corn soup is not only a taste adjustment but also a deep understanding of the Taiwanese dining habits. A bowl of hot soup carries the expectations and comfort of the Taiwanese people for their meals.

In addition, McDonald's Taiwan also launched **golden sweet potato fries** made from Taiwan's local Tainung 64 sweet potatoes[^1]. This product not only satisfied Taiwanese consumers' love for sweet potatoes but also showcased the brand's connection with local agriculture in ingredient selection. These localized products have successfully transformed McDonald's from a simple "American fast food" into a more "Taiwanese-flavored" dining option.

## KFC: Egg Tarts Dominance and Innovation in Paper-Wrapped Chicken

KFC's localization strategy in Taiwan is exemplified by the tremendous success of its **Portuguese egg tarts**. This dessert was even nicknamed "a custard shop accidentally selling fried chicken"[^7]. The introduction of Portuguese egg tarts stemmed from the egg tart craze that swept Taiwan in the 1990s. KFC introduced the recipe from Macau's Margaret's in 1998 and quickly made it a signature item[^7]. This strategy successfully expanded KFC's brand image in Taiwan from a simple fried chicken shop to a dining choice where "desserts and main dishes coexist," with egg tarts even becoming one of the primary reasons many consumers visit KFC.

In addition to egg tarts, KFC in Taiwan has also launched its **paper-wrapped chicken series**. This cooking method, which uses paper packaging to lock in meat juices, is relatively rare across KFC globally but aligns with Taiwanese preferences for tender, juicy textures[^2]. Furthermore, products like **sweet potato _QQ_ balls** and **sweet potato fries** similarly utilize locally sourced sweet potatoes and incorporate elements of Taiwan's night market snacks, further deepening KFC's localized connection in the Taiwanese market[^2].

## KFC: Creative Pizzas That Challenge the Limits of Taste

KFC Taiwan's localization strategy is among the most experimental and discussion-worthy in international brands. From _Cilantro Egg and Blood Sausage Pizza_ to _Stinky Tofu Pizza_ and _Bubble Milk Tea Pizza_, these creative products that challenge the limits of taste have repeatedly sparked heated discussions on social media [^3][^8]. For example, the _Cilantro Egg and Blood Sausage Pizza_ launched in 2021 set a record of over 100,000 Google keyword searches in a single day on its launch day [^3].

Behind these seemingly "unique" flavors is the "e-commerce mindset" and "WOW Pizza strategy" championed by KFC's general manager Liang Chia-chun (from Hong Kong) [^3]. Liang believes that in Taiwan, where delivery platforms are prevalent, KFC is effectively an e-commerce business, making "Talkability", "Traffic", and "Transaction" the key 3T strategies [^3]. By continuously launching highly discussion-worthy localized products, even if consumers don't necessarily purchase these special flavors, it effectively boosts the brand's online presence and attention, thereby driving overall sales [^3]. This strategy not only breaks the preconceived notion that Taiwanese people only eat pizza during festivals but also successfully positions KFC as a brand full of innovation and vitality [^3].

## 7-ELEVEN: Practitioner of Lifestyle Proposals and the Charm of OPEN General

As a representative of Taiwan's convenience store culture, 7-ELEVEN's localization has long transcended the realm of mere products, deeply embedding itself into the daily lives of Taiwanese people. Beyond the well-known **tea-leaf eggs** (with annual sales exceeding 100 million pieces), in recent years 7-ELEVEN has launched "Star Buffet Snacks," standardizing the culture of traditional night-market snacks and offering consumers 24-hour access to convenient delicacies[^4][^9].

> 📝 **Curator's Note:** 7-ELEVEN's success lies not only in the diversity of its products, but in how it transforms the convenience store into a platform that provides "lifestyle proposals," satisfying the dual needs of convenience and local cuisine among Taiwanese consumers.

Another successful localization case is its exclusive mascot, **OPEN General**. OPEN General was jointly developed by Uni-President and Dentsu of Japan in 2005. Not only does it have complete character settings and background stories, but it has also successfully integrated into Taiwan's pop culture through peripheral products and themed activities, even creating a commercial value of NT$1 billion in annual revenue for 7-ELEVEN[^4][^10][^11]. The success of OPEN General proves the tremendous potential of brand mascots in the Taiwan market, as well as their importance in building brand emotional connections.

## Conveyor Belt Sushi: Taiwan's Salmon Obsession

In recent years, conveyor belt sushi brands such as Sushiro and Kura Sushi have rapidly risen in the Taiwan market, showcasing intriguing localization phenomena—namely, the Taiwanese consumer's intense obsession with **salmon**. Compared to Japan, Taiwan's conveyor belt sushi restaurants typically offer far more salmon items and achieve significantly higher sales volumes for salmon than other fish varieties[^5]. This trend even sparked the "salmon chaos" in 2021, when Sushiro ran a renaming promotion that drew over 300 people across Taiwan to legally change their names to "salmon" for free sushi[^5][^12].

Taiwanese people's fondness for salmon can be attributed to its widespread perception as offering high cost-performance value and a rich, buttery flavor, as well as salmon being the most commonly available sashimi option during the early promotion of Japanese cuisine in Taiwan[^5]. Data shows that Chia Hsin, Taiwan's largest salmon importer, annually imports approximately 2,500 metric tons of salmon, accounting for about 20% of the total imported volume in Taiwan[^5][^13]. This passion for salmon has driven conveyor belt sushi brands to introduce an array of salmon-based products in the Taiwan market to meet consumer demand.

## Starbucks: The Aesthetics of Localization in the Third Space

Starbucks' localization strategy in Taiwan is not only reflected in its beverages but also deeply embedded in its "third space" business philosophy. Taiwan Starbucks has transformed historic sites or integrated local landscapes to create distinctive stores, such as the Bo'an Store located in the historic Yejin Tu residence in Dadaocheng, the former joint office building store in Huwei, Yunlin, and the Ideal Land Store in Hualien, among others[^14][^15]. These themed stores not only serve coffee but also act as carriers of local culture and history, reducing the gap between international brands and the local environment, and enhancing consumers' spatial experiences[^15].

In addition, Starbucks has actively launched festive gift boxes with Taiwanese characteristics, such as mooncakes for the Mid-Autumn Festival, zongzi for the Dragon Boat Festival, as well as local ingredient products, including a series of local tea drinks and taro Mont Blanc, among others[^14]. The launch of these products aims to cater to Taiwanese consumers' emphasis on festive customs and their preference for local flavors, further deepening the connection between the brand and Taiwanese culture.

## Costco: A Perfect Blend of Global Store King and Localized Services

The American warehouse club Costco has achieved remarkable success in Taiwan, serving as a model for international brand localization. Costco's Taiwan stores, particularly the South Taichung location, have held the title of "world's most profitable store," while the Neihu and Zhonghe branches have consistently ranked among the global top ten[^16][^17]. With over 4 million members in Taiwan, Costco's annual membership fee of NT$1,350 generates approximately NT$5.4 billion in membership revenue alone, underscoring its business model where 80% of profits come from membership fees rather than product margins[^17].

Costco's localized services in Taiwan are also highly prominent, including the rare installation of gas stations, with the North Taichung branch leading the island in fuel volume[^18]. Additionally, while Costco is known for its bulk-sized products, Taiwan stores have expanded their offerings of ready-to-eat meals and snacks tailored to local family structures[^17]. This strategy precisely targets Taiwanese consumers' pursuit of high cost-performance value and family shopping habits, leading to significant market success.

## MOS Burger: The Eastern Fast Food Representative of Rice Culture

Originating from Japan, MOS Burger has successfully promoted its "oriental flavor" and "high-tech service" features in the Taiwan market. Its most representative product is the **rice burger**. In 1991, MOS Burger Taiwan launched the "MOS Barbecue Pearl Burger," using locally sourced rice as the main ingredient, successfully integrating rice culture into fast food products and establishing it as a signature item of the brand[^19][^20].

MOS Burger has also actively promoted the use of local ingredients, such as being the first store in Taiwan to receive the "Taiwan Pig Certification Mark" in 2020, and emphasizing the use of products with the Taiwan Rice Certification Mark[^19]. Compared to other Western fast food brands, MOS Burger is closer to Taiwanese people's reliance on refined fast food and rice culture, successfully carving out a unique market position.

## IKEA: The Restaurant Delayed by Furniture and Creative Marketing

The Swedish furniture brand IKEA in Taiwan has become deeply associated with its image as a "restaurant delayed by furniture." IKEA Taiwan's food revenue accounts for as high as 12%, nearly three times the global average (4-5%) and ranks first worldwide[^21][^22]. Among these, the mere NT$10 **soft-serve ice cream** achieves annual sales of over a million units, with its sales volume even accounting for 30% of food revenue[^23]. This "leading chicken strategy" uses affordable food to attract foot traffic, not only enhancing customers' shopping experience but also creating the psychological impression that "IKEA products are all very cheap"[^24].

IKEA's localized marketing in Taiwan is also full of creativity, such as its **BLÅHAJ** shark plush toy sparking a craze in Taiwan, and combining Taiwanese festivals to launch themed foods, such as wasabi soft-serve ice cream and soy milk soy sauce-flavored soft-serve ice cream[^25]. These strategies have successfully transformed "furniture shopping" into a "family dining itinerary," satisfying the Taiwanese people's high interest in dining.

## Carrefour: From International Brand to Local "Prosperity Plaza" Deep Localization

The French hypermarket brand Carrefour's localization strategy in Taiwan has undergone significant transformations, from aggressive acquisitions to brand repositioning. In 2020, Carrefour acquired Wellcome and Jasons supermarkets for NT$3.2 billion, aiming to strengthen its presence in the "community supermarket" market to respond to intense competition in Taiwan's retail sector[^26][^27]. Through these acquisitions, Carrefour rapidly expanded its store network, penetrating deeper into Taiwan's communities to provide services more aligned with residents' needs.

Carrefour also actively promoted its "Influence Center" initiative, partnering closely with Taiwanese small-scale farmers to promote local ingredients and friendly eggs, demonstrating its support for local agriculture[^28]. However, with Uni-President completing the acquisition of 100% of Taiwan Carrefour's shares on June 30, 2023, making it a purely Taiwan-funded company[^29][^30]. This change in ownership also led to a brand name transition. Due to the expiration of the brand licensing agreement with the French Carrefour, Taiwan Carrefour decided to terminate the authorization on December 19, 2025, and will officially rebrand starting **July 1, 2026**[^31][^32][^33].

The hypermarkets will be renamed **"Prosperity Plaza" (Prosperity Plaza)**, while the supermarkets will be renamed **"Uni-Prosperity" (Uni-Prosperity)**[^34][^35]. This rebranding is not merely a change of signage but symbolizes Carrefour's formal integration into the Uni-President retail system, creating deeper synergies with brands like 7-ELEVEN and RT-Mart, and representing a shift in brand image from "French brand" to "local brand," emphasizing the auspicious meanings of "Prosperity Plaza" and "Uni-Prosperity," which better align with Taiwanese consumers' cultural preferences[^36][^37].

## Conclusion

The localization strategies of international brands in Taiwan represent a gustatory evolution that fuses global vision with local insight. From McDonald's corn soup and KFC's Portuguese egg tarts, to Pizza Hut's creative pizzas, 7-Eleven's braised snacks and OPEN's bento offerings, and the salmon craze at rotating sushi chains, these cases are not merely commercial successes but vivid portrayals of Taiwan's distinctive food culture and consumer habits. Going further, Starbucks' specialty stores, Costco's worldwide top-performing store, MOS Burger's rice-culture adaptation, IKEA's dining miracle, and even Carrefour's local acquisitions and brand transformation all collectively illustrate how international brands on this land of Taiwan achieve unique success formulas through cultural integration and business innovation. These brands, through constant innovation and adjustment, prove that only by deeply understanding and embracing local culture can one stand out in a fiercely competitive market and forge deep emotional connections with consumers.
