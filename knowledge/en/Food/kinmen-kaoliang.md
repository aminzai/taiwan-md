> **30-second overview:** In 1952, Kinmen Defense Commander Ku Chuanchieh established a distillery on a war-compressed island. Drought-resistant kaoliang was incorporated into frontline supply and local production arrangements; later, the distillery also grew up between military administration, the government monopoly system, and local governance. What people raise their glasses to today is not only 38 or 58 proof liquor, but also the process by which a locality rewrote a military institution into life memory. [^1] [^2] [^3]

## Let's Start with a Distillery

In 1952, Kinmen was still a frontline island after the Chinese Civil War. The official history of Kinmen Distillery records the founder as Kinmen Defense Commander Ku Chuanchieh. On February 1, 1953, the Longjiang Distillery officially began operations, with Zhou Xinchun as the first factory manager. [^1]

This starting point is not romantic at all. On a frontline island, supply is not only a cost issue, but also tied to military risks, local production, and market circulation. Research on the sales of Kinmen kaoliang begins by looking back at this supply and market chain from the product launch in 1953 and the policy shift in 1977. [^3]

Kinmen's liquor was not naturally grown from an "ancient tradition." It is more like a policy solution of a military government facing material shortages, tying together land, the military, alcohol, and the local market. This is also the easiest place to overlook about Kinmen kaoliang: its first identity is not a gift, but a supply item.

> 📝 **Curator's note:** A bottle of wine may look like a consumption choice, but looking back, it is a trace of how a military regime arranges land and taste. The "local flavor" of Kinmen kaoliang was not entirely decided by the locality from the beginning.

## Ye Huacheng and the Captured Technology

In this history, Ye Huacheng is a name that cannot be omitted. Local Kinmen articles record that Kinmen kaoliang liquor originated from his brewing in the ancient house on Daxilu Road, and was later taken over by General Ku Chuanchieh and expanded into the Longjiang Distillery. This is the origin narrative of local memory, and should not be directly expanded into the conclusion of "the only inventor." [^4]

Ye Huacheng's story is not equal to "a genius invented Kinmen kaoliang." Kinmen originally had folk brewing, and technology would not start from just one person. A more accurate way of saying it is that he turned the existing folk brewing activities into a technical node that could be seen, taken over, and amplified by the military. The National Taiwan History Museum lists the pre-1952 Kinmen folk brewing industry and the changes in post-war regulations as research topics, which reminds us: before the distillery was established, the island already had its own brewing context. [^5]

This memory is clearly controversial: the same thing can be said as "local technology was seen by the state," or it can be said as "private business was requisitioned under an authoritarian environment." This article does not wash the latter into the former, nor does it push all the details into a single conclusion. What is certain is that today's Kinmen distillery is not a purely military startup separated from civil society.

## From the Island to Taiwan

After the distillery began operations in 1953, Kinmen kaoliang was still restricted by the market and the system. The official data of Kinmen Distillery records that in 1977, the distillery was included in the national monopoly sales system, and Kinmen liquor was officially easier to enter the Taiwan market. This change pushed the product, which was mainly serving the military and the island, to a larger consumer network. [^1]

The 1977 shift is not only "increased sales." It made Kinmen kaoliang leave the local scale of the frontline, becoming a bottle that could be recognized in Taiwan's banquets, gifts, and family gatherings. The bottle then began to carry information beyond the liquor itself: from Kinmen, from the frontline, from a locality packaged by the national monopoly system. [^3]

The National Taiwan History Museum lists the pre-1952 folk brewing and post-war liquor laws and regulations as research topics, reminding us not to write Kinmen kaoliang as simply a corporate history of a distillery. The bottle and the liquor system actually connect local society with national management. [^5]

| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1952–1953 | Distillery established and officially operated                                              | Solve the supply of frontline liquor and incorporate kaoliang into military-civil supply |

> 📝 **Curator's note:** 1977 is not that a bottle of wine suddenly became better to drink, but that it was finally allowed to exist in another dimension: not only to supply the frontline, but also to become the consumption memory of the people of Taiwan. [^1]

## The Military Administration Ended, but the Distillery Remained

In 1992, Kinmen's military administration ended, and Kinmen Distillery was transferred to be under the Kinmen County Government. In 1998, it was reorganized as Kinmen Distillery Industry Co., Ltd. [^1] These two years took Kinmen liquor's history from military administration to local governance: the distillery was no longer just a production unit in the frontline system, but a public asset that the county government had to operate and the local residents continued to expect.

This transformation made the liquor more like Kinmen's "common property," and also brought new problems. When a company is under the local government and is placed in local memory and tourism narratives, the success of the liquor is not only a brand success. The local lecture puts Ye Huacheng's former residence, traditional settlements, and kaoliang liquor on the same walking route, which shows that the distillery has been placed in Kinmen's local memory and tourism narratives. [^4]

It also makes Kinmen liquor have a different relationship with [Kinmen County](/en/geography/kinmen-county/) 's other frontline relics: some history is preserved as a historical site, and some enters the wine cellar, bottle body, and banquet. Kinmen is not keeping all the frontline history in the museum, but putting part of it into daily consumption. This transformation is creative, but also risky: if only the experience of "drinking wine in the trench" is left, the history of war and military control may be replaced by consumption scenery.

## Who Drinks, Whose Flavor Counts as Kinmen

Looking at Kinmen kaoliang from the perspective of local consumption culture, we get another answer: it is not only the liquor provided by the military, but also re-used by residents in banquets, gifts, and daily gatherings. English media observed that kaoliang liquor has long been associated with the drinking occasions of older men, masculine style, and political gift culture. These clues show that the system provides the framework, and local life decides what meaning it finally leaves behind. [^6]

This view is important because it makes the story of "Kinmen kaoliang is the liquor invented by the military" no longer the end of the story. The system can stipulate how the liquor is produced, who can sell it, and which flavor is worth promoting, but it cannot completely determine how the residents finally use it. Some people drink at banquets, some use it as gifts, some take it as the memory left by their elders, and some don't drink, but instead treat the rejection of high-alcohol and male wine table culture as their own choice.

The same English report describes Kinmen kaoliang as a common high-alcohol liquor in Taiwan, and points out that 38% and 58% are the two most easily identified strengths. These observations can provide cultural clues, but should not be misread as the common experience of all Taiwanese or all Kinmen people. [^6]

> **Before opening the bottle, look at the bottle first:** The flavor of Kinmen kaoliang is not only on the tongue. Alcohol content, ceramic bottle design, gift-giving occasions, and who sits at the table are all telling you which kind of social relationship this bottle belongs to.

## A Bottle of Local Finance

The official data of Kinmen Distillery refers to the period after 1992 as the "prosperity" and "transformation" of the distillery. The official English page also records that in 1992, the distillery was transferred to Kinmen County Government, and in 1998, it became a company. [^1] These institutional changes show that the locality of Kinmen liquor is not only "the water in Kinmen is good and the climate is suitable for kaoliang," but also who owns the distillery, how the revenue returns to the locality, and how the local government turns the brand into a governance tool.

Kinmen kaoliang later became one of the most recognizable products in Kinmen, and was also included in the narrative of distillery visits, local gifts, and cultural walks. Local Kinmen articles put the distillery, settlements, Ye Huacheng's former residence, and kaoliang culture in the same narrative, making "drinking wine" one of the ways to understand the Kinmen landscape. [^4] But this road cannot only talk about success: the more public the distillery is, the more the locality needs to publicly discuss its operations, revenue distribution, labor conditions, and agricultural supply, rather than just ending with the word "golden goose."

From an industrial point of view, kaoliang liquor still has an invisible part: the brewing relies not only on the brand, but also on the whole set of technology such as making liquor, solid fermentation, distillation, aging, and water source. Business articles sort out the process of making liquor, brewing, blending, and aging of Kinmen kaoliang, but its product recommendations belong to the views of the operators. More reliable worker details come from the Overseas Chinese Affairs Commission's report on the liquor-making technician Chen Qiliang. [^7] [^8] If you only remember Ku Chuanchieh and Ye Huacheng, you will write the history of the liquor as the story of a general and an entrepreneur. Putting the liquor-making workers back, you can finally see how a bottle of wine is made day by day.

Chen Qiliang joined Kinmen Distillery at the age of 19, followed his master to learn liquor-making, and later served for 41 years and 6 months, and returned to the company as a consultant. This length of time reminds us that the so-called "traditional craftsmanship" is not an abstract ancient method, but a job that someone has learned, repeated, and passed on to the next person in the factory. [^8]

The locality of kaoliang liquor is therefore not only in the original materials. It needs drought-resistant crops in the fields, microorganisms and hand feeling in the liquor, and workers who can identify changes in the factory. Finally, it leaves the island in a bottle that everyone can see. [^7] This road connects agriculture, industry, and the family dining table.

Therefore, the flavor of the distillery has never been a single person's secret formula, but the result of long-term collaboration: raw materials, liquor, time, equipment, and human judgment are all indispensable. [^7]
This is also the part of the local brand that is most difficult to copy. Every process puts Kinmen's land and people's time into the liquor. [^7]
What the distillery leaves behind is not only the formula, but also the workers' judgment of time and quality.

## How the Bottle Remembers a Set of Systems

The bottle of Kinmen kaoliang is also worth a separate look. The official history puts 1977 in the "development period," and puts the end of the 1992 military administration and the 1998 company reform in the "prosperity" and "transformation" periods. These names are not neutral year labels, but the narrative way that the distillery chose when it transformed from military administration to a local enterprise. [^2]

The research of the collections of the National Taiwan History Museum discusses the 1977 "dark to bright" policy in the process of selling to Taiwan. This shows us that the market is not a naturally open door, but a channel that appears after the institutional change. Kaoliang liquor can enter Taiwan, not only because consumers like it, but also because it can finally circulate in another set of monopoly rules. [^3]

Therefore, the bottle can be both a gift to relatives and friends, and a reduced model of the institution. The origin, brand, year, and commemorative image on the bottle bring an outlying island to the center of the dining table. And when people drink it, they often do not need to know the entire administrative history behind it, but will drink Kinmen, the frontline, and a certain family gathering atmosphere together.

> 📝 **Curator's note:** The history of Kinmen kaoliang is not over when it changes from "military" to "locality." The real transformation is that a system designed for the frontline is finally used by local people to preserve their own identity.

## From Frontline Provisions to Local Memory

The most worth keeping of Kinmen kaoliang is not the easily repeated name of "Taiwan's national liquor," but the contradiction on it: it was established under military control, but reinterpreted in local life. It was once the solution to the cost of the frontline, and later became the reliance of local finance and tourism. It was used by the state to represent a certain order, and was used by Kinmen people to remember their own homes.

Therefore, Kinmen kaoliang is not a linear brand story from 1952 to success. It has Ye Huacheng being seen, and also folk technology being incorporated. It has the market opening in 1977, and also the restrictions of the monopoly system. It has the local autonomy after the end of the 1992 military administration, and also the reality that the locality cannot easily lose the distillery's income. [^1] [^3]

Next time you see a transparent bottle, ceramic bottle, or commemorative liquor bottle at a banquet, you can first think of a more concrete picture: an island that was originally used for defense, allowing drought-resistant kaoliang to grow on the land. A distillery established for frontline supply, which eventually became a name that Kinmen people could not avoid when talking about the future of the locality. After drinking the wine, what is left is not a single answer, but the process by which Kinmen slowly turned a history arranged by the state into its own daily life.
