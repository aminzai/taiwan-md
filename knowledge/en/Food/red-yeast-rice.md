# Shulin Red Yeast Rice: The Winery is Gone, But the Fungus Remains in the Sausage

> **30-Second Summary:** To rewind a century, Shulin, in the southwest corner of the Taipei Basin, was the only factory in Taiwan that made wine from red yeast rice. During the Japanese colonial period, the local beverage industry called it "the Holy Land of Taiwanese Winemaking" [1](#user-content-fn-1). After 1945, _honglu_ produced here sold up to 150,000 _gongshi_ (a unit of volume) by 1969, making it an essential drink for banquets and celebrations in Taiwan [1](#user-content-fn-1). The winery, which operated for 96 years, closed in 2004, and the site became a science park. But the red yeast fungus did not die—it was reborn as sausages, sauces, and an annual cultural festival. Furthermore, the ancestor of modern cholesterol-lowering drugs like statins, monacolin K, was isolated from red yeast rice in 1979 [2](#user-content-fn-4).

## Rice from Song Dynasty Pharmacies, Arriving in Shulin during the Qing Guangxu Period

Red yeast fungus (_Monascus_) grows and ferments on grains, dyeing originally white rice a reddish-brown color while releasing unique ester aromas. This technology was mature as early as the Song Dynasty: the Yuan Dynasty court recipe _Yinshan Zhengyao_ described red yeast as "strengthening the spleen, boosting energy, and warming the middle," while Wu Duan's _Riyong Bencao_ from the Ming Dynasty wrote that "red yeast brews wine and dispels blood ailments" [3](#user-content-fn-2). Li Shizhen included the method of making red yeast in his _Bencao Gangmu_, bringing it from the kitchen into the pharmacy [3](#user-content-fn-2). In fermentation history, red yeast simultaneously served three roles: brewing ingredient, natural red pigment, and meat preservative [3](#user-content-fn-2). The Ming Dynasty's Song Yingxing also pointed out the antifungal properties of red yeast—later experiments confirmed that red yeast extracts do inhibit various food spoilage bacteria [4](#user-content-fn-7).

China and Korea have long used red yeast to make alcoholic beverages and fermented foods; this is a shared East Asian fermentation heritage [5](#user-content-fn-3). This thousand-year-old technology arrived in Shulin during the Qing Guangxu period. At that time, Shulin already housed many small private distilleries making rice wine and _lao honglu_ (old red wine). The brewers imported red yeast brewing techniques from Fujian because Fujian was a traditional stronghold of red yeast winemaking [3](#user-content-fn-2).

## A Rebellious Poet Who Wrote Poetry, and the Winery He Established with 3,200 Yen

In 1907 (Meiji 40), the Shulin red yeast industry formally took shape, and the founder's identity is more complex than you might imagine. Huang Chun-ching (黃純青), born in Shulin (then known as Haishanbao) in 1875, studied at the Shulin Yangshan study house from age 9 for 13 years. At age 18, he passed the county and prefectural exams but his path to officialdom was cut short after the Sino-Japanese War [6](#user-content-fn-13). In 1895, when Japanese forces advanced south, he took up arms with local villagers, attacking the Japanese near Pi-chiao Station and Ta-liao Keng. Thirteen residents of Shulin died in the attack and were buried at the "Thirteen Martyrs Cemetery" in Fenggui Village [6](#user-content-fn-13).

This gentleman from Shulin, who once clashed with the Japanese soldiers with swords, later became one of the most senior officials during the Japanese colonial period: he served as the District Chief of Shulin, Taoyuan Prefecture, and the Zhuangzhang (village chief) of Yingge, Taipei Prefecture, for 33 years. In addition to over a hundred official positions—including chairman of credit unions and livestock associations, and council member of the Governor's Office—he was awarded the Sixth Class Order of Merit by Japan in 1928 [6](#user-content-fn-13). In 1931, he traveled with Guo Ting-chun to Tokyo to negotiate directly, forcing the government of Prime Minister Saito Riichi to retract its decree restricting the transfer of Taiwanese rice to the mainland [6](#user-content-fn-13). In literature, he was a poet of Ying Society and later used the pseudonym "Qingyuan Laoren," authoring _Qingyuan Shicao_ [6](#user-content-fn-13).

In 1906, Huang Chun-ching invested 3,200 yen with Xie Wang, Zhou Ju-shan, and Wang Wang, among 7 shareholders, to establish the "Shulin Winery"—the first enterprise in Shulin to use a collective investment model for winemaking [6](#user-content-fn-13). In Meiji 40, Huang Chun-ching formed the "Shulin Brewing Company" with Xie Dao-pi and Chen Yan to market red wine across Taiwan [3](#user-content-fn-2). The claim that Huang Chun-ching took over from South Africa is inaccurate—the true fermentation artisan who returned from South Africa was another individual, Hsu Chao-hua, representing two different eras. This will be recounted later [7](#user-content-fn-9).

In 1924, the company was reorganized into the "Shulin Winery of the Taiwan Governor's Office Monopoly Bureau" [3](#user-content-fn-2). Under the patronage of the monopoly system, this winery entered a golden age: it was the only red yeast rice manufacturing plant in all of Taiwan and the site where the _Amiluo_ (rice wine) production method was successfully experimented with. It also developed scientific methods for making red wine using red yeast fungus and root mold. During the Japanese colonial period, Shulin held an almost mythical title in the beverage industry: "the Holy Land of Taiwanese Winemaking" [1](#user-content-fn-1).

## Honglu Wine: That Bottle of Red at Post-War Taiwanese Banquets

After the war in 1945, the winery was taken over by the Provincial Government Monopoly Bureau and renamed the Shulin Winery. The two signature red wines, "Golden Rooster" and "Yellow Rooster," were given a more elegant name—**Honglu Wine** [1](#user-content-fn-1).

How red was _honglu_ in post-war Taiwan? It was an indispensable celebratory drink for private weddings and festivities, rumored by the common people to "nourish the body and boost energy" [3](#user-content-fn-2). The Public Sales Bureau set production increase plans to meet market demand. Under the guidance of technical staff, the Shulin Winery continuously improved red yeast and red wine processes, with output climbing steadily: reaching 60,000 _gongshi_ in 1958 and exceeding 150,000 _gongshi_ in 1969, setting a production record for the Japanese colonial period [1](#user-content-fn-1). From then on, Shulin's name as "the home of Honglu Wine" became widespread [3](#user-content-fn-2).

> 📝 **Curator's Note** The most curious aspect of the Shulin story is that its fame was built on "wine," but the winery no longer exists. In 2004, the Shulin Winery, which operated for 96 years, became history, and the site turned into Taiwan Datong Science Park [3](#user-content-fn-2). A city lost its landmark, but it did not lose its aroma—this is the key to understanding Shulin red yeast rice.

## How a Vat of Red Yeast Rice is Made

To understand why people in Shulin are so devoted to red yeast rice, one must first look at how a vat of it is made.

Red yeast fungus is an aerobic mold; the first key step in making the starter culture is "oxygen." The steamed rice must be spread out and kept loose, with enough air gaps between grains for circulation. If it becomes sticky into a clump, the red yeast fungus grows poorly, and the resulting wine will not be fragrant [4](#user-content-fn-7). The second key element is "the rice." Glutinous rice is almost entirely composed of branched starch, which has many molecular ends where the amylase secreted by the mold can attach, leading to fast decomposition. This naturally results in higher sugar and alcohol content compared to using non-glutinous or regular rice—this is the scientific explanation for why "glutinous rice must be used for flavor" [4](#user-content-fn-7).

The traditional folk method involves spreading steamed glutinous rice onto a cool surface, maintaining a fermentation temperature of 25–32°C. If it gets too cold, cotton blankets are used to keep it warm, and it is stirred once a day until completion in about 12 days [9](#user-content-fn-14). The owner, He Fuzhen, would wake up in the middle of the night to stir the fermenting red yeast rice [7](#user-content-fn-9). This sounds romantic, but it is science—the mycelial growth during fermentation releases heat, and stirring is done to evenly dissipate this heat and prevent localized overheating from killing the strains [9](#user-content-fn-14).

Scientific research has turned these experiences into numbers. Regarding the production of the health component monacolin K in red yeast rice, scholars found the optimal parameters for solid fermentation: an initial pH of 5.5, a moisture content of 40%, maintaining 30°C for the first 3 days, then dropping to 24°C for 15 days, totaling 18 days, with a 10% water addition on day 4—under these conditions, the monacolin K content in red yeast rice can reach 9.5 mg per gram, and no batches have shown renal toxicity from citrinin [10](#user-content-fn-15). Traditional craftsmanship and laboratory thermometers achieved the same goal in this vat of fermentation.

## After Closure: The Winery is Gone, But the Fungus Remains in the Sausage

The winery closed, but the red yeast fungus continues to live in local vats. After losing the state-run winery, people in Shulin successively established private wineries and Taipei distilleries, and red yeast production techniques were passed down among neighbors [3](#user-content-fn-2). The current private Shulin Winery still claims to be "one of Taiwan's oldest wineries" and continues to produce _honglu_ wine, aiming to build a "Red Yeast Rice Cultural Museum" [11](#user-content-fn-5).

A more grounded continuation occurred in the kitchens of two local businesses.

**Ju Jia Food Store** started producing red yeast processed goods in 2004. The first generation struggled when trying to incorporate red yeast into meat products, as the muscle fibers became loose and the texture was poor. After repeated formula adjustments, their signature Red Yeast Sorghum Sausage was mass-produced in 2005 [3](#user-content-fn-2). This sausage won a Top Ten Souvenir award for New Taipei City in 2008 [8](#user-content-fn-12) and is now a must-buy red yeast specialty for Shulin tourists—its deep red cross-section and faint wine aroma are memories left on the palate from the winery's era. Ju Jia Chairman Li Xiong-yang continues to research diverse products from red yeast, from red yeast lemon spicy jerky to red yeast jugstone grape pork jerky, moving red yeast from sausages into the snack cabinet [12](#user-content-fn-11).

Another company, **First Red Yeast**, is the only local factory in Shulin that produces raw red yeast ingredients. The owner, Hsu Chao-hua, originally ran a fermented soy sauce and vinegar business. After returning to Taiwan from South Africa, he settled in Shulin and transitioned to red yeast based on his fermentation background [7](#user-content-fn-9). This couple is almost devoutly devoted to red yeast: they drive weekly to Sanxia Fo Shan to collect spring water for soaking the rice, using expensive organic rice from Fuli. The owner, He Fuzhen, even wakes up in the middle of the night to stir the fermenting red yeast rice to cool it down, saying she "treats the red yeast fungus like life" [7](#user-content-fn-9). In 2005, they won the Food Golden Ding Award, and their red yeast sauce later became an ingredient for the beverage giant Pomelo Red Yeast drink [7](#user-content-fn-9).

## Red Mold on the Table: Another Face of Red Yeast Rice in Dining

The residue left after red yeast rice brewing is called "red mold" (_hongzao_)—this fermented byproduct is another face of Shulin's red yeast culture. The famous Fujian dish, "Lychee Meat," involves slicing pork finely and marinating it with red mold to achieve a reddish appearance resembling lychees [4](#user-content-fn-7). Red mold meat on Taiwanese tables is also common: the recipe from Taiwan Liquor Company’s Yilan Winery calls for pork belly cut into six to eight slices, marinated in red mold, soy sauce, sugar, and sesame oil. It is stir-fried to color and then braised for half an hour until bright red and glossy with a faint yeast aroma—the perfect companion for plain rice [13](#user-content-fn-16).

There are also standards for the rice used in making the starter culture. Foodpower's scientific analysis points out that glutinous rice, due to its branched starch structure, is decomposed faster by mold amylase than non-glutinous or regular rice, resulting in higher sugar and alcohol content [4](#user-content-fn-7). Furthermore, the longer the marinating time and the larger the surface area of the meat chunks, the more effectively the red mold flavor permeates the muscle tissue [4](#user-content-fn-7). Red yeast has also entered desserts: local bakers in Shulin incorporate red yeast powder and red mold into longan pound cake from the Shulin Winery. The faint rice aroma and stable natural red color present red yeast in a brand new form on the cross-section of the cake.

## 1979: Japanese Scholars Extract the Blueprint for Statins from a Vat

Beyond the kitchen, there is another story that changed world medicine. In 1979, Professor Endo Sho from Tokyo University of Science isolated **monacolin K**—a substance that inhibits cholesterol synthesis—from red yeast fungus. This discovery ignited a global research craze and became the chemical blueprint for the first statin drug, lovastatin, adding a new family to the history of cholesterol-lowering drugs [2](#user-content-fn-4). Mayo Clinic in the United States still states in its drug supplement database that monacolin K contained in red yeast rice has the same chemical structure as the drug lovastatin [2](#user-content-fn-4). International literature generally considers red yeast to be a relatively safe and effective auxiliary choice for patients with mild to moderate hypercholesterolemia [2](#user-content-fn-4).

Scientifically, the molecular structure of monacolin K is similar to HMG-CoA in the cholesterol synthesis pathway, allowing it to competitively inhibit HMG-CoA reductase—the rate-limiting enzyme in cholesterol synthesis [10](#user-content-fn-15). Other secondary metabolites from red yeast fungus, including the pigments monascin and ankaflavin, GABA, and dimeric acid, have also been experimentally proven to have potential for lowering blood pressure, blood sugar, and antioxidant activity [4](#user-content-fn-7).

However, in 2024, red yeast rice faced its most severe test globally. Red yeast health foods from Kobayashi Pharmaceutical in Japan caused kidney damage in multiple consumers; at least 5 people died and over a hundred were hospitalized due to the problematic raw material "citrinin" (also known as orange mold acid) [14](#user-content-fn-17). Citrinin is one of the metabolic byproducts of red yeast fungus itself, primarily damaging the kidneys, and has also been linked to endemic kidney diseases in the Balkans [4](#user-content-fn-7). The controversy spread to Taiwan; after investigation, the Food and Drug Administration preemptively removed 226 related products [14](#user-content-fn-17).

> **✦** "Fermentation is alive, and living things need care."

Yet science also provided answers. Animal trials by Taiwanese scholars showed that concentrations of citrinin in red yeast rice below 200 ppm do not affect liver or kidney function; the Ministry of Health and Welfare's Food and Drug Administration established a standard for health foods in 2007, requiring citrinin content in red yeast products to be less than 2 ppm [4](#user-content-fn-7). Japan stipulated an upper limit of 200 ppb for citrinin in red yeast pigments in the _Japanese Food Additive Standards_ in 1999 [4](#user-content-fn-7). Researchers studying fermentation processes have also demonstrated that by controlling the pH, moisture, and temperature curve, it is entirely possible to produce red yeast rice with high monacolin K and zero citrinin [10](#user-content-fn-15). For Shulin, the controversy of 2024 was an unexpected reminder: the fermentation technology that traveled from the Qing Guangxu small winery is both a calling card for the city and a food requiring rigorous quality control. Fermentation is alive, and living things need care—just like He Fuzhen stirring the red yeast rice in the middle of the night [7](#user-content-fn-9).

## Picnic in Longevity Park: Proof That the Winery Never Vanished

In 2007, Shulin Mayor Chen Shi-rong first held the **Shulin Red Yeast Rice Cultural Festival** under the name "One Specialty per Township" [3](#user-content-fn-2). The local government recognized red yeast as an asset of the people of Shulin and authorized local businesses to use the term "Shulin Specialty" on their products [7](#user-content-fn-9). The cultural festival continues: in 2014, it linked historical tours with red yeast food competitions, connecting the train station, Ji'an Temple, and Shengji Ting into a "Red Yeast Time Tunnel" [15](#user-content-fn-6); in 2025, it moved to Longevity Park, the "Lungs of Shulin," hosting red yeast picnics and starlight children's events [16](#user-content-fn-10).

The public also took up the mantle. The "Shulin Red Yeast Culture Promotion Association," established in 2009, has high-ranking members from the Shulin Winery, continuing the legacy of "the home of red yeast rice" [17](#user-content-fn-8). The community did not miss out: the "Red Yeast Foodie" series by the Shanjia Community Development Association features videos of making red yeast cakes and dumplings for use in community classes [15](#user-content-fn-6). When New Taipei City Museum opened in 2025, the Shulin Winery collaborated with an international youth cultural exchange program to introduce the winemaking process of _honglu_ wine through a "bottle fishing" game—one participant said: "So the Shulin Winery hasn't disappeared." [11](#user-content-fn-5)

### Key Eras of Shulin Red Yeast Rice

1. **Qing Guangxu Period**: Brewers from Fujian brought red yeast rice winemaking technology to Shulin, and local small distilleries began making red wine [3](#user-content-fn-2).
2. **1906**: Huang Chun-ching established the "Shulin Winery" with 7 shareholders investing 3,200 yen [6](#user-content-fn-13).
3. **1907**: Huang Chun-ching formed the "Shulin Brewing Company" with Xie Dao-pi and Chen Yan, shaping the Shulin red yeast industry [3](#user-content-fn-2).
4. **1924**: Reorganized into the "Shulin Winery of the Taiwan Governor's Office Monopoly Bureau," becoming the only red yeast rice manufacturing plant in all of Taiwan [3](#user-content-fn-2).
5. **1945**: Renamed Shulin Winery after the war, and signature wines were renamed "Honglu Wine" [1](#user-content-fn-1).
6. **1969**: Honglu wine production exceeded 150,000 _gongshi_, setting a record for the Japanese colonial period [1](#user-content-fn-1).
7. **2004**: The Shulin Winery, which operated for 96 years, closed, and the site became Taiwan Datong Science Park [3](#user-content-fn-2).
8. **2007**: The first Shulin Red Yeast Rice Cultural Festival appeared, with the government recognizing red yeast as an asset of the people of Shulin [3](#user-content-fn-2).

## Advice for Travelers

If you want to take some red yeast rice home, Ju Jia Food Store on Boai Street is the first stop: sliced red yeast sorghum sausage served with scallions is the most classic way to eat it in Shulin. To see the original form of red yeast rice, look for organic red yeast sauce from First Red Yeast, or visit the free tasting booths at the cultural festival. If you want to taste another face of red yeast rice on the dining table, try red mold meat at a Taiwanese restaurant, or go to the Shulin Winery for a tasting experience. Visiting in September allows you to attend the annual Red Yeast Rice Cultural Festival [16](#user-content-fn-10). Take the train to Shulin Station or Shanjia Station; it is within walking distance—just like the batch of red yeast rice that crossed the sea from Fujian over a hundred years ago, this journey has never been far.
