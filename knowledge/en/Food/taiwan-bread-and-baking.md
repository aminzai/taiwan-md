# Taiwanese Bread and Baking

Taiwan's bread culture presents a unique blend of East and West. From the foundational techniques introduced during the Japanese colonial period, to the American influence post-war, and finally to the European trends in recent years, Taiwan's baking industry has forged its own path. In 2010, [Wu Bao-chun](/en/people/wu-bao-chun/) established a reputation for Taiwanese baking on the international stage by winning the individual category at Mondial du Pain (World Bread Competition) in Paris, France.[^1]

## The Wu Bao-chun Phenomenon and the Road to World Champion

In March 2010, [Wu Bao-chun](/en/people/wu-bao-chun/), from Pingtung, Taiwan, shocked the world by winning the individual category at Mondial du Pain (Lesfoux World Bread Competition) held at the Europain exhibition in Paris, France, with his winning creation, "Rice Fermented Lychee Fragrance" (made with lychee, rose, and enzyme starter from a Puli winery).[^1] This was a milestone in Taiwanese baking history. It is important to note that this competition was Mondial du Pain, not the larger Coupe du Monde de la Boulangerie (which is a team event).

Behind Wu Bao-chun's success is an inspiring story. Coming from poverty, he started as an apprentice at a bakery after graduating from junior high school, beginning with basic cleaning work. In an era before baking departments existed, word-of-mouth instruction from his master was the only method of learning. Through his passion for bread and relentless effort, he gradually honed his skills.

The inspiration for "Rice Fermented Lychee Fragrance" comes from local Taiwanese ingredients: the tropical sweetness of lychee, the floral scent of rose, combined with enzyme starter made from wine at a Puli winery, creating an unprecedented layer of flavor. This practice of integrating local Taiwanese elements into Western baking later became a characteristic of Taiwanese bread.

Wu Bao-chun's victory was not the end point for Taiwan in this competition. Wu Tzu-ching won the individual category in 2015, Chen Yao-chun in 2017, and Wang Peng-jie in 2022, making Taiwan one of the countries with the most champions in the history of this competition.[^2]

## The Unique Aesthetics of Taiwanese Bread

Taiwanese bread culture integrates multiple elements to form a unique "Taiwanese aesthetic." The greatest characteristic of this aesthetic is its "limitlessness"—any ingredient can become an accompaniment for the bread, and any flavor is worth trying.

Scallion bread is the most typical Taiwanese innovation. To Europeans, scallions and bread are completely incompatible combinations, yet Taiwanese people created this classic. The bread body is soft, topped with green onions and mayonnaise, creating a surprising interplay of savory and sweet flavors. This bold spirit of innovation is the essence of Taiwanese bread.

Meat floss bread is another representative work. Meat floss, a traditional Taiwanese side dish, combined with Western bread to create a unique sensory experience. The meat floss on the outside provides umami and chewiness, while the interior bread remains soft and sweet, forming an interesting contrast.

Pineapple bun originated in Hong Kong and developed its own version after being introduced to Taiwan. Although named "pineapple bun," it does not actually contain pineapple; it is named because the flaky crust pattern resembles a pinecone. The Taiwanese version of the pineapple bun is usually sweeter and has a thicker crust than the Hong Kong version, catering to the Taiwanese preference for sweet foods.

## European Bread vs. Taiwanese Bread: A Dialogue Between Two Philosophies

In recent years, European bread has swept through Taiwan, with many bakers traveling abroad to study authentic techniques in France. This European trend forms an interesting dialogue with traditional Taiwanese bread.

European bread pursues "purity." It uses natural yeast, long fermentation, and pays close attention to flour quality and water temperature. The finished product is usually hard on the outside and soft on the inside, with a solid texture and a natural sourness. This type of bread requires careful tasting and slow chewing to appreciate the sweetness of the flour.

Taiwanese bread pursues "richness." With various ingredients, flavors, and shapes, the goal is to provide maximum satisfaction to the consumer. The bread body is usually softer and sweeter, making it suitable for quick consumption. This difference reflects different cultural understandings of food.

Interestingly, these two styles in Taiwan do not repel each other; rather, they complement each other. Many bakeries sell both European and Taiwanese breads simultaneously to meet the needs of different customers. Some innovative bakers even combine both techniques to create a new category called "Taiwanese European-style bread."

## The International Legend of 85°C

If Wu Bao-chun represents the technical height of Taiwanese baking, then 85°C symbolizes the commercial achievement of Taiwanese baking. This brand, founded in 2003, evolved from a local Taiwanese coffee shop into an international chain: entering the Chinese market in 2008 and expanding to the United States in 2016.[^3]

The secret to 85°C's success lies in its positioning of "affordable luxury." They offer cakes and bread equivalent to five-star hotels, but at the price point of a regular coffee shop. This strategy accurately captured consumer psychology, allowing ordinary people to enjoy exquisite baked goods.

The internationalization process was not smooth. In the Chinese market, 85°C faced intense local competition; in the US market, they had to adapt to different consumption habits. However, through continuous adjustment of product mixes and operating models, 85°C gradually established a foothold overseas.

The success of 85°C abroad also proves the international competitiveness of Taiwanese baking. Although techniques originated from Europe, America, and Japan, after innovation and refinement in Taiwan, it has developed a unique style and advantage. This soft power export is more persuasive than any official promotion.

## Baking Education and Skill Inheritance

The development of Taiwan's baking industry cannot be separated from the support of its educational system. From the early apprenticeship model to today's vocational colleges, baking education has undergone significant transformation.

Baking departments in vocational colleges have trained a large number of talents for the industry. Students not only learn basic techniques but also gain professional knowledge in food science, nutrition, and cost control. This systematic education has raised the overall professional standard of the industry.

Various baking competitions have also played an important role. From school competitions to international events, these contests are platforms for technical exchange and nurseries for talent cultivation. Many famous bakers gained recognition through competitions.

In recent years, baking education has also moved toward diversification. In addition to traditional technical training, courses in entrepreneurship management, food safety, and international certification have been added. This comprehensive training model makes Taiwanese baking talent more competitive in the international market.

## Food Safety Challenges and Industrial Transformation

Food safety is the biggest challenge facing the baking industry. From the plasticizer storm to the spice additive controversy, every food safety incident has caused a major impact on the industry. Consumer demands for food safety are increasing, requiring businesses to establish stricter quality control systems.

Many businesses have begun adopting a "transparency" strategy, disclosing ingredient sources and production processes. Some bakeries even install open kitchens, allowing consumers to watch the production process directly. Although this practice increases costs, it also enhances consumer trust.

The rise of health consciousness has also driven industrial transformation. Health-oriented breads such as low-sugar, low-oil, and whole grain are gaining popularity. Although the texture may not be as good as traditional bread, they meet the needs of health-conscious consumers.

Technological innovation is another important trend. Automated equipment has increased production efficiency, freezing technology has extended shelf life, and new baking methods have created more possibilities. These innovations not only improve product quality but also reduce labor costs.

The development trajectory of Taiwan's bread and baking industry is clear: from Wu Bao-chun winning first in 2010, to achieving three consecutive individual category crowns at Mondial du Pain in 2015, 2017, and 2022, and finally to 85°C expanding into the European, American, and Asian markets, Taiwanese baking has demonstrated tangible international competitiveness. This is not just a technical upgrade but a complete evolution from production to branding.[^4]
