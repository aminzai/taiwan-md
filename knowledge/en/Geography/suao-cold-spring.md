> **30-Second Overview:** Su'ao Cold Spring is located in Su'ao Township, Yilan County. The spring water has been consistently around 22°C for many years and contains a large amount of carbon dioxide, which forms fine bubbles at the bottom of the pool. It is both a geological phenomenon and a local amenity: people once washed clothes, bathed, and made soda near the springs; today, Cold Spring Park and Ali Shi Cold Spring continue this local memory.[↩︎](#參考資料)

Su'ao Cold Spring is not located deep in the mountains, but about 300 meters from Su'ao Train Station in the urban area. The Ministry of Transportation and Communications' Tourism Administration records it as a low-temperature mineral spring discovered in 1928; local cultural memory preserves the story of Zhu Zhong-sinjing drinking the spring water in 1895, followed by research and development. Both dates exist in official documents and cannot be crudely merged into one undisputed year.[^1] [^2]

Its temperature is about 22°C, which feels cool for a rainy town in Lanyang, but this does not mean the groundwater is physically "abnormally cold." The Lanyang Museum points out that shallow groundwater in the Lanyang Plain is generally around 22–25°C; what truly makes Su'ao Cold Spring difficult to replace with other springs is the large amount of dissolved carbon dioxide in the water.[^3]

This 2006 photograph also retains the urban scale of the cold spring: bathing pools, shelters, and mountain slopes appear in the frame simultaneously. The cold spring is not a resource that can only be understood from a geological cross-section; it is concretely fixed within the Su'ao urban area through cement boundaries, public facilities, and human use.[^11]

**The core counter-intuitive point here is: the most recognizable feature of Su'ao Cold Spring is not "cold," but "bubbling."** The bubbles give bathing a feeling akin to sugar-free soda, transforming the cold spring from mere groundwater into a local culture.[^3] [^4]

## Geological Cross-Section in the Urban Area

Su'ao Cold Spring is distributed along the Cold Spring Creek area. The main public usage areas include Cold Spring Park and Ali Shi Cold Spring, which are less than 200 meters apart. The Lanyang Museum records that both locations are near Su'ao urban areas and major roads, accessible by walking from Su'ao Station; this scale of "being able to bathe right after leaving the station" is a crucial factor in its becoming a popular attraction.[^3]

The formation of the cold spring requires two things to happen simultaneously: sufficient water underground, and rock layers providing large amounts of carbon dioxide. Su'ao has abundant rainfall, allowing rainwater to seep into the ground; local articles from the Ministry of Transportation and Communications link the water source, rock fractures, rising carbon dioxide, and surface emergence in a single geological process.[^4]

In simple terms, precipitation in the mountains first enters the ground and seeps along fractures. Carbonates in deep rock layers provide carbon dioxide through geological action, and the gas-containing groundwater rises along the fractures. When pressure decreases, the carbon dioxide escapes from the water, allowing people to see continuous fine bubbles rising in the spring pool.[^3] [^4]

Su'ao Cold Spring is not an isolated well. The Lanyang Museum explains that the springs and $\text{CO}_2$ vents extended along the downstream section of Cold Spring Creek; today, some creek sections are covered, and public baths and parks have transformed underground phenomena into accessible urban spaces.[^3]

This also explains why "Cold Spring Park" and "Ali Shi Cold Spring" cannot be treated as two homogeneous bathing spots. The former is a planned park with paid bathing facilities, while the latter retains the character of a public bath closer to daily use; both locations share the cold spring water system but serve different urban functions. Data from the Ministry of Transportation and Communications places them within the overall tourism context of Su'ao Township, showing that the springs are not isolated from the harbor, railway, and mountain-sea landscape.[^3] [^4]

The cold spring also provides an entry point to understanding Su'ao's geography. To the east is Nanfangao and the Pacific Ocean; to the northwest are Qixingling and the Lanyang Plain; rainwater, slopes, underground fractures, and urban bathing pools form an invisible water system over a very short distance. This scale often leads visitors to mistake "nature" for a facility within a park, when in reality, the park is just a small section that has been reorganized after groundwater circulates to the surface.[^3] [^4]

## "Washing Sulfurous Water": Cold Spring Was Daily Life Before Tourism

Before tourism slogans appeared, the cold spring was already a basic life facility for the people of Su'ao. During the Japanese colonial period, there were baths, washing platforms, and steps by Cold Spring Creek; women washed clothes and vegetables by the creek, and children played in the downstream pools. These details mean that the cold spring was not just a "scenic spot," but a public place frequently used by local residents.[^3]

Local cultural historian Zhuang Wensheng mentioned in an interview with _Taiwan Guanghua_ that the people of Su'ao did not necessarily say "washing cold springs" (洗冷泉), but rather "washing sulfurous water" (洗磺水). This term encapsulates the mineral smell of the spring, the physical sensation, and local vernacular into one word, reminding us that the cold spring culture is not a label later applied by tourism operators, but a habit accumulated by residents through their bodies.[^5]

The modern commercialization of the cold spring was not a straight ascent. The Lanyang Museum records that public baths and bathing facilities appeared during the Japanese colonial period; there was a period of stagnation after WWII until local governments redeveloped Cold Spring Park in the 1980s and 1990s, at which point the cold spring was packaged again as a leisure and tourism resource.[^3] [^5]

The key to this transformation was not making the water more special, but rearranging the usage patterns that were originally scattered along the creek into parks, bathing pools, paths, and service facilities. Current park data from Yilan County Government also describes Cold Spring Park as a public area with significance for bathing, drinking, and geological education.[^6]

## From Springs to Soda, Then to Yanggang

The most interesting industrial history of Su'ao Cold Spring is that the water was not kept only in baths. Because the water contained carbon dioxide, people used the cold spring water to make soft drinks during the Japanese colonial period; the Lanyang Museum records that there was a soda factory at the time that used marble-stoppered "Namnei Soda," linking it to Su'ao.[^3]

The local data preserved in the National Cultural Memory Database identifies Zhu Zhong-sinjing as an important figure in cold spring development and records his later memory of making drinks and sodas from the spring water. However, this material carries the nature of oral tradition and local narrative; therefore, a more cautious way to write is "official cultural materials preserve this memory," rather than claiming that all details are proven by archives to an equal degree.[^2]

The tourism page for the Ministry of Transportation and Communications also records another industrial line: Su'ao Yanggang was made using cold spring water and became a representative local specialty; marble soda production has ceased. Thus, the cold spring not only provides a bathing experience but has entered into souvenirs, shops, and urban identity.[^1]

Two photos separated by sixteen years cannot directly prove how the park facilities have changed, but they help readers see one thing: what is preserved about the cold spring is not just the water itself, but also the entrance, the monument, the pathways around the pools, and the local memory of "I was here." This is the intermediate layer most easily overlooked before a natural resource becomes cultural heritage.[^12]

> **Curator's Note:** The industrial history of Su'ao Cold Spring is not as simple as "turning a natural resource into a commodity"; it is more like a watercourse, flowing from underground to the bathing pools, and then into soda bottles, yanggang shops, and local vernacular.

## One Date, Two Memories

The "discovery history" of Su'ao Cold Spring deserves ambiguity rather than rushing to choose a single correct answer. The tourism page for the Ministry of Transportation and Communications writes of its discovery in 1928; while the township articles from the Ministry of Transportation and Communications and the National Cultural Memory Database place Zhu Zhong-sinjing's contact with and study of the cold spring around 1895 at the core.[^1] [^2] [^4]

This may reflect that "local residents knew about the spring long ago" is a different event from being "formally recorded or developed," or it might be a chronological difference left by different material traditions. The existing sources available for direct verification are insufficient to allow this article to assert how the two must correspond, so the article merely juxtaposes the materials without rewriting one date as the sole answer.

This uncertainty does not diminish the importance of the cold spring; rather, it shows that the history of a natural landscape is often pieced together from multiple materials: official park data records development nodes, local cultural data preserves personal and family memories, and museum articles place geology and daily life on the same stream. What the reader sees is not a seamless story, but how the township remembers its water.[^2] [^3] [^5]

## The Boundaries of Tourism Are Also in the Water

The fact that the cold spring can be used for bathing does not mean that any pool or any time is suitable for swimming. The park page from Yilan County Government lists heart disease, myocardial infarction, asthma, stroke, respiratory illnesses, as well as pregnant women and the elderly/frail, as unsuitable for entering the pools. Both the Lanyang Museum and _Taiwan Guanghua_ remind that spaces with carbon dioxide must be ventilated to prevent dangerous gas accumulation.[^3] [^5] [^6]

These reminders are not mere footnotes of tourism; they are another side of the cold spring's geological characteristics: the gases causing the water to bubble can also become a safety issue in poorly ventilated areas. The charm and limitations of the cold spring come from the same source, and one cannot only leave behind the "fun like soda" part.[^3] [^5]

Su'ao Cold Spring also faces longer-term management issues. Analysis of service quality and tourist demand from academic research shows that preserving the cold spring is not just about the water itself, but also about how public facilities, safety usage, and tourism management are maintained together.[^7]

Academic research also treats the cold spring as an object of local services and tourism management, not merely a natural wonder. A 2010 study based on 500 questionnaires and 424 valid responses analyzed 20 service quality variables of Su'ao Cold Spring Park and proposed improvement directions with different priorities.[^7] This indicates that the future of the cold spring depends not only on whether the water continues to bubble, but also on how the park cares for users, residents, and the resource itself.

## Looking at Su'ao Cold Spring Today

The open data from the Ministry of Transportation and Communications positions Su'ao Cold Spring Park as a hot spring-type attraction in Su'ao Township, providing coordinates, address, and site description. Yilan County Government places it within the local tourism system as a public attraction accessible by walking from the train station.[^6] [^8]

But if it is only described as "a place to cool down in summer at 22°C," the most important part will be missed. Here, the geological relationship between groundwater circulation, limestone, and carbon dioxide is preserved; the life history of washing platforms, baths, and public pools is preserved; and the memory of how soda and yanggang grew from the spring water is preserved.[^3] [^4] [^5]

The value of Su'ao Cold Spring lies in the fact that it has not been isolated into a natural exhibition hall far removed from life. It is next to the train station, under the urban creek, within local vernacular and public baths. When people bathe here, they encounter not just 22°C water, but also how a port town transformed underground gas into something shareable and something that must be carefully managed.
