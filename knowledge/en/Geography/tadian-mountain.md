# Luo Di Shan

## 30-Second Overview

A small hill measuring only 53 meters in elevation, 800 meters in length (east-west), and 600 meters in width, yet it is the **mud volcano badland closest to the coastline in Taiwan**. Formed by the accumulation of mud volcano sludge, it currently features two active mud vents with diameters of 3–5 centimeters. Having served as a military fortress since the Japanese colonial period, the site transitioned into a natural park managed by the Mituo District Office after the military withdrew its posts in 2006 (Republic of China year 95). A one-hour loop trail allows you to observe badland landscapes up close, cross suspension bridges, and ascend old bunkers to viewpoints overlooking the Taiwan Strait and military aircraft taking off and landing at nearby Gangshan Air Base.

**The takeaway?** A former "off-limits" military restricted zone has now become a paradise for family hiking and geological education.

## Why It Matters

Many in Taiwan assume that "badlands" only exist in Yuese World (Moon World) in Zuojin, Tainan, or in high mountain ranges. However, Luo Di Shan proves that even in the western plains, just a few kilometers from the sea, living mud volcanoes and rain-eroded gullies can be found. This is more than just a geological wonder; it is a microcosm of Taiwan's transition from wartime readiness to peace, and from restricted control to openness. Reaching the summit, you suddenly realize that Taiwan's nature and history are never isolated from one another.

## Main Content

### Geological Formation and Features

Luo Di Shan is a badland formation created by **mud volcano** accumulation, representing an extension of the Nanhua mudstone layer. Geological surveys indicate its formation is related to "mud diapirs": high-pressure fluids from deep underground rise along fault fractures, mixing with groundwater to form mud, which then erupts and accumulates into five small circular hills. The soil consists of impermeable fine-grained mudstone (grayish-white or grayish-black, with a pH of approximately 6.7). Rainwater erosion creates steep gullies, while the surface remains cracked and loose, making it difficult for vegetation to take root.

Currently, two small mud vents (3–5 cm in diameter) remain at the central summit. Although some have been partially diverted with cement, they still slowly bubble—serving as the representative mud volcano closest to the coast in all of Taiwan.

### Historical Evolution

Since the Japanese colonial period, the area has been a strategic military site due to its proximity to the Gangshan Air Base; the artillery of the Republic of China Army's 8th Corps was once deployed here. For a long time, it was classified as a restricted zone, appearing to onlookers from afar as merely an "unremarkable small hill." It only gained attention after the discovery of the Tsatsong Cultural Layer (a prehistoric site) at the foot of the mountain. Following the military withdrawal in 2006, management was handed to the Mituo District Office, which gradually constructed trails, viewpoints, and access roads, officially unveiling its mysterious veil.

### Park Facilities and Trails

The loop trail spans approximately 2 kilometers (comprising North, South, and Outer loops). The path consists of paving stones, boardwalks, and asphalt. With an elevation change of only 30 meters, the difficulty is low (suitable for strollers or the elderly). A suspension bridge at the entrance provides quick access, and the old artillery bunker at the highest point has been converted into a 360-degree observation deck, offering views over the Mituo neighborhoods, Greater and Lesser Gangshan, Yong'an Fishing Port, and the Taiwan Strait. The park preserves military relics (such as sentry posts and camouflage-themed art installations) while providing children's sandpits and treehouses.

### Ecology, Landscape, and Activities

Due to the fragile soil, the ecology is relatively simple (sparse vegetation), but this is precisely the beauty of the badlands—the exposed ridges and canyons resemble a miniature Moon World. The best way to experience it is to summit at dusk to watch the sunset sink into the sea, or in the early morning to observe military aircraft at Gangshan Air Base. Nearby sites can be integrated into a single day's "Geology + History + Coastline" tour, including the Tsatsong Cultural Site, Nanliao Fishing Port, and Yong'an Wetlands. These two tiny mud vents serve as a quiet reminder that even when humans draw military boundaries, the earth continues to tell its story.
