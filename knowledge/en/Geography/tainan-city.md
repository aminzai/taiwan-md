# Tainan City: A Layering of 261 Years as Capital, 400 Years of Antiquity, and 21st Century Chips on One Land

> **30-Second Overview:** The Dutch East India Company began building Fort Zeelandia on the sand dunes in Dàyuán (Anping) in 1624, completing it in 1634. The walls were constructed using glutinous rice paste, sugar syrup, sand, and oyster shell powder[^1]. On the morning of April 30, 1661, Zheng Chenggong led 400 warships and 25,000 soldiers into Tainan Inner Sea through Lukaimen[^2]. On February 1, 1662, Coyett signed the Treaty of Peace between the Qing and Dutch in this fort; on February 9, 2,000 Dutchmen departed by sea from the beach[^3]. The Dutch occupation lasted 38 years. In 1665, Chen Yonghua built the Kong Miao (Confucian Temple), which was called the "First School of All Taiwan" after the Qing period[^4]. In 1684, the Qing court established Tainan Prefecture in present-day Tainan; from 1624 to 1885, Tainan served as the administrative center of Taiwan for 261 consecutive years. On noon, March 13, 1947, Lawyer Tang Dezhang was tied up and paraded through the streets before being shot in the MinSheng Green Park roundabout[^5]. On December 25, 2010, Tainan County merged to become a municipality with 37 districts and approximately 1.86 million residents. In 2023, the annual revenue of Southern Science Park exceeded 1.5855 trillion NTD, surpassing Hsinchu by 142 billion NTD[^6]. The investment of 700 billion NTD for TSMC's 3nm wafer fab is built upon this plain where the Dutch raised their flag 400 years ago.

## The Day Coyett Handed Over the Keys on the Beach

On that day, 2,000 Dutchmen stood on the beach.

On February 9, 1662, Dutch settlers from various parts of Taiwan arrived in Dàyuán. They boarded 8 Dutch ships awaiting departure. The Chinese Wikipedia records: "Coyett handed over the keys to Fort Zeelandia to Zheng officials, and Zheng Chenggong entered Fort Zeelandia to receive the fortress and its assets."[^3]

⚠️ There is a detail often confused in Chinese sources regarding the surrender date: **February 1, 1662, Coyett signed the Koxinga–Dutch Treaty inside Fort Zeelandia**; **February 9 was the day the Dutch finally left**. English Wikipedia and most European historical records use February 1 as the surrender date (signing date), while Chinese sources often use February 9 (departure date). This article reconstructs this process using both dates: "**Signed on Feb 1, 1662, departed on Feb 9**"[^3].

The fort stood for 38 years.

In 1624, the Dutch East India Company began constructing Fort Zeelandia (also known as Tainan Fortress) on the sand dunes in Dàyuán (present-day Anping). The _Taiwan Guanghua_ magazine's verbatim record states: "The 'Fort Zeelandia,' built on the sand dunes, and 'Tainan Town' was constructed to the east of the fort."[^7] It was completed in 1634. The walls were made from **glutinous rice paste, sugar syrup, sand, and oyster shell powder**, with three layers inside and five cannons at each bastion in the four corners[^1].

In 1653, following the Guo Huaiyi Incident (an armed resistance by Han immigrants against heavy taxation by the Dutch) in 1652, the Dutch built **Fort Provintia** (the precursor to Chihkan Tower) on the east side of Dàyuán to consolidate their power, completing it in 1655[^7]. From this time, Tainan's dual-fort structure was established: Fort Zeelandia by the sea and Fort Provintia on the shore.

The Dutch brought more than just castles. On May 26, 1636, they opened Taiwan's first school in Xinguang Village. Missionary Georgius Candidius used **Roman script to transcribe the language of Xinguang Village (Xiraya)**, which is the earliest systematic written record in Taiwanese history[^8]. Later generations called this the "Xinguang Documents" or "Fanzi Zi." This text was used until the Qing dynasty, with records extending to 1813 (Jiaqing 18), where the bilingual contract between the Xiraya and Han people was still in use[^8].

200 years. A script left by a Dutch missionary in 1636 survived longer than the Zheng dynasty or the early Qing period.

## Lukaimen at Dawn: 400 Ships Enter Tainan Inner Sea

On the morning of April 30, 1661, **400 warships and 25,000 soldiers** approached Tainan Inner Sea through Lukaimen[^2].

The academic consensus is that according to Dutch Batavia documents, the precise time Zheng's army arrived at Lukaimen was **6:30 AM Batavia Time on April 30, 1661** (7:30 AM Taiwan Time), while Zheng's own records state **the first day of the fourth month in Yongli 15**[^2]. The vanguard landed on the north bank of Beishanwei and defeated a small contingent of Dutch troops stationed there. Zheng's army rested at Lukaimen until noon, during high tide, when He Bin navigated them through the Lukaimen waterway into Tainan Inner Sea.

From entering Tainan Inner Sea on April 30th to Coyett signing the surrender document in Fort Zeelandia on February 1st of the following year, it was **9 months**.

> **📝 Curator's Note:** History classes often portray Zheng Chenggong as a "national hero who recovered Taiwan" in a single sentence. But on April 30, 1661, "Taiwan" was Formosa belonging to the Dutch East India Company—an island where Xiraya and Han immigrants lived together for 38 years, completely different from later Qing-controlled Taiwan or modern ROC Taiwan. Zheng Chenggong came from Minnan; what he did was take this island from the Dutch East India Company and turn it into his anti-Qing base, not "recover" land that was originally part of China. The moment Coyett handed over the keys on February 9, 1662, Taiwan gained its first **Han regime**. For the preceding 38 years, it belonged to the Dutch; before that, it was inhabited by the four major Xiraya communities (Xinguang, Xiao, Mazu, Mujialuan) for thousands of years. The status of a "Han regime" and "returning to the motherland" are two different things; this was a dynastic change. Reducing this period to a single narrative of "Zheng Chenggong recovering Taiwan" is like compressing the multilingual, multi-ethnic, multi-imperial complexity of 17th-century Taiwan into a singular ethnic story. Tainan's multiple histories began with this nine-month siege.

Zheng only stayed in Tainan for 13 months.

On June 23, 1662 (the eighth day of the fifth month in Yongli 16), Zheng Chenggong died in Chengxian Prefecture (present-day Tainan), **at the age of 38**[^9]. The cause of death is unclear, with no detailed medical records. From landing on April 30, 1661, to dying on June 23, 1662, his time in Taiwan was just over one year and two months.

Zheng Jing succeeded him, moving the Eastern Capital to Dongning (in 1664), appointing **Chen Yonghua** as an advisory general. Chen Yonghua accomplished two things that defined Tainan's shape for the next 350 years. First, in 1665 (Yongli 19), he proposed to Zheng Jing to "**build a sacred temple and establish a school**," selecting Guizai Village in Ningnan Ward of Chengxian Prefecture to build the Ancestral Temple (Kong Miao) and Minglun Hall[^4]. The official description of Tainan Kong Miao states: "The Tainan Confucius Temple was established in Yongli 19 (1665), as a school founded on the suggestion of Chen Yonghua, an advisory general during Zheng Jing's reign."[^4] It was also noted that it "**was the only school for local scholars in the early Qing period, hence called the 'First School of All Taiwan.'**"[^4]

Second, in the same year, he reformed the salt production method, changing from "boiling to drying" (from firewood boiling to sun-drying crystallization), marking the official start of Tainan's modernization of the salt industry[^10]. This reform led to the 300-year history of Qixu and the Waban Salt Fields.

In July 1683, Shi Lang led the Qing army with over 200 warships and 20,000 soldiers to attack Taiwan. Zheng Kehai surrendered to the Qing. **The Ming-Qing era ended after 21 years**[^9].

## The Plaque of the First School Still Hangs; The Red Bricks of Billion-Year Golden City are from Fort Zeelandia

In April 1684, the Qing court established **Tainan Prefecture** in Tainan, with its seat located in Dong'an Ward and belonging to Fujian Province[^11]. The Qing established a "One Prefecture, Three Counties" system: Tainan Prefecture (seat in Tainan), Tainan County (present-day Tainan), Fengshan County (present-day Kaohsiung/Pingtung), and Zhulu County (north of Chiayi).

In 1664, Zheng Jing welcomed King Ningjing Zhu Shugui to Taiwan, establishing the Ningjing Prince's Mansion (precursor to Dàtiānhòu Temple). After Shi Lang occupied Taiwan in 1683, the Ningjing Prince committed suicide as a loyal subject. Shi Lang petitioned the Qing court to convert the prince's mansion into a Mazu temple, and the Qing court elevated Mazu to "Queen Mother"[^11]. Dàtiānhòu Temple is the earliest official Mazu temple in Taiwan and the **only one directly commissioned by the Qing court**.

In the Qing dynasty, Tainan was the "**Prefecture**" of the "One Prefecture, Two Deer, Three Wancai." It ranked first among commercial cities in Taiwan[^11]. The three suburbs (North: Suwanli, South: Jin Yongshun, Sugar: Lishengxing, established around the 1720s) renovated the Five Routes Ports to control Tainan's trade power with mainland China.

![Chihkan Tower, originally Fort Provintia built by the Dutch in response to the Guo Huaiyi Incident in 1652. After Zheng Chenggong took it in 1661, it was converted into a Chengxian Prefecture office; after the Qing period, it was rebuilt as a tower. Over 300 years, it has seen five regimes: Dutch, Ming-Qing, Qing, Japanese, and post-war, but its foundation remains the original red bricks.](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8a/Chihkan_Tower_in_Tainan_City.jpg/1280px-Chihkan_Tower_in_Tainan_City.jpg)
_Chihkan Tower. Photo: Wikimedia Commons contributor, [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Chihkan_Tower)._

The Five Routes Ports (from North to South: **Xinguangzaipo, Fotou Port, Nanxi Port, Nanhe Port, Anhai Port**) were the busiest commercial waterways of the Prefecture during the Qianlong and Jiaqing reigns[^12]. Shennong Street (originally Beishi Street) was the commercial center of the Five Routes Ports for 300 years. However, a major storm in July 1823 (Qing Daoguang 3) caused Zeng Wenxi to change course, carrying large amounts of soil and rock, leading to **large-scale land reclamation of Tainan Inner Sea within about seven days**[^12]. The last remnant of the inner sea shrank into today's Qixu Lagoon. Anping Port became connected to the land; in the late 19th century, the port suffered severe siltation, forcing ships to anchor two nautical miles from the old harbor.

The port was lost, but **the street pattern remained**. The stone roads where suburban merchants hauled goods ashore from the port—which are photographed by young intellectuals in Tainan today at Shennong Street, Hai'an Road, and Five Routes Port Cultural Park—are a testament to this. **Tainan is a city preserved by siltation.**

In 1874, the Mudan Incident occurred when Japanese forces invaded Pingtung. The Qing court appointed **Shen Baozhen** of Fujian as the minister to handle the matter. Construction began in September 1874 (Tongzhi 13) and was completed in August 1876 (Guangxu 2), resulting in the construction of **Eternal Golden City**[^13]. The Cultural Affairs Bureau of Tainan City government explicitly records: "**Construction started in September 1874, completed in August 1876**."⚠️ Some sources state "built in 1875" (counting from Guangxu Year 1), but the official record shows construction and completion in 1874 + 1876. This article uses this timeline.

Designed by French engineers Bordeaux and Ruff. **The four-cornered bastion structure is Taiwan's only surviving bastion fort**[^13]. It was equipped with five British Armstrong 18-ton muzzle-loading cannons. The main gate bears the inscription "Eternal Golden City," and the back bears "Ten Thousand Streams Grinding Stone," both from Shen Baozhen's handwriting. **Construction materials were taken from the dismantled red bricks of Fort Zeelandia**. The Dutch castle was demolished and rebuilt as a cannon fort to defend against foreign forces during the Sino-French War. In 1884, when French forces blockaded Taiwan Strait and shelled Anping Sea, the defenders of Eternal Golden City fired back, driving away the French, who **failed to land**[^13].

The inscriptions "Eternal Golden City" on the main gate and "Ten Thousand Streams Grinding Stone" on the back are both from Shen Baozhen's handwriting, and some construction materials were taken from the dismantled red bricks of Fort Zeelandia.](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8f/Eternal_Golden_Castle_Tainan.jpg/1280px-Eternal_Golden_Castle_Tainan.jpg)

The Sino-French War ended in 1885. The Qing court declared **Taiwan a province**, appointing a Governor of Taiwan. The provincial capital was finally established in Taipei. The old Tainan Prefecture was renamed "Tainan Prefecture," and it **lost its status as the administrative center of Taiwan for 261 years**[^11].

Counting from the Dutch establishment of Fort Zeelandia in 1624, from 1624 to 1885, Tainan was the administrative center of Taiwan for **261 years**.⚠️ The calculation for 261 years: 38 years of the Dutch (1624-1662) + 21 years of Ming-Qing resistance (1662-1683) + 201 years of early Qing rule (1684-1885). From this year, the "capital" status was taken by Taipei. But what remained from those 261 years did not leave: the Kong Miao, Dàtiānhòu Temple, Chihkan Tower, Anping Fort, Eternal Golden City, and the street pattern of Five Routes Ports—**Tainan became a city that "lost its capital status but kept its entire structure."**

## The Three Gunshots in MinSheng Green Park Roundabout

It was noon.

On March 13, 1947, Lawyer **Tang Dezhang** was tied up and paraded through the streets for public viewing. The location was the MinSheng Green Park roundabout in downtown Tainan, which was originally a square in front of the police station during the Japanese colonial period. _Juzhen Taiwan_'s verbatim record states: "**On March 13, 1947, Tang Dezhang was executed by Chinese troops in the center roundabout of Tainan City.**"[^5]

After the outbreak of the 228 Incident, Tang Dezhang was appointed as the head of the Security Group of the Tainan branch of the 228 Incident Handling Committee to maintain order and protect citizens[^14]. On March 11, the National Army's reorganized 21st Division entered Tainan for suppression, and two or three special police officers raided his home and arrested him. _Juzhen Taiwan_'s verbatim account: "**In order to protect related individuals, he refused to disclose any names even after various interrogations.**"[^5]

The execution took place at noon on March 13. _Juzhen Taiwan_ records: "**At noon on March 13, Tang Dezhang was tied up and paraded through the streets for public viewing, walking toward the execution ground with a calm demeanor. He ended his life with a shocking cry of 'Long Live the Taiwanese!', followed by three gunshots.**"[^5]

Tang Dezhang was born on January 6, 1907; his father was from Udu City, Kumamoto Prefecture, Japan, and his mother was from Nanhua, Tainan[^14]. He audited law school at Tokyo Imperial University, passed the "Judicial Department High Civil Servant Examination" in September 1941, and opened a law office in Nanmen Town, Tainan, in 1943. **He was only 40 years old when he died.**

> **✦** "He ended his life with a shocking cry of 'Long Live the Taiwanese!', followed by three gunshots." (_Juzhen Taiwan_, _Tang Dezhang Executed by Chinese Troops at MinSheng Green Park Roundabout on March 13, 1947_[^5])

In 1998, the Tainan City government renamed MinSheng Green Park to "**Tang Dezhang Memorial Park**"[^14]. In 2014, Lai Ching-te, then Mayor of Tainan, declared March 13 every year as "Tainan City Day of Justice and Courage."[^14]

⚠️ This roundabout holds a timeline compressed by history: the Qing dynasty streets after Shen Baozhen renovated Tainan in 1875; the police square during the Japanese colonial period in the 1900s; the 228 execution site where Tang Dezhang was shot in 1947; the renaming in 1998; and the designation as a memorial day in 2014. **A roundabout holding memories of five eras layered on the same asphalt.**

## Liu Yongfu's Inland Migration, Barclay Brings Japanese Troops into the City from Xiaonanmen

On April 17, 1895, the Treaty of Mastung was signed, ceding Taiwan to Japan. Local gentry in Tainan installed **Liu Yongfu** as the second President of the Black Flag Army and moved the capital to Tainan to continue resisting[^15]. Liu Yongfu established "Duhu Post" and issued "Duhu Stamps" (July 31, 1895).

On October 20, 1895, seeing the tide turn, Liu Yongfu crossed over from Xiamen on a British merchant ship[^15]. On October 21, local gentry Chen Xiufu and Wu Daoyuan requested intervention from the British missionary **Thomas Barclay** (1849-1935) and Pastor Song Zhongjian to negotiate with Nogi Kichiten of the Japanese Second Division. The Japanese army entered Tainan City through Xiaonanmen, marking the formal end of the Republic of Taiwan.

Barclay was a Scottish missionary who arrived in Taiwan on June 5, 1875. In 1877, he founded **Tainan Seminary** (precursor to the missionary training class). On July 12, 1885, he established the _Taiwan Prefecture Church Gazette_, **the first church newspaper in East Asia**[^15]. Late on October 20, 1895, local gentry pleaded with him to help Tainan **peacefully surrender**, thereby **avoiding a massacre**. Tainan was one of the few cities not massacred during the 1895 campaign, thanks to a British pastor who had been preaching here for 20 years.

Tainan's position during the Japanese colonial period declined. In 1920 (Taisho 9), administrative reforms established Tainan Prefecture, with its capital in Tainan City. From the 1930s onward, while Tainan City remained Taiwan's second-largest city (after Taipei), it gradually faded after **Kaohsiung Port rose**[^16]. After Kaohsiung merged surrounding towns in the 1940s, its population surpassed Tainan City, and **Tainan fell to third place**.

But the Japanese era left behind something that is still operating today: **the Jianan Great Canal**.

It was completed on April 10, 1930, led by the Japanese engineer **Hatta Yoichi**, over a period of 10 years (1920-1930)[^17]. _Juzhen Taiwan_'s record states: "**On April 10, 1930, the Jianan Great Canal, which was the largest in Asia in terms of scale and technology at the time, was finally put into use.**"[^17] The total irrigation canal is 10,000 km long, with a drainage channel of 6,000 km. It irrigated **150,000 hectares** of farmland in the Jianan plain, benefiting **600,000 farmers**. Wushan Head Reservoir (Guantian District, Tainan): when completed, it had a dam height of 56 meters and a storage capacity of 150 million tons, making it the **largest in Asia and the third largest in the world at the time**[^17].

From the "boiling to drying" reform by Chen Yonghua in 1665, through the relocation of the Saidong salt fields to Beimen Jingzai foot in 1818, the completion of the Jianan Great Canal in 1930, the start of Qixu salt fields in 1971, and the announcement of the last mechanized harvest in May 2002, **Taiwan's 337-year history of salt production reached its conclusion in May 2002**[^17]. Today, Qixu Salt Mountain is a tourist attraction; visitors climb the salt mounds to take pictures, and this white mountain under their feet holds memories dating back to the 17th century.

## Yanshui Firecrackers, Tainan Delicacies, and Five Routes Ports Young Intellectuals

In 1885 (Qing Guangxu Year 11), an epidemic swept through the Yanshui region.

The residents invited Guan Shengdi Jun from Wumiao to tour the area, and believers set off firecrackers along the way to drive away the plague gods. _Tainan Travel Network_'s verbatim record states: "**During the persistent epidemic in Yanshui, residents prayed for protection from Guan Shengdi Jun at Wumiao. They later decided to invite the divine sedan chair to tour from the 13th to the 15th day of the first lunar month, and believers set off firecrackers along the road to scare away the plague gods; after being purified by sulfur smoke, the epidemic was indeed removed.**"[^18]

This tradition continues to this day (about 140 years). It was listed in the Ministry of Transportation's tourism calendar in 2015. "**Northern Sky Lanterns and Southern Firecrackers**" are among Taiwan's two major New Year festivals, alongside Pingxi lanterns, and have been selected as one of the "Top Ten Global Festivals."[^18]⚠️ Dispute over origin year: some sources say "early Guangxu period" (from 1875), but Wikipedia and official travel networks use "Guangxu Year 11 (1885)," which this article adopts.

Yanshui also has another identity: **Yuejin Port**. It was the fourth commercial port in the early Qing era's "One Prefecture, Two Deer, Three Wancai, Four Yuejin." Located on the windward side of the inner sea, it got its name because the river channel resembled a crescent moon. Like Tainan Inner Sea, the windward inner sea later became the plains of Yanshui Township, Xijia Township, and Jiali Township due to river siltation and land reclamation. **The fourth largest port in the Qing dynasty became one district of Tainan City after the 2010 merger.**

The origin of Tainan's local delicacies is also tied to the late Qing period.

**Du Xiaoyue Danzai Noodles** originated in 1895 (Qing Guangxu Year 20). The fisherman Hong Yutou sold noodles on a shoulder pole in front of the Water仙 Palace during the fishing off-season ("Xiaoyue"), naming it "Du Xiaoyue" to mean getting through the lean season, and it is a family recipe involving seasoned meat[^20]. 1895 was the same year as the Treaty of Mastung signing, Taiwan's cession to Japan, and Liu Yongfu moving the capital to Tainan to resist. It was also in this year that Hong Yutou sold noodles on his shoulder pole in Tainan Prefecture City. The original Du Xiaoyue shop is still in Zhongzheng Road, Tainan today.

**Beef Soup** represents Tainan's morning market culture: using tender beef from local cattle (now slaughtered) and pouring hot broth over it to maintain tenderness. Many beef soup shops around Guanhua Street and Yongle Market are busy from dawn until morning. **Coffin Board** was invented in the 1940s by Xu Liuyi near Chihkan Tower, Tainan. It is made by frying thick slices of white toast into a box shape, filled with cream white sauce and seafood, and named for its coffin-like appearance—**Taiwan's only local delicacy with Western flavors**[^20].

> **💡 Did You Know:** The four places that young intellectuals in Tainan love to visit today—**Yongle Market, Guanhua Street, Shennong Street, and Hai'an Road**—are all located on the street pattern of the silted Five Routes Ports from the 18th and 19th centuries. After the Five Routes Ports were abandoned due to siltation in 1823, **the port was lost, but the streets remained**. Three centuries later, Tainan residents drink coffee and sell cultural products on the stone roads where suburban merchants once hauled goods from ships. The reason "Old House Revitalization" is particularly established in Tainan is that the old city's fabric has not been completely demolished by redevelopment. Siltation led to economic stagnation and population outflow in the late 19th century, but looking back at the 21st century, **that siltation became a cultural asset for Tainan**. Failure turned into preservation; this is the rhythm of Tainan's history.

## From Three Birds in Blood to 6,988

On December 28, 2009, **Tainan National Park** was established. The official website of Tainan National Park states: "**Established on December 28, 2009, it is the eighth national park in Taiwan and the first urban national park in Taiwan.**"[^21]

It covers 4,905 hectares of land and a total area of 39,310 hectares (land and sea). It includes 2 international wetlands (**Zengwenxi Mouth, Sicao**) + 2 national wetlands (**Qixu Salt Fields, Yanshui Creek Mouth**). It is **Taiwan's only wetland national park**[^21].

But the true significance of this park lies not on the map, but in the gunshots at the end of 1992.

At the end of 1992, there were only about 200 global **Black-faced Spoonbills**. An illegal shooting incident occurred in Qixu. Three birds lay dead in a pool of blood; two were found deceased, and one died shortly after being sent to Taipei Zoo[^22]. This became a front-page story in Taiwan and attracted international conservation attention.

In 2002, about 300 hectares on the north bank of Zengwenxi Mouth were officially designated as the "Black-faced Spoonbill Protection Area." On December 25, 2009, the Qixu Black-faced Spoonbill Protection Area was incorporated into Tainan National Park[^22]. **From those three black-faced spoonbills lying in blood to a global census total of 6,988 in January 2024**. The global population increased by 30 times in 32 years. The Zengwenxi Mouth wetlands in Qixu are a core wintering ground for Black-faced Spoonbills globally, with the number visiting Taiwan each year exceeding half the global total[^22].

Milkfish is another Tainan symbol spanning 400 years. During the Dutch era (approx. 1624–1662), milkfish farming technology was introduced from Indonesia to Taiwan, with **Lukaimen (Anping) being the first experimental site**[^23]. Lukaimen, where Zheng Chenggong landed, was also where the Dutch introduced milkfish. Today's scale: Tainan City has about 4,980 hectares of milkfish farming area and an annual production of about 24,000 tons, **ranking first in Taiwan**[^23]. In the 1980s, fisherman Lin Lietang broke through breeding technology, allowing milkfish to reproduce naturally, earning him the title "Father of Milkfish."

## 37 Districts, 1.5 Trillion NTD: Southern Science Park Surpasses Hsinchu

On December 25, 2010, **Tainan County merged and was upgraded to a municipality**[^24].

The Tainan City government states: "**On December 25, 2010 (Year 99 of the Republic), Tainan County merged to become 'Tainan City,' covering 37 districts, with Lai Ching-te as the first mayor.**"[^24] The original 6 districts of Tainan City (Zhongxi, Dong, Nan, Bei, Anping, Annan) + all 31 townships and towns of Tainan County were reformed into districts: Yongkang, Xinying, Yanshui, Baihe, Liuying, Houbi, Dongshan, Mazu, Xiaying, Liujia, Guantian, Danei, Jiali, Xijia, Xigang, Qixu, Jiangjun, Beimen, Xinhua, Shanhua, Xinshi, Anping, Shanshan, Yujing, Nanxi, Nanhua, Zuozhen, Rende, Guiren, Guanmiao, Longqi[^24].

The 37 districts span a geographical range of about 50 kilometers, from the coastal wetlands (Qixu) to the residual mountains of Alishan (Nanhua). The area is 2,191.6531 square kilometers, and the population is approximately 1.85 million (about 1,849,762 in April 2026)[^24].

After the merger, **the gap between urban and rural areas widened**. Yongkang District (235,000) + Annan District (205,000) vs. **Longqi District with only over 3,000 residents**[^25]. North of Zengwenxi Creek (Xibei): townships like Xinying, Yanshui, and Jiali have experienced long-term population outflow. South of Zengwenxi Creek (Xinai): about three-quarters of the population lives here, with districts like Yongkang growing every year. **One merger packed three worlds—Yanshui, Jiali, and Yujing—into one ballot.**

Then there is Southern Science Park.

**Southern Science Industry Park** was established in 1996 in Xinsi District, Tainan. **TSMC's Fab 18** is TSMC's most advanced ultra-large wafer fab in Taiwan, producing **3nm processes**, with a total investment of about 700 billion NTD[^6]. In 2023, the annual revenue of Southern Science Park reached **1.5855 trillion NTD**, surpassing Hsinchu's 1.42 trillion NTD by 165.5 billion NTD. **Southern Science Park took over the title of top-revenue technology park in Taiwan from Hsinchu**[^6].

> **📝 Curator's Note:** From Chen Yonghua building the Kong Miao in 1665, to Hatta Yoichi completing the Jianan Great Canal in 1930, to TSMC Fab 18 producing 3nm chips in 2023, **what Tainan has done has always been "the largest on Taiwan."** The Kong Miao in 1665 was the **only** school for local scholars in the early Qing period; the Wushan Head Reservoir in 1930 was the **largest in Asia and third in the world** at the time; Southern Science Park in 2023 is the **top-revenue technology park in Taiwan**. This is not a coincidence. Tainan has the Jianan plain, one of Taiwan's largest plains (4,550 km²), the population and infrastructure base left by its 261 years as an old capital, and the multi-lingual trading city DNA cultivated during the 38 years of Dutch rule. When this land needed to transition from agriculture to industry, and from industry to technology, it had **enough physical space and historical depth** to accommodate each upgrade. Therefore, "the three layers: 261 Years as Capital, 400 Years of Antiquity, and 21st Century Chips" are the stories of three generations nurtured on the same land: the Dutch, the Qing suburban merchants, and the TSMC engineers—all doing vastly different things in the middle section of the Jianan plain, but using the same water (Jianan Great Canal), the same land (Tainan Plain), and the periphery of the same port (Anping Port).

**National Sun Yat-sen University** is also part of this timeline. On January 15, 1931, the "**Tainan College of Technology**" (established by the Taiwan Governor's Office) was founded, offering Mechanical Engineering, Electrical Engineering, and Applied Chemistry[^26]. In 1956, it was reorganized into **National Sun Yat-sen University of Taiwan Province**, named after Zheng Chenggong to commemorate his contribution to opening Taiwan. It became National Sun Yat-sen University in 1971. Today, located in the East District of Tainan City, its College of Engineering, School of Architecture, and Medical School are top-tier in Taiwan. **From the College of Technology in 1931 to the 1.5855 trillion NTD revenue of Southern Science Park in 2023**, Sun Yat-sen University embodies this evolution from industry to technology.

## Xiraya Reclaims Its Name After 389 Years

On October 17, 2025, the Legislative Yuan passed the **Act on the Status of Indigenous Peoples of Taiwan**[^27].

Returning to the starting point in time. In 1636, Dutch missionary Candidius used Roman script to transcribe Xiraya language in Xinguang Village[^8], leaving the first written record in Taiwanese history. However, after the 17th century, the Xiraya people began Sinicization, and the inhabitants of the **four major communities** (Xinguang, Xiao, Mazu, Mujialuan) gradually became classified as "Pinhu" peoples, not among the 16 indigenous groups recognized by the Republic of China.

On October 28, 2022, the Constitutional Court of the ROC declared the current _Indigenous Peoples Act_ unconstitutional in the case concerning the Xiraya name, requiring amendments within three years[^27]. On October 17, 2025, the Legislative Yuan passed the _Act on the Status of Indigenous Peoples of Taiwan_. On October 23, 2025, President Lai Ching-te officially promulgated it. The Xiraya people of Tainan City struggled to restore their indigenous status for **25 years**, and after winning the constitutional review in 2022, they officially obtained the legal status of "**Pinhu Indigenous Peoples**" this October[^27].**From Candidius establishing a school in Xinguang in 1636 to the Legislative Yuan passing the name restoration law in 2025, the Xiraya spent 389 years reclaiming their name.**

The year 2024 marks 400 years of Tainan's establishment.

The Tainan City government promotes "**Tainan 400**," with the core spirit: "**Together Tainan - Global Exchange**"[^28]. It seeks to host three national-level events—the Taiwan Lantern Festival (host), Taiwan Cultural Expo, and Taiwan Design Exhibition—in Tainan in the same year.

"**Jiaopèi**" (_tsiâu-puê_) is a Minnan word meaning interaction and mutual respect. In Tainan's 400-year history, the **Dutch, Xiraya, Han, Japanese, Overseas Chinese, and New Immigrants**—the "**Global Exchange**"—have always been part of Tainan's DNA[^28].⚠️ Mayor Huang Weizhe explicitly stated in 2024 that framing "Tainan 400" is to avoid being constrained by historical nomenclature disputes ("Is 1624 the founding year of Tainan?"), offering an **inclusive interpretation of 400 years of diverse history**.

Returning to the beach on February 9, 1662.

2,000 Dutchmen stood on the sand in front of Fort Zeelandia, waiting for 8 ships to take them away. Coyett handed the keys to Zheng officials. **The 38-year Dutch era ended; the 21-year Ming-Qing resistance began; the 201-year early Qing capital followed; 50 years of Japanese rule and 80 years of ROC, continuing to this day.**

If you visit Tainan next time, don't just tour Chihkan Tower and eat beef soup. Try going to Anping Fort at 7 AM, standing before the red brick wall of the ruins of Fort Zeelandia. This wall was completed in 1634 using glutinous rice paste, sugar syrup, sand, and oyster shell powder. Then go to Eternal Golden City and look at the red bricks beneath the inscription "Eternal Golden City" on the main gate. Some of these were **removed from this Fort Zeelandia** and used by Shen Baozhen when building the cannon fort in 1875. Then go before the plaque "First School of All Taiwan" at the Kong Miao Main Hall, a school suggested by Chen Yonghua to Zheng Jing in 1665. At noon, visit Tang Dezhang Memorial Park; that roundabout is where Tang Dezhang was shot on March 13, 1947. In the afternoon, go to Shennong Street for a cup of coffee. The spot you sit on was once a stone road where suburban merchants hauled sugar and rice from ships. In the evening, head to Qixu Salt Fields or Tainan National Park to see Black-faced Spoonbills, **from 200 birds to 6,988**. Then return to Yongle Market at night to eat a bowl of Danzai Noodles and a bowl of Beef Soup.

That taste—from the bowl Hong Yutou sold in front of the Water仙 Palace in 1895, to the bowl queued for in Guanhua Street in 2026—**has never changed its recipe.**

Tainan is the location where Taiwan was first colonized, where the Han regime was established first, where a prefecture was set up first, where schooling began first, where salt production started first, where cannon forts were built first, where irrigation was completed first, and where local delicacies originated first; it is also home to the largest technology park today. **The three layers—261 Years as Capital, 400 Years of Antiquity, and 21st Century Chips—are a microcosm of all of Taiwan's history on one piece of land.**

Mayor Huang Weizhe uses an inclusive interpretation to avoid being constrained by historical nomenclature disputes.
