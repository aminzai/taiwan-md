# Where Chou: The Female Voice Behind 25 Years of "The Promise," A Long Run from Cartoon Doll to Taipei Arena

> **30-Second Overview:** On August 26, 1999, Fu-ma Music released a peculiar "greatest hits" album: the cover featured a faceless cartoon doll named Hui-er; the music video was animated; the singer herself was 22 years old and did not appear on camera[^1]. The album's lead single, "The Promise," sold 250,000 copies in 45 days[^2] and over a million across Asia[^3]. That unseen female voice sang this cover of Faye Wong’s song into the karaoke charts of every Taiwanese KTV for the next 25 years[^4]. From five albums in five years at Fu-ma to being tricked by her manager into signing a transfer deal with BMG, where she was shelved for three years, from joining Qunshi in 2009 and Hanyan in 2013 to returning to Qunshi in 2025 to produce the Disco-style album _Where X Walk_, she has released 12 official studio albums[^11]. Twenty-five years and one year later, on April 25, 2026, she stood on the Taipei Arena stage for the first time. After performing a setlist of 25 songs over three hours, she told the audience at the end: "I don't know if this will be the last time I sing for everyone"[^5]. The doctor told her her vocal cords had atrophied.

Walk into any KTV in Taiwan, select "The Promise," and the moment the intro plays, someone in the booth will hum along, "Two distant worlds, growing ever farther apart"[^6]. Ask again: "Who sang this?" The answers split: some say Faye Wong, some say Fish Leong, some can't say, but all insist, "I definitely heard it."

That is Where Chou’s situation. Her songs walk ahead of her, far ahead of her, for 25 years.

## The Female Voice Hidden by the Cartoon Doll

In 1996, Where Chou[^7], a graduate of the Drama Department at Kaohsiung Private Chung-Hwa Arts School, did not originally intend to be a singer. She took her saved pocket money into a recording studio, recorded 10 songs as a graduation memento, and originally planned to go to the UK to study stage direction. She later said, "Like I said, going to the UK to study was not to become an actor; I wanted to be a director"[^8]. Her attitude toward the record contract was simply, "Let's experience it first; at worst, I'll go back to school if it fails."

The studio owner took that graduation demo to musician Chi Chung-ping. Chi signed Where to Fu-ma Music, releasing her first album, _Where Chou's Favorites_[^9], on August 26, 1999.

Calling the first album a "Greatest Hits" was strange in itself: compilations are usually released years after debut. Fu-ma reversed this strategy, using it as a promotional hook, with almost every one of the 10 songs becoming a chart hit[^9]. Even stranger was the cover: a cartoon doll, "Hui-er," replaced her photo. The MV was also animated; Where Chou’s face was not shown to the public at all.

This decision stemmed from Where Chou’s own lack of confidence in her appearance[^10]. Fu-ma’s response was to abstract her completely, drawing her as a cartoon and focusing entirely on her voice. Fu-ma internally called this strategy "capturing the audience with voice first"[^9].

The strategy worked. "The Promise" began to ferment in KTVs in the second half of 1999. In 2000, the second album, _Where Chou's Favorites 2: I Really Want to Love You_, sold over 250,000 copies in 45 days[^2]. The first album cumulatively reached 500,000 in Taiwan and over a million in Asia[^3]. In an era when people still queued at record stores, this volume was enough to push a 22-year-old newcomer into mainstream charts.

> **📝 Curator's Note**
> Behind the four characters "capturing the audience with voice first" lies a reverse logic of packaging Taiwanese female singers. In the late 1990s, Taiwan's idol industry was beginning to mass-produce female singers who were "visual-first," such as Jolin Tsai, Vanessa Chien, and Stefanie Sun, all of whom had strong visual debuts. Fu-ma chose the opposite direction, hiding Where Chou’s face and betting on her voice. They won the bet, but this decision also defined her situation for the next 25 years: the audience remembers the song, but struggles to remember the person.

In 2001, _Cherish This Night_ was released with a real-person cover for the first time[^11], but sales declined, triggering negative reviews. The contrast between the cartoon doll and the real person reminded people of an impolite truth: for some listeners, they loved that voice, but might not accept that face.

## The One Song with a 25-Year Karaoke Rate That Didn't Drop

To understand Where Chou’s position in Taiwan’s music history, one must first clarify the lineage of "The Promise."

![Faye Wong's Cantonese version of "The Promise" from her 1997 EP *Toy*, lyrics by Lin Xi, music by Chen Hsiao-hsia, winner of the Commercial Radio HK叱咤903 Professional Recommendation Song Award](/article-images/music/wang-fei-yueding-1997.webp)
_Faye Wong's 1997 EP *Toy* Cantonese version of "The Promise," lyrics by Lin Xi, music by Chen Hsiao-hsia. Fair use editorial commentary on the original work. [Source via YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gjC00DJPeLs)._

The original version is by Faye Wong. In 1997, she included "The Promise" in her EP _Toy_, in Cantonese, with lyrics by Lin Xi and music by Chen Hsiao-hsia[^12]. This version won the Commercial Radio HK叱咤903 Professional Recommendation Song Award in Hong Kong; Lin Xi won the Best Chinese Pop Lyrics Award at the 20th Top Ten Chinese Golden Songs for these lyrics. It was already a great song in the Cantonese circle.

Two years later, Fu-ma producers heard Faye Wong’s version and felt this song could be done again. They asked Yao Jue-long to rewrite the Mandarin lyrics, kept Chen Hsiao-hsia’s melody, handed the arrangement to Chen Fei-wu, and had Where Chou interpret it[^13]. In August 1999, the Mandarin version entered _Where Chou's Favorites_.

This was a re-creation, not simple搬运 (transport). Lin Xi’s original version was introverted and suppressed; the line "separated in the north and south" wrote of a more mature sense of distance. Yao Jue-long’s Mandarin version was directly lyrical; "Two distant worlds, growing ever farther apart" struck the hearts of those in their 20s experiencing romantic setbacks[^14]. Chen Hsiao-hsia’s melody remained unchanged, but the center of gravity of the lyrics shifted. The whole song changed from a suppressed story to one of youthful regret.

More unexpectedly, the Mandarin version’s spread exceeded the Cantonese original. In Taiwanese KTV booths, radio stations, and talent shows, every generation of fans in the next 25 years ordered the same "The Promise," and they ordered Where Chou’s version, not Faye Wong’s[^4].

> **💡 Did You Know**
> From the late 1990s to the early 2000s was the golden age of Taiwan’s KTV culture. When the two chain systems, Gin House and Goodie House, had their highest density on Taipei streets, one had to queue for a number even on weekday afternoons; the karaoke rate was the most immediate indicator for record companies to confirm a song had "really gone viral." "The Promise" entered during this period, catching the tail end of the KTV industry, and thus, after the end of the physical record era, continued to be rediscovered by new generations of listeners through karaoke machines.

Chen Hsiao-hsia herself has a preference for this song. She is one of the most important female composers in Taiwan in the 1980s and 1990s, having written many representative works such as Chang Ching-fang’s "If Flowers Fall from Branches," Su Rui’s "Same Moonlight," and Hsiao Chih-chi’s "Realization"[^15]. This melody was first given to Faye Wong, then to Where Chou. She acknowledges both versions, but the most widely circulated is Where Chou’s.

## Five Years at Fu-ma: Five Albums and the Exit of the Cartoon Doll

If one only looks at "The Promise," Where Chou’s Fu-ma period would be reduced to "one cover song made her famous for life." But between 1999 and 2003, she actually released five albums at Fu-ma, each with its own story[^11].

After _Where Chou's Favorites_ in August 1999, 2000’s _Where Chou's Favorites 2: I Really Want to Love You_ continued the "Greatest Hits" naming strategy. The lead single of the same name, "I Really Want to Love You," broke 250,000 copies in 45 days[^2]. The track on the adjacent album, "Don't Want You to Know," was another dark thread: lyrics by Yao Chien, music by Chen Wen-hua and Zhou Bing-xing[^36], speaking of the sense of distance of being misunderstood yet unwilling to speak. This song was not as explosive as "The Promise," but in the batch of KTV hits from 1999-2000, it survived quietly and steadily.

In 2001, _Cherish This Night_ used a real-person photo for the cover for the first time, but sales did not meet expectations[^11]. In 2002, she released _The One Who Loves Me, The One I Love_. The songs "Body Temperature" (Lyrics by Wang Yu-tsung / Music by Huang Pin-guan / Arranged by Wu Qing-long[^37]) and "Lonely City" (Lyrics by Yang Li-de / Music by Ye Liang-chun / Arranged by Chung Hsing-min[^38]) became the two most complete samples of her vocal characteristics. "Body Temperature" had rounded articulation; the chorus did not scream forcefully, singing "missing" softly. "Lonely City" put the loneliness of the urban night into string arrangements, a standard template for Taipei female voices in the early 2000s.

This song proved she was not just a recipient of covers; the same voice could carry a theme song in an original position. But it did not cross the threshold of KTV karaoke rates, slowly sinking into the tracks of adjacent albums.

2003’s _Hui-er Out of Print_ was the finale of the five-year Fu-ma period; the name itself implied an exit. The cartoon doll Hui-er officially retired from Where Chou’s covers after this[^11]. "Lonely City" had already been steadily on KTV machines for a year.

Looking back at these five years, Where Chou’s position at Fu-ma is clear: she was not a heavenly queen-level explosive artist, but she was a female singer who steadily made money for the company, relying on song selection, covers, and that non-aggressive yet gripping voice, holding up the tail end of the physical record era.

> **📝 Curator's Note**
> Taiwan’s record industry from 1999-2003 was at the last peak before the physical CD sales curve declined. When Where Chou debuted in 1999, it was still an era where "selling 500,000 copies for one album was common." By 2003, when digital downloads began eroding the market, Fu-ma’s female singer roster had already adjusted. Where Chou’s ability to release five albums in these five years, with at least one KTV hit per album, is evidence of commercial success. But because the drama of the next phase (2004-2007 shelving) was too strong, the density of these five years was covered up. Only by looking at the complete discography does one realize: she is not a "one-song singer"; she is a singer who "releases one album a year, with an anchor in every song," but the halo of "The Promise" was so large it overshadowed all other songs.

## A Blank Paper Signed Away Three Years

The next part is the most dramatic and awkward part of Where Chou’s story, one she rarely talks about. But some fragments have been repeatedly cited in multiple media outlets and are already publicly verifiable facts.

When she signed with Fu-ma in 1999, Where Chou and Chi Chung-ping simultaneously signed a 5-year management contract. When Fu-ma’s 5-year contract ended in 2004, Chi Chung-ping handed her a piece of paper to sign, telling her, "It's just a simple consent form for going to the mainland to perform"[^16].

That was not a consent form. It was a "Power of Attorney for Handling Matters," authorizing Chi Chung-ping to transfer her contract from Fu-ma to BMG (later merged into Sony BMG). After signing, Chi Chung-ping took the advance royalties and production fees from the new contract; multiple media reported the figure as 24 million New Taiwan Dollars[^16], and took the money away.

BMG signed the artist, but could not get money to continue investing, so they shelved Where Chou. From 2004 to 2007, she could not officially release an album for three full years. She later said in an interview that at her most difficult, her monthly income dropped to just over 1,000 NT dollars[^16].

Around 2007, her father passed away[^17]. BMG finally released the last album, _Bloom_, before the contract expired. The three-year contract ended this way, and she once again faced a situation of having no agency.

> **⚠️ Controversial Viewpoint**
> The narrative of "signing a blank paper contract" is not uncommon in Taiwan’s artist contract history; similar plots emerged successively from the 90s to the 00s. But because contract disputes often end in private settlements and lawsuits are often sealed by confidentiality agreements, the versions the public gets are always from the parties involved / interviews / secondary retellings. Where Chou chose not to sue and not to publicly tear off the face. In a 2024 interview, she said, "I believe no one has a smooth sailing life, because life is inherently imperfect"[^18]. Placed in a news headline, this sounds like motivational chicken soup; but placed in the real situation of 2007, when her monthly income was a thousand dollars, it is a quiet decision.

She was originally placed in the "Four Little Heavenly Queens" list, alongside Jolin Tsai, Stefanie Sun, and Vanessa Chien, with sales supporting this frame[^21]. During the 2004 shelving period, the media replaced her with Fish Leong, and "The Four Little Heavenly Queens" no longer included Where Chou[^21]. In a 2022 interview, she said, "I always regarded these three female singers as my goals"[^22], referring to Tsai, Chien, and Sun, not including herself.

## From Qunshi to Hanyan: The Slow Singer Who Releases One Album Every Few Years

In 2009, after BMG’s three-year contract expired, Where Chou joined Qunshi International, releasing the self-titled album _Where Chou_, her first since coming out of shelving[^19]. In the next two years, she released _Her Own Room_ in 2011, accumulating two albums during the Qunshi period[^11]. Songs like "Body Temperature," "Favorite," and "Not Loving Is Also a Kind of Love," which she would repeatedly sing at the Taipei Arena, were mostly accumulated during this period. A singer just coming out of shelving pieced herself back together slowly, at a pace of one album every two years.

In 2013, she transferred to Hanyan International, holding the "First Promise" tour and releasing _The World I See_[^20]. The title track "The World I See" became a permanent fixture in her concerts after 2013, still on the setlist for the 2026 Taipei Arena concert.

From age 30, Where Chou became another version of a professional singer: no newcomer halo, no heavenly queen position, a singer who releases one album every few years, holds one tour every few years, and walks slowly. The media rarely treats her as a hot topic, but she has new works every two or three years. She didn’t disappear; she was just slow.

In May 2020, HBO Asia’s drama _People at Work_ aired. She played Pei Pei, a domestic violence victim who could sing on a construction site[^40]. This was an actor position she actively stepped into from her singer identity. In the drama, there was a scene of her singing on a construction site, a very rare singer × drama crossover moment. In November of the same year, she held the "Strolling Under the Moonlight" concert in Taipei, which sold out and added a show[^31]. In 2022, she released _Unforgettable Times 2_, the second installment of her cover tribute project, which will be discussed separately[^25].

## Covers Are Also Re-creation

The label "cover singer" followed Where Chou for 25 years.

Media easily writes her as "made famous for life by one cover song." In fact, her entire discography is a mix of originals and covers. "Substitute," "Body Temperature," "Don't Want You to Know," "Lonely City," and "The World I See" are her originals[^23]; what remembers the most people is still "The Promise." She did not fight this label; instead, she chose to respond to it head-on.

In 2018, she released _Unforgettable Times_ at Hanyan, paying tribute to 1960s-1980s Taiwanese female singers, re-singing representative works by Teresa Teng, Fei Fei, Yao Su-rong, Ouyang Feifei, Chen Ni, Chang Li-min, Huang Ying-ying, Chen Chiu-hsia, Tsai Chin, and Su Rui, ten predecessors[^24]. She said herself that this was the "most difficult work" she had considered since debut. The challenge was not in singing the songs, but in how to reinterpret a song already defined by predecessors with the ears of 2018 without losing respect. Teresa Teng’s "The Moon Represents My Heart," Tsai Chin’s "Unforgettable Times," Su Rui’s "Same Moonlight"—each carries its original era’s background and the original singer’s tone. Doing a cover project is only imitation if it is just mimicry; it equals doing nothing.

In 2022, she did _Unforgettable Times 2_, continuing the cover as an independent curation project[^25]. After completing two installments, she effectively circled back, defining herself in the position of "cover singer," this time more as an active choice. From 1999, when she was pushed to the KTV charts by a Faye Wong cover, to 2018/2022, when she actively made covers into a tribute project, her relationship with "covers" changed from passive acceptance to active curation. In a TVBS interview that same year, a reporter asked if she was afraid of being treated as a "cover singer," she replied:

> "I need to thank that video about the 'Four Little Heavenly Queens' made before, which gave me the inspiration to bravely try challenging this cover medley in concerts."[^22]

The subtext of this sentence is that she is not afraid. She accepted this label and turned it into her working method.

> **📝 Curator's Note**
> Common online narratives downgrade "covers" to a second-class position below originality, but this hierarchy is a product of the commercialization of the Mandopop industry after the 1980s. In the diva era of the 1950s-70s, Teresa Teng, Fei Fei, and Yao Su-rong could all include the same "When Will You Return" in their respective albums; no one thought it was a problem. At that time, the "song" was a public good; the competition among "singers" was in interpretation, not ownership. Where Chou doing the two installments of _Unforgettable Times_ is equivalent to taking back that era’s interpretation logic and doing it again: the same song, a new era has new interpreters, which conflicts with nothing about the original. Treating covers as the opposite of creation is itself an outdated question.

## Where X Walk: A 49-Year-Old’s Disco Crossover

In December 2025, Where Chou left Hanyan and returned to Qunshi International, the agency that gave her the opportunity to come out of shelving in 2009, releasing her 12th official studio album, _Where X Walk_[^26].

This is essentially an album of Where Chou’s identity transformation: she served as producer for the first time, collaborating with Wang Zhao-quan of the icyball band to lead the entire production, changing from a produced singer to a singer who decides how she is heard. The album includes new works like "Walk," "Emotional Creature," "I Sing Alone," "Misunderstanding Song," and "Not Loving Is Also a Kind of Love." The lead single "Walk" was arranged in a Disco style by Zhao Quan, the leader of icyball. For listeners familiar with Where Chou’s past 26 years of "sad female voice" position, this was an obvious style reset: she wanted to walk out of the KTV ballad karaoke box and try a version that had not yet been defined.

> **💡 Did You Know**
> A 49-year-old female singer crossing genres and serving as producer for the first time is not rare in the Mandopop scene, but placed in the frame of "Where Chou," defined by "The Promise" for 26 years, it is a structural choice. From the 1999 cartoon doll hiding the face, to the 2018 cover tribute project reconciling with the label, to the 2025 self-production and crossing into Disco style, her relationship with "how she is packaged" has蜕 (transformed) every seven years.

## The Cross-Strait Main Stage Without Taking Sides

After the 2010s, there was a common route question for medium-sized Taiwanese singers: whether to go to the Chinese market? Where Chou’s answer was vague but concrete: she went, but she never left.

In 2014, she served as a mentor on Zhejiang TV’s _The Voice of China_ Season 3[^27]. In 2015, she participated in the mainland version of _The Hidden Singer_[^28]. On September 18, 2016, she appeared on stage wearing a "Santa Claus's Reindeer Who Isn't at Home" mask on the first broadcast of Jiangsu TV’s _Masked Singer Season 1_; in the third episode, she was guessed; in the annual gala of the same season, she sang "Wind and Rain of Longing" with Jacky Cheung[^29]. In 2024, she appeared on China’s _Time Music Concert_[^30].

These appearances brought her voice to different audiences. But unlike many peers in the same period, she did not move to Beijing or Shanghai to develop; she never left the Taiwan main stage. In November 2020, she held the "Strolling Under the Moonlight" concert in Taipei, which sold out and added a show[^31]; in September 2022, after one postponement, she made up for it at the Kaohsiung Music Center[^31]; in February 2024, she performed with Xiao Chong, Chien Wen-bin, and the National Symphony Orchestra at the Weiwuying Opera Theater for _Grand Playhouse 3: Cross-Music Weiwuying Concert_[^32]; in May of the same year, she held the _25_ 25th Anniversary Debut Concert at the Taipei Music Center[^33]. Her concert venue distribution, from medium-sized venues before the Taipei Arena (Taipei International Convention Center, Legacy, Kaohsiung Music Center), to the 2024 cross-music concert at Weiwuying Opera Theater, to the 2026 first attack on the Taipei Arena, is a clear Taiwan music venue ladder, every step of which is in Taiwan.

So the frame "she went to develop in China" is inaccurate. She worked in China, but her work field and life center did not overlap. This is actually not a rare position in Taiwan’s pop music scene from the 2010s-2020s; many singers of the same generation adopted similar "main stage in Taiwan, appearances cross-Strait" working methods, but because it is not dramatic, it is rarely written as the main narrative. Media prefers the binary frames of "going to China to develop" or "sticking to the local," because these two versions are easier to write; but the actual ecology is that most singers live in the gray area between these two poles, and Where Chou is one such sample.

## The Saw on April 25 at the Taipei Arena

Saturday night, April 25, 2026, Taipei Arena.

This was Where Chou’s first Taipei Arena concert in her 27 years of debut[^5]. The concert was named _I Really Want to Love Where Chou_. The stage design was given to Golden Melody/Golden Horse veteran designer Li Shi-qi, adopting a kaleidoscope concept. The whole show was 3 hours, 25 songs[^34]. Tickets went on sale on January 22 at noon and sold out quickly, adding a show.

For the first nine songs, Where Chou barely spoke. She rose, moved around, and sang continuously in coordination with stage mechanisms. After the ninth song, she finally spoke to the audience:

> "Saying hello to everyone at the Taipei Arena, I have been practicing this sentence for 26 years!"[^34]

> "Singing nine songs before speaking, plus moving around various mechanisms and lifts, transforming into a singing and dancing singer, it’s really tiring!"[^34]

> "This counts as Where Chou’s moon landing!"[^34]

She sang "I Really Want to Love You," "Meeting Too Early," "Topic," "Curved Moonlight," "I Like," "Lonely City," "Passerby," "Don't Cry," "Don't Want You to Know," "I Want Your Love," "Body Temperature," "The World I See," ending with "The Promise"[^34]. The entire setlist compressed 26 years of career into 3 hours—the representative works of the four record companies (Fu-ma period, Qunshi first time, Hanyan period, Qunshi second time) appeared together on one night.

Then, before the encore, she read a letter written to her fans. In the last paragraph of the letter, she said that at the end of last month, on the eve of her birthday, she caught a severe cold. Afterward, she lost her voice; medical examination revealed her vocal cords had atrophied[^5]. She described her vocal cords now as "like a saw"[^5]. This Taipei Arena concert was supported by medication recommended by the doctor.

> "I don't know if this will be the last time I sing for everyone, but as long as I recover, I will continue to sing."[^5]
>
> "I hope we can meet again next time, okay?"[^5]

Placed in the mouth of a 49-year-old, standing on the Taipei Arena for the first time, having just sung 25 of her own songs, the weight is completely different.

## Two Versions of the Same Song

The end of the 1999 _Where Chou's Favorites_ was "The Promise." At that time, the album cover was a cartoon doll, the singer did not show her face, and 22-year-old Where Chou was still trying to see if she could become a singer.

The 2026 Taipei Arena encore was still "The Promise." This time the cover was her own face, 49-year-old Where Chou standing for the first time in the center of the stage she had practiced for 26 years, telling the audience this might be the last time.

Between them lay 26 years, 12 official studio albums, million sales, contract disputes, shelving, comeback, tribute albums, cross-music Weiwuying, self-production, first attack on the Taipei Arena, and a "cover singer" label that always followed her. Her response to this label was to turn it into her working method over 25 years.

When contemporary Taiwan listeners next walk into a KTV, when someone in the booth selects "The Promise," the intro plays, and they hum the first line, remember that this song has two versions existing simultaneously: Faye Wong’s 1997 Cantonese original, and the 1999 Mandarin version with lyrics rewritten by Yao Jue-long, music by Chen Hsiao-hsia, arrangement by Chen Fei-wu, and interpretation by Where Chou. It could hold on in the KTV machine for 25 years without dropping from the charts because there was a voice that continuously existed for it.
