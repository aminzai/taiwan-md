# Sunset Rollercoaster: The 14 Years from a Photo Booth Background to Coachella, Singing Taipei Sun in English

> **30-Second Summary:** Sunset Rollercoaster is an independent band formed in Taipei in 2009. They did something few others in the Taiwanese indie scene did: they sang about subtropical Taipei entirely in English. After their debut album _Bossa Nova_ was released in 2011, the band disbanded. In 2015, lead singer Kuo-kuo reformed the group after being discharged from military service due to -1300 myopia and completing his duties as a guitarist for the Algae band Zhang Hsien. The track 〈My Jinji〉 from the 2016 EP _JinJi Kikko_ accumulated over 100 million streams on Spotify[^1]; they became the first Taiwanese band to perform on Audiotree Live in the United States in 2018; and in 2023, they became the first Taiwanese band invited to play on the main stage at Coachella in over two decades[^2]. In 2024, their collaboration _AAA_ with the Korean band Hyukoh was nominated for nine awards at the 36th Golden Melody Awards, winning three. A casually taken Photo Booth background picture became the light of Taiwanese music that the world heard most after 14 years.

In Taipei in 2010, a 19-year-old student sat in front of a MacBook. He had just created a MySpace band page and needed a profile picture. Not knowing what to use, he opened the built-in Photo Booth on his Mac. There was one background image preset: a silhouette of a roller coaster against a sunset. He chose that one.

"The rollercoaster came about like this," Kuo-kuo (Tseng Kuo-hung) said in an interview eight years later[^3].

That casually chosen background image later became everything for the band: their name, the cover of their first mini EP, and the atmosphere of their entire musical universe. It was not calculated to represent anything, but 14 years later, it represented something no one could have predicted back then. The path of Taiwanese music reaching the world did not come from the K-pop industrial system, nor from government cultural diplomacy; the starting point of the whole thing was a 19-year-old boy randomly selecting an image in his MacBook's Photo Booth.

## The Pre-set in the Photo Booth

Sunset Rollercoaster did not appear out of nowhere.

Before forming Sunset Rollercoaster, Guoguo had been in several bands. The earliest was Acid Lips (Sour Lips), a post-punk band from high school. Later, there was "F.L.A.T CLUB" [^4], Boyz & Girl, and "Come On! BeiBei!" which recorded an album around the same time. At that time, the drummer for Come On! BeiBei! was Huang Shi-wei, nicknamed "Birdman," and one of the members was Chen Hongli, who later joined Sunset Rollercoaster as a bassist [^5].

In 2009, Guoguo formed Sunset Rollercoaster with Kevin Lee (Li Bo-wei, then a student at Taipei Medical University) and drummer Luo Zunlong. Their first performance was on January 29th at Underworld in Shida Road [^6]. That dingy little bar, which was later redeveloped, was a landmark of the 2000s indie scene in Taipei.

The band name was decided afterward. Guoguo's version in an interview:

> "I didn't know what to use for a profile picture on MySpace, so I just used one from a pre-set in my Apple Photo Booth called Sunset Rollercoaster... that's how it came to be." [^3]

This anti-industrial origin story later defined the band's atmosphere over 14 years. There was no brand consultant, no naming meeting, and no SEO consideration. A default photo from a Photo Booth eventually became the backdrop text on the Coachella desert stage.

> 📝 **Curator's Note**: The common narrative of "indie going international" tends to attribute every turning point to "the band making the right strategic decision." But Sunset Rollercoaster's story is actually the opposite: all key moments were unplanned, spontaneous choices. That photo from the Photo Booth was just a casual snapshot in a dorm room; the choice to use English was something Guoguo himself said felt "less awkward"; and Coachella was an invitation sent by the organizers themselves. This band from Taiwan, which is heard around the world, feels the least like it was meticulously designed.

## Abbey Road Master, Then Gone

On September 22, 2011, Sunset Rollercoaster self-released their first album, _Bossa Nova_[^7].

The album is named after Bossa Nova, but the music itself is closer to a mix of lo-fi surf rock and post-punk. The recording was done at Rooftop Audio in Taipei, and the master tapes were sent to Abbey Road Studios in London, where Steve Rooke personally handled them[^8]. This first album, cobbled together by a group of university students, went through its final processing stage in the same studio that recorded _Abbey Road_ by The Beatles.

Then they disbanded.

Fans thought it was just an unsuccessful small band from Taipei that disappeared after releasing one album. In late 2012, Guoguo joined Algae, a band featuring An Pu as a guitarist (replacing the original member), and toured for four years[^9]. The drummer, Luo Zonlong, went on to form a dark industrial side project called "Fossil" with Guoguo. The band's official website was not updated for four years.

"We wanted to try something else," Guoguo later said in an interview with Bandcamp Daily[^10].

This four-year silence became the most significant contrast to the later "Coachella myth." The story of Sunset Rollercoaster is not a motivational example of an "indie band making it big from scratch"; rather, it is about "a group of people doing completely different things for four years and then returning having truly matured."

## 1300 Diopter Vision Notification

One afternoon in 2015, Kuo-guo received a military draft notice.

He was supposed to enlist, but one month before induction, the Ministry of National Defense sent him a discharge notice because of his -1300 diopter myopia. His fate—to shave his head and wear a uniform in less than a month—was decided by an eye exam form.

In 2015, Kuo-guo was 24 years old. He had just finished touring with the band Algae, and Sunset Rollercoaster had disbanded four years prior; he did not have a steady job. The stage experience accumulated over four years performing alongside An Pu, combined with old friends from his alma mater's theater scene who played synthesizers, saxophones, and clarinets, suddenly made Kuo-guo realize he could bring these threads together.

"I decided to take the band seriously," he recalled later[^11].

On July 1, 2015, Sunset Rollercoaster held its first reunion show at The Wall in Taipei[^12]. Out of the original trio (Kuo-guo, Kevin, Zunlong), only two remained. Kevin did not return, and Chao-ren also did not return. New members included Chen Hongli (bass + synthesizer) and Wang Shaoxuan (keyboard), who were former bandmates from "Come On! BeiBei!", and Huang Haoting (saxophone). With a five-person lineup, the addition of brass instruments and synthesizers gave the band a funky soft rock texture that was unique in the Taiwanese indie scene.

That year, they began making the three songs that would later become _Jinji Kikko_.

> 💡 **Did you know**: During his four years as a guitarist in Algae, Kuo-guo performed on over 100 stages with An Pu (Chang Hsien). After Sunset Rollercoaster's reunion, An Pu and Kuo-guo have never officially collaborated on any songs. Both are iconic indie creators from Taiwan who were once colleagues in the same band but have since taken completely different paths.

## Jinji is Not Kumquat

On March 1, 2016, the EP [《JinJi Kikko》](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d1REzQ75COs) was released[^13]. It features three songs: 〈Burgundy Red〉, 〈My Jinji〉, and 〈New Drug〉.

〈My Jinji〉 became the most popular first. Starting in 2017, Spotify's algorithms began placing this song in Discover Weekly and Indonesian and Korean Indie/City Pop playlists. Within a year, the song surpassed Taiwan[^14] for monthly listeners in Indonesia. NME later reported that Jakarta, Indonesia, was the city with the most global monthly listeners on Spotify for Sunset Rollercoaster[^15].

_The band's official channel video for 〈My Jinji〉, released in 2020, was a retrospective. The song had already gained popularity through Spotify algorithms for four years before the visual counterpart was "revealed" to global audiences. A lazy afternoon, blown open by a saxophone, represents the two syllables that this band has been heard around the world for over 14 years._

Many people assumed the word "Jinji" meant "kumquat." The band never explained it. However, according to an interview with Guoguo (An Pu), the sound is similar to "Dindi" in the classic Portuguese Bossa Nova song 〈Dindi〉, which means "dear" or "baby"[^16]. The atmosphere of the three songs has a strange resonance with 1960s Brazilian jazz by Antonio Carlos Jobim, yet it is rooted in 2010s Taipei.

The first major overseas performance after the EP's release was SummerStage Taiwanese Waves[^17] in Central Park, New York, on July 16, 2016. Sunset Rollercoaster performed alongside An Pu and Wang Fu. This marked the first time a Taiwanese band had systematically appeared at SummerStage in Central Park with three groups performing together.

Then, international fans began sending messages.

"I feel like Taipei has a somewhat flat feeling; everyone is similar... the whole city offers a slightly comfortable feeling but one that can never break through its predicament," Guoguo told reporter Jiang Yiting[^18]. Yet, this "flat" city sung in English was heard by audiences worldwide as a Taipei that they thought was flat but was actually complex.

> 📝 **Curator's Note**: Why choose English? Guoguo’s answer is counterintuitive to common assumptions. "When you are a band who sings in English, the Western music audience already has the least friction absorbing it. The third thing is that if it is English, your clientele is the whole world."[^19] This statement resembles a calm calculation by a producer regarding the base of the audience, which is completely different from an ambition to "make international music." Most in the Taiwanese indie scene choose Tâi-gí (Taiwanese), Mandarin, Hakka, or indigenous languages; each language carries an answer about "who I am." Sunset Rollercoaster's answer was: I want to be heard by the whole world, and English is the greatest common denominator. This calculation cannot be placed in the same drawer as the Anglicization of the K-pop industrial system.

## The Five Tracks on Audiotree

Chicago, September 26, 2018.

Sunset Rollercoaster entered the recording studio at Audiotree Live and recorded a full session of five songs: 〈Greedy〉, [〈Summum Bonum〉](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wNp7WJusiHQ), 〈New Drug〉, 〈My Jinji〉, and 〈I Know You Know I Love You〉. It went live on October 18[^20]. Audiotree is one of the most important in-studio session platforms in the American independent music scene, selecting a few bands each year to record full sessions. Sunset Rollercoaster became the first Taiwanese band invited.

The recording format at Audiotree was a test. A small room, minimal post-production, and live takes uploaded directly—every pitch deviation, pause, or timing issue was visible. The aesthetic stance of this setup was: show me you can do this in the room, not the layered version from a studio. What Sunset Rollercoaster delivered that day was 40 minutes where synthesizers, saxophones, bass, and drums were all breathing together in the same space. The live saxophone solo on 〈Summum Bonum〉 was looser than the studio version, and the synthesizer tone of 〈New Drug〉 carried the subtle reverb inherent to the recording room. After the session went online, it accumulated over 1.1 million views[^20], marking the first large-scale growth in Sunset Rollercoaster's international fanbase.

_The full session on the Audiotree official channel: a small recording room in Chicago, an afternoon in the autumn of 2018. When the saxophone played 〈My Jinji〉, Taiwanese indie music was established live for the first time before microphones in the American Midwest. Before this day, it was data pushed to headphones by Spotify algorithms; from this day on, it became people breathing in the same room._

In March of the same year, they released their second full album, _Cassa Nova_ (Half-Ripe Prince)[^21]. The 9 songs shifted completely from the lo-fi surf rock of _Bossa Nova_ to a disco, funk, and synthesizer-dominated City Pop. The mood of the entire album was like a subtropical afternoon in Japan's Tatsuro Yamashita or Mioko Ōtsuka in the 70s, but with a late-2010s vaporwave coating on the synthesizers.

Throughout 2018, they toured over 100 times, from Tokyo, Seoul, Shanghai, and Hong Kong to Jakarta, Los Angeles, New York, and Mexico City[^22].

One show was held in a mall parking lot in Jakarta, Indonesia. The audience crowded the stage until it shook, leading to an early finish. This performance was later documented in a reporter's long article as the moment subtropical City Pop was truly rocked by its subtropical audience for the first time[^23].

BIOS Monthly gave them a description that year: "Wandering Overseas to Earn Money" (飄洋過海來拼酒)[^24]. The band members were clear about their situation during interviews: the Chinese-speaking market was too small to support an all-English band, forcing them abroad to find audiences; and international touring costs were so high that most independent bands hesitated. Sunset Rollercoaster carved out its own path within these two constraints—connecting directly with overseas promoters whenever possible, not relying on Taiwanese agencies as intermediaries, keeping stage setups as simple as possible, and only bringing necessary synthesizers in their luggage. The four characters "Wandering Overseas" (飄洋過海) were later frequently cited in the Asian indie scene to describe a band turning "lack of industrial backing" into a viable business model: geographical movement was merely the surface; the real core was the redefinition of commercial rhythm.

## Sonora Stage in the Desert

On July 27, 2019, they performed on the Red Marquee stage at Fuji Rock Festival in Japan[^25]. This is one of Asia's largest rock festivals, and the Red Marquee is the second-largest stage.

On October 30, 2020, they released their third album, _Soft Storm_[^26]. During a tour in Los Angeles in 2019, the band met an idol from his youth—Ned Doheny, a session musician from the 1970s in the United States who had written for Eagles and Linda Ronstadt. The band invited Ned to come to Taipei on the spot. Ned did come, collaborating with Sunset Rollercoaster on [〈Overlove〉](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GnJOxq0flo4). Then, COVID-19 broke out, halting subsequent recording plans in Los Angeles[^27].

After _Soft Storm_ was released online, NME ranked it as the fourth best Asian album of 2020. Daniel Peters commented:

> "It's a left-turn by a band fully confident in their strengths, looking inward to focus on craft and instinct."[^28]
>
> (A left turn by a band who is completely confident in their strengths, turning inward to focus on craft and instinct.)

On August 21, 2021, they won the Best Band award at the 32nd Golden Melody Awards with _Soft Storm_. The judges commented: "Breaking the boundaries of Chinese-language music aesthetics, not only are they technically proficient but also influence many young bands; they have international appeal and a sense of the future."[^29]

Two years later, on January 11, 2023, Coachella announced its full lineup for 2023. Sunset Rollercoaster's name appeared on the Sonora stage. The headline from Blow Street Voice that day read:

> "For the first time in over twenty years of Coachella, a Taiwanese band is invited to perform on a major stage."[^30]

On April 15, 2023, at 7:55 PM Pacific Daylight Time, at the Empire Polo Club in Indio, California, Sunset Rollercoaster performed on the Sonora stage[^31]. The 9-song setlist began with Shostakovich Waltz No. 2 (a tribute to _The Eye of the Needle_), followed by 〈Vanilla Villa〉, 〈Burgundy Red〉, 〈My Jinji〉, 〈Summum Bonum〉, [〈Jellyfish〉](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RLULD4LEN-I), 〈I'm a Fish〉, and concluded with 〈Candlelight〉.

_The band's official channel video, 〈Vanilla Villa〉Official Video (2019), was the lead single from the EP of the same name and was the first song after the Shostakovich overture on the Coachella 2023 Sonora stage. The MV features a coating aesthetic reminiscent of 70s Japanese sci-fi films, but the narrative is about "aliens falling in love with humans." This album was selected as NME's fourth best Asian album in 2020 and was appreciated by five thousand American fans in the California desert three years later._

"Hello, we are Sunset Rollercoaster from Taiwan, and we are happy to be here tonight," began the band[^32].

The Sonora stage is one of Coachella's smaller indoor stages, with a capacity of about 5,000 people, but that day, the crowd was packed outside the tent. Most of the European and American fans were generations who discovered 〈My Jinji〉 through Spotify Discover Weekly; they were seeing the person who sang this song for the first time—and that person was Asian, specifically Taiwanese, not Japanese or Korean. A few scattered audience members held up black T-shirts with "SUNSET ROLLERCOASTER" written on them, and several Taiwanese students studying abroad drove across states to see the performance. The guidebook of Taipei, the desert of California, and subtropical City Pop—three lines that shouldn't have intersected stacked into an afternoon inside the Sonora tent.

A week later, on April 22, during Coachella's second weekend, they performed on the Sonora stage again with a 10-song setlist[^33].

"I feel like there are no bands in Taiwan right now who are as eager to please foreign audiences and seek global recognition like us, so I wanted to test how far we could go," the band described their ambition in an interview with VERSE magazine[^34].

> ⚠️ **Controversial Viewpoint**: The claim that this was "the first Taiwanese band at Coachella for 33 years" was spread by some Chinese-language media copying Taiwan News in 2023, but it is logically impossible: Coachella started in 1999 and was only in its 24th year in 2023. The Blow Street Voice version stating "the first invited in over twenty years" is the correct description. This distortion, where sensational numbers are amplified, is a typical pattern seen after Taiwanese bands gained large-scale visibility in the English-speaking world, with feelings of pride overriding factual verification.

## Becoming Your Own Light

In 2017, the South Korean band Hyukoh toured Taipei for the first time. The lead singer Oh Hyuk was recommended by "The Black Skirts" backstage to see Sunset Rollercoaster perform. After watching, Oh Hyuk recognized _Guo Guo_ [^35]. The two frontmen connected on Instagram, and _Guo Guo_ took Oh Hyuk for a walk around Taipei.

Three years later, on October 5, 2020, Sunset Rollercoaster quietly released the single 〈Candlelight〉, featuring OHHYUK [^41]. This song, over four minutes long, had _Guo Guo_'s saxophone part intentionally left incomplete halfway through, allowing Oh Hyuk to finish the second half in Korean. There was no press conference and no announcement of an international collaboration; it was simply the two frontmen putting each other's voices into a song for the first time after being friends on Instagram for three years. 〈Candlelight〉 later became the closing track at the Coachella 2023 Sonora stage—something written in DMs three years prior, becoming the final song listened to by five thousand people in the desert three years later.

The official band channel, 〈Candlelight feat. OHHYUK〉Official Video, was uploaded on October 5, 2020. The version where Oh Hyuk continued the second half of Sunset Rollercoaster's song in Korean became the first seed for the entire album _AAA_, four years before the official collaborative album in 2024.

Over the next four years, this seed slowly grew into a complete album. From 2023 to 2024, _Guo Guo_ and Oh Hyuk met monthly between Jiaxing, Seoul, and Jeju Island to write songs. They wrote eight songs in one year. Without agency-arranged meetings, without cooperation memorandums, and without a preset single schedule, the two frontmen lived in each other's city studios for a week, setting up instruments, drinking, talking about their dreams from the previous night, and then writing a song—much like musicians in the 1970s. _Guo Guo_ later described this process in an interview with Interview Magazine: "We were both lazy, lazily enough to do this because being too diligent would have ruined it." This anti-industrial pace of collaboration is almost a violation of physical laws within the industrial systems of K-pop and Mandarin pop music.

On July 10, 2024, _AAA_ was released, the collaborative album by Sunset Rollercoaster × Hyukoh [^36]. It features eight songs: 〈Kite War〉, 〈Y〉, 〈Antenna〉, 〈Glue〉, 〈Young Man〉, 〈Do Nothing〉, 〈Aaaannnnteeeeennnaaaaaa〉, and 〈2F Young People>. The entire album did not follow any existing "international collaboration" formula (there was no Taiwanese-Korean duet or bilingual splicing); rather, it was a bridge built by two Asian indie bands between City Pop and alt-rock where none previously existed.

_HYUKOH's official channel track "Antenna," which is one of the songs on 《AAA》. The two frontmen from Taipei and Seoul trade vocals in this song; there is no duet or translated subtitles, they simply take turns performing. This arrangement itself is a direct demonstration that "Asian indie can stand on its own without relying on European or American translations."_

Jian Miao-ru wrote something at Mingren Hall in 2018, which is now more accurately used to describe 《AAA》: "After digesting the influence of a large amount of Anglo-American rock music... it is both retro and avant-garde. The gentle and smooth vocal style is still very Asian."[^37]

On June 28, 2025, the 36th Golden Melody Awards ceremony was held at the Taipei Arena. 《AAA》 was nominated for nine categories, including Song of the Year, Best Band, Best Album Producer, Best Vocal Album, and Best Music Video. They ultimately won three: Best Music Video, Best Vocal Album, and Best Design Award[^38]. Having a cross-national band's work recognized in the core categories of Taiwan's most credible Chinese music award mechanism—Best Album and Best Producer—is something that has not happened since the Golden Melody Awards began in 1990.

After Sunset Rollercoaster's performance at Coachella, a reporter from VERSE asked Kuoguo one question: Do you think you are "a light of Taiwan" now?

> "I don't want to carry the title of 'a light of Taiwan'; I just want to do my own thing and be my own light; that is enough."[^39]

On August 8, 2025, they unexpectedly released their fifth full album, 《QUIT QUIETLY 悄悄消消》[^40]. With 11 songs, it accumulated over five years since 《Soft Storm》, featuring cross-genre collaborations including Wu He, Dou Jingtong, the Brauryang Band, and Yuuma Abe, lead singer of Never Young Beach.

> ✦ No one in a small bar on Shidao Road in Taipei in 2010 predicted that a silhouette of a roller coaster and a sunset in a Mac's Photo Booth preset would become text on the desert stage backdrop at Coachella 14 years later. No one in the afternoon Kuoguo received his discharge notice in 2015 predicted that a boy with 1300-degree nearsightedness who almost couldn't be drafted, would eventually sing about a hot Taipei afternoon entirely in English, packing an island into two syllables that the world hears most: JinJi.
>
> The sunset of Taipei sung entirely in English, instead made Taiwan the most heard by the world.
>
> That light, from a random Photo Booth background photo, shone onto the desert stage.
