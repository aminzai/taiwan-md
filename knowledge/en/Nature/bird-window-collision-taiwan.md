> **30 Second Overview**
>
> **Bird-Window Collision** refers to the phenomenon where birds are injured or killed after high-speed impacts because they cannot recognize transparent or reflective glass. In Taiwan, window collision is one of the primary causes of non-natural death for wild birds, with victims ranging from common urban birds to rare raptors.

- **Scale of Casualties**: It is estimated that a large number of wild birds die annually in Taiwan due to window collisions. The top five species affected are: **Five-colored Parakeet (17.67%)**, **Green Pigeon (9.59%)**, **Sparrow (5.34%)**, **Spotted Dove (5.29%)**, and **Eurasian Sparrowhawk**.
- **Core Causes**: The **transparency** of glass (mistaken for passability) and its **reflectivity** (mistaken for sky or forest), as well as the disorientation caused by night **light pollution** to migratory birds.
- **Prevention Key**: Implementing the "**5x10 Rule**" (horizontal spacing within 5cm, vertical spacing within 10cm) using collision-prevention decals, grids, or etched glass.
- **Policy Progress**: The Institute of Architecture, Ministry of the Interior, plans to officially incorporate "bird-friendly collision prevention design" into green building evaluation indicators in **2026**.

## Why It Matters

As Taiwan's urbanization increases, large areas of curtain walls and mirrored stainless steel materials have become mainstream in construction, yet they also serve as deadly traps for birds. Window collisions not only threaten common birds but also frequently affect second-level protected species such as the **Eurasian Sparrowhawk**. This issue reflects the conflict between **urban development and ecological conservation**. Promoting "bird-friendly architecture" is not just about protecting biodiversity; it is also an important indicator of Taiwan's progress toward becoming a sustainable city and implementing **SDG 15 (Life on Land)**.

## Scientific Principles and Misconceptions of Window Collisions

The visual structure of birds differs from that of humans; their eyes are located on the sides of their heads to achieve a wide field of view, but they lack deep perception of flat transparent surfaces.

1.  **Transparency**: When both sides of a building feature transparent glass (such as in corridors, corners, or transparent soundproof walls), birds mistakenly believe they can pass through the structure to reach the other side.
2.  **Reflectivity**: Glass reflects surrounding greenery, trees, or the sky, leading birds to mistake it for habitat and fly into it at high speed.
3.  **Night Light Pollution**: Many migratory birds navigate at night using starlight. Intense urban lighting can cause these migrants to lose their bearings and subsequently collide with illuminated buildings.
4.  **Common Misconceptions**: Placing a "hawk sticker" in the center of a window is often ineffective. Birds treat the static sticker as an obstacle they attempt to fly around, but they still strike the transparent area nearby.

## Analysis of Window Collision Status in Taiwan

Window collision research in Taiwan mainly relies on **citizen science** reports, revealing significant seasonal and species characteristics.

### Seasonal Peaks

- **Spring and Autumn**: Migratory seasons for birds; migrants unfamiliar with the terrain are prone to casualties.
- **Summer (June–August)**: Fledgling periods when young birds practice flying; due to insufficient flight experience and low environmental alertness, they become high-risk groups for window collisions.

### Characteristics of Affected Species

- **Five-colored Parakeet**: Due to its strong territorial nature, it often flies at low altitudes in forests and attacks its own reflection in the glass, making it one of Taiwan's most frequently affected species.
- **Raptors**: Such as the Eurasian Sparrowhawk, often collide with glass while rapidly pursuing prey (such as Sparrows or Doves).

### Hotspot Case: National Taiwan University Campus

Records from the window collision research group at National Taiwan University (NTU) show over 300 cases within the campus in the last five years. Ironically, some buildings that have received green building certifications or architectural awards have become window collision hotspots precisely because of their large-scale glass designs.

## Prevention Measures and Friendly Design

The core of effective prevention is "disrupting the continuous reflective surface of the glass" so that birds can perceive an obstacle.

### The 5x10 Rule

Research indicates that the spacing of anti-collision patterns must be smaller than the bird's wingspan. It is recommended that horizontal spacing does not exceed 5 centimeters, and vertical spacing does not exceed 10 centimeters.

### Existing Improvement Solutions

- **Anti-Collision Window Decals**: Must be applied to the **exterior** of the glass to be effective (application on the interior still causes reflection).
- **External Fixtures**: Installing grids, blinds, insect screens, or ropes (such as Acopian BirdSavers).

### Architectural Design Stage

Using sandblasted glass, etched glass, or special glass with UV reflective coatings (which birds can see but humans cannot).

## Taiwan Regulations and Policy Progress

Taiwan is gradually integrating window collision prevention into systematic management:

1.  **Green Building Indicator Updates**: The Institute of Architecture, Ministry of the Interior, has drafted incorporating "bird-friendly collision prevention design" into green building evaluation indicators, scheduled for announcement in **2026**.
2.  **Local Government Actions**:
    - **New Taipei City**: Built the first bird-proof window green building in Taiwan (Ruifang Animal Home) in 2025.
    - **Tainan City**: The city council comprehensively replaced windows with bird-friendly anti-collision decals and established a window collision reporting network.
    - **Taipei City**: The Animal Protection Agency launched an environmental education program using bird window collision picture books.

## Citizen Participation and Reporting Platforms

1.  **Roadkill Observation Network**: Taiwan's largest reporting platform, assisting in the creation of window collision hotspot maps.
2.  **Taiwan Raptor Research Society**: Maintains a "Window Collision Museum," providing professional consultation and educational resources for prevention.
3.  **FB Group "Bird Window Collision Reporting"**: A community-driven space for real-time reporting and discussion.
