> **30-Second Overview**
>
> Sun Link Sea is located at No. 6, Xishan Road, Daan Village, Zhushan Township, Nantou County. Environmental education certification data lists the park elevation at approximately 1,600–1,800 meters, extending along both banks of Jiazouliao Creek. Its most memorable feature is Songlongyan Waterfall, but the deeper question is: why did a valley that once lived by logging — after road construction, tourism operations, typhoons, and the 921 earthquake — put forest, ecology, and nature education back at the center?[^1] [^2] [^3]

## Moisture Arrives First, Then Sun Link Sea Begins to Speak

Entering the mountains from Zhushan, Nantou, the urban scale gradually recedes behind the road. Sun Link Sea Forest Ecology Resort sits in Daan Village, Zhushan Township, stretching along both banks of Jiazouliao Creek. Official sources describe this as Taiwan's mid-elevation cloud forest belt. Mist, streams, slopes, and forest are not four separate attractions but interdependent conditions within a single valley.[^1] [^2]

The park's current forest composition also preserves traces left by both humans and forest. Certification records note the original vegetation was mixed coniferous-broadleaf forest, once containing Taiwan red cypress and Taiwan yellow cypress. The dominant vegetation now is Japanese cedar plantation from artificial regeneration, along with moso bamboo stands and Taiwan rhododendron stands. To imagine Sun Link Sea only as a "natural cedar forest" without history is to miss the most important question here: how the forest was used, and how it came to be re-understood after use.[^1]

> **📝 Curator's Note**
>
> Sun Link Sea's nature is not a fingerprint-free landscape photo. Japanese cedar, logging roads, stream revetments, and the nature education center all tell us: humans never stood outside the forest looking in; humans have always left choices inside the forest.

## The Name "Creek Bottom" Still Carries the Shoulder-Pole Carriers

The park's official cultural history page states: "Sun Link Sea was originally named Xidi (Creek Bottom)." (Source: Sun Link Sea Forest Ecology Resort official cultural history page) Early settlers walked five to six hours from Xitou to reach Xidi, the path paved with stones or logs. Later, to transport forest products, horse trails, crude plank roads, and half-bridges appeared in the mountains; human labor or water buffalo pulled log sleds, then timber was sent to Liulongtou and transferred via steel cable to Xitou.[^4]

The weight of this history lies not only in the four words "once logged." It points to a very concrete form of mountain labor: carrying heavy loads, climbing dangerous slopes, moving forest products out on one uneven road after another. When today's visitors walk on gentle boardwalks, the sense of security underfoot forms a contrast with the transport roads of the past.[^4]

The official chronology records that after Taiwan's retrocession, precious timber gradually diminished, and logging ceased in 1963 (ROC year 52). In 1969 (ROC year 58), the Xitou-Alishan traverse route was discovered, and mountain paths gradually became routes for hikers crossing the mountains. The same mountain area successively bore the functions of extraction, transport, mountaineering, and tourism.[^4]

### From Logging Camp to the Road That Brought Visitors

In 1973 (ROC year 62), Liu An-ding and others organized the Sun Link Sea Recreation Co., Ltd. In 1975 (ROC year 64), the park was approved to lease NTU Experimental Forest land and build the connecting highway from Xitou to Sun Link Sea. The park's chronology treats this project as a precedent for privately built public roads. In 1979 (ROC year 68), the Xitou-Alishan Highway project began; in 1980 (ROC year 69), the approximately 17 km road from Xitou to Sun Link Sea was completed.[^4]

In February 1979 (ROC year 68), the park chronology records the first guest staying at Sun Link Sea. On 29 March 1980 (ROC year 69), the first tour bus entered the park. These dates should be understood as records in the park's public historical chronology, cited with clear attribution to their source nature, rather than expanding corporate self-narration into an undisputed full account.[^4]

1. **1963 (ROC 52):** Official chronology records logging cessation.[^4]
2. **1969 (ROC 58):** Xitou-Alishan traverse discovered; mountain roads take on hiking function.[^4]
3. **1973 (ROC 62):** Sun Link Sea Recreation Co., Ltd. organized.[^4]
4. **1980 (ROC 69):** Xitou–Sun Link Sea road completed; park records first tour bus entry.[^4]
5. **1999 (ROC 88):** 921 earthquake severs sole access road; park closes.[^1] [^4]
6. **2003 (ROC 92):** Anding Tunnel opens; park chronology records reopening 1 September.[^4]
7. **2010–2012 (ROC 99–101):** Park renamed, nature education center established, environmental education facility certification obtained.[^1] [^4]

## Disaster Did Not Return the Valley to Zero

Sun Link Sea's turning point was not a smooth brand upgrade but began with road severance. Certification records state the 1999 921 earthquake cut the access road; subsequent impacts from Typhoons Herb and Toraji kept the park closed from 1999 until reopening in 2003. The park's historical chronology records emergency road clearance in 2001 (ROC 90) and Anding Tunnel completion in 2003 (ROC 92); the reconstruction period was not a blank where nothing happened.[^1] [^4]

The park chronology also records that in 1996 (ROC 85), Typhoon Herb brought over 2,000 mm of rain in one day; flash floods struck vehicles, boats, and the access road. This is the park's public chronology narrative; this article does not extend it into a rainfall record for all of Nantou. In Sun Link Sea's story, it mainly illustrates how roads, slopes, and hydrological risk directly determine whether a mountain recreation park can operate.[^4]

After reopening, following suggestions from the Forestry Bureau and the Endemic Species Research Institute (Council of Agriculture), the park abandoned mechanical amusement facilities and shifted to an ecological and natural orientation. This decision made "nature" no longer just a backdrop for facilities, but the subject the park must manage, interpret, and continuously maintain.[^1]

In 2010 (ROC 99), the park name changed from "Sun Link Sea Forest Recreation Area" to "Sun Link Sea Forest Ecology Resort." In 2011 (ROC 100), the nature education center was established. In 2012 (ROC 101), environmental education facility certification was obtained. The name change and the establishment of an educational institution mark a redefinition of the park's role.[^4]

## Songlongyan's Water Teaches People to See Drought First

Songlongyan Waterfall is approximately 3.0 km from the main building; official data lists a drop of 38 meters or more, earning it the title "Sun Link Sea's First View." The natural rock cave beside the waterfall is called Songlongyan, composed mainly of sandstone. Official sources link the cave's form to thermal expansion and contraction, weathering exfoliation, and differential erosion.[^5]

The Water Resources Agency e-newsletter further explains that the waterfall, cave, channel, and boulders are the result of long-term headward erosion, downcutting, and infiltration by stream water. What a person sees before the cave is a scenic spot; what the rock records is time slower and longer than humans.[^8]

![Songlongyan Waterfall](/article-images/nature/sun-link-sea-songlong-falls-2024.webp)

_Sun Link Sea Songlongyan Waterfall, photographed 30 September 2023; Ephemeral Days／Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0._

In May 2023, Nantou Travel reported Songlongyan Waterfall shrank during drought, then recovered after plum rains replenished the upstream forest. The report quotes the park describing the drought trickle as "peeing boy" and the post-rain surge as "flying dragon in the sky." These two on-site expressions turn the waterfall from a fixed landmark back into a stream with hydrological variation.[^6]

The same report records that during drought, negative ion measurements near the waterfall were approx. 2,000 per cm³; after plum rains, 198,000 were measured. Park scenic data describes normal levels at 20,000–50,000 per cm³, rainy season approx. 190,000. These are measurements or park descriptions for specific locations and times, not fixed standards for every visit, and cannot be used to derive medical efficacy.[^5] [^6]

> **✦** "Sun Link Sea's First View" is not a guarantee of perpetual waterfall grandeur, but a reminder: you must first ask which stretch of mountain today's water has traveled.

## Disaster Prevention Engineering Also Learning to Retain the Stream

Below Songlongyan Waterfall there was once a deep pool and flood overflow history; park data notes the original revetments and fixed-bed works also faced weathering issues. In 2021 (ROC 110), related engineering expanded the check dam flood discharge cross-section and improved structures using GRC (glass fiber reinforced concrete) artificial rock technique. The project simultaneously created fish ladders, deep pools, shallow riffles, and rapids, and installed longitudinal and lateral wildlife corridors.[^5]

The significance of this project lies not in claiming "nature is fixed once engineering completes," but in placing several usually separate issues together: visitor safety, flood passage, aquatic habitat, and animal movement. Whether it proves effective must be tested by maintenance, rainfall events, and ecological monitoring. What can be confirmed is that the design goals described in park data are no longer just a structure that blocks water.[^5]

## Qinglong Waterfall, Shijingyi, and Water's Different Speeds

Qinglong Waterfall descends 116 meters over tiered cliffs. Both the Water Resources Agency e-newsletter and park official data describe it as a thin stream in dry autumn/winter, growing thunderous after heavy rain. On clear days, mist and sunlight may form rainbows, but this depends on weather and viewing position, not guaranteed on every visit.[^7] [^8]

Shijingyi is not a waterfall from height but a pothole landform where stream water carries sediment, rotating and abrading on rock layers of differing hardness to form circular depressions. Park data notes over ten stone wells locally, some reaching 5.5 meters deep. These figures come from park scenic introductions; when near water, visitors must still follow on-site regulations and not treat scenic descriptions as permission for wading, swimming, or climbing.[^7] [^10]

Longlin Waterfall puts seasonality even further forward. Park data notes that in dry autumn/winter only dry rock walls may be seen, while in spring/summer rainy season water flows down the rock face. Sun Link Sea's waterfall group is therefore not copies of the same "spectacle": Songlongyan shows cave and water curtain; Qinglong shows drop and mountain mist; Shijingyi shows how stream water grinds the riverbed; Longlin shows seasonal water volume difference.[^7]

## One Trail Connecting Logging, Streams, and Giant Trees

The official touring routes page divides itineraries into half-day, full-day, and overnight two-day trips. The half-day hiking route can start from the service center, passing the nature education center, Yueling Ancient Trail, Qinglong Waterfall, 88 Suspension Bridge, Shijingyi, and Fude Temple before returning. Official reference times: Yueling Ancient Trail approx. 40 minutes, Shijingyi trail approx. 15 minutes, but actual times vary with stops, weather, and trail conditions.[^9]

Full-day hiking Route A moves from service center toward Qinglong Waterfall and Shijingyi, officially marked at approx. 2 hours total. Route B passes through Chuanlin Boardwalk, Flower Center, Leshan Trail, Songlongyan, Tianyan Trail, Xianmin Tunnel, Thousand-Year Red Cypress, and Tiandi Yan, officially approx. 4 hours. The former places streams, waterfalls, and potholes on one road; the latter connects flowers, waterfalls, logging relics, and giant trees.[^9]

Chuanlin Boardwalk is approx. 1,800 m long, official walking time approx. 40 minutes; it was once a logging transport plank road. Tianyan Trail is approx. 900 m, official time approx. 25 minutes, passing Xianmin Tunnel, Thousand-Year Red Cypress, and Tiandi Yan. Leshan Trail is approx. 1,200 m, official time approx. 20 minutes, connecting Flower Center and Songlongyan Waterfall. These figures are not just itinerary tools; they also let one see the different layers of mountain forest use.[^7] [^9]

## Under the Taiwan Rhododendron Forest, Season Becomes Habitat Observation

_Taiwan Rhododendron Forest Trail; image from Sun Link Sea Forest Ecology Resort official scenic page. This image is an official remote hotlink._

The Taiwan Rhododendron Forest Trail is approx. 1,700 m total; official data lists elevation approx. 1,691–1,802 m, elevation gain 103 m, walking time approx. 60 minutes. The trail includes sleeper-log sections and natural earth sections covered in fallen leaves, looping in a circle; at the fork, either side returns to the starting point.[^10]

The park introduces Taiwan rhododendron along both sides of the trail, with approx. 1,500 plants in the park, reaching up to 8 m tall. Along the trail approx. 120 plant species, representative species including crystal orchid. These figures are the park's official scenic page descriptions; this article does not extend them into Taiwan-wide population estimates.[^10]

The nature education center's activity page places crystal orchid and Taiwan rhododendron in habitat context, noting that visiting the rhododendron understory March–May may reveal leafless crystal orchid, with interpretive activities introducing its survival environment and conservation methods. The most important action here is not finding the most, but staying on the trail — no trampling, no picking, and no stepping into vegetation for photos.[^11]

The Flower Center represents another mode of viewing. Park official scenic data lists representative flowers including peony, ornamental kale, hydrangea, tulip, campanula, and mountain cherry. Human-curated display space and rhododendron understory habitat observation are both worth seeing, but cannot be understood by the same standard.[^7]

## From Timber to Nature Education

Sun Link Sea Nature Education Center's programs include outdoor activities, professional teaching, and regional guided tours. The exhibition center introduces park landscapes, trails, and mid-to-high-elevation flora and fauna resources, with an ecology library and outdoor activity grounds. Program themes cover ferns, fireflies, butterflies, astronomy, birds, water quality, and forest.[^11]

Wood culture activities transform the park's logging history into experiential courses, including sawing wood, splitting wood, dragging log sleds, tree measurement, and carbon sequestration discussion. The value of this design is that conservation need not pretend the forest was never used by humans; instead, it lets participants understand the ways of use, the costs, and the subsequent responsibilities.[^11]

Certification records state Sun Link Sea Nature Education Center obtained private enterprise nature education center certification in 2011; current certificate valid until 26 August 2027. The park's nature education center writes its direction as "Explore the Ecological Mysteries of Sun Link Sea." (Source: Sun Link Sea Nature Education Center official website)[^1] [^11]

The Water Resources Agency e-newsletter also records Sun Link Sea has volunteers and instructors leading environmental education activities, covering Songlongyan Waterfall, Golden Metasequoia Forest, Botanical Garden, and Qinglong Fern Trail. The value of guiding lies not only in adding scenic names but in pushing "what did you see" toward "why is it here."[^8]

>
> Sun Link Sea turns nature education into a continuous sequence of actions on a single trail: first see a flower, then ask about its habitat; first touch a piece of wood, then ask for whom the forest once worked.

## Before Entering the Valley, Check Today's Rules First

The official touring routes page still lists electric shuttle bus itineraries, but the same page also has an announcement that from 9 March 2026, the park shuttle bus suspends operation. The park homepage also reminds visitors to slow down and stroll the park. Therefore, one cannot rely solely on old route pages to arrange shuttles; before departure, confirm the day's service status again.[^3] [^9]

As of 15 August 2026, the official website further announces that from 1 September 2026, highway bus 6277 "Xitou–Sun Link Sea" ceases operation, and suggests alternative routes 6883B, 6883C. This is date-sensitive transport information, not a permanent rule. If transferring from Xitou or Taichung, be sure to re-check before departure.[^3]

The park official website announces prohibition of remote-controlled drones or aerial photography. Taiwan Rhododendron Forest Trail data also reminds that some sections are sleeper-log stairs, unsuitable for wheelchairs and strollers. The park also prohibits open flame, barbecue, cooking, swimming, fishing, and fireworks, and requires visitors not to illegally harvest, damage, carve, pollute, or hunt.[^3] [^10]

## After the Water Sound Leaves, What Does the Forest Keep

Sun Link Sea's story begins with the "Creek Bottom" logging camp, passes through road construction, tourism operations, Typhoon Herb, the 921 earthquake, and access road reconstruction, finally arriving at nature education. This path did not erase the past: the park's existing Japanese cedar forest is the forest composition left by artificial regeneration. Nature education activities also bring logging culture back into curriculum, letting visitors know a forest's today often contains yesterday's human choices.[^1] [^4] [^11]

Songlongyan Waterfall can turn frail in drought, then regain force after plum rains. Qinglong Waterfall may leave only a thin stream in dry season, becoming a rushing curtain after heavy rain. Shijingyi hides the stream's power in riverbed potholes. Taiwan rhododendron and crystal orchid hide the season in the understory. Sun Link Sea is not a fixed postcard, but a set of mountain-forest relationships that change with rainfall, season, engineering, and human behavior.[^5] [^6] [^7] [^10]

![Sun Link Sea Flower Center](/article-images/nature/sun-link-sea-flower-center-2024.webp)

You can leave photos of Sun Link Sea, or you can leave only a few slower actions: listen to the water at Songlongyan for a while, not step on a single small plant under the rhododendrons, watch how stream water grinds rock caves at Shijingyi. When you start noticing changes in water sound, Sun Link Sea is no longer just a scenic spot you arrived at, but a valley that will keep changing.
