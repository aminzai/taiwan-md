> **30 Second Overview:** Three Degrees C is the YouTube brand created by Xie Meng-hang. This name did not come from a 3C product but from when he took cold showers in winter at minus 3 degrees Celsius while studying on the mainland. Friends nicknamed him "Three Degrees" because of this. He edited endurance, dancing, memes, challenges, and true stories into entertainment content, later participating in music video productions as a director, photographer, and editor. The counterintuitive aspect of Three Degrees C is that although he seems to gain traction through nonsensical content designed to make people laugh, what he truly accumulated was a creative methodology—organizing personal performance, editing rhythm, and visual work into something sustainable for the long term.

In an interview in 2022, Xie Meng-hang stated that many asked where "Three Degrees" came from, and his answer was taking cold showers at minus 3 degrees Celsius while studying on the mainland.[^1] This is a small, even somewhat absurd, snippet of life that became the name by which he was recognized online. A feature interview in November 2025 further completes his journey: he transitioned from a cosmetology student to a full-time visual creator, accumulating 7 years of creative experience by the time of the interview, with over 1.39 million subscribers.[^2]

This path is most easily misinterpreted as "a person making funny videos who then became famous." A more accurate description is that Three Degrees C placed an easily recognizable character into a constantly changing format of visual content. The endurance tests, dancing, and memes reported by Mingbao do not look like parts of the same program; rather, they seem like the creator testing how far the audience is willing to follow him.[^1] The themes change, but the person recognized remains the same.

## Name Precedes Persona

Online names often function like brand design: positioning is decided first, and then visuals and tone are arranged. Three Degrees' name is the reverse. It started as a nickname given by friends before it became a channel name, social media handle, and professional identifier. This origin makes "Three Degrees" seem less like a carefully packaged character and more like a joke left behind by a friend in winter.

The detail of "cold showering at minus 3 degrees Celsius" is useful not because it proves how much endurance Three Degrees has, but because it grounds an abstract online moniker into the physical body. Readers can imagine the sensation of cold water hitting the skin and understand why this name does not need excessive explanation. It is a naming story characterized by few objects and clear actions.

As for where he studied on the mainland, the feature interviews available currently only state "studying on the mainland," without specifying the city, province, or school.[^1] Therefore, this article does not treat online encyclopedia summaries as definitive resumes, nor does it push vague descriptions like "second-tier city" into a specific place name. This data gap must be retained because the reliability of biographical writing lies not only in supplementing details but also in clearly marking which details have not been verified by reliable sources.

> **📝 Curator's Note:** A good online name doesn't always start with a business plan. Sometimes, something foolish a person has done leaves more recognition than an entire brand strategy.

Mingbao organized Three Degrees' early channel content into diverse themes such as endurance tests, dancing, and memes.[^1] These terms do not look like parts of the same program; rather, they seem like the creator testing how far the audience is willing to follow him. Dancing requires bodily memory, endurance relies on facial expression and rhythm, and memes require recognizing a shared context. The sense of character in Three Degrees C emerges from these different forms being repeatedly recognized.

## From Street Experiments to Shared Audience Humor

In 2018, Tson Entertainment reported a street fashion experiment involving Three Degrees and Xiao Ma. The two outfits had a large price difference: one was around NT$80,000, and the other was around NT$1,500. They exchanged clothes sequentially before surveying female interviewees on the street about their preferences.[^3]

What truly remained from this video was not the conclusion that "high-end is always better" or "affordable clothing can win." After the clothes were exchanged, the respondents still focused on Xiao Ma, and the feedback even pushed the issue toward skin tone and appearance.[^3] A re-post by Yahoo News on the same day recorded the identical two rounds of experiments, retaining the context where netizens discussed body proportions and garment tailoring.[^4]

This is a scenario well-suited for Three Degrees C: first designing an issue everyone understands, and then letting the experimental results make the issue more awkward. Entertainment does not have the responsibility to provide a beautiful answer for the audience. It places one person in front of the camera, enduring judgments that people originally thought only happened to others.

> **📝 Curator's Note:** The humor in Three Degrees C often isn't "he is funny," but rather his willingness to place himself in an experiment where the joke might turn back and sting him.

This also explains why "nonsensical" cannot be used to summarize his creation alone. Nonsense is merely a surface style. Beneath it lies theme design, on-site reactions, editing sequence, and the work of the protagonist bearing the results. What the audience sees is an absurd test; what the creator handles is a small social observation.

## Million Subscribers Are Not the End Point, But a Renaming of Work Mode

The 2025 interview placed Three Degrees C within the context of "full-time creation." The article mentioned his transition from a cosmetology student to a full-time creator, discussing creative inspiration, traffic, bottlenecks, negative commenters, and the initial intention to provide positive feelings to the audience.[^2] These terms together indicate that for him, YouTube is not just a place to upload content but a job requiring continuous output.

"Million YouTuber" is a convenient label for the media, but it easily reduces the process to a number. What makes Three Degrees C's case worth looking at is that he did not confine his persona to a single series. The endurance tests, dancing, and memes recorded by Mingbao already show that Three Degrees C's early creation was not a singular format.[^1] It is not always starting over; it is testing different entry points under the same name.

For the creator, traffic brings not just applause but also higher demands for repetition. The audience wants the next video to still be "Three Degrees," yet it cannot just be another title change from the last one. Therefore, the persona must remain stable, the themes must change, and the editing must make the variation appear to belong to the same person. This is a very specific labor, not merely inspiration.

> **📝 Curator's Note:** Subscribers push a person into the spotlight, but what truly keeps the channel alive is the meticulous work of deciding "how this time will be different from last time."

## The Person in Front of the Camera Also Works Behind It

Three Degrees C does not only appear on his own YouTube channel. A case study page for Brook Image Culture records another role he played in music video production. In the 2024 "Meteor Sweep" case, the director is listed as Xie Meng-hang, and he is also credited with post-production.[^5] In the Huichun Dan (Rejuvenation Pill) <Flower> case, the official page lists Three Degrees as the director, photographer, and editor, with production completed by Brook Image Culture Co., Ltd.[^6]

These two cases slightly shift the name "Three Degrees C." What the audience is familiar with is a person who is put into challenges and memes. However, what the visual production site requires is another set of judgments: how to arrange camera angles, how to make actors' movements connect, and how to edit the footage in post-production to create rhythm. The former places oneself at the center of content; the latter organizes the entire work.

The official case study pages also place the same person in different job roles. From director, photographer, to editor, Three Degrees is not just an on-screen personality but someone who can complete a visual narrative.[^5][^6] This overlap between front and back stage might be more accurate than "internet celebrity crossing into visuals." Because he never only stayed in front of the camera.

Other case study pages from Brook Image Culture also show the production environment and collaborative network where Three Degrees is involved. <Classic 90 Yingge Old Street Photography Night> and <Pigeon Tycoon> are listed as company featured cases.[^7][^8] These pages are not video listings for the Three Degrees C channel, but they reveal another parallel line of work: producing visuals for brands, events, or music projects, using similar tools to creating entertainment content for one's own audience, yet bearing different responsibilities.

## Beyond Humor, There Is a Line of Authenticity

Three Degrees C's content often begins with challenges, imitations, or absurd premises. The risk in this type of video is that if the humor relies solely on others making mistakes, it easily divides the audience and the subjects being filmed. In the street fashion experiment, Three Degrees put himself into the evaluation field, at least pulling some risk back onto himself. This does not mean every video was controversial because of it, but rather that his creation has an observable ethical choice: is the creator willing to be part of the test?

This line becomes clearer after going full-time. When a video is simultaneously entertainment, work, brand collaboration, and audience relationship, the cost of the joke is not borne only by the person filming. The 2025 interview mentioned how he dealt with creative bottlenecks and negative commenters.[^2] This content does not need to be packaged as success theory. It is more like routine maintenance after achieving millions of subscribers: maintaining output, maintaining character credibility, and maintaining a state where one can continue to film.

The significance of Three Degrees C is not that he represents all Taiwanese YouTubers. That statement is too broad and cannot be supported by one person's data. He is more like a clear slice, showing an often-overlooked path in Taiwan's internet culture: the creator first gains attention using their body and reactions, and then uses planning, editing, and visual production to turn that attention into sustainable work.

> **📝 Curator's Note:** The gap between someone who "is filmed to make the audience laugh" and someone who "determines how a work will be seen" is not fame; it is the entire set of professional capabilities.

## Conclusion: Three Degrees Is Still Three Degrees

The story of Three Degrees C can begin with a very cold bathroom or with a million-subscriber channel. The former explains how the name was remembered; the latter explains how attention was accumulated. What truly connects the two is Xie Meng-hang repeatedly placing himself in different visual problems.

He can be the person judged in a street fashion experiment, and he can stand behind the camera in a music video. He can make people laugh with memes and challenges, but he must also handle traffic, negative commenters, bottlenecks, and work pace. These are not footnotes to an "internet celebrity halo"; they are the profession of the creator itself.

Therefore, Three Degrees C is not just the owner of the name "Three Degrees." He is more like a case study in Taiwanese internet culture: transforming a private joke into a public persona, and then deconstructing that public persona back into planning, shooting, editing, and collaboration. From the cold water at minus 3 degrees Celsius to the final edit before a video goes online, this path was never completed by one magical idea.
