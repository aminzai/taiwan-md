# Duo Duo Luo: Turning "Spotting Errors" into a Public Performance, Asking Who Has the Right to Correct Whom Behind the Laughter

> **30-Second Overview**
>
> In September 2024, Duo Duo Luo, in his capacity as "Japanese Police," corrected the Japanese copy of the brand Ba Yao He Cha; the brand apologized, proofread, and even launched an 87% discount promotion using the phrase "Bei Qi Ai" (Bitch).[^1] In June 2026, he applied the same criteria to the works of Huang Shanliao, moving from linguistic errors to literary criticism.[^2] Duo Duo Luo’s key significance lies not just in his willingness to speak out, but in transforming "pointing out what is wrong" into a public performance with a distinct character, rhythm, and audience participation; after the laughter subsides, the question remains: who has the qualification to judge, and can the judged respond?

In September 2024, a video placed the Japanese copy of the Taiwanese bubble tea brand Ba Yao He Cha under the lens. As Duo Duo Luo corrected the grammar, he repeatedly shouted "Bei Qi Ai" (Bitch). Ba Yao He Cha later apologized in the video comments, stating that they had hired a Japanese teacher for proofreading and launched an 87% discount promotion.[^1] This was not a typical brand crisis management case: a YouTuber living in Tokyo became the law enforcement officer in the video by spotting errors, while the brand turned the caught errors into their own promotional language.

By June 2026, his video commenting on writer Huang Shanliao sparked discussion again. Reports indicated that Duo Duo Luo discussed multiple works by Huang Shanliao in one go, with the video title using the harsh phrase "The Worst in History"; Huang Shanliao himself responded that he would continue to learn and grow.[^2] The two events, separated by nearly two years, shifted from the signage copy of a drink to a bestselling author's book, yet the role remained unchanged: he always first identifies something "off," then turns the correction itself into a performance.

This is the most significant aspect of Duo Duo Luo to understand. The common narrative tends to categorize him as an "internet celebrity who ruthlessly mocks celebrities," but this view reverses the causal order. He does not first have a sharp-tongued persona and then randomly pick things to criticize; a more accurate description is that he binds the criteria for criticism, the rhythm of speech, and a recognizable character together, allowing the audience to know why they should stay to watch the next typo, the next line, or the next book.

> 📝 **Curator’s Note:** Duo Duo Luo’s signature is not that "he dares to scold," but that he processes "I disagree" into a watchable format; while the audience laughs, they are also invited to temporarily stand beside his criteria.

## The Character Precedes the Resume

Public reports introduce Duo Duo Luo as a YouTuber whose real name is Lin Wei-ting (林煒庭), who studied in Japan. Reports also mention that he attended Aoyama Gakuin University, has experience with the Japanese-Language Proficiency Test (JLPT) N1 level, and was exposed to the training system of Japanese comedians.[^3] These resume details can explain why he has the ability to discuss Japanese, but they do not explain why he films Japanese as a comedy.

His channel does not only deal with language. From "feces art" to controversial works, he gradually turned "evaluating why a work is not good" into content itself.[^3] The important shift in this trajectory is moving professional knowledge from the classroom to the character: N1 is no longer just an exam score, but an ID card he can flash when "Japanese Police" is on duty; reading experience is no longer just private taste, but evidence that can be judged on camera.

What can be directly accounted for in an article page is how he later lived in Japan, accumulated recognizability through manga and cultural commentary, and then made "Japanese Police" a character remembered by the public in the 2024 Ba Yao He Cha incident.[^1] For a profile article, this trade-off is important: there is no need to fill a timeline with unverified resume details presented as established facts.

## "Japanese Police" is Not a Teacher, It Is an Entrance Method

DailyView’s description of the Ba Yao He Cha incident was specific: Duo Duo Luo, in his capacity as "Japanese Police," corrected the copy; in the video, after correcting each error, he ended with "Bei Qi Ai"; Ba Yao He Cha then admitted in the comments that the copy had problems, stating that proofreading was completed and the official website would be updated.[^1] What is truly interesting here is not how many errors the brand made, but that the errors were placed into a rhythm familiar to the audience: discovery, arrest, sentencing, and the culprit apologizing.

Ba Yao He Cha’s response also ensured this performance did not stop at humiliation. The official account wrote "So many mistakes, we will work hard to improve" in the comments, and the brand responded to "Bei Qi Ai" with an 87% discount.[^1] Duo Duo Luo was thus not just correcting the other party, but also giving the other party the opportunity to return to the stage using the same language. Criticism did not disappear, nor did commercial operations. It is the superposition of the two that formed the viral power of this video.

Calling him "Japanese Teacher" would actually miss this point. A teacher’s job is to lead students to the correct answer; the Japanese Police’s job is to make errors visible in public. The former requires stability, completeness, and patience; the latter requires a clear culprit, a repeatable catchphrase, and a punchline the audience can understand in seconds. Duo Duo Luo chose the latter, so his most successful content is not necessarily the most complete language lesson, but the scene that most clearly makes people instantly understand "something is really weird here."

## From Japanese to Works: Criteria Can Be Transplanted

The 2026 Huang Shanliao incident shows that "Japanese Police" is merely a superficial character. ETtoday reported that Duo Duo Luo released a video commenting on Huang Shanliao’s works on June 5, and Huang Shanliao later responded that he would continue to learn.[^2] Subsequent Yahoo news described this viral moment as a rapid rise in channel subscribers after he named Cai A-ga and Huang Shanliao in succession. The report also mentioned that he gradually accumulated an audience early on through controversial works and negative-review manga commentary.[^3]

The common structure here is not "Japanese" or "literature," but the publicization of criteria. Duo Duo Luo first tells the audience what standard he uses to watch, then demonstrates how a work fails to meet that standard. When the object is Ba Yao He Cha, the standard is Japanese copy; when the object is a novel, the standard becomes narrative, text, and reading experience. The subject matter can change, but the character’s actions do not: turning private judgment into public trial, and turning the trial into a shareable video.

Another example from Liberty Times further illustrates this transplantability. Duo Duo Luo once shared an experience of encountering a Chinese student in a Japanese boxing class who demanded "sparring"; the video was dug up again and sparked discussion. He edited the scene where the Japanese coach stopped the conflict into a pun joke about the "First Island Chain."[^4] This is not Japanese teaching, nor a book review, but still the same content machine: a specific scene, an unexpected behavior, and a judgment that allows the audience to immediately retell it.

> 📝 **Curator’s Note:** What he truly sells is not the answer, but the speed of judgment. When the audience shares the video, they are often sharing not just "which Japanese sentence was wrong," but "look, we now have a common punchline and a common standard."

## Controversy Pushes the Character Off-Camera

The most difficult part of Duo Duo Luo to simplify comes from the sexual harassment controversy after collaborating with Li Yu-hsi in 2023. Mirror Media reported Li Yu-hsi’s allegations at the time, as well as Duo Duo Luo’s rebuttal on social media. The report simultaneously presented his admission that some jokes might cause discomfort, but his denial of physical boundary-crossing.[^5] These contents cannot be rewritten as "who told the truth," because at that time it was still a stage where the statements of both parties collided.

Subsequent reports in December 2024 indicated that the Banqiao Summary Court of the New Taipei District Court sentenced Duo Duo Luo in the first instance to pay Li Yu-hsi 100,000 NTD. The report also recorded Duo Duo Luo’s statement that he would appeal, and his public reaction to the judgment.[^6] Reports from October 2025 stated that the second instance determined the relevant behavior did not reach the degree of sexual harassment, overturning the original judgment, and the case could not be appealed. The same report listed the dialogue, video footage, and statements from both parties involved in the judgment.[^7]

This judicial battle cannot be used to endorse Duo Duo Luo’s commentary style, nor compressed into an "upset victory" entertainment headline. It truly reveals another risk: when a creator speaks in a judgmental tone for a long time, the audience easily transplants the "this is wrong" from the video into real-life personal controversies. But judicial procedures handle evidence, behavior, and legal constitutions, not who speaks better or whose editing has more rhythm.

In the 2023 reports, Duo Duo Luo said that his channel originally just wanted to make funny videos, but it "continuously caused disturbances."[^5] This sentence has an uncomfortable accuracy: his content relies on making friction funny, but friction in reality does not necessarily recover after the laughter ends. From brand copy to sexual harassment controversies, the difference is not traffic, but whether the object of criticism can withstand the same performance rules.

## After Being Seen, Who Can Still Be an Audience

Duo Duo Luo’s viral success did not start from "he is qualified," but from the audience being willing to temporarily lend him that qualification. DailyView’s article noted netizens calling him an N1 Japanese master, and also noted that Ba Yao He Cha, after being corrected, still chose to publicly correct it.[^1] These two reactions together form the complete picture of internet influence: on one side, the audience hands the criteria to the creator; on the other, the criticized party decides whether to catch these criteria.

Therefore, Duo Duo Luo is not simply someone who became famous by roasting. He turned commentary into a fixed format: first establish his own criteria, then find a visible error, and finally use laughter to pull the audience to his side. This format allows language, manga, books, and daily events to enter the same stage, and makes each of his "error spottings" carry an unresolved question: when we laugh and agree with him, are we understanding a problem, or handing the power of judgment to someone better at performing?

His most powerful aspect is precisely that this question is not concluded by the video. Duo Duo Luo caught the typos, finished the book reviews, edited the boxing class jokes; what remains is not a police officer who is always correct, but a group of audiences re-deciding in front of the screen: who can be corrected next time, who can rebut, and when the laughter should stop.

## How the Audience Is Brought In

Duo Duo Luo’s videos often break down complex judgments into several steps the audience can follow. First, an object appears: a signboard, a book, a manga, or a conversation that happened in a boxing class. Then he points out the gap between that object and common sense. Finally, he encapsulates the judgment with a catchphrase, reaction, or pun. This format lowers the entry threshold; the audience does not need to understand all the background first, but can find a retellable moment within the fragments.

In the Ba Yao He Cha incident, the brand’s apology was not an附带 news item of the video, but part of the plot. DailyView quoted the brand’s comments: Ba Yao He Cha admitted "so many mistakes" and said they would work hard to improve; then they launched an 87% discount, turning the corrected vocabulary into a discount.[^1] An external critic was thus placed into the brand’s own narrative, turning an error that might only have had negative effects into an image project of "we caught this joke."

The same structure also appears in book reviews, only the criticized party is no longer a brand that can be modified immediately. ETtoday’s report wrote about Huang Shanliao’s response after being commented on, rather than judging the literary value for either party.[^2] This difference reminds readers: commentary content can be very entertaining, but the consequences of commentary depend on whether the object is a brand, an author, media, or someone with a private cooperative relationship with the creator.

This is why "sharp-tongued" is not a sufficient explanation. Sharp-tongued only describes the tone, not the distribution mechanism; Duo Duo Luo’s videos can be retold because they simultaneously provide a character, criteria, and nodes that can be cut into short videos. The audience is not passively receiving a string of insulting adjectives, but learning how to look at that error within the video’s beat.

## How a Name Replaces the Original Name

Profile articles often write internet celebrities as the sum of subscriber counts, controversial events, and a few viral videos. Yahoo’s report placed Duo Duo Luo’s early direction, study-abroad background, and 2026 subscriber growth on the same line, explaining how his visibility increased rapidly after commenting on Cai A-ga and Huang Shanliao.[^3] But subscriber counts can only explain that more people see him, not why the audience remembers him.

What is more memorable is the title. "Japanese Police" compresses a person’s expertise into a role that can be repeatedly used in video titles, comments, and brand responses. DailyView even listed related tags as "Big-Tail YouTuber," "Japanese Police," and "Bei Qi Ai"; these words are not a resume, but are closer to how internet figures exist than a resume is.[^1]

This character-ization has benefits and costs. The benefit is that the audience can quickly recognize his position in unfamiliar subject matter. The cost is that every appearance is expected to be faster, heavier, and more like the last time. When a creator’s recognizability is built on correction, silence, reservation, or admitting uncertainty instead appears unlike his content. The person is thus chased by their own format.

Between the 2024 brand copy and the 2026 book review, what truly continues is not a specific fixed joke, but the fact that "criteria must be seen first." This makes Duo Duo Luo’s channel look like a review room that is always open: the subject matter can change, but the signboard at the door does not; every time the audience walks in, they know someone will point out that something is wrong in a certain place.

## Before the Conclusion, Uncertainty Must Be Retained

The easiest place to get wrong about Duo Duo Luo is to organize him into a beautiful success timeline: study abroad, learn Japanese, start making videos, become Japanese Police, become viral due to controversy. This line can indeed quickly explain the background, but it cuts out all accidents, loss of control, and costs.

For example, reports on the sexual harassment controversy leave reading requirements completely different from brand copy. Mirror Media’s 2023 report had Duo Duo Luo’s point-by-point response; the 2024 report recorded the civil first instance judgment to pay 100,000 NTD; the 2025 report explained the second instance overturning the original judgment.[^5] [^6] [^7] ETtoday’s 2024 article also handled the progress at the time with the title "Judged to Pay 100,000 for Sexual Harassment Lawsuit."[^8] These articles can form a timeline, but cannot be pieced together into a simple moral conclusion.

A creator’s public role is often larger than the scope the creator can control. Duo Duo Luo can decide how the video is edited, how the catchphrase is said, but he cannot decide how the brand, the criticized author, the news media, and the audience will catch this role. The boxing class story reported by Liberty Times was spread again not because it provided new expertise, but because the audience put that "First Island Chain" joke back into the 2026 public context.[^4]

Therefore, reading Duo Duo Luo cannot just ask "is he right." It must also ask: where do his criteria come from? Who can respond? Which errors are suitable to be made into jokes, and which harms should not be handled the same way? If only leaving the two words "dares to speak," the person is flattened; if treating all controversies as traffic strategies, one also loses the facts.

Duo Duo Luo turned "spotting errors" into a public performance; this is why he is remembered, and also the weight he must bear. The audience can temporarily borrow his criteria in the laughter, but does not need to always leave the power of judgment in his hands. When the next video lights up the subtitle "Japanese Police Moving Out," the truly mature viewing is not to immediately follow with a verdict, but to first clearly see: is the error pointed out this time copy, a work, a system, or a person’s boundary?
