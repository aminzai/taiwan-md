> **30-Second Overview:**
> The Idiot Princess (Dai Ping-ya) did not start with a pre-set "influencer persona" to run her channel. She began with voice-over assignments at the Department of Broadcasting and Television, National Taiwan University of Arts, first hiding her face behind a mosaic, then transforming parodic voice-overs originally restricted by copyright into original programs she could control. [^1][^2]
> Her most distinctive trait is not making videos the fastest, but incorporating planning, filming, editing, performance, and self-deprecation into a single creative method. As she moved from a solo worker to a team member and producer, a real question emerged: Do audiences like the character "The Idiot Princess," or do they like the work Dai Ping-ya wants to complete seriously? [^3][^4]
>
> 📝 Curator’s Note: This article does not treat "The Idiot Princess" as a collection of single jokes, but places her back into the labor history of Taiwan’s online creators. The mosaic, editing software, camera, team, and actor training are all the working conditions behind the character.

The Idiot Princess’s channel is most easily remembered for its punchlines. She used a lazy vocal tone to dub Disney princesses with completely unseemly lines; after revealing her face, she used her appearance, body, and "unprofessionalism" for self-deprecation. However, if you only see her as a YouTuber who pleases audiences with low-brow jokes, you miss the most interesting part of this figure. What she has actually been cultivating long-term is the idea that "I can turn imperfection into a working method."

In a 2021 Newtalk report on her collaboration with the Taiwan Family Medicine Medical Association, her channel already had over 1.53 million subscribers. This number illustrates the scale of her reach at the time but cannot be directly equated with influence. What is truly worth observing is how she took a channel founded on parodic voice-overs into different scenarios such as health education, quiz shows, and film. [^14]

## Hiding Behind the Mosaic First, Then Having the Chance to Step in Front of the Lens

The Idiot Princess’s early creative work started with voice-over assignments during university. She and partners from the same school re-dubbed Disney animations, and the videos gained significant attention online. Later, she continued to produce content on weekends, gradually adding series on cooking, daily life, and collaborations from simple parodic voice-overs. She did not initially decide to become a full-time YouTuber; rather, she slowly confirmed between her original work and continuous creation that what she wanted to do was image work where she could control the content. [^1][^5]

The mosaic was not just a visual punchline, but also a psychological distance. She once said she was afraid of being attacked when stepping in front of the screen, especially having her appearance scrutinized. Between 2016 and 2017, her path from not showing her face, to blurring it, to officially appearing on camera was not a sudden "unlock," but a practice of repeatedly placing her creative ability onto her own person. [^1][^6]

This turning point also had very realistic reasons. Early voice-over content was limited by secondary creation and copyright restrictions, making it impossible to extend themes infinitely. In an interview published by the Taipei City Employment Service Office, she was direct about the shift to original content: "I want to produce my own programs, create my own content." [^2] Revealing her face was therefore not to make the channel look better, but to allow her to put all her experiences, observations, and performances into her work.

## Beneath the "Buddhist" Surface, Work Is Very Slow

In a 2018 interview, The Idiot Princess repeatedly stated that she was not good at rapid production. She handled everything from scriptwriting, filming, to retouching and editing personally; finishing one video in a week was already tight. She also said she was not in a hurry to hand off work because "I can't find a second me," and no one could fully replicate her understanding of punchlines and rhythm. [^3]

This slowness is contrary to the general imagination of traffic-driven work. Platforms encourage fixed posting schedules, clients want efficient videos, and audiences mistake creators' rest for laziness. The Idiot Princess chose to keep a cumbersome solo workflow because what she cared about was not just "whether a video was made," but whether that video still looked like her. She once described this trade-off as: "Since I don't know the answer, I might as well make content I like." [^3]

The interview with the Taipei City Employment Service Office supplemented another side of this method. When she was in university and entering the workforce, she worked in online marketing, digital music marketing, online shopping, and TV station-related jobs, and also handled video post-production and event planning at an adult video company. These experiences made her familiar with scripts, editing, retouching, and on-site processes. The later "from script to performance, all done by myself" was not a natural genius narrative, but the accumulation of many less glamorous jobs. [^2]

## Self-Deprecation Is Not the End, But Her Protective Layer

The Idiot Princess makes jokes about her appearance and body, often regarded as her hallmark. However, in an interview with _Womany_, she stated clearly that appearance criticism still causes harm. Choosing to joke about herself first does not mean she doesn't care; rather, it is an attempt to take back the initiative of evaluation. She said: "I can shoot, edit, and retouch; I won't waste such abilities." [^4]

This also explains why she could switch between "playing the fool" and being "professional." She could use exaggerated expressions and dirty jokes to let audiences relax, but could also personally experience agriculture, service industry, or funeral-related work in _The Corner of the Fool_ (Chi De Jiao Luo). The focus of these plans was not to pretend to be an expert, but to admit she was originally unfamiliar, and then show these strange jobs to the audience. In an official workplace interview, she reminded creators not to underestimate the influence a video has on audiences. [^2]

A 2021 _Mirror Weekly_ report recorded how self-deprecation changed from personal defense to a language connecting with audiences. The Idiot Princess talked about being mocked for her body during student days, and later used self-deprecation to reduce the force of attacks. This is not a simple story of "just being optimistic can overcome bullying," but rather that after turning embarrassing experiences into public performance, she still had to bear the cost of family worry and online comments. [^7]

## When "Doing It Yourself" Becomes Learning to Let Go

By 2022, The Idiot Princess began building a more complete team. A profile interview in _VERSE_ recorded her transition from long-term solo handling of planning, filming, and editing to moving into an office and letting more people participate in brand management. Her past reluctance to let go was partly due to high standards for herself, and not wanting others to see a large amount of NG (No Good/take) footage. Building a team, for her, was not just expanding capacity, but acknowledging that creators also need rest, get sick, and take leave. [^5]

She collaborated with Li Ke Tai Tai (Dr. Mom) to produce the quiz show _More Reason More Knowledge_ (Duo Li Duo Zhi). The production cost and scale of the six-episode series were larger than before. This attempt was not reported as a flawless victory. _VERSE_ wrote directly that the ratings after broadcast were not ideal, but The Idiot Princess still believed the team enjoyed the process of raising the production scale. This detail is important because it makes "transformation" no longer just a pretty route organized by successful people looking back, but a choice that is truly possible to do imperfectly. [^5]

She also opened a café she had long wanted to open on Shizi Island, filming the process of finding a shop, selecting products, and opening the store as a series. The store later closed due to lease issues, but she did not hide this experience. Failure in her channel could still become material, but not consumed and forgotten; rather, it left a trace of "I really did it." [^5][^8]

## The 70,000 NTD Camera: Creation Does Not Start with Traffic

The Idiot Princess’s entrepreneurial story did not start with an investment or a successful business plan. Looking back at her university days, she mentioned that for half a year she ate 20 NTD instant noodles for three meals, sustaining her life with part-time jobs and freelance projects. Later, she and friends entered a Miaoli tourism short film competition, and the team won a 70,000 NTD prize. Her friend left most of the prize money for her to buy a DSLR camera. That camera was not a symbolic prop, but the actual equipment that allowed her to continue filming, editing, and accumulating work later. [^12]

This experience is worth understanding before "million-dollar income." Media headlines easily link 70,000 NTD with million-dollar income into an upward curve, but the report content was actually a start full of instability: no fixed salary, rent and living expenses, even needing to borrow 1,000 NTD from colleagues to get through a month. She stayed not because she knew from the start which video would go viral, but by turning limited prize money into a reusable production tool. [^12]

📝 Curator’s Note: "70,000 NTD births million-dollar income" is easily read as an inspirational formula, but what is truly valuable in the article is not the return multiple, but how equipment changes a person's feasible scope. The camera allowed her to continue producing, but could not bear unstable income, long post-production hours, and audience evaluation for her.

For online creators, equipment is often imagined as a consumer good. The Idiot Princess’s story reminds people that early equipment could also be a working condition. The DSLR allowed her to shoot videos, but did not write scripts, perform, edit, or bear negative comments for her. What truly formed the channel's特色 (characteristic) was that she connected the equipment to a workflow controlled by herself.

## From Solo Editing to Team Office

When the channel scale grew, the question was not just "whether to hire people," but whether a creator could admit that a program does not have to grow entirely on its own. Around 2022, The Idiot Princess collaborated with the Li Ke Tai Tai team to produce the large-scale quiz show _More Reason More Knowledge_ (Duo Li Duo Zhi). A _CTWANT_ report placed this collaboration in the context of the Li Ke Tai Tai team expanding and moving to a new office; The Idiot Princess's team also moved in. [^13]

This change was different from her past solo mode. Early on, she explained her unwillingness to easily hand over editing and rhythm by saying "I can't find a second me," but large-scale programs require hosting, question design, photography, post-production, stage management, and coordination to operate simultaneously. Moving from one person finishing a video to having to let others understand the program she wants to make is management ability beyond creative ability.

_More Reason More Knowledge_ was not written as a route where success is inevitable just by expanding scale. The more important harvest was that The Idiot Princess began to think from the position of a producer: Can a creator turn the personal style familiar to audiences into a program language completed by multiple people? This also allowed her, when venturing into film later, to be not just an "influencer found to act," but someone already familiar with how a work goes from planning to finished product.

## When Comedy Enters Public Communication, Punchlines No Longer Belong Only to the Creator

In 2021, The Idiot Princess collaborated with the Taiwan Family Medicine Medical Association to shoot HPV health education short videos, attempting to talk about safe sex, HPV vaccines, and regular Pap smear tests using the puns and humor she was familiar with. After the video went online, some audiences believed the metaphor helped reduce the distance of health education, while others criticized this expression method as mocking professionalism. [^14]

The importance of this controversy lies not in judging whether she "succeeded in being funny" or "failed in being funny," but in clearly drawing the new responsibility for creators entering public communication. The punchlines of entertainment videos are usually shared by the creator and the audience, but health education content involves medical professionalism, disease prevention, and audience understanding. When The Idiot Princess brought the language originally used to bridge distance into health education, the partner and the audience would demand it maintain both accuracy and appropriateness. This is exactly the boundary her creative method must face.

Her value is therefore not just "being able to shoot serious topics funny." A more precise statement is that she can bring topics audiences might not actively click on to the forefront, but whether audiences understand correct information cannot rely solely on the creator's personal charm. This experience made "Idiot Princess-style humor" first obviously touch upon the issue of professional trust.

📝 Curator’s Note: Public welfare cooperation is a checkpoint for judging whether an influencer's influence is mature. When content only pursues sharing and laughter, the creator can control the rhythm. When content involves medicine and prevention, the creator must accept joint examination by professional groups, audiences, and opposing opinions. This is not driving humor out, but requiring humor to know what it is handling.

## After Traffic Declines, What Still Counts as Success

In a 2024 interview, The Idiot Princess did not package platform changes as a simple inspirational story. She admitted that early on, investing 80% effort might yield one million views; later, investing the same 80% effort might yield only 100,000 views. She placed this gap back into changes in audience habits, rather than pushing all responsibility onto audiences or algorithms. [^15]

She also proposed another measure to cooperating brands: If audiences are willing to share the video, it means the content is indeed considered useful or worth forwarding; effectiveness should not be judged solely by likes. This is not denying data, but putting data back into the context of how content flows. For a creator who has worked for many years, the real difficulty is not not knowing traffic is important, but knowing traffic will change, yet still deciding which standards cannot be handed over completely. [^15]

This returns to her early slow workflow. When platforms reward speed and freshness, The Idiot Princess still cared whether a video looked like her. She was not ignoring numbers, but unwilling to let numbers become the sole explanation of the work. From the 70,000 NTD camera, to large-scale programs, to declining view counts, her creative history has actually been answering the same question: When external conditions change, what is left that allows a person to continue making content?

## From YouTuber to Actor, the Body Becomes a Working Site Again

In 2024, The Idiot Princess made her first film appearance in _Ghost Tower of Inverness_ (Gui Tian Xia). Interviews with _ETtoday_ and Sanli recorded that when she first faced film shooting, she had to accept acting training, action direction, and special makeup. She clearly stated the difference between film and regular video shooting: acting work requires more preparation and needs to complete emotions different from daily humor in front of the lens. [^8][^9]

She did not deny her original influencer identity because of this. On the contrary, she said in interviews that she would continue to do online content and hoped to try more decoration, house-hunting, or deeper topics. Another interview during the film promotion showed that she viewed her first acting role as a challenge different from her original comedic image, rather than abandoning her past creative methods. [^11] This is not graduating from "influencer," but treating the influencer identity as work that can continue to grow. A _Mirror Weekly_ interview published by TVBS also recorded her hope to balance being an actor and a YouTuber, imagining herself as a "veteran YouTuber star." [^9][^10]

This route has a cost that is not easily seen. She must let audiences accept that the same person can be absurd and also serious. She can use self-deprecation in videos to handle appearance and gender expectations, but can also bear a role in film where the body is injured and emotional pressure is higher. If audiences only demand she always maintain the most familiar punchline, any new attempt will feel like betrayal. Her transformation truly has to handle the audience's imagination of "what The Idiot Princess should look like."

## What She Leaves Behind Is Not a Success Formula

Taiwan's online creators are often required to explain success methods: how to pick topics, how to grasp algorithms, how to take sponsorships, how to increase subscribers. The Idiot Princess’s interviews, however, repeatedly point to another answer. She knows data is important, and knows commercial cooperation must consider whether products and audiences are suitable, but she cannot guarantee which video will definitely succeed. What she can control is whether she truly invests in the work, and whether she is still willing to let audiences see imperfect places. [^1][^3]

📝 Curator’s Note: The Idiot Princess’s transformation is not changing from "not serious" to "serious," but letting work originally treated as a joke by audiences gradually become visible. She has always been writing, shooting, editing, discussing cooperation, and handling criticism, but later the scale of works made these labors harder to hide behind laughter.

Therefore, The Idiot Princess’s importance lies not in her being a million-subscriber of a certain period, nor only in her turning Disney voice-overs into a type of Taiwan online memory. She lets people see that a female creator can gain attention without relying on propriety and perfection, and can also not treat humor as the upper limit of ability. Stepping out from behind the mosaic was not becoming real from fictional, but gradually letting more real work, fear, failure, and attempts enter that name originally only responsible for making everyone laugh.
