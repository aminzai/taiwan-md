> **30-Second Overview**: Jiu Jiu Xie initially gained attention by translating foreign memes, then deleted his most popular early works to transition from a translation-based channel to a knowledge channel focused on research, explanation, and continuous self-study. This is a story of reclaiming creative ownership after stepping back from pure traffic generation.

In 2013, while in a dormitory at National Central University (NCU), Xie Renjie began recording before a camera that did not have high audience expectations, while his roommates attended classes. At the time, few people were certain that being a YouTuber was a profession; he first translated foreign memes and comedy videos, establishing the name "Jiu Jiu Xie" online.[^1]

Five years later, he had become a knowledge-based Taiwanese YouTuber. The most noteworthy aspect of this journey is that he once deleted all his translation works—the ones that were easiest to generate views from. To establish a channel truly run by him, he voluntarily gave up some traffic.[^2]

## A Pair of Shoes and an Unsettled Path

Xie Renjie graduated from the Department of Chemistry at National Central University. During his university years, he balanced studying chemistry with making videos in the dormitory. These two activities seemed unrelated but later became one job: finding information, understanding the information, and explaining content that others found too difficult so that it could be understood.[^1]

He did not initially know he wanted to do science popularization. A 2017 self-media lecture from Shih Hsin University recorded that he had previously made translated comedy videos, and also tried software production, food reviews, and English teaching, before gradually finding a suitable genre after receiving feedback on knowledge-based videos.[^3]

In that lecture, he broke down the long-term operation of his channel into three things: mindset, personal style, and action. He also stated that to make being a YouTuber a profession, one cannot rely solely on confidence; one must first prepare goals and passion for continuous creation.[^3]

This answer did not promise that "being oneself leads to fame." It was more like a job description: having goals, being able to repeat the process, and continuing to adjust even when no one is applauding.

## Deleting Translations, Keeping Questions

Jiu Jiu Xie's early popularity came from translating foreign graphics and videos. A 2018 interview recorded that he later removed all translation works because those contents were not his own creation and involved derivative work and copyright issues.[^4]

He said: "I am very clear that to appear as a creator, I cannot keep translated works."[^4]

The weight of this statement lies in the fact that there was indeed a loss after the deletions. A report by Cheers mentioned that subscribers from old works gradually left, and self-made content did not immediately fill the gap, leading to a transition period lasting 20 months. At that time, a video might only have 2,000 to 3,000 views, and the ad revenue for an entire month of videos was about $500.[^5]

During the traffic decline, he admitted that he had to start over. This choice transformed "Jiu Jiu Xie" from a mere online name into a creative identity that required his personal responsibility.

## A Steak That Brought the Channel Back Under His Control

The turning point was not a grand awards ceremony, but a short video about cutting steak. Jiu Jiu Xie explained that the red liquid flowing out when steak is cut is not blood, but myoglobin. This topic was both relatable and gave a clear use to his background in chemistry.[^5]

A 2024 report by _Yang Taichung_ reviewed this process. The article pointed out that during the relatively early YouTube ecosystem of 2015 and 2016, he chose a niche knowledge path; later, "the blood of steak is not blood" became his first video to exceed 100,000 subscribers, confirming to him that what he was good at was organizing information and sharing it.[^6]

The same report placed his creative starting point in Taichung and the NCU dormitory. His first video was a free software tutorial recorded in the men's dormitories of NCU; later, he continuously filmed in his dormitory, rented housing, and his family home in Tai Ping, Taichung. The channel did not sprout from a media center but slowly developed its own format in everyday spaces.[^6]

The success of the steak video does not mean that Jiu Jiu Xie only makes chemistry content from then on. It was more like an editorial discovery: viewers do not need to like science first to wonder what is happening inside a piece of steak.

## Chemistry Didn't Disappear, It Just Changed Jobs

In an IOH interview, after his grades declined in his sophomore year, Jiu Jiu Xie began to doubt whether he should continue down the path of graduate school and technology industries. He said that pure theoretical courses sometimes made him feel "disconnected from reality," leading him to question what he was doing by studying these things.[^1]

However, leaving a typical career path in chemistry does not mean abandoning chemistry. To make science videos, he usually spent 10 to 20 hours reading literature and designing scripts. His university seminar courses trained him to find academic papers, organize presentations, and deal with professors and classmates—these skills later became the basic workflow for his channel.[^1]

![NCU Library, representing the knowledge work extended from Jiu Jiu Xie's reading and seminar discussions](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1c/Library_of_National_Central_University.jpg/1280px-Library_of_National_Central_University.jpg)
_Image: NCU Library, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0. From [File Page](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Library_of_National_Central_University.jpg)._

A 2020 report by _Fan Zhuan Education_ described him as a "knowledge omnivore." He did not just read science; he also studied psychology and business management because, after setting up his studio, he needed to supplement the management knowledge he had previously lacked. He turned _Jiu Du_ (Jiu Reading) into a storytelling program partly to force himself to keep reading.[^7]

In that interview, he said: "Basically, every video is self-study!"[^7]

Interest here is an entry point for labor. Every video requires finding data, judging sources, writing scripts, filming, editing, marketing, and also responding to audience comments pointing out where he was unclear.

## Knowledge Videos Also Have Their Commercial Issues

Jiu Jiu Xie's channel is not a classroom isolated from the market. In a 2018 interview, he mentioned that YouTube ad revenue is usually insufficient to support creators, so he still needs to take on sponsored content, lectures, or subscription plans. However, his limitation was that sponsorships cannot just deliver product messages; they must also provide something for viewers who do not need the product.[^4]

He said: "Even when doing sponsorships, I must give my audience something so that 99% of viewers who do not need this product can still gain something from the video."[^4]

This principle did not eliminate commercial issues. It laid them out: when knowledge becomes a commodity, how does the audience know if a video is explaining the world or building credibility for a product? What Jiu Jiu Xie can do is state his standards, but ultimately, he must let the audience retain the right to judge.

At the same time, he participated in a subscription project with PressPlay. A Knowing interview recorded that after three months on the market, the total amount reached second place on the platform. The report also noted that in early 2018, he was listed as one of Taiwan's top 33 YouTubers and an early creator in the knowledge channel category. These numbers are snapshots from that time and should not be taken as current rankings.[^4]

## From One Person to a Studio

In 2018, Jiu Jiu Xie began thinking about establishing a studio. In an interview, he said he hoped someone could share planning, data collection, filming, and editing, and he also hoped one day to step back into the background so that new faces could appear on camera.[^4]

_Fan Zhuan Education_ recorded in 2020 that he had established a studio, with channel subscribers exceeding 650,000, and the PressPlay project having over 350 subscribers, generating nearly NT$120,000 per month. These figures show that for an individual channel to evolve into a team, it requires not just more manpower but also income sufficient to pay for new work.[^7]

An Apple Podcasts interview in 2022 listed him as a knowledge-based YouTuber with 1.58 million subscribers, discussing topics from self-media trends to investment and finance. The page is program information, not an independent statistical report, so this number should be understood as the host's introduction when the show was released, and not used to extrapolate long-term growth curves.[^8]

A press release from the collaboration between Chungking University of Science and Technology and the Taipei City Youth Bureau in 2025 recorded him lecturing on the topic "From a STEM Geek to a Million-View YouTuber," attracting over 300 students. This is a first-hand page from the event organizer, proving the activity and theme, but not sufficient to prove all achievements of the channel alone; therefore, this article only uses it in the context of public sharing.[^9]

He transitioned from filming alone in a dormitory to collaborating with planners, editors, and studio partners. This change also altered the meaning of the name "Jiu Jiu Xie": it is not just a face, but a system of research, narration, and production processes.

## From Dormitory Creation to Public Sharing

Jiu Jiu Xie's story can be seen through several time points, but these numbers only represent cross-sections from different reports and cannot be pieced together into an exact subscription growth curve. In 2013, he started translating and recording videos in his NCU dormitory. In 2018, he faced the pressure of transitioning after deleting translated works and began discussing studios, sponsorships, and subscription plans. In 2020, _Fan Zhuan Education_ recorded his studio and the _Jiu Du_ project. In 2022, Apple Podcasts introduced him as a million-subscriber YouTuber. In 2025, he shared his experience of transitioning from a STEM background to self-media at Chungking University of Science and Technology. In 2026, NOWnews reported on his life from the perspective of family and continuous updates.[^1] [^4] [^7] [^8] [^9] [^10]

The most important thing about this timeline is not proving his ascent, but showing that the scope of the channel's work continuously increased. The early core was translation and recording; the transition period added topic selection and original scripts; after the studio was established, management, division of labor, and income arrangement were added. When a person becomes a team, the creator's "work" expands from one video to a process that can be handed over, corrected, and maintained.

## Taichung: The Conditions for Work

A 2024 report by _Yang Taichung_ placed Jiu Jiu Xie within the creative landscape of Taichung. Apart from his time in university dormitories, he rarely left Taichung, living there, getting married, and establishing a studio. The report noted that he felt the streets, housing prices, transportation, and spatial conditions of Taichung allowed the creation team to operate outside of Taipei.[^6]

This detail is worth noting. Online creation appears to have no geographical limits, but platforms still have centers. Interviews, branding, agency, and media resources are often concentrated in Taipei; Jiu Jiu Xie remaining in Taichung does not mean he was disconnected from the industry, but rather that he incorporated filming space, living costs, and local belonging into his creative decisions.

He did not turn Taichung into a tourist spot; he treated it as a condition for long-term work. The dormitory, his family home, the studio, and the park are all parts of his life outside the channel, which is also why the channel has been able to continue.

## Not Everyone Has Zero Negative Reviews, Only Those Who Continuously Correct

A 2026 NOWnews report described Jiu Jiu Xie as a "Taiwanese YouTuber with zero negative reviews," recording that after running the channel for 11 years and becoming a father of two, he still maintained video updates. The report quoted his public statements, presenting a state where family and work became increasingly tight.[^10]

"Zero negative reviews" is a media label, not a perfectly verifiable personality conclusion. Jiu Jiu Xie's importance is not whether he has truly never been criticized; the focus is on how he has consistently built his channel on researching, admitting trial-and-error, and repeated correction. This low-dramatic style of work is rare in the online environment driven by sensational headlines.

He once said: "I hope to step back into the background someday."[^4] Reading this statement now feels more like a pre-acknowledgment of the creator's life cycle. The person on screen will age, platform rules will change, and audiences will change; what remains is not a fixed persona, but the methodology that is still willing to verify, organize, and explain.
