> **30-second overview**: Liù Zhǐyuān did not first learn to please the algorithm before starting to make effects. Lùn Zǐyuān encountered the fully English After Effects interface in high school, breaking down a software that was originally hard to enter into a series of tutorials and experimental videos. When special effects became the language of his channel, he also moved the same set of skills into client work, the sixvfx special effects tutorial website, and the Infinite Design College. This is the story of a Taiwanese creator turning behind-the-scenes processes into public courses.

In 2011, Lùn Zǐyuān was still groping for what he wanted to do in the future. He began self-studying from the English interface of After Effects, and later put his learning process and image experiments online. According to public interview records, he would break current events into ideas that could be visualized, and use Notion to list props and draw storyboards, then use Premiere and After Effects to complete the video in stages.[^2]

The counterintuitive point of this character story is not "a young person succeeds through the internet." More worth remembering is: Liù Zhǐyuān turned the work that is easiest for the audience to ignore—special effects, storyboards, props, editing, and teaching—into the theme of the channel itself. When he laid out the software interface, shooting process, and post-production time in front of the audience, what the audience saw was not just the result, but how the result was made.

📝 **Curator's note**: Most celebrity stories start with the persona in front of the camera. Liù Zhǐyuān's entrance is behind the camera, where the technical work that is usually not written in the credits becomes his most recognizable language.

## How a Fully English Interface Becomes a Starting Point

The 2024 public interview records from Nanhua University's Department of Fine Arts show that Lùn Zǐyuān had already self-taught Adobe After Effects during high school. That page also uses time points to divide the channel's growth: about 3,000 subscribers in January 2016, 100,000 in January 2019, and one million in January 2024. These numbers are the records compiled by the school's page at that time, and should not be misappropriated as the channel's current unchanged statistics.[^2]

From the book introduction compiled by Geizi, Lùn Zǐyuān was not a model student who was pushed along by the school in his early years. The author described him as being questioned by classmates with "it's just making a video and adding subtitles," and this sarcasm actually prompted him to face the fully English software interface. This is a secondary retelling of the content in the book, and this article treats it as a book narrative, not rewriting it as Lùn Zǐyuān's original words in another interview.[^3]

He did not treat learning the software as a personal secret afterwards. The sixvfx special effects tutorial website and the Infinite Design College turned "I can do it" into "others can follow along." The Apple Podcasts program introduction also placed him in the context of parent-child career dialogue, discussing how families face dreams, skills, and uncertainty when children want to become YouTubers.[^4]

📝 **Curator's note**: Self-learning is most easily described as a myth of one person's willpower. But the truly Taiwan-flavored part here is that he left behind the walls he had crashed into, allowing others to take fewer detours.

## Special Effects Are Not Decorations, but a Kind of Content View

In the creation interview data, Liù Zhǐyuān said he would treat special effects as embellishments, while the main axis of the video still lies in the content to be expressed. He also mentioned that when a topic cannot be completed after thinking for more than two days, he will look for alternative solutions, because once the video is uploaded, it will exist permanently.[^2]

Putting these two sentences together, one can see a work ethic that is not very much like a "show-off celebrity." The number of special effects is not the goal, it must serve what the video wants to say. The creator's technical ability must therefore obey the rhythm of the content. This is also why his works can be close to technology education, life experiments, and entertainment at the same time, and not just stay in the narrow category of software teaching.

The 2024 Nanhua University interview added another time section. The school's page records that Liù Zhǐyuān's YouTube subscription count was 1.5 million on April 26, 2024, and his Instagram follower count was 2.48 million. The page also retains the discussion between the interviewer and the interviewee on the market, entrepreneurship, and value accumulation. These numbers should be kept in their dates, because the creator's scale will change, but the source time will not.[^2]

## From Creation to Teaching, and Then to the Team

The public interview data mentioned that Liù Zhǐyuān's team is connected remotely, and they have also planned tools for automatic editing and automatic subtitling, hoping to let creators spend less time dealing with trivial work. His direction is no longer just one person shooting, but trying to deal with the workflow that creators encounter every day.[^2]

When a YouTuber starts to do education platforms, tools, and teams, the problem shifts from "what should the next video shoot" to "where does the creator spend his time every day." Liù Zhǐyuān's direction at least raises three work questions: which processes need human judgment, which repetitive work can be handed over to tools, and which knowledge is worth organizing into courses. This makes his channel not only a work display, but also a set of answers to content labor.

In the 2022 Podcast program, the program introduction described Liù Zhǐyuān as the founder of sixvfx and the Infinite Design College, and linked his self-directed learning experience with "Your Dream May Be a Bit Risky." This is the program's introduction to the guests, not equivalent to an independent financial audit, so this article only uses it to support the career identity and program topic, and does not expand the program's brief income statement into an accurate business conclusion.[^4]

The Geizi article extracts several methods from the book content: using competitions, customer feedback, portfolios, and search results to turn skills into recognizable services for others. These are the author's summaries of the book's content, and also the materials for understanding how Liù Zhǐyuān brings technology into the market, but they are not a success formula that all self-learners in Taiwan can copy.[^3]

📝 **Curator's note**: Teaching skills to others does not necessarily make oneself lose an advantage. Liù Zhǐyuān's case actually shows that teaching may turn a personal skill into a community, a service, and a professional language.

## The Cycle of Works, Courses, and Communities

Liù Zhǐyuān's creative path also has an easily overlooked detail: the work is not the end, but the entrance to the next learning question. The Flip Education of Parent-Child Heaven takes his experience as a self-directed learning and error. Geizi puts the book's compiled competitions, customer feedback, portfolios, and search results in the context of "how to make skills visible." Putting the two data together, one can see a cycle: first make the work, then find the problem from the feedback, then organize the problem into teaching, and finally let the teaching return to the new work.[^1] [^3]

This cycle also explains why his content is not only about displaying special effects. The display can only prove a certain result. Teaching must break the result into steps, admit which parts are easy to fail, and give another person the opportunity to redo it on different equipment, at different times, and with different experiences. The Nanhua University interview puts this continuous creation of value in the context of entrepreneurship and the market, rather than just in the software operation.[^2]

📝 **Curator's note**: The real publicity is not that everyone has to become Liù Zhǐyuān, but that after a person's profession is organized, it can allow the next person to find the entrance with a little less effort.

## After the Traffic Came, the Story Did Not End

In October 2024, DailyView reported that Liù Zhǐyuān won the "Annual Most Valuable Internet Celebrity YouTuber." The award uses internet interaction, positive volume, and related data as the evaluation context. This award can prove that he won the award in that year, but it cannot directly prove that he is the most important or most influential creator in Taiwan.[^5]

Sanli News Network's report on the same day supplemented that Liù Zhǐyuān talked about short videos and overseas markets at the ceremony, and described the traffic expansion with the highest 150 million views of a video. This is the interview statement in the report, and this article retains the attribution, not writing the highest number of views of a single video as the channel's average performance.[^6]

Zhixin News's same-day report also listed Liù Zhǐyuān as the winner of the award, and mentioned that he won the award at the age of 27. It and Sanli's report jointly explain that the award event was indeed reported by multiple media, but both revolve around the same ceremony, and cannot be mistakenly counted as two independent awards.[^7]

Liù Zhǐyuān's traffic growth therefore has two different faces. One is the subscription, viewing, and awards, which can be listed as numbers by the platform or media. The other is what he has been doing in the early days: breaking the video into prop lists, storyboards, editing, special effects, and teaching, and organizing personal experience into knowledge that others can use. The former makes him visible, the latter makes him worth writing into Taiwan's internet culture history.

📝 **Curator's note**: Traffic is not the end of the creator's story. For Liù Zhǐyuān, the real question is whether technology can still be taught after the viewing count becomes large, whether the team can still work, and whether the content can still make things clear.

## After a Video Is Uploaded

In a 2020 interview, Liù Zhǐyuān used a very specific metaphor: after the video is uploaded, it will exist permanently, and seeing a work that is not satisfactory is like having something in the body that is not comfortable whether it is cut or not. This is not simply perfectionism, but the special sense of time of the platform content. Traditional homework is only seen by the teacher or client at a certain point in time. YouTube videos will be searched, recommended, and re-watched, and creators find it difficult to pretend that it only exists on the day of publication.[^1]

Therefore, his editing process has never been to pile up special effects until the end. First do the rough cut in Premiere, see if the video is smooth, then use marks to separate the paragraphs that need special effects, and finally enter After Effects according to the topic. This process divides creativity and engineering into two stages, and also lets team members know when to join in. To the audience, this may only be a video of a few minutes. To the producer, it is a project that requires scripts, props, storyboards, editing, and post-production to work together.[^1]

He also does not regard "having ideas" as innate talent. In the interview, Liù Zhǐyuān said that he would practice finding things that can be turned into pictures in life and current events, but also admitted that not every thing can be turned into a video. Sometimes he is just particularly moved by a certain topic, and then finds excitement in the production process. This statement is closer to the creator's actual work than "inspiration explodes every day": inspiration needs habits, and also needs to abandon unsuitable plans.[^1]

There is another level of technical education here. When Liù Zhǐyuān breaks the special effects into observable steps, he is equivalent to lowering the psychological cost of the first attempt for many Taiwanese students who are not familiar with post-production software. In the interview data saved by Nanhua University, he talked to students about continuously creating value and finding a place to play their value at the present time. This sentence should not be treated as an inspirational slogan, but should be put back into his work experience to understand: value is not an abstract talent, but something that gradually becomes "you can't do it without you."[^2]

## From Student Identity to Market Presence

The Nanhua University interview also kept his retrospective on the student identity. He mentioned that even if one fails in school, there is still time to readjust, so he treated the school stage as a trial period that can set goals and bear costs. This idea is easily misinterpreted as a request for students to start making money early, but his speech actually discussed direction, profession, and value accumulation at the same time, and did not take income as the only measure.[^2]

The Geizi article breaks this road into several observable actions based on the book content: participating in competitions, leaving customer feedback, organizing portfolios, and letting others be able to search for themselves. These suggestions have a common point: they require creators to turn "I think I can" into externally verifiable works. For design and special effects work, the portfolio is not an addition, but the menu that lets strangers know what problems they can handle.[^3]

But the portfolio also brings new pressure. When skills are packaged as services, creators must respond to customer constraints, deadlines, and budgets. When services are put into YouTube, creators must also respond to audience expectations and platform rhythms. Liù Zhǐyuān later did videos, tutorial websites, online schools, and tool development at the same time, precisely because a single work is difficult to bear all the work. Some people need to see the result, some people need to learn the steps, and some people need to complete the editing faster. Different products deal with different needs, but they are all based on the same special effects and video capabilities.[^1] [^3] [^4]

The Apple Podcasts program puts this experience in a parent-child dialogue: children say they want to become YouTubers, and parents should support or oppose. Liù Zhǐyuān, as a guest, is not invited to guarantee that this road will definitely succeed, but to discuss how to find direction, how to self-directed learning, and that there is no need to be immediately judged as backward when there is no dream. This makes his story leave the single framework of "celebrity inspiration," and enter the question of how Taiwan families imagine new jobs.[^4]

📝 **Curator's note**: Liù Zhǐyuān's story not only answers "how to become popular," but also answers a more difficult question: when school, family, and market have not prepared a name for a new profession, how does a person speak his skills to others.

## Another Career Imagination of Taiwanese Creators

Liù Zhǐyuān's importance is not only in making special effects videos, nor only in winning internet reputation awards. He made a road that is usually divided into different professions appear on the same person: self-learner, freelancer, YouTuber, lecturer, author, founder of educational platform, and tool developer.

The fully English software needs time, content creation is affected by platform distribution, team work needs management, and educational products must face the market. Liù Zhǐyuān himself mentioned in the interview that creators must first understand the audience and the topic, and then decide how to modify the work. These conditions explain that self-directed learning is not to exclude school, but to put feedback, trial and error, and market into the learning process. Writing his experience as "as long as you persist, you will succeed" actually erases the technical threshold and uncertainty he encountered.

His working methods provide a set of observable steps. Starting from a software interface, break down the incomprehensible parts. Then organize the problems left by client work into teaching, turn the audience's questions into works, and turn the works into courses. Finally, the courses return to the new creative needs. This is a working order accumulated by a creator over many years, and also the way he turns personal skills into public resources.[^1] [^2] [^3]

In the 2024 award report, Liù Zhǐyuān said that he once increased his subscription count in a short period of time due to short videos and overseas markets. This reminds readers that the change of the platform does indeed rewrite the creator's scale, but it also reminds us not to retroactively turn his early technical accumulation into his later traffic password.[^5] [^6]

📝 **Curator's note**: What is worth preserving in Taiwan's internet culture is not only who is the most popular. More worth preserving is that someone has turned the original hidden skills into public resources that others can learn, use, and continue to rewrite.

## The Sayings of Family, School, and New Jobs

Liù Zhǐyuān's story did not only happen in front of the comptuer. Apple Podcasts put him in a parent-child dialogue of "children want to become YouTubers, should parents support or oppose." This arrangement is very important, because it pulls internet creation back to the family decision-making from personal interest: does the child need a sentence of support, or does he need to first understand the skills, time, income, and risk? The program page can confirm the discussion topic and the identity of the guests, but it cannot provide a set of universal answers for all families.[^4]

The Nanhua University interview puts the question back to school and career. The school's page records the discussion, including continuously creating value, finding a place to play value at the present time, and thinking about the market and entrepreneurship. If these words are only picked up as inspirational slogans, they will lose their original working background. Looking back at Liù Zhǐyuān's experience, they refer to a method of repeated adjustment: first break down the software that cannot be done, then obtain feedback through works, and finally decide which skills are worth teaching to others.[^2]

📝 **Curator's note**: Celebrity articles often write school as a place that is only useful after the creator leaves. Liù Zhǐyuān's materials show that school, family, platform, and market are actually naming a new profession at the same time, just using different languages.

## Put the Traffic Data Back to the Date

The 2024 media report made another turning point in Liù Zhǐyuān's career. The early data focused on After Effects, self-learning, and teaching. The later report talked about short videos, Instagram fan growth, overseas markets, and the high number of views of a single video. This is not a contradiction between the front and back, but the result of the same set of video capabilities entering different platforms and facing different distribution logic.[^5] [^6] [^8]

However, the traffic numbers cannot explain the value of the creator alone. The award report records the evaluation of a specific year, and the viewing numbers quoted by the media also have their statistical time and narrative purposes. Taking "the video creates over 100 million traffic per year" as a long-term income or stable influence directly will exceed the scope that the source can support. A safer reading is: Liù Zhǐyuān once transplanted his mature special effects capabilities to short videos and overseas content, and was seen this turn in the public report in 2024.[^5] [^6] [^8]

📝 **Curator's note**: What really needs to be preserved is not an ever-rising traffic curve, but how the creator re-explains his technology, audience, and working methods when the platform changes.
