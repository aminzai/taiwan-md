> **30-Second Overview:** Park Seong-eun officially joined the Fubon Angels in 2026, becoming one of the "Five Korean Pillars" for the Fubon Guardians. Her career trajectory follows a clear path: first being recognized by Taiwanese fans through exchange activities, and subsequently being formally integrated into the team's primary roster. A biographical summary is also available on the Wikipedia entry for "Park Seong-eun."[^4]

She did not appear in Taiwan for the first time in 2026. Prior to joining officially, she had already built audience familiarity through exchange activities, including events at the Taipei Dome and cross-league appearances with the club.

This path of "exchange first, permanent second" reduced the sense of unfamiliarity during her official debut and made it easier for fans to view her as a core member of the squad.

## Official Joining of Fubon in 2026

Before the 2026 season, Fubon announced Park Seong-eun's addition to the Fubon Angels; a report re-published by _Fan News_ described the season-opening lineup of the "Guardians Cheerleaders' 5 Korean Pillars" as consisting of Lee Ya-young, Lee Ju-eun, Nam Min-jeong, Lee Ha-jin, and Park Seong-eun.[^1]

During the "Fubon Angels Season Opening Press Conference" in late March 2026, the digital edition of _United Daily News_ / _udn.com_ reported on several Korean members appearing on stage together; Park Seong-eun attended wearing an eye patch on her right eye due to a stye, explaining to the media that her eye was recovering and expressing gratitude to Fubon for providing the opportunity.[^2]

At the Xinzhuang home opener in April, she stood on the Xinzhuang cheering stage for the first time as an official member; in a pre-game interview, she admitted that "every part of it, from the cheering to the opening performance, had me trembling."[^3]

## Style and Recognition

Park Seong-eun's stage presence is characterized by her blonde styling, sharp movement lines, and high-energy performance style. Within group dances, she can quickly align with formations and maintain a strong presence during camera transitions.

This dual capability—being both "scalable for solo focus" and "effective in group choreography"—gives her high utility in the production of CPBL home game content, making her suitable for large-scale events and high-traffic short-form video environments.

> Park Seong-eun's importance lies in the fact that she did not "become an overnight sensation out of thin air," but was first seen and then institutionalized. This is a signal of the maturity of the Korean cheerleader ecosystem in Taiwan.

## Real-world Challenges During Early Membership

New members must find their rhythm amidst the simultaneous pressures of language, culture, and social media discourse; for Korean cheerleaders, "adaptation" is often not a single task, but an ongoing process that repeats throughout the season. The high density of public appearances around the start of the season also brought physical conditions (such as eye discomfort) directly before the camera, serving as one of the clues for audiences to recognize the "professional" reality of cheerleading.[^2]

## Why She is a Pop Culture Figure

Park Seong-eun is considered a pop culture figure because her career milestones are highly synchronized with changes in Taiwan's baseball stadium culture. Her joining marks an environment where Taiwan's professional baseball has developed the capacity to "absorb, transform, and retain" Korean cheerleading talent.

When an exchange guest can transition into a permanent fixture within a short period, it indicates that the sports entertainment structure of the Taiwan market is moving from an experimental phase toward a normalized era.
