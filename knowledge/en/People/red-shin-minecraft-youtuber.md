> **30-Second Overview:** On July 15, 2026, A Shen's official channel showed 3.31 million subscribers and 5,622 videos; the latest collaborative Minecraft long-form video was uploaded only one day prior, yet its description still read "Retiring, I only make videos when I feel like it." He once claimed to have uploaded daily for eleven years, leaving his full-time YouTube career in 2021 and announcing a halt to uploads on his main channel in 2023, while continuing to produce "Gaoshénma" with Ma Tian via live streams. Seeing these three statuses together makes the meaning of retirement clear: daily updates were no longer an open commitment, nor had creation permanently ended.

> **Verification Note | A Shen's Real Name?** Several conflicting names circulate online; A Shen has responded to circulating rumors using [official secondary channel videos](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F7jWpA8_RAs), but reliable public data is insufficient to confirm his real name. This article uses the name "A Shen," which he has publicly maintained for a long time, and does not reveal his identity through search summaries or third-party compilations.

## New Video Appears on Homepage After Three Years of Hiatus

On July 15, 2026, A Shen's YouTube homepage displayed two seemingly contradictory messages. The first line of the channel description still read, "Entering retirement mode!! Retiring, I only make videos when I feel like it." Yet, at the top of the video page was a collaborative Minecraft video uploaded one day prior, running for approximately 49 minutes and 59 seconds. The footage featured fake monster mods and multiple creators; the official page listed it as a regular long-form video released on July 14th, not a live stream recording.[^1]

On the same day, the official page showed 3.31 million subscribers and 5,622 videos. Looking back, new videos were marked five days ago, eight days ago, twelve days ago, and two weeks ago. There were also verifiable main channel long-form videos on June 26 and July 7, 2026.[^2] This set of dates refutes a simple conclusion: the "permanent hiatus" mentioned in news headlines from 2023 did not result in the main channel remaining blacked out.

A Shen established his channel in 2009 and publicly shared Minecraft content by 2012.[^3][^4] Video recording can be edited and uploaded, while live streaming occurs in real-time; these two distribution systems are different. Understanding this difference is necessary to see why the retirement message and the new video can coexist on the homepage without one having to be immediately dismissed as a lie.

On July 14, 2026, the main channel did indeed have a new long-form video. This video turned "return" into too broad a question: what kind of working relationship was A Shen in when he retired across different years? The answer requires looking back at that once-a-day schedule.

## Channel Scheduled Eleven Days to Have an Episode Every Day

A Shen's official channel was established on May 11, 2009. The verifiable starting point is the channel creation; there is no reliable public record of the title of the first video.[^3] By July 2012, he had shared Minecraft content on Bahamut using his own account, and in a post, he asked: "What is this 'Peanut' drama www!" followed by, "Can any Bobs give me the correct answer XD?"[^4] The emojis and questions show the early conversational style of the channel: players seeking answers from viewers whom he had dubbed "Bobs" while playing. "Bob" later became a common fan nickname. Existing posts at least prove that he used this address in 2012, and this form of interaction was public.

The real shift was in the upload frequency. In 2022, A Shen recalled his work history during a Q&A on Dcard: "I have been running YouTube for 13 years; I uploaded daily for 11 of those years without missing a single day."[^5] This is his recollection of his career. For eleven years, there was an episode every day.

This eleven-year span crossed the era when YouTube transitioned in Taiwan from an interest site to a professional platform. When A Shen started his channel, "YouTuber" was not yet a term everyone understood. After he stopped daily uploads, game streaming, brand collaborations, agency involvement, and cross-channel cooperation had already become recognizable industry segments. The formation of the platform industry has no single starting date, but A Shen's body of work keeps that process year by year on the same channel. However, his self-description is enough to show the system. Beyond the total number of videos, "there will be one tomorrow" became a predictable supply. A TV schedule entrusts audience time to broadcasters. Personal YouTube channels allow for on-demand viewing and are influenced by recommendation systems. These are not the same, but fixed publishing brings similar life cues: viewers know an episode is coming, and creators know that the next slot cannot be empty.

This regularity exists in two directions on the platform. For viewers, missing a premiere does not require waiting for a TV rerun; the video remains in the channel library. However, daily new content still provides a fixed answer to "what to watch today." For creators, videos can be pre-recorded and scheduled, and collaborators may participate, making the actual working hours invisible on the public page. The supply side never stopped: regardless of backend arrangements, the next slot must appear in front of the audience on time.

Eleven years lasted longer than many platform trends. Game updates, viewer tastes, and video formats change, but the calendar advances one day at a time. The regularity of the supply side allows viewers to condition viewing habits. It cannot prove the same emotion for all Taiwanese children, but it genuinely forms a platform timeline.

In 2021, his agency described the channel's scale with over 4,500 videos and 1.7 billion total views.[^6] By 2026, the homepage showed 5,622. The two snapshots cannot be subtracted directly: videos might be deleted, set to private, or re-released, and whether live streams are included changes the numbers. They show a massive archive. The dates of each video reveal the publishing rhythm.

The number of videos is inventory; views continue to flow after publication. Old works are searched, recommended, or rewatched, increasing total views. Even if a channel pauses uploads, the accumulated content has not stopped being played. Therefore, "retirement" does not mean there is no massive viewing afterward, nor does an increase in new videos directly translate into how many processes he personally completed. The platform homepage compresses works into a string of numbers, while the production relationship is hidden in the end screens and descriptions of each video.

There are still unseen parts behind the scenes. Selection, gameplay, recording, editing, thumbnails, and publishing may be completed by different people, inventory, and processes; public data cannot reconstruct the division of labor across periods. Counting over five thousand videos as a single person's ascetic practice erases potential collaboration. Treating on-screen gameplay as non-labor also overlooks how content becomes a sustainable product. What can remain on paper is an open scheduling system that delivers the next episode daily. Once the system was established, the real question—who keeps filling tomorrow?

## Cannot Blame the Algorithm for Everything

In 2011, the official YouTube Creator Playbook encouraged regular, high-frequency updates: frequent uploads help videos reach more potential viewers, and periodic releases can form a reliable structure for audiences. However, the typical frequency of successful channels cited in the handbook was actually one video per week.[^7] A Shen's self-description of daily uploads far exceeded this early recommendation.

The platform encourages regularity but does not explicitly demand daily uploads. Daily uploads are usually influenced by the recommendation system, audience expectations, collaboration needs, and revenue. Public data cannot quantify how much each force contributed in A Shen's case. Two studies on creator labor in Taiwan also place the platform algorithm, sponsors, viewer preferences, view counts, and life boundaries on one chart.[^8] This chart is closer to reality than "the algorithm commands the creator." The platform displays views, recommended traffic, and subscriber changes in the backend, from which creators judge topics based on curves. Audiences form expectations through clicks and comments, while collaborations exchange visibility for commercial value. The rules may not state "a video must be uploaded tomorrow," but every signal can increase the cost of interruption. The production competition mentioned in Taiwanese academic research involves dedicating rest time for planning, sourcing, shooting, and editing, rather than just scheduling with a line of code.[^8]

This also explains why platform work might form a fixed schedule without a traditional clock-in/out system. Creators simultaneously face the performance of the previous video, the topic of the next video, the games audiences are discussing, and whether collaborations can be delivered on time. Public research describes this collective pressure but cannot answer the daily trade-offs for A Shen. Daily uploads are the visible result of organizing these conditions; the homepage only shows the finished product. High frequency also brings rewards. It builds a massive archive, lets viewers know the channel is active, and becomes A Shen's clearest public commitment. The simultaneous appearance of high frequency and success cannot be reversed as cause and effect. Topic format, audience demand, and working resources also influence who can continue updating.[^8] When A Shen shared his experience on Dcard, he first said "though it cannot be called successful," hoping to "clear up some confusion about YouTube for the questioner."[^5] That was a sharing of experience after going through a professional path, not a winning formula applicable to everyone.

In 2026, YouTube's official statements changed to prioritize quality and sustainable scheduling, explicitly stating that rest would not be penalized by the algorithm.[^9] This does not mean rest is without cost: audience viewing habits may change, and collaborations and income are not guaranteed to wait. It only clarifies one thing: the platform's current public rules do not write rest as a violation subject to penalty. A Shen's own turning point still requires returning to his words. In 2021, he described making videos and playing games as seemingly easy, yet feeling anxious upon waking up.[^11] Later, he summarized it with an even shorter sentence: "Don't want YT to tie me down anymore."[^10]

![A Shen on the left wearing a mask facing the camera, and on the right are his Twitter post announcing the halt of eleven years of daily uploads and a waiting room illustration](/article-images/people/ashan-stops-daily-upload-2021.webp)

_The March 2021 hiatus announcement placed A Shen's portrait, text declaration, and waiting room illustration in one image. Photo: A Shen Twitter, reported by DailyView.[Fair use editorial commentary via DailyView](https://dailyview.tw/popular/detail/9864)._

The post stated, "Starting tomorrow, I will rest for a while, stopping the daily uploads that have been continuous for 11 years."[^11] The text in the image also mentioned caring for family and the pressure of his own physical and mental state. This was the reason he publicly gave for resting at the time; this article does not extend it into a medical diagnosis. What was announced was "resting for a while" and stopping daily uploads, without pre-determining whether to make videos for every subsequent year.

The video description during the same Q&A listed editor AF, subtitle creator Yu Miao, and cover designers Junp, RiuPedro, Osla, and Kreps.[^18] This list only applies to that specific video and cannot represent the division of labor across eleven years, but it shows a glimpse behind the scenes of daily uploads. The audience ultimately sees A Shen's face, voice, and gameplay progression. Before publication, the footage may have undergone editing, subtitling, and multiple cover designs. Daily updates were thus both an open commitment and the result of various works converging on the same release day.

The description also reminds us that the "personal" in a personal channel first refers to the public entity, not necessarily meaning all processes are handled by one person. A Shen is responsible for the channel name, on-screen presence, and speaking to the audience; collaborators may have their names on the finished product. As for how long collaborations last, how they are priced, or who decides the upload date, current data does not reveal this. Clearly stating the known division of labor is closer to the true path of the work than trying to deduce a single person's routine from five thousand videos.

The same "retirement" points to two different things: first, stopping daily uploads; second, later announcing the main channel hiatus.

## He First Stopped Daily Uploads, Then Announced Main Channel Hiatus

In 2021, A Shen stopped maintaining his years-long daily uploads. Both the contemporaneous explanations and the subsequent Dcard recollections described this change as retirement, pointing to stepping down from a full-time YouTube job and switching to "casual uploading," not ceasing creation entirely.[^11] The commitment withdrawn at that time was precise: there would be an episode every day.

In the complete statement he used on Dcard the following year, he said, "Until last year I left my full-time YouTube job and switched to casual uploading."[^5] "Full-time job" refers to the working relationship; "casual uploading" still implies uploading. The same sentence encompasses both retirement and continued creation: the channel can have works, but the creator no longer defines whether they are working by delivering daily episodes.

![A composite image of A Shen in a gray suit adjusting a red tie on the left, and four adults and children posing in front of a white background on the right](/article-images/people/ashan-capsule-2021.webp)

_Composite image used for CAPSULE's 2021 character report, anchoring the node where A Shen continued to work publicly after stopping daily uploads. Photo: CAPSULE.[Fair use editorial commentary via CAPSULE](https://www.capsuleinc.cc/creators-news-inner/867)._

The people in the photo did not leave public work because of stopping daily uploads. In September 2021, he still worked with his agency, and the old channel archive and new content continued to operate. He only referred back to this turning point as "last year" the following year.[^6] The order of time is important here: in 2021, daily uploads ended first; there was no simultaneous announcement that the main channel would stop publishing videos from then on.

In September 2023, the second announcement advanced things further. The announcement image read, "《Major Notice》A Shen Channel Permanently Halting Updates starting 10/15."[^12] After October 15th, the main channel stopped publishing videos.[^12] Live streams might still appear irregularly, and "Gaoshénma" produced with Ma Tian continued as usual.[^13] Recorded videos, live streams, and collaborative shows were thus divided into three lines.

The main channel hiatus was a clear decision at the time and cannot be reduced to merely canceling a schedule. The live streams and Gaoshénma were exceptions mentioned in the same farewell and cannot be magnified into leaving YouTube. That announcement closed one door; two others remained lit.

Only by placing the three nodes together is the person not frozen by a single title. The new videos in 2026 rewrite the aftermath without erasing the period when the main channel truly went dark in 2023.

## Gaoshénma Continues, Main Channel Also Uploads Again

The farewell in 2023 left another door open from the start. "Gaoshénma" is a show produced by A Shen and Hong Kong creator Ma Tian with its own distribution channel; it is not a playlist on the main channel. The two of them appear together on camera. Public data does not answer how planning, editing, revenue, and decision-making are allocated, nor can on-screen interaction judge their friendship.[^14]

By 2026, this door was still open. The Gaoshénma official channel had new videos on June 10, July 11, and July 15. The last one was uploaded on the day of this article's verification.[^15] The collaborative show explicitly stated as retained in 2023 continued three years later.

![The Gaoshénma thumbnail shows blonde A Shen and black-haired Ma Tian standing left and right, with a green melon dessert and the text "Seasonal Goods" in the center](/article-images/people/ashan-gaoshenma-2026.webp)

_Gaoshénma official video thumbnail from July 15, 2026: The A Shen and Ma Tian collaborative show is still updating. Photo: Gaoshénma Official YouTube.[Fair use editorial commentary via official video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CAOpagY34UI)._

A Shen's own main channel has also lit up again. There were Minecraft long-form videos on June 26, 2026, and July 14, 2026, with other collaborations in between. The official agency page listed new videos consecutively in early July.[^16] The opening video was not an isolated upload; the main channel had been releasing regular long-form videos on multiple dates.

These dates do not constitute a new schedule. June 26 to July 7 was eleven days, and July 7 to 14 was seven days. "Five days ago" and "eight days ago" on the homepage also shift depending on the verification date. The daily update era allowed viewers to know there would be content tomorrow; the 2026 list can only show a period of intense production in retrospect.

The collaborative show remained active, and the main channel that had been announced as paused resumed uploading. High output and the self-description of retirement could coexist because production volume and public obligation are two different scales: the homepage shows the release date, but deadlines for collaboration and internal production remain off-screen. Daily delivery is no longer A Shen's full-time qualification to promise viewers.

## New Videos Exist, But No Fixed Next Episode Day

Returning to the July 15, 2026, homepage, over five thousand videos are still listed on the channel, with gaps between recent uploads sometimes only a few days. The description still retains "Retiring, I only make videos when I feel like it."[^17] This verification did not find any information on the homepage promising a fixed release date.

"Retirement" became A Shen's viewing note for the channel. It does not guarantee low output, nor does it explain how he works day by day off-camera. It revokes the certainty that viewers once held. During the eleven years of daily uploads, opening the channel meant expecting tomorrow. Now, one still encounters new videos, and the next meeting is known only on the day of release.

The Minecraft video from July 14, 2026, remains at the top of the homepage. Looking up, the description says retirement. Looking down, there is no date for the next video. New videos exist, but a fixed next episode day was not written. This is the boundary between two types of channel time, leaving a blank space that turns waiting into an unannounced encounter.
