> **30-Second Overview:** 3 Degrees C is Hsieh Meng-hang’s YouTube creative brand. The name does not come from 3C products; rather, it stems from his time studying in mainland China, where he took cold showers in 3°C winter weather, leading friends to call him "3 Degrees." He edits together laughter, dance, memes, challenges, and true stories into entertainment content, and later participated in music video production as a director, cinematographer, and editor. The counter-intuitive aspect of 3 Degrees C is that while he appears to rely on absurd content to make people laugh, what he has truly accumulated is a creative methodology that organizes one person’s physical performance, editing rhythm, and image production workflow into a sustainable long-term practice.

In a 2022 interview, Hsieh Meng-hang said that when many people asked where the name "3 Degrees" came from, his answer was taking cold showers in 3°C low temperatures while studying in mainland China. [^1] This is a small, even slightly absurd life snippet, yet it became the name by which he was recognized online. A November 2025 profile from 1111 Human Resources Bank completed the picture of his path: he went from a cosmetology student to a full-time audio-visual creator, and at the time of the interview, he had accumulated seven years of creative experience, with channel subscribers exceeding 1.39 million. [^2]

This path is most easily misread as "one person filming funny videos, who later became famous." A more accurate description is that 3 Degrees C places a memorable character into a set of constantly changing video formats. The content on Ming Pao, including laugh-holding, dancing, and memes, does not look like the same program schedule; instead, it resembles a creator testing how far the audience is willing to follow him. [^1] The topics change, but the audience still recognizes the same person.

## The Name Precedes the Persona

Internet names often resemble brand design: positioning is defined first, followed by a visual and tonal arrangement. 3 Degrees’ name, however, works in reverse. It began as a nickname used by a friend to address him, and only later became the channel name, social media account, and work identifier. This origin makes "3 Degrees" less like a carefully packaged character and more like a joke left by a friend in the winter.

The detail of "taking a cold shower at 3°C" is useful not because it proves how much 3 Degrees can endure, but because it connects an abstract internet nickname back to the body. Readers can imagine the cold water hitting the skin and understand why this name requires little further explanation. It is a naming story with few objects and clear actions.

Regarding where he studied in mainland China, the only directly verifiable profile article states only that he "studied in mainland China," without specifying the city, province, or school. [^1] Therefore, this article does not treat internet encyclopedia summaries as definitive curriculum vitae, nor does it push vague descriptions like "second-tier city" into specific place names. This data gap should be preserved, because the reliability of biographical writing lies not only in supplementing details, but also in clearly marking which details have not yet been proven by reliable sources.

> **📝 Curator’s Note:** A good internet name does not necessarily start with a business plan. Sometimes, the silly things a person has done leave a stronger mark of recognition than a set of brand strategies.

Ming Pao organizes 3 Degrees’ early channel content into diverse themes such as laugh-holding, dancing, and memes. [^1] These terms do not look like the same program schedule; instead, they resemble a creator testing how far the audience is willing to follow him. Dance requires bodily memory, laugh-holding relies on expression and rhythm, and memes require recognition of a shared context. The sense of character in 3 Degrees C emerges precisely from being repeatedly recognized across these different forms.

## From Street Experiments to Shared Laughter with the Audience

In 2018, EBC Entertainment reported on a street fashion experiment involving 3 Degrees and Xiao Ma. The price gap between the two outfits was significant: one cost approximately NT$80,000, the other around NT$1,500. The two exchanged clothes and then asked female interviewees on the street for their preferences. [^3]

What remains from this video is not the conclusion that "luxury is always better" or that "affordable clothes can win." After the clothes were exchanged, the interviewees’ choices still centered on Xiao Ma, with on-site feedback even pushing the issue toward skin tone and facial features. [^3] Yahoo News’ same-day reprint recorded the same two rounds of experiments, preserving the context of netizens discussing body proportions and clothing cuts. [^4]

This is a scene well-suited to 3 Degrees C: first design a question everyone understands, then let the experimental results make the question more awkward. Entertainment is not responsible for tidying up pretty answers for the audience. It places a person in front of the camera, forcing them to bear evaluations that everyone thought would only happen to others.

> **📝 Curator’s Note:** 3 Degrees C’s humor often lies not in "he is very good at being funny," but in his willingness to place himself in an experiment where the punchline might backfire and sting him.

This also explains why "absurdity" alone cannot summarize his creativity. Absurdity is merely the surface style. Beneath it lie topic design, on-site reactions, editing order, and the work of the protagonist bearing the consequences. What the audience sees is an absurd test; what the creator handles is a small-scale social observation.

## Million Subscribers Are Not the Endpoint, but a Renaming of Work Style

The 2025 1111 interview places 3 Degrees C within the career context of "full-time creation." The article mentions his journey from a cosmetology apprentice to a full-time creator, discussing creative inspiration, traffic, bottlenecks, trolls, and his original intention to continue bringing positive feelings to the audience. [^2] These phrases together indicate that YouTube has become more than just a place for him to upload videos; it is a job that requires maintaining output.

"Million-subscriber YouTuber" is a convenient label for media, but it easily compresses the process into a single number. The more interesting aspect of 3 Degrees C’s case is that he did not limit his character to a single series. The laugh-holding, dancing, and memes recorded by Ming Pao already show that 3 Degrees C’s early creation was not a single program format. [^1] This is not starting over every time, but testing different entry points for viewing under the same name.

For creators, traffic brings not only applause, but also higher demands for repetition. The audience wants the next video to still feel like "3 Degrees," yet it cannot simply be the previous video with a new title. Thus, the persona must remain stable, the topics must change, and the editing must make the changes still appear to belong to the same person. This is a very specific form of labor, not merely inspiration.

> **📝 Curator’s Note:** Subscriber counts push people into the spotlight; what keeps the channel alive is the fine work of repeatedly deciding "how to be different from last time" in each instance.

## The Person in Front of the Lens Also Works Behind It

3 Degrees C appears not only on his own YouTube channel. Brook Image Culture’s official case study page records another identity for him in music video production. The 2024 case "Meteor Crossing" lists Xie Meng-hang as the director and notes he was responsible for post-production. [^5] In the case of Hui Chun Dan’s "Fresh Flowers," the official page lists 3 Degrees as director, cinematographer, and editor, with production completed by Brook Image Culture Co., Ltd. [^6]

These two cases cause the name "3 Degrees C" to shift slightly. The audience is familiar with a person who is placed into challenges and memes. The image production site, however, requires a different set of judgments: how to arrange camera angles, how to make actors’ movements connect, and how to edit footage into a time structure with起伏 (ups and downs) in post-production. The former places oneself at the center of the content; the latter organizes the entire work.

The official case study page also places the same person in different work columns. From director to cinematographer to editor, 3 Degrees is not just a person appearing on camera, but someone who can complete the visual work. [^5] [^6] This overlap between front-stage and back-stage roles is perhaps more accurate than "internet celebrity crossing into image production." Because he never stayed only in front of the lens.

Other case study pages on Brook Image Culture’s site reveal the production environment and collaborative network in which 3 Degrees operates. "Classic 90 Yingge Old Street Photography Night" and "Racing Pigeon Tycoon" are both listed as the company’s featured cases. [^7] [^8] These pages are not a video list for 3 Degrees C’s channel, but they reveal another parallel work line: producing images for brands, events, or musical works, and producing entertainment content for one’s own audience, use similar tools but bear different responsibilities.

## Beyond Humor, There Is a Red Line of Authenticity

3 Degrees C’s content often begins with challenges, imitation, or absurd settings. The risk of such videos is that if the humor relies solely on others’ embarrassment, it easily divides the audience and the filmed subjects into two opposing sides. In the street fashion experiment, 3 Degrees included himself in the evaluation field, at least pulling some of the risk back onto himself. This does not mean every video is free of controversy, but rather that his creation has an observable ethical choice: whether the creator is willing to become part of the test.

This line becomes clearer after becoming a full-time creator. When a video is simultaneously entertainment, work, brand collaboration, and audience relationship, the cost of the joke is no longer paid only by the shooter. The 2025 interview mentions how he faces creative bottlenecks and trolls. [^2] These contents do not need to be packaged as success stories. They are more like daily maintenance after reaching a million subscribers: maintaining output, maintaining the credibility of the character, and maintaining the state of being able to continue uploading videos.

3 Degrees C’s importance does not lie in him representing all Taiwan YouTubers. That statement is too broad and cannot be supported by one person’s data. He is more like a clear slice, allowing us to see a path often ignored in Taiwan’s internet culture: a creator first gains attention using their own body and reactions, then uses planning, editing, and image production to turn that attention into sustainable work.

> **📝 Curator’s Note:** The gap between a person going from "being filmed to amuse the audience" to "deciding how the work is seen" is not fame, but a complete set of work capabilities.

## Ending: 3 Degrees Remains 3 Degrees

3 Degrees C’s story can start with a very cold bathroom, or it can start with a million-subscriber channel. The former explains how a name is remembered; the latter explains how attention accumulates. What truly connects the two is Xie Meng-hang repeatedly placing himself into different image problems.

He can become the person being evaluated in a street fashion experiment, and he can also stand behind the lens in a music video. He can use memes and challenges to make people laugh, and must also handle traffic, trolls, bottlenecks, and work rhythm. These are not footnotes to the "internet celebrity halo"; they are the profession of the creator itself.

Thus, 3 Degrees C is not just the owner of the name "3 Degrees." He is more like a work sample in Taiwan’s internet culture: turning a private joke into a public character, and then breaking down that public character back into planning, shooting, editing, and collaboration. From 3°C cold water to the final edit before a video goes live, this path has never been completed by a single magical idea.
