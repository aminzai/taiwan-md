# Morris Chang (張忠謀)

Morris Chang (張忠謀), a legendary entrepreneur hailed as the "Godfather of Semiconductors," is the founder of Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC). He created the world's first professional foundry company and pioneered a business model that reshaped the global technology industry ecosystem. From a Chinese executive at Texas Instruments in the United States to founding TSMC in Taiwan, his life trajectory witnessed the development of the global semiconductor industry and established Taiwan's critical position in the global tech supply chain.

## 30-Second Overview

**Why should the world know Morris Chang?**

TSMC, founded by Morris Chang, is the world's highest-valued foundry company and a cornerstone of modern digital civilization. Most advanced semiconductors globally—from smartphones and computers to AI chips—are manufactured by TSMC. The "pure-foundry" business model he pioneered allowed countless technology companies to focus solely on chip design without investing massive capital in building fabrication plants, completely reshaping the global tech industry ecosystem.

TSMC is known as Taiwan's "National Treasure," holding an irreplaceable strategic position in geopolitics. Morris Chang was one of the few individuals in the 20th century who truly changed industrial structures—he redefined the business boundaries of the semiconductor industry, enabling Taiwan to start from a foundry base and become a core node in the global technology supply chain.

Born in Ningbo, Zhejiang in 1931; founded TSMC in Taiwan in 1987: Morris Chang's life is a microcosm of Asia's technological rise in the 20th century.

## Early Life and Education

### Growing Up During Turbulent Times

**Birth and Family Background:**
On July 10, 1931, Morris Chang was born in Ningbo City, Zhejiang Province, China[^1]. His father, Chang Weiguan (張蔚觀), was a banker, and his mother, Xu Yunzheng (徐韻徵), came from an educated family. In that turbulent era, his father, Chang Weiguan, was knowledgeable in literature and history, and the family possessed rich books, providing him with a humanistic perspective even while pursuing engineering.

**Childhood Migrations:**
Due to war, Morris Chang's childhood was marked by migration. From Ningbo to Shanghai, Nanjing, Chongqing, and Shanghai, then to Guangzhou, Hong Kong, and finally to the United States, this tumultuous experience cultivated his adaptability and international outlook.

**Studies in Hong Kong:**
In 1945, at age 14, Morris Chang moved with his family to Hong Kong and received secondary education under the British colonial system. The international environment and English education in Hong Kong laid an important foundation for his later studies and work in the United States.

### Key Turning Points in American Education

**Brief Experience at Harvard University:**
In 1949, at age 18, Morris Chang entered Harvard University[^2] to study literature. However, after one year, due to a lack of passion for literature and economic considerations, he transferred to the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT).

**Engineering Education at MIT:**
At MIT, Morris Chang chose mechanical engineering as his major. This choice seemed accidental but provided him with firsthand engineering intuition in the core of semiconductor manufacturing (precision machinery processes), which became a crucial foundation for later leading process improvements at TSMC.

**Bachelor's Degree in 1952:**
Morris Chang earned a Bachelor of Science in Mechanical Engineering from MIT in 1952[^3]. He graduated during the Korean War, and because he was a foreign student unable to secure work related to the U.S. military, this restriction instead led him toward civilian industry.

## Career Start: Sylvania Company

### Entering the Semiconductor Industry

**The Opportunity in 1955:**
After graduation, Morris Chang worked at Sylvania for three years[^4], which was his first exposure to the semiconductor industry. At that time, the semiconductor industry was still in its infancy, but Morris Chang keenly perceived the immense potential of this emerging industry.

**Cultivation of Technical Skills:**
At Sylvania, Morris Chang was responsible for manufacturing semiconductor devices and learned the basics of semiconductor processes. This experience made him aware of the complexity and precision required in semiconductor manufacturing and cultivated his attention to technical details.

**Initial Display of Management Talent:**
Even in a grassroots technical position, Morris Chang demonstrated excellent management skills. He was good at organizing teams and solving technical problems; these three years taught him the precise requirements of semiconductor manufacturing.

## The Texas Instruments Period: Demonstrating Managerial Ability

### A Major Turning Point in 1958

**Joining Texas Instruments:**
In 1958, Morris Chang joined Texas Instruments (TI)[^5], a crucial turning point in his career. At the time, TI was aggressively expanding its semiconductor business and needed talent capable of bridging engineering and management.

**From Engineer to Manager:**
At TI, Morris Chang started as a semiconductor engineer, responsible for wafer production matters. He performed excellently technically while also demonstrating outstanding managerial talent, quickly gaining recognition from superiors.

**Contributions to Process Improvement:**
During his time at TI, Morris Chang significantly improved the company's semiconductor processes, increasing product yield and production efficiency. These technical improvements saved the company a large amount of cost and earned him renown.

### Advanced Studies at Stanford University

**The Learning Opportunity in 1961:**
In 1961, Texas Instruments supported Morris Chang to pursue a Ph.D. in Electrical Engineering at Stanford University. This was recognition of his abilities by the company and demonstrated the importance American corporations placed on talent development.

**Acquisition of the Doctorate:**
Morris Chang obtained a Ph.D. in Electrical Engineering from Stanford University in 1964[^6]. This advanced study enhanced both his theoretical depth and industry network, leading to rapid promotions upon returning to TI.

**New Role at the Company:**
After earning his doctorate, Morris Chang returned to Texas Instruments to hold higher-level management positions. He successively served as General Manager of the Transistor Division, Silicon Transistor Division, and Integrated Circuit Division, with his status at TI rising year by year according to performance.

### Breaking the Racial Ceiling

**The Historic Promotion in 1972:**
In 1972, Morris Chang was promoted to Vice President of Texas Instruments[^7], becoming one of the highest-ranking Chinese executives in a large American corporation at that time. This promotion was an extremely rare achievement in the American business environment of that era.

**General Manager of the Semiconductor Group:**
Morris Chang also served as the General Manager of the semiconductor group at Texas Instruments, overseeing the company's core business divisions. Under his leadership, TI's semiconductor business developed rapidly, becoming a major global semiconductor supplier.

**Summary of 25 Years in America:**
In his 25 years at Texas Instruments, Morris Chang rose from a grassroots engineer to a senior executive, gaining a deep understanding of the technical rhythm and commercial logic of the semiconductor industry. This experience allowed him to make market judgments when he started his company in Taiwan in 1987 that were far more precise than those of any purely academic background individual.

## The Call of Taiwan: During His Time at the Industrial Technology Institute

### A Life Turning Point in 1985

**The Invitation from Sun Yun-sze:**
In 1985, Morris Chang was invited by Sun Yun-sze (孫運璿)[^8], former Premier; Xu Xianxiu (徐賢修), Chairman of the Industrial Technology Institute; Yu Guohua (俞國華), Premier; and Li Guoding (李國鼎), a political commissioner, to serve as the Director of the Industrial Technology Institute. Sun Yun-sze had long promoted Taiwan's development of high-tech industries and was a major proponent in inviting Morris Chang back to Taiwan. This decision changed his life trajectory and altered the fate of Taiwan's technology industry.

**The Courage to Leave Comfort Zone:**
At 54 years old, Morris Chang already enjoyed a successful career and comfortable life in America. Choosing to return to Taiwan was a risky and challenging decision, demonstrating his sense of mission for Taiwan's technological development.

**Reforms at the Industrial Technology Institute:**
During his tenure as Director of the Industrial Technology Institute, Morris Chang promoted the close integration of research and industry, reforming the institute's research direction with an American management mindset. His international perspective transformed the institute from a government research body into a technology incubator with greater business awareness.

### Assessment of Taiwan's Semiconductor Industry

**Analysis of the Industrial Environment:**
Morris Chang carefully assessed Taiwan's industrial environment and advantages. He believed that Taiwan possessed excellent engineering talent, lower costs, and flexible manufacturing capabilities—conditions highly suitable for developing the semiconductor industry.

**Conception of the Foundry Model:**
During his time at the Industrial Technology Institute, Morris Chang began to conceive a revolutionary business model—the professional foundry. This concept stemmed from his deep insight into industrial trends and was the culmination of his many years of industry experience.

**The Importance of Government Support:**
Morris Chang realized that developing the semiconductor industry required full government support, including capital investment, policy cooperation, and talent cultivation. He communicated closely with government officials to secure a special structure for TSMC's establishment, obtaining technology authorization from the Industrial Technology Institute and securing government equity investment.

## The Birth of TSMC: Innovation in 1987

### Innovation of the Pure-Foundry Model

**The Breakthrough of the Foundry Concept:**
In 1987, Morris Chang proposed the innovative concept of "professional foundry"[^11]. Unlike the traditional Integrated Device Manufacturer (IDM) model, TSMC focused on manufacturing chips for clients without designing its own products.

**Revolutionary Business Model:**
The revolutionary nature of this model was that it allowed many companies lacking the ability to build fabrication plants to design advanced chips, drastically lowering the barrier to entry into the semiconductor industry and leading to exponential growth in chip design companies from the 1990s to the 2020s.

**Impact on the Industrial Ecosystem:**
The professional foundry model created an entirely new industrial ecosystem where design companies could focus on innovation and foundries focused on manufacturing, achieving specialization and increasing overall industry efficiency.

### The Founding Process of TSMC

**A Historic Moment on February 21, 1987:**
On February 21, 1987, Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company was officially established with an initial capital of NT$22 billion[^9]. This was the result of cooperation between the government, private enterprises, and foreign investors.

**Design of Equity Structure:**
TSMC's equity structure included the Industrial Technology Institute, Philips Netherlands, and Taiwanese private enterprises[^10]. This diversified equity structure provided TSMC with technology, capital, and market resources.

**Morris Chang's Dual Role:**
Morris Chang served as the Chairman and General Manager (later CEO) of TSMC, responsible for the company's overall strategy and daily operations. His leadership style combined the efficiency of American management with the wisdom of Chinese culture.

## Development History of TSMC

### Early Challenges and Breakthroughs

**1987–1990: The Founding Struggle:**
TSMC faced enormous challenges in its early days. The pure-foundry model was brand new, and the market was skeptical; clients needed time to accept this concept. Morris Chang had to simultaneously build manufacturing capabilities and persuade customers.

**Establishment of Technical Capabilities:**
TSMC's initial technology came from the transfer from the Industrial Technology Institute and cooperation with Philips. Morris Chang led the team to rapidly learn and improve process technology, establishing reliable manufacturing capabilities.

**Cultivating the First Clients:**
TSMC's first clients were mainly fabless companies in the United States. These companies precisely needed professional foundry services, providing TSMC with an early business foundation.

### Rapid Development in the 1990s

**Technology Leadership Strategy:**
In the 1990s, Morris Chang formulated a "technology leadership" strategy, investing heavily in R&D to ensure that TSMC did not fall behind its competitors in any process generation. TSMC successively built multiple fabrication plants in Taiwan, and its customer base expanded from early American fabless companies to major global design firms like Qualcomm, Broadcom, and Nvidia.

### Leadership in the 21st Century

**Competition in Advanced Processes:**
In the 21st century, the development of semiconductor processes became increasingly difficult and expensive. TSMC maintained a leading position in advanced process technology by investing in R&D annually above the industry average.

**Breakthroughs from 28nm to 5nm:**
From 28nm to 16nm, 7nm, and then 5nm and 3nm, TSMC maintained technological leadership in each generation of advanced processes, solidifying its position in the high-end market.

**The Milestone of Apple Cooperation:**
Cooperation with Apple began with the A-series chips in 2013. TSMC manufactured core processors for iPhones and iPads, bringing massive order volumes and providing the strictest commercial validation of TSMC's advanced process capabilities[^12].

## Management Philosophy and Operating Principles

### Adherence to Technological Innovation

**Emphasis on R&D Investment:**
Morris Chang always emphasized the importance of technological innovation. TSMC has invested about 8% of its revenue in R&D every year, ensuring it does not fall behind competitors technologically[^13]. In an era where Moore's Law faces physical limits, Morris Chang led TSMC to successively break through 7nm, 5nm, and 3nm processes, providing manufacturing capabilities for the development of the entire semiconductor industry.

**Balance Between Technology and Market:**
Morris Chang was skilled at finding a balance between technological leadership and market demand; he needed to maintain technological advantage while ensuring commercial value, avoiding excessive advancement that leads to high costs.

### Talent Cultivation and Corporate Culture

**A Culture of Integrity:**
Morris Chang established a corporate culture centered on integrity at TSMC. He emphasized core values of "integrity, commitment, innovation, and customer trust," which became the cultural foundation of TSMC.

**Emphasis on Talent Development:**
Morris Chang placed great importance on talent cultivation. He established a systematic engineer training and promotion system at TSMC. He believed that human resources were the company's core competitiveness; while TSMC maintained a much lower turnover rate than the industry average, it also nurtured core teams who later led TSMC, such as Liu Deyin (劉德音) and Wei Zhejia (魏哲家).

**Internationalized Management Style:**
Morris Chang introduced the efficiency and transparency of American management into TSMC, establishing a modern corporate governance structure. At the same time, he integrated the wisdom of Chinese culture, creating a unique corporate culture.

### Strategic Thinking and Execution Power

**Formulation of Long-Term Strategy:**
Morris Chang possessed outstanding strategic thinking capabilities, capable of discerning industry trends and formulating long-term development strategies. TSMC's "technology leadership" strategy is an embodiment of his strategic vision.

**Emphasis on Execution:**
In addition to strategy formulation, Morris Chang highly valued execution. He established a precise management system that allowed the technological blueprint to move from decision-making to fabrication plant implementation, which was key to TSMC keeping pace with each process generation cycle.

**Wisdom in Crisis Response:**
When facing various crises and challenges, Morris Chang demonstrated excellent leadership and crisis response wisdom, leading TSMC through multiple difficult periods and maintaining stable corporate development.

## Retirement and Succession

### The First Retirement in 2005

**Initiation of the Successor Plan:**
In 2005, at age 74, Morris Chang announced his retirement from the CEO position, handing over the role to Tsai Lixing (蔡力行)[^14]. This was part of a carefully prepared succession plan, demonstrating his sense of responsibility for the company's sustainable development.

**Retention of Chairman Role:**
Although he stepped down as CEO, Morris Chang retained the position of Chairman, continuing to participate in major strategic decisions and providing guidance and support to the successor team.

**Arrangements for Retirement Life:**
After retirement, Morris Chang did not completely leave business activities; he attended international forums such as APEC as a representative of Taiwan and shared his views on geopolitics and the future of semiconductors in many speeches.

### The Comeback in 2009

**The Challenge of the Financial Crisis:**
The global financial crisis in 2008 significantly affected TSMC, which faced dual challenges of declining performance and intensifying competition. At this critical moment, Morris Chang decided to return.

**Reappointment as CEO:**
In 2009, at age 78, Morris Chang returned to serve as the CEO of TSMC[^15], personally leading the company through difficult times. His comeback stabilized market confidence and provided leadership for the company's recovery.

**Cultivation of Liu Deyin and Wei Zhejia:**
During his second term, Morris Chang focused on cultivating successors such as Liu Deyin (劉德音) and Wei Zhejia (魏哲家), preparing talent for the company's future development.

### Formal Retirement in 2018

**Completion of the Succession Plan:**
On June 5, 2018, at age 87, Morris Chang officially retired, ending his legendary 31-year career at TSMC[^16]. He handed over the Chairman position to Liu Deyin and the CEO position to Wei Zhejia.

**Establishment of Dual Leadership:**
Morris Chang established a "dual leadership system," with Liu Deyin serving as Chairman for external relations and Wei Zhejia serving as CEO for internal management. This arrangement helped the company develop stably.

**A Model of Dignified Retirement:**
Morris Chang's retirement was regarded as a model of corporate succession; he stepped down at the right time, giving the new generation of leaders ample space to demonstrate their abilities while ensuring the stable transition of the enterprise.

## Influence on the Global Semiconductor Industry

### Innovation in Business Models

**Popularization of the Foundry Model:**
The professional foundry model created by Morris Chang has become one of the standard business models in the semiconductor industry. Hundreds of fabless companies globally rely on foundry services, and this model greatly promoted industrial development.

**Reshaping the Industrial Ecosystem:**
The professional foundry model reshaped the semiconductor ecosystem, promoting specialization and increasing industrial efficiency while lowering the barrier to innovation, allowing more companies to participate in chip design.

**Establishment of the Global Supply Chain:**
TSMC became a critical node in the global semiconductor supply chain, providing manufacturing services to technology companies worldwide and establishing a truly globalized semiconductor industry supply chain.

### Driving Technological Advancement

**Leading Process Technology:**
Under Morris Chang's leadership, TSMC maintained a leading position in process technology, driving technological progress across the entire semiconductor industry and sustaining the vitality of Moore's Law.

**Democratization of Advanced Processes:**
TSMC's advanced process services allowed smaller design companies to use the latest technologies, achieving the "democratization" of advanced processes—a startup with only a few dozen employees can have its 7nm chips mass-produced by TSMC.

**Pioneering New Technology Fields:**
TSMC's manufacturing capabilities cover various application areas such as digital chips, AI accelerators, and automotive semiconductors, providing the manufacturing foundation for various emerging technologies.

## Significance for Taiwan: The Pioneer of the Tech Island

### Development of the Tech Industry

**Establishment of the National Treasure:**
TSMC is known as Taiwan's "National Treasure," not only because of its immense economic value but also due to its critical position in the global technology supply chain. Morris Chang established this strategic asset that is irreplaceable outside of national defense for Taiwan.

**Cultivation of Tech Talent:**
The development of TSMC has cultivated a large number of semiconductor professionals, who have supported TSMC's growth and provided a talent base for Taiwan's entire tech industry.

**Formation of Industrial Clusters:**
Centered around TSMC, Taiwan formed a semiconductor industrial cluster encompassing equipment suppliers, material suppliers, and packaging/testing plants, creating enormous industrial value.

### Contribution to Economic Development

**Major Contribution to GDP:**
TSMC has become one of Taiwan's largest enterprises and makes an extremely important contribution to Taiwan's GDP. The company's success also drove the development of related industries, creating a large number of jobs.

**Pillar of Export Trade:**
Semiconductors account for about one-third of Taiwan's total exports. TSMC's success greatly enhanced Taiwan's status in global trade and strengthened Taiwan's economic competitiveness[^17].

**Improvement of the Investment Environment:**
TSMC's success story attracted more international investment, improving Taiwan's investment environment and raising its standing in the eyes of global investors.

### Geopolitical Impact

**Importance of Technological Sovereignty:**
In the current geopolitical climate, semiconductor technology has become a core bargaining chip in great power competition. TSMC controls the world's most advanced processes, giving Taiwan an irreplaceable strategic position in international society.

**A Bargaining Chip in International Relations:**
TSMC's critical position in the global technology supply chain gives Taiwan significant leverage on semiconductor issues—a topic that even major powers must pay attention to. The successive efforts by the United States, the EU, and Japan to offer subsidies to attract TSMC to build factories demonstrate the true weight of this status.

**Considerations for Security Strategy:**
The focus of various countries on semiconductor technology also presents Taiwan with new security challenges and opportunities. How to balance the interests of all parties while maintaining TSMC's competitive edge is one of the core issues in Taiwan's diplomatic and security policy.

## Personal Qualities and Leadership Style

### Combination of Vision and Execution

**Excellence in Strategic Vision:**
Morris Chang's greatest quality was his outstanding strategic vision. He could discern industry trends and foresee future developments; this foresight was a key factor in TSMC's success.

**Pragmatic Execution Ability:**
In addition to vision, Morris Chang possessed excellent execution ability. He could transform strategic concepts into concrete action plans and track every detail of the execution. This combination of strategic thinking and execution is rare in the business world.

**Attitude of Lifelong Learning:**
Even at an advanced age, Morris Chang maintained a habit of extensive reading and monitoring industry developments. He publicly stated that he was still studying literature and philosophy, keeping his mind sharp in his later years.

### Characteristics of Leadership Style

**Charismatic Leadership:**
Morris Chang possessed strong personal charisma and persuasive power, inspiring teams to strive for common goals. His speeches and articles always clearly conveyed complex concepts, earning the respect of employees and industry peers.

**Adherence to Rational Decision-Making:**
When facing major decisions, Morris Chang always insisted on rational analysis, making judgments based on facts and data, avoiding emotional or political considerations from affecting the accuracy of his decisions.

**Practice of Long-Term Thinking:**
Morris Chang always adhered to long-term thinking, unswayed by short-term difficulties or profits. During the deepest part of the 2008 financial crisis, he still approved R&D budgets for advanced processes; this long-term perspective allowed TSMC to pull away from its competitors after the crisis.

## Honors and Recognition

### International Awards and Recognition

**IEEE Honorary Medal:**
Morris Chang received multiple honorary medals from the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE), which is an important recognition of his contributions to semiconductor technology development.

**Honorary Doctorates from Various Countries:**
Prestigious universities such as Stanford University, MIT, and Tsinghua University awarded Morris Chang honorary doctorates in recognition of his contributions to industry and education.

**Reviews by Business Magazines:**
Morris Chang was repeatedly listed by international business magazines like _Fortune_ and _Business Weekly_ as a leading figure in the contemporary technology industry, establishing his status in global business circles.

### Recognition from Taiwan

**Awarded by Government:**
The Taiwanese government awarded Morris Chang several important medals to commend his outstanding contributions to Taiwan's economic development and technological progress.

**Esteem from Industry:**
Morris Chang is revered by the Taiwanese industrial sector as the "Godfather of Semiconductors," and his experience and wisdom serve as a model for younger entrepreneurs.

**Recognition of Social Influence:**
In addition to business achievements, Morris Chang's social influence has been widely recognized, and his contributions to Taiwan's social development are highly valued by all sectors.

## Philosophical Thoughts and Life Wisdom

### Understanding Success

**Combination of Strength and Opportunity:**
Morris Chang believed that success requires a combination of strength and opportunity; strength is the foundation, but one must also seize the moment. He emphasized the importance of preparation: opportunities are always given to those who are prepared.

**The Value of Long-Term Perseverance:**
He stressed the importance of long-term perseverance, believing that true success requires the accumulation of time and cannot be achieved hastily. TSMC's success is the result of long-term persistence.

**Necessity of Innovation:**
Morris Chang believed that innovation is a necessary condition for survival in the technology industry. The history of TSMC shows: as long as breakthroughs in processes stop, competitors will fill the gaps.

### Reflections on Life

**Work-Life Balance:**
Although his career was extremely successful, Morris Chang also emphasized the importance of work-life balance. He enjoyed reading and listening to music, which provided him with spiritual nourishment.

**Assumption of Social Responsibility:**
Morris Chang believed that successful entrepreneurs have a responsibility to give back to society. He led TSMC in donating to academic institutions like Tsinghua University and participated in discussions on education policy, offering specific suggestions for reforming higher education in Taiwan[^18].

**Importance of Succession:**
He placed great importance on the transmission of knowledge and experience, not only by cultivating successors within the company but also by sharing his experiences and wisdom through various channels.

## Historical Evaluation

When Morris Chang retired in 2018, TSMC's market capitalization surpassed Intel, making it one of the world's most valuable semiconductor companies[^19]. In 31 years, he validated a counter-intuitive proposition: a foundry that does not design its own chips can become the manufacturing foundation for an entire digital age.

From Ningbo, Zhejiang, to Harvard and MIT, and from a senior executive at Texas Instruments to abandoning a comfortable life in America at age 54 to return to Taiwan—every one of his turns was not the mainstream choice, yet they all pointed in the same direction. The current status of TSMC is both the result of technological competition in semiconductors and the outcome of betting on a business model that few believed in back in 1987.

In 2024, when TSMC's market capitalization exceeded NT$10 trillion, becoming one of Asia's highest-valued technology companies[^20], this was an unimaginable scale compared to the bet he made in 1987, and it is the clearest annotation of his life.
