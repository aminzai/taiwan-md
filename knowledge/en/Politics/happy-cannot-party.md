On November 14, 2019, in Taipei, a founding convention was held by City Council members Chiu Wei-chieh, YouTuber Zhang Zhiqi, and "Eye Center Television" host Chen Zijian—a convention that did not look much like a political party formation. The event featured a party platform, an election for party chairman, and a piece of cilantro cake. In the end, Chen Zijian, who reportedly disliked cilantro the most (having eaten the most screws), was forced to eat it.[^1]

On November 21, the organization named "Joyful Incompetence Party" also announced a list of non-affiliated legislative candidates, with Shiba Inu Yuzu, whom Chen Zijian owned, listed first.[^2] On February 19, 2021, the Ministry of the Interior announced the cancellation of its party application because corporate registration was not completed within one year of the filing.[^3]

These three dates tell a very short story, yet they leave an unresolved question: **If a group of online creators genuinely wants to bring people closer to politics, do they need to form a viable political party, or do they need to create a public classroom that makes people want to understand political parties for the first time?**

## A Flower Basket, and the Outside World's Understanding of the Experiment

What PDIS presented on the day of the founding convention was not a standard wreath, but a basket of flowers primarily featuring catnip. The sign read "To Gain Joy," and English Catnip was also placed nearby.[^11] This object transformed the name "Joyful Incompetence Party" from an internet joke into a public proposition that could be publicly blessed.[^11]

PDIS’s English article was not official documentation for the Joyful Incompetence Party, nor did it endorse it as a long-term political party. What it did was more subtle: it placed joy, generational integration, freedom, democracy, dialogue, and consensus within the same text to explain why an seemingly frivolous formation could be viewed as a democratic experiment.[^9]

This photo is therefore not just decoration. It allows readers to see that the Joyful Incompetence Party's political language exists not only in its party platform but also in a flower basket, a sign, and how onlookers choose to name this action.

## Politics Is Too Hard, So Let’s Start With Jokes

The Joyful Incompetence Party did not appear out of nowhere. As the 2019 presidential and legislative elections approached, public discourse in Taiwan was filled with factional clashes, mockery, and emotion. At the beginning of November, Chiu Wei-chieh, Zhang Zhiqi, and Chen Zijian announced their formation.[^4] They had already completed the signature process for 100 founding members.[^4]

These numbers cannot be directly equated to votes. They are more like a signal: a group of people willing to click "join" on social media platforms, but not necessarily willing to commit to a traditional political party. The Joyful Incompetence Party probed the distance between these two things.

Chiu Wei-chieh said at a press conference, "Politics shouldn't be this difficult." He also mentioned that after engaging in politics, his life was full of "hatred, malice, and negative emotions."[^5] This is not a blueprint for national governance, but a political question arising from daily feelings: why must one first learn a language that makes them want to flee when participating in public affairs?

📝 **Curator's Note:** What the Joyful Incompetence Party is most worth remembering is not that it made politics funny, but that it acknowledged that "being funny" itself might be an entry point. An entrance does not equal an answer, but without an entrance, no one can come in to listen for the answer.

## A Founding Convention Where the Platform Was Read Until Screws Were Eaten

CNA’s coverage of the founding convention left behind an object that institutional documents could not easily capture: the cilantro cake. The three founders took turns reading the party platform; the numbers calculated on the projection screen kept going wrong, so a rule was established at the scene: whoever ate the most screws would have to eat the cilantro cake. Chen Zijian, who disliked cilantro the most, lost.[^6]

This segment looked like entertainment, yet it was connected to what they were trying to do. The founding convention was not packaged as a sacred ritual; instead, mistakes, awkwardness, and unfamiliar procedures were laid bare before the audience. For someone encountering a political party for the first time, this was more memorable than a flowchart filled with jargon.

But the Joyful Incompetence Party did not cancel the procedure. CNA reported that at the founding convention on November 14, Chiu Wei-chieh was elected as the Secretary General, which is also the party chairman. They still needed to apply to the Ministry of the Interior and process the party charter, responsible parties, and the party application.[^7]

This was the first major contrast for the Joyful Incompetence Party: **it lowered the barrier using the format of an online show, yet it had to prove itself not just as an online show using the format of a political party.**

The focus of this timeline is not a straight story of "formation—failure," but rather that each node tested the same thing: can a political organization gathered through social media attention withstand the fixed procedures, responsibilities, and continuity required by party systems?

## The Joy in the Platform Is Not Just a Gimmick

_Key Opinion Net_ translated several key articles from the Joyful Incompetence Party's platform. The first article broke down the party name into a declaration: "Cannot Be Stopped by Joy." The third stated, "Politics is not difficult; it is just about finding joy." The platform also claimed to allow dual membership, no membership fees, and even included cilantro in its jokes.[^8]

If these texts were only seen as comedy, their political function would be missed. Political parties usually first ask people to state a position: which side are you on? what ideology do you support? The Joyful Incompetence Party reversed this question: can you not be afraid yet?

Allowing dual membership means it did not design joining a party as a one-time identity cleavage. Not charging fees lowers the barrier between organization and support. These designs may not be enough to run a long-term political party, but they clearly expressed a soft vision of a political party: an organization does not have to demand loyalty first in order for people to get involved.

PDIS, in its English post on the day of the founding convention, linked "joy" with generational integration, freedom, and democracy, believing that any way to promote dialogue and build consensus is worthy of encouragement and experimentation.[^9] This text was not the official party platform of the Joyful Incompetence Party, but it provided an external perspective: joy is not an escape from politics; it can also be understood as a social technology for reducing conflict and restarting dialogue.

📝 **Curator's Note:** The seriousness of this experiment lies in its refusal to put seriousness on display. It did not claim that laughter could solve housing, gender, or generational problems. It only asked first: can we make more people willing to stay and talk about these issues?

## Why Was Yuzu Listed First?

On November 21, the Joyful Incompetence Party announced 13 non-affiliated legislative candidates. CNA reported that Yuzu was listed first, followed by celebrities Joseph, Cheng Jia-chun, Chen Zhihan, and Chiu Wei-chieh's cat.[^2] Each candidate had a nomination reason, making the entire list look like both political satire and a roster rearranging social influence.[^2]

If one only looks at the jokes, Yuzu and the cat are just a joke about "any pet being able to be a legislator." But when placed back into the media environment of 2019, it was actually questioning: is a non-affiliated legislative candidate an internal professional representative of the party, a spokesperson for social issues, or someone who can bring attention to public issues?

Chiu Wei-chieh told CNA that the main consideration in the nomination was focusing on social issues and having influence, and that they had consulted the individuals.[^2] This explanation shows why the list is not just a pet joke: it attempts to discuss "who can make people pay attention" as political capital.

Of course, influence does not equal governance capacity, nor does fame equal representation. The Joyful Incompetence Party did not actually send Yuzu to the legislature; the value of the list was not whether it could become a candidate roster, but in turning the boundaries of the nomination system into an issue that everyone can understand.

## "Goodbye" Was Not Dissolution, But Class Dismissal

When the 2020 election registration deadline passed, the Joyful Incompetence Party had not officially become a corporation, so Facebook posted, "Goodbye, Joyful Incompetence Party?" CNA reported that Chiu Wei-chieh explained that this post was not an announcement of dissolution, but rather that the phase objective had been completed. They were not formed to participate in the 2020 election.[^7]

This explanation made the Joyful Incompetence Party look more like a public class than an incomplete electoral team. The "large civic education class" mentioned by Chiu Wei-chieh included the founding convention, the party chairman election, non-affiliated nominations, and knowledge videos. Every procedure was teaching material; every joke was a way to present the material to the audience.[^7]

But even a course can leave behind procedural work. In February 2021, the Ministry of the Interior's announcement did not use the tone of "course completion," but clearly stated: because corporate registration was not completed within one year of filing, the party application was canceled.[^3]

Yu Media quoted Chiu Wei-chieh, saying that if they had completed incorporation, outsiders might have thought he wanted to run again in 2022. For him, the Joyful Incompetence Party had achieved its phase objectives and had nothing left to do afterward.[^10] Therefore, "natural extinction" was not a dramatic failure, but a deliberate choice to stop at the procedural threshold.

📝 **Curator's Note:** The Joyful Incompetence Party did not prove itself through an election, nor did it prove itself through longevity. The question it left behind is even harder: if political education is truly effective, does the student still need the teacher on stage after learning the process?

## A Long Tail Left by a Short-Lived Party

The legal life of the Joyful Incompetence Party was short, but its cultural life did not end with the 2021 announcement. It allowed "internet celebrities" to become more than just external figures invited for promotion; they became people who could gather members themselves, design party platforms, and explain political procedures.

This does not mean that all creators are suitable for politics, nor does it mean that making politics entertaining can solve public problems. On the contrary, it exposed the gap in responsibility between entertainment and politics: videos can retain viewers with pacing, but a political party must deal with organization, finance, incorporation, and representation. Jokes can open dialogue; they cannot substitute policy.

The most honest thing about the Joyful Incompetence Party may be that it did not pretend to have completed this transition. It brought politics before the camera, allowing the audience to see the entire process: formation, nomination, waiting for review, and finally having the application canceled. These processes are not beautiful, but they explain how democracy works better than a success story with only slogans.

If viewed as a political party, it truly did not complete incorporation. If viewed as a public experiment, it at least completed one demonstration: that a party can first be a scene that makes people willing to approach, and _then_ begin discussing positions, systems, and responsibilities.

The cilantro cake from 2019 was eventually eaten, and Yuzu did not become a legislator. What remains is not an answer where "joy" solved politics, but a more durable question: **Can we make politics engaging enough to get people involved, and then hold ourselves responsible for what happens after they join?**

## International Comparison: When Does Satirical Party Become an Institutional Role?

The Joyful Incompetence Party is not the only one to turn politics into jokes. In 2010, Best Party, founded by Icelandic comedian Jón Gnarr, gained 34.7% of the vote in Reykjavik local elections, securing 6 out of 15 city council seats. It proposed absurd platforms such as free towels, polar bears, and a "drug-free parliament," yet it genuinely engaged in local governance.[^12]

The Polish Beer Lovers' Party brought another type of political situation into the parliament. A TIME article page recorded that it won 16 seats in the 1991 election before splitting into the "Big Beer" and "Small Beer" factions. This result reminds us: a party name can be a joke, but the number of seats brings factional and representative responsibilities.[^13]

The UK's Official Monster Raving Loony Party is closer to institutional parody in terms of continuous voting records. A UK Parliament Election Results website recorded several by-elections for this party, such as Nick The Flying Brick in the 2024 Wellingborough by-election, who received 217 votes (0.7% of the vote). It continued to run, but these records do not equate to gaining a parliamentary seat.[^14]

Germany's Die PARTEI demonstrated that proportional representation allows satirical parties a larger entry point. DW reported that it gained nearly 900,000 votes and 2 seats in the 2019 European Parliament elections.[^15] Compared to these cases, the Joyful Incompetence Party stopped before formal participation: it completed the public display of the founding convention, party platform, and nomination list, but did not get the Yuzu list onto the ballot, nor did it bring the organization into local or national governance.

Therefore, the four foreign cases cannot be ranked in a "who is funniest" leaderboard. A more useful question is: does the satire occur in the platforms, candidates, party names, or the entire organizational form? Did it gain seats? After gaining seats, was it forced to deal with policy, factions, and governance? On this scale, the Joyful Incompetence Party is closest to a civic education experiment that publicizes political procedures, rather than a satirical party that has completed institutional transformation.
