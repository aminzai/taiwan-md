# Watchout: A Decade of Taiwanese Civic Technology from Parliamentary Oversight to Civil Defense

> **30-Second Overview:** Watchout was established in November 2013 and is a significant milestone in the history of civic technology and independent media in Taiwan[^1]. As Asia's first online platform focused on monitoring government operations, Watchout continuously lowered the barrier for citizen participation in politics through "Parliamentary Oversight," "Mayor, Do You Have Questions?", "Lao Philosophy," and public defense guides co-published with international NGOs[^1] [^2] [^10]. This article comprehensively reviews its founding intent, its role in the Sunflower Movement, core projects, community management, and organizational governance challenges to demonstrate its historical positioning as a "digital sentinel" of Taiwan.

In contemporary Taiwanese civil society, **Watchout Co.** holds a highly iconic position[^1]. Its name is derived from the homophone of the English phrase "Watch out," meaning "to be vigilant or cautious." Its core mission is to promote transparency in public issues by pressuring elected representatives and government officials through digital tools[^1].

## Key Turning Points: The Sunflower Movement and Civic Awakening

Watchout encountered the **Sunflower Movement (318 Movement)** in 2014 early in its history. In that movement, which reshaped Taiwan's political landscape, Watchout played a crucial role in information dissemination. When traditional media struggled to convey complex details from legislative sessions in real-time, Watchout collaborated with civic technology communities like g0v Zero Hour Government to transform rigid legislative disputes into easily understandable digital information, leading more young generations to pay attention to the operations of the legislature[^9].

To make politics less solemn and distant, Watchout created the virtual character **"A-Cao."** Through personified narratives, humorous community interactions, and visualized information, Watchout successfully "softened" complex public issues, giving them high virality on social media and attracting audiences who were previously indifferent to politics[^12].

---

## Core Project Implementation: Deepening Democracy and Civil Defense Resilience

Watchout transformed rigid political agendas into understandable information through "Parliamentary Oversight." During the 2014 Taipei Mayoral Election, Watchout launched the "Mayor, Do You Have Questions?" interactive platform, breaking the limitations of one-way communication in traditional press conferences and laying an important precedent for subsequent digital participation efforts across Taiwan.

To deepen the foundation of rational discourse in civil society, Watchout established the "Watchout Civic Academy" in September 2014, with philosopher Chu Chia-an serving as editor-in-chief, developing the "Lao Philosophy" community[^6]. This community explores the core values and ethical principles behind public policies through accessible philosophical articles and comics.

_Figure: Watchout co-published the "Active Action Guide" with international NGOs, marking its strategic shift toward strengthening societal defense resilience._

In recent years, in response to geopolitical threats, Watchout collaborated with the American NGO **"Spirit of America" (SOA)** to publish the "Active Action Guide"[^10]. This manual differs from official versions as it focuses more on the psychological impact, material preparation, and practical responses of citizens when war breaks out. Its goal is to reduce public fear of "hybrid warfare" and military threats, transforming that anxiety into concrete action[^4] [^11].

## Organizational Governance and Challenges: The Friction Between Ideal and System

As a private organization identifying as a social enterprise, Watchout has also faced severe internal governance tests during its development.

In June 2015, Watchout experienced a scandal involving founder and then-CEO Liu Lin-wei, who was accused of financial disputes and unauthorized misappropriation of public funds, drawing intense societal attention to the transparency of crowdfunding finances[^5]. Watchout immediately dismissed him and commissioned an accounting firm to conduct a thorough financial audit, publicly releasing related reports to clarify the flow of funds[^5].

In May 2021, Watchout faced another major management crisis when then-CEO Lin Tzu-yi was accused of abnormal financial flows[^3]. The Watchout board promptly dismissed him and filed a lawsuit with judicial authorities[^3]. Although these two incidents impacted the brand image, they also prompted Taiwanese civic technology communities to deeply reflect on the importance of organizational governance and public accountability.

---

## Historical Positioning and Future Outlook

In reviewing Watchout's decade-long development, it is not just an independent media outlet but a microcosm of Taiwan's evolution in digital democracy. Faced with rampant misinformation and hybrid warfare threats, whether Watchout can continue to function as a digital sentinel tests not only the internal governance wisdom of its management team but also serves as an important indicator of whether Taiwanese civil society can continuously deepen democratic resilience.
