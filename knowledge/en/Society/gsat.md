In 1994, the General Scholastic Ability Test was first administered. By the 115th academic year, the number of registrants had reached 121,535, distributed across 35 test zones, 93 districts, and 3,536 test venues. This exam lasts only three days but simultaneously affects the recommendations for outstanding students, applications for admission, and distribution-based admissions.

The most counterintuitive aspect is here: **The university entrance exam appears to be a test that ranks students, but institutionally, it does not aim to assess all high school knowledge. Instead, it asks a more narrow and critical question: whether you possess the basic academic ability required for university education.** [^1][^3]

> **✦** "The university entrance exam is not an endpoint; it is more like a door: outside the door is the high school curriculum, inside the door is where each university department gets to know you in different ways."

## Three Days in January, Starting with an Admission Ticket

The 115th academic year university entrance exam was held from January 17 to 19, 2026. Test-takers could freely choose from six subjects: Chinese, English, Mathematics A, Mathematics B, Social Studies, and Natural Sciences. Chinese is divided into two sections: the Comprehensive Chinese Ability Test and the Chinese Writing Ability Test, making a total of six subjects and seven sessions. [^1]

The schedule divides this exam into clearly defined time slots: Mathematics A and Natural Sciences on the first day, English and Comprehensive Chinese, Chinese Writing on the second day, and Mathematics B and Social Studies on the third day. Comprehensive Chinese and Chinese Writing each last 90 minutes, English and both mathematics subjects each last 100 minutes, and Social Studies and Natural Sciences each last 110 minutes. [^1]

| ----------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |

| Number of exam days                                         | 3 days                       |
| Subjects / Sessions                                         | 6 subjects / 7 sessions      |
| Test zones / Districts                                      | 35 test zones / 93 districts |
| Total test venues                                           | 3,536 venues                 |

These numbers may seem like mere test statistics, but they also illustrate the public nature of the university entrance exam. It is not a classroom test between a teacher and students, but a social system that must simultaneously process hundreds of thousands of answer sheets, different physical conditions, different geographical locations, and the same set of fair rules. The 115th academic year work report also records that the test administration center will compile the test administration, verification, statistical charts, and questions for each subject into a work report for universities, high schools, educational researchers, and the general public to review. [^1]

> **📝 Curator's Note**
>
> What is most worth observing about the university entrance exam is not just who scored how many points, but how Taiwan turns an exam held in winter into a fair procedure that the entire society must believe in.

## It Has Never Been a Fixed Exam

The university entrance exam was launched in the 83rd academic year. The test administration center explains that the test content is adjusted according to changes in the high school curriculum. Starting from 2008, the Chinese Writing Ability Test was independently administered, and the test content for Chinese and English was also adjusted to the fifth semester of high school. [^2]

This history means that "what exactly is being tested in the university entrance exam" cannot be answered simply by the names of the subjects. As the curriculum changes, so does the ability the exam aims to capture: from remembering how much content, it gradually shifts to understanding the context provided in the questions, integrating data, and applying learned concepts to unfamiliar problems. The test administration center's exam instructions applicable from the 111th academic year explicitly place the 108 curriculum standards, proficiency-oriented test questions, and exam instructions for each subject of the university entrance exam under the same set of instructions. [^3]

Therefore, the "life-oriented" nature of the university entrance exam is not about making the questions into everyday common sense, nor does it mean that textbook knowledge is unimportant. It is more like extracting knowledge from familiar chapter names, placing it into a piece of text, a chart, a social event, or a context requiring reasoning, and then seeing if the test-taker can recognize that the concept is still there. This is a change in the direction of question-setting, and also the reason why students' preparation methods must change. [^3][^4]

## Before the University Entrance Exam, the Joint Entrance Exam Was a Narrow Path

If we place the university entrance exam back into the history of Taiwan's education, it was not the first system to attempt to answer "who can enter university." The National University Joint Entrance Exam was implemented in 1954, during the 43rd year of the Republic of China, and lasted for nearly half a century. It provided a national comparison standard through a common exam and distribution mechanism. [^11][^12]

The fairness of the joint entrance exam was first technical fairness: everyone took similar subjects at the same time and was distributed according to publicly announced rules. This system reduced the burden on test-takers traveling to various schools and reduced the burden of each university conducting its own recruitment. However, it also concentrated the competition for admission into a single exam, easily leading to "exams leading education" and an overemphasis on academic advancement. [^12][^13]

> **📝 Curator's Note**
>
> The joint entrance exam is not a system that can be simply summarized as "good" or "bad." It once provided predictable fairness through a single standard, but it also caused schools to revolve around the exam due to excessive concentration. Understanding this contradiction is the key to understanding why the university entrance exam, application for admission, and subject test were later introduced.

In 2002, during the 91st academic year, the National University Multi-Directional Admission Program began to replace the overall framework of the traditional joint entrance exam. The direction of institutional reform shifted from "one exam, unified distribution" to "separation of exams and recruitment" and multiple channels. The university entrance exam is responsible for evaluating basic academic abilities, while other exams and departmental procedures take on the functions of filtering and selecting at different stages. [^12][^13]

## Beyond the Six Subjects, There Is Another Kind of Selection Pressure

Starting from the 115th academic year, test-takers can freely choose six subjects. This "freedom" allows students with different aspirations not to prepare every subject to the same depth, but it also returns the responsibility of choice to the test-takers: which subjects should be selected for the target departments? Does the difference between Mathematics A and Mathematics B affect the application? Should Natural Sciences be included? The answers are not within the university entrance exam itself, but in the recruitment channels and departmental guidelines. [^1][^5]

The National University Recruitment Committee Association divides university admission into recommendations for outstanding students, applications for admission, and distribution-based admissions, and provides information on the subjects selected by each department and various recruitment details. In other words, the same university entrance exam score does not automatically generate the same admission result. It must be placed into a department's assessment, screening, adoption, or ranking rules before it starts to take effect. [^5]

| Possible Roles of University Entrance Exam Scores | Mode of Action                                                                    |
| ------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Recommendations for Outstanding Students          | Used as an assessment or ranking item                                             |
| Applications for Admission                        | Used for assessment, first-stage screening, or adoption                           |
| Distribution-Based Admissions                     | Can be used together with subject test scores according to department regulations |

This is also where many test-takers first feel confused: scores are not a ruler that applies equally to everyone. The test administration center stipulates that the scores for each subject of the university entrance exam are expressed in levels, and when used for recommendations for outstanding students, applications for admission, and some distribution purposes, the maximum for each subject is 15 levels. If the university entrance exam is used for distribution-based admissions, there is another representation method with a maximum of 60 levels. [^1][^6]

### The Choice of Subjects Is Not More Beneficial with More Choices

"Free selection" is easily misunderstood as "if you can take it, take it all," but the system actually leaves strategic space, not more exams necessarily meaning more paths. Departments can choose which subjects to adopt from the university entrance exam and subject test, and test-takers need to compare their target departments, preparation time, and subject requirements on the same chart. [^5][^10]

If a department only lists certain subjects as assessments or adoptions, the additional investment in other subjects may not change the application result. Conversely, if a department requires a certain subject but the test-taker did not select it, no matter how high the scores in other subjects are, they cannot directly fill that gap. This is not a conclusion about the individual, but a reminder that the choice of subjects must start from the department's rules, not from the anxiety of the exam room. [^5]

## One Level Is Not One Meter, But a Relative Position

"Levels" are easily misunderstood as fixed scores. In fact, the levels of the university entrance exam are converted through the statistical distribution of all test-takers in that year. The five score standards announced by the test administration center explain the top, front, average, back, and bottom percentiles. [^6]

This means that what test-takers care about is not only how many questions they answered correctly, but also the overall score distribution of that subject. The same original score, placed in different years and different groups of test-takers, may not correspond to the same level. This is not making the scores untransparent, but using relative positions to handle the differences in difficulty and performance distribution across subjects. [^6]

> **📝 Curator's Note**
>
> What the levels truly measure is not "how far am I from the full score," but "where do I stand among the people who entered the exam room together."

## In the Exam Room, Fairness Is Not an Abstract Concept

Exam fairness is often thought of as everyone receiving the same test paper, but there are also smaller and more practical issues on site: admission tickets, entry times, whether mobile phones are fully turned off, the temperature of the exam room, and whether sudden sounds will interrupt a person's thinking.

On the first day of the 115th academic year university entrance exam, the Central News Agency reported that parents in southern regions complained about the stifling heat in the exam room. The test administration center explained that if test-takers believe the temperature affects their performance, they can report it to the proctor, and after obtaining the consent of all test-takers in that venue, windows or doors can be opened. [^7] This handling is very careful because it takes into account both the individual physical feelings of test-takers and the common conditions of the same exam room.

The director of the test administration center, Zhang Xinzhen, stated during the explanation: "In order to maintain the fairness of the exam, the environmental settings of the exam room are consistently determined." [^7] This statement reveals another side of fairness: fairness is not only about everyone accepting the same calculation method when the scores are announced, but also about minimizing the inconvenience of one person becoming an interference to another during the exam.

Interference does not necessarily come from the system. Once, the broadcasting from a community office near the exam room lasted for about five minutes, and test-takers complained that the sound disrupted their thinking. The community leader later said: "I had no idea there was an exam nearby, and I apologize for affecting the test-takers." Thus, the boundary of a large-scale exam extends from the entrance of the exam room to the nearby streets: garbage collection broadcasts, windows, air conditioning, and traffic may all become part of the exam.

## The Chinese Writing Test Examines Life, But Not Just Any Life

After the Chinese Writing test of 2026 ended, the Central News Agency reported that test-takers felt the questions referenced comics by Kei Kuan, requiring a shift in perspective, and they could also approach it through experiences of family communication and interactions with teachers. This is a retelling in the report, not the test-taker's direct quote. [^9]

There is an easily overlooked action in this statement: it is not "having life experience means you can write," but reorganizing life experience into a response to the question. Therefore, the Chinese Writing test is neither simple recitation of model essays, nor dumping private stories directly onto the answer sheet. It requires test-takers to read the questions, grasp the constraints, and organize their observations into text. [^1][^3]

This also explains why the proficiency-oriented approach of the university entrance exam often feels unfamiliar. What is unfamiliar is not all knowledge, but the fact that the questions do not necessarily tell you "which chapter to use first." You must first identify the problem, then decide which knowledge is worth bringing in. [^3][^4]

## After the University Entrance Exam, the Scores Begin to Be Interpreted

The announcement of scores does not mean the end of admission. The 115th academic year university entrance exam scores were announced on February 25, and the score notices were mailed the next day. Test-takers could also apply for score review from February 26 to March 4. [^1]

The subsequent path depends on the departments and recruitment channels: some use the university entrance exam scores to participate in recommendations for outstanding students, others enter the first-stage screening for applications for admission, others prepare for the subject test, or choose other channels based on their qualifications and preferences. The Recruitment Committee Association website continuously publishes recruitment announcements, screening results, review materials, and distribution information for each academic year. These are the next layer of systems that translate "a score notice" into "where you can go." [^5]

## Subject Test: Leaving More Detailed Academic Abilities for Later

The subject test is mainly used for distribution-based admissions, assessing the test-taker's abilities in key subjects. Departments that adopt distribution-based admissions can choose which subjects to adopt from the six subjects of the university entrance exam and the various subjects of the subject test according to their characteristics and needs. The 115th academic year subject test actually lists eight subjects: Mathematics A, Mathematics B, History, Geography, Civics and Social Studies, Physics, Chemistry, and Biology. [^10][^15]

This makes the subject test and the university entrance exam form a division of labor, rather than simply being "the second university entrance exam." The university entrance exam occurs earlier and is often used for assessments and screenings in recommendations for outstanding students and applications for admission. The subject test, on the other hand, allows certain departments to conduct more in-depth selection in specific subjects during the subsequent distribution stage. Which subjects are actually adopted and how the university entrance exam and subject test are weighted still need to be confirmed based on the distribution-based admissions and departmental regulations of that academic year. [^5][^10]

Taking the 115th academic year as an example, the subject test is scheduled for July 11 and 12, 2026, with subjects including Mathematics A, Mathematics B, History, Geography, Civics and Social Studies, Physics, Chemistry, and Biology, and test-takers can freely choose. These are the specific arrangements for that academic year and do not represent that there will be no adjustments in any academic year. [^1][^14]

| Recommendations for Outstanding Students / Applications for Admission | Which university entrance exam subjects does my department list as assessments, screenings, or adoptions?                                                       |
| Distribution-Based Admissions                                         | Which university entrance exam and subject test subjects does the department adopt? How are the weights calculated?                                             |
| Reapplying                                                            | Am I participating in the subject test of the same academic year, or re-registering for the next academic year? How does the current year's guidelines specify? |

## Retaking the Exam Is Not a Button, But a Process of Recalculation

In everyday language, "retaking the exam" often mixes many situations. After the university entrance exam ends, test-takers can register for the subject test in the same academic year. Some people re-register for the university entrance exam or subject test in the next academic year. Both situations are exam registration issues, different from the personal decisions of giving up admission after enrollment, taking a leave of absence, or re-preparing. [^1][^10]

More importantly, retaking the exam does not mean that the new scores will necessarily replace the old scores, nor does it mean that the original application preferences can be retained. Test-takers must separately confirm three things: whether registration is open in the current academic year, which year or category of scores the target department adopts, and the registration and distribution deadlines of that recruitment channel. The test administration center is responsible for the exam, while the National University Recruitment Committee Association and various recruitment units announce the recruitment rules. The two cannot be mixed into the same set of rules. [^5][^10]

Therefore, the core of retaking the exam is not "taking it again will be better," but recalculating the relationship between time, subjects, and target departments. Some people need to use the subject test to strengthen specific academic subjects, others need to wait for the next academic year to re-prepare for the university entrance exam, and some discover after reviewing the department's adoption methods that the subject they originally wanted to focus on is not the most effective path. These are all strategic judgments and should not be written as guarantees of results. [^5][^10]

Retaking the exam should also not be written as simply "trying again after failure." During the joint entrance exam era, the competition was mainly concentrated on the same set of exams and distribution rules. After the implementation of multi-directional admissions, retaking the exam also involves whether departments adopt the scores, how the scores are used, how the registration schedule is connected, and whether test-takers are willing to bear an additional year of time cost. As the system options increase, the decision-making responsibility also becomes more explicit. [^5][^12][^13]

> **✦** "After taking the university entrance exam, the most important question is no longer 'how many points did I score,' but 'what does this score represent in the rules of the department I want to apply to.'"

The pressure of the university entrance exam comes from the fact that it is simultaneously a test and a filtering tool. It measures basic abilities, but different departments use it for different purposes. This difference cannot be explained with a single sentence of "scoring high is enough." Students need to understand how subject selection, level systems, departmental adoption, and subsequent data preparation are connected. [^1][^5][^6]

## People Beyond the Statistics

Looking back at the 121,535 test-takers, the statistics only show the number of people, subjects, absentees, and level distributions. But behind each number, there is someone confirming their ID before the exam, someone waiting for their child outside the exam room, someone discovering for the first time that the questions can connect to their family experiences, and someone being pulled back to reality by a sudden broadcast from the street. [^1][^7][^8][^9]

The university entrance exam will not decide everyone's future, nor can it condense three years of learning into a single fair and perfect number. What it can do is provide a common starting point under the same time and rules, where universities can begin to get to know the test-takers. What truly needs to be answered afterward is not only "how many levels did you score," but also where you want to take these abilities and how you are willing to continue using them.
