# HahaTai: The Taiwan Street Interview Kingdom Built on Getting Roasted by Passersby

> **30-second overview**: HahaTai is one of the most successful street-interview channels in Taiwan's online creator ecosystem. Launched in 2015, it went viral in 2019 with "Idlers of Daan District During Work Hours," accumulating over a million subscribers and 200 million views through its formula of "randomly stopping passersby" plus "Narrator RJ's snarky commentary." In 2022 it faced a trust crisis over the "fake passerby" scandal in Wugu; in 2023 it won both Video of the Year and Best Documentary at the Walk Bell Awards with a migrant-worker special; in 2024 it held a 10th-anniversary event at Songshan Cultural and Creative Park; in 2025 it surpassed one million subscribers, earned five Walk Bell Award nominations, and expanded its street-interview footprint from Taiwan to Hong Kong, Singapore, and Australia. This article retraces its ten-year trajectory from talent agency to street corners to the international stage.

A channel that shoves a microphone in strangers' faces and then lets a narrator ruthlessly mock the interviewees walked away in 2023 with Taiwan's highest honor for online creators — the Walk Bell Awards' Video of the Year — for a street interview featuring migrant workers [^10]. This is precisely HahaTai's central contradiction: it built its reputation on mocking passersby, yet what earned it recognition was that it actually listened to them. As of 2024, this channel operated by Jade Creative Video Management Co., Ltd. has accumulated over a million subscribers and more than 200 million total views [^1].

📝 Curator's note: Taiwan's online video scene is often asked "why Taiwan?" HahaTai's answer is simple — ask "people who don't have to work during work hours" once, and Taiwan's humanity and absurdity will reveal themselves.

## From Talent Agency to Street Interview Kingdom

Jade Creative Video Management Co., Ltd., the company behind HahaTai, was founded in July 2005. Founder Lin Chih-chieh (Jay) got his start in foreign program licensing and video distribution [^1]. On April 1, 2015, the channel officially launched [^1] [^2]. For its first four years, HahaTai was indistinguishable from most influencer channels: scattered content, low visibility, a wobbly business model. The turning point came in April 2019 — under pressure to "produce decent ratings or get fired," producer Sun-nu and director Jin-tong released the first street interview, "Idlers of Daan District During Work Hours," followed immediately by "Idlers of Wanhua District" [^6].

The show's formula was starkly simple: during work hours, find "people who don't have to work" — idlers — on the street, interview them at random, then have the unseen "Narrator Bro" RJ roast them line by line in post-production [^4]. The very first episode caught a shirtless uncle sunbathing, an audience member struck by Amis humor, and a teacher-uncle living in the "backbone of society, pillar of the nation" [^3]. Comments flooded in: "So bad it's funny, kneeling for a sequel," and the series was cemented as the channel's longest-running flagship [^3].

## Anatomy of a Video: Roasting, Heart-to-Hearts, and a Twelve-Person Division of Labor

Street interviews themselves are nothing new; HahaTai's secret lies in how it combines interviewing with post-production. TVBS observed their workflow on site: host Gary spots "people who look like they have time" on the street, opens with "How've you been lately? Anything interesting you want to share?" — and as they chat, it "accidentally turns into a heart-to-heart," with interviewees beginning to reveal real experiences [^4]. Back in the editing room, Narrator Bro RJ records his voiceover in a makeshift booth draped with blackout cloth; a single line — "Conclusion: Yilan people breathe a sigh of relief" — locks in the entire video's rhythm [^4].

RJ admits the channel's early roasting was "very aggressive, very sharp," and the reason for the change "is simple: we got cursed at" — cursed at by viewers and interviewees alike, they learned to pull back [^4]. This also reveals the channel's true operational scale: roughly twelve people behind each video, divided across scripting, post-production, and interviewing, calibrating the balance between roast-worthy moments and humor-driven traffic [^4]. Early member Sun-nu revealed in a _Marie Claire_ interview that despite signed release forms, some interviewees later objected to how they were portrayed, and she could only comply [^5]. Ordinary people magnified on camera — the cost borne by both interviewees and the channel.

> "The opener is often just 'How've you been? Anything interesting to share?' Keep talking, keep talking — accidentally it becomes a heart-to-heart. Heart-to-heart to the end, and they start slowly revealing things they actually went through." — HahaTai Narrator Bro RJ [^4]

📝 Curator's note: There's an invisible line on HahaTai's editing timeline — roasting can mock behavior, but it cannot crush dignity. That line wasn't designed; it was pushed into place, inch by inch, by the audience's curses.

## Generational Handoff of Hosts: From a Desperate Ratings Save to Everyone's Idlers

HahaTai's host lineup has gone through clear generational transitions, each coinciding perfectly with a channel expansion inflection point. The first generation centered on Sun-nu (from a web-editor background) and director Jin-tong; they accidentally pioneered the street-interview format amid 2019's workplace crisis. In a 2020 interview, Sun-nu still self-deprecatingly called herself "a resource to be cherished; trying to squeeze me is impossible" [^5]. Starting in 2021, Sun-nu officially joined the host roster, and the channel simultaneously launched the dating-reality sub-channel "HazKaHi," attempting to extend their "heart-to-heart" skill into amateur matchmaking [^1].

The third generation — Jenni (Hong Chieh-ni) and Gary (Hong Shao-chin) — took over in 2023, riding the channel's most glorious year: Walk Bell Awards double crown, "Boys Like Boys" premiere, overseas street interviews launch. In a Ming Chuan University media-department interview, Jenni and Gary described their leadership philosophy: "approachability is the entry ticket for street interviews; curiosity is the key to keeping passersby engaged" — first lower the interviewee's guard, then ask the questions no one else dares [^2]. The one constant is Narrator Bro RJ: faceless, appearing only in post-production, yet the channel's most recognizable voice. From early "very aggressive, very sharp" snark, cursed at by viewers into learning restraint, he became "roasting with warmth" [^4]. Three host teams in ten years, three new faces — but the microphone's opening line, "How've you been lately?" has never changed.

## From Mockery to Warmth: The Migrant-Worker Special and the Walk Bell Coronation

In the second half of 2022, Taiwan society began paying attention to migrant-worker issues due to the TV drama _The Defender of the Eight Gates_. HahaTai released the street-interview special "Idlers of 'Migrant Workers' on Their Days Off," asking workers who left home for Taiwan: Do you miss home? What's the secret of the perfume? How many years of work to build a house back home? Would you want your children to work in Taiwan? The video neared a million views and earned three Walk Bell Award nominations in September 2023 [^9].

On October 14, 2023, the 5th Walk Bell Awards ceremony at the Kaohsiung Music Center awarded HahaTai both "Video of the Year" and "Best Documentary," tying with A-Han Po Video and HOOK for most wins [^10]. The Baishatun Mazu Pilgrimage street interview, originally entered in the documentary category, was actually meant to be a pilgrimage-and-dating-matchmaking show — two days before departure, participants tested positive, forcing a last-minute pivot to street interviews. Jenni joked on stage, "This might be Mazu's will" [^10]. Gary dropped his usual comedy for a steady tone: "Prejudice against migrant workers still exists in society, but I believe our video can reduce mutual misunderstanding." Founder Lin Chih-chieh spoke emotionally: "My mother and my children have received tremendous help from migrant workers; they're like family to me." Both men thanked the audience in Vietnamese and Indonesian before leaving the stage [^10].

## Trust Crisis: The Wugu "Fake Passerby" Scandal

The road wasn't all smooth. In early November 2022, netizens alleged that "local residents" in a Wugu District, New Taipei City street-interview video appeared to be temp actors hired by the production company, not random interviewees. Public opinion flared; HahaTai stayed silent for nearly five days [^7] [^8]. On November 10, the channel responded in the comments of a newly uploaded video, stating that "over three years of street interviews, there have been persistent voices about fake passersby, actors, staged setups," but citing interviewee privacy, they could not address each speculation. They explained that street interviews cover all of Taiwan, and the team adjusts methods based on regional interview difficulty — besides going directly to locations, they also post advance notices inviting fans who want to be interviewed, and apologized to affected interviewees [^8].

The statement sparked a two-sided debate. Critics slammed "the entire statement dodges the real issue" and "what's a talent agency doing hiring temp actors?"; others argued "if a video makes people happy, who cares if they're real passersby?" [^8]. This scandal exposed the structural dilemma of the street-interview genre: when "randomness" is the channel's brand, any non-random intervention shakes the foundation of audience trust; yet real-world filming costs — spending a whole day finding only a few usable interviewees means losses — push teams toward semi-staged approaches [^7].

📝 Curator's note: The Wugu scandal didn't truly destroy HahaTai, because what audiences ultimately voted on wasn't "whether passersby were 100% random," but "whether these stories were real enough to make you laugh, then pause and think." That's probably the most realistic verdict for a content creator.

## Crossing Over: Gay Dating Reality, Golden-Bell-Level TV, and a 10th-Anniversary Wedding

Beyond street interviews, the HahaTai universe expanded rapidly. In 2021 it launched the dating-reality sub-channel "HazKaHi" [^1]; in 2023, HahaTai-produced _Boys Like Boys_ became Taiwan's first large-scale gay dating reality show, premiering free on HazKaHi, then streaming globally on GagaOOLala, and airing on Warner Bros. Discovery's TLC channel — Taiwan's first homegrown LGBTQ+ dating reality show on traditional TV. Broadcast rights sold to Japan, the Philippines, Thailand, and beyond [^10] [^11] [^12]. _Deadline_ and _Variety_ in November 2023 specifically noted the co-production by GagaOOLala and its subsidiary HahaTai [^11] [^12].

In 2024, HahaTai marked its 10th anniversary. From July 19–21, the channel held its first large-scale physical event, "Idlers Pass By: Songyan Stroll Market," at Songshan Cultural and Creative Park, summoning classic interviewees "back home" for thirty market stalls. On the Haha Grand Ballroom stage, they witnessed interviewees' life changes — from anonymous university student to frontman of rising rock band Mango Jam packing the Songyan Baroque Garden; singer Rosa Rosa, once interviewed, fulfilled a video promise by co-writing and performing the new song "I Get Your Weirdness" with host Gary, marking Gary's stage debut [^13]. On November 30, HahaTai joined Videoland's reality competition _Night Market King_, handling field hosting and interviews; the first two episodes broke a million views [^15] [^16].

## From Street Interviews to a Universe: Program Spectrum Expansion and Five Years of Walk Bell Footprints

Over ten years, HahaTai's program spectrum has long exceeded the two words "street interview." The official channel now updates every Thursday evening, with at least four regular series: "HahaTai Street Interviews," "HahaTai Local Experiences," "HahaTai Local Companions," and "HahaTai Rental Housing Survey" [^19], plus special projects like "Taiwan Homophone Sufferers Association" — from Taipei to Kaohsiung (RJ's hometown), from street corners to B&Bs and rental units [^1]. The Singapore Rental Housing Survey special released in August 2025 juxtaposed HDB flats and private condos, taking Taiwan audiences' familiar "rental pain" and reframing it in the world's most expensive city [^18].

The Walk Bell Awards nomination trail conveniently timestamps this expansion. 2022: first nomination for "Interview That Hits Home Award"; 2023: sweeps Video of the Year and Best Documentary; 2024: 10th anniversary, converting award glory into an interviewee reunion on the Songyan stage; 2025: 6th edition, five nominations again — "Ultimate Interview: The Married and Divorced" for Interview That Hits Home, "Randomly Sending Thai Colleague Home" for Spontaneous Travel Award, "Taiwan Homophone Name Collection" for Best Impact Award, Ukraine street-interview special and Singapore street-interview special for Group Creator of the Year and Best Program Award respectively [^18] [^20]. Though none won, parent company Portico Media's post-ceremony press release wrote that the more important milestone was: subscriber count officially broke one million that year, "representing long-term audience trust and support, and entrusting us with significant social influence and responsibility" [^20].

## Walking Taiwan, and Walking Out of Taiwan

In December 2023, HahaTai announced its first overseas street-interview stop: Australia, spanning Melbourne, Bernie, and Eden [^13]; followed by Hong Kong and Singapore. The Singapore special "Idlers of 'Singapore' During Work Hours" English-subtitled version surpassed 1.2 million views [^14]. During its 10th anniversary in 2024, the channel re-translated Taiwan-region street-interview videos into full English for broadcast on international video channel TaiwanPlus [^17]. From Daan District's shirtless uncle to Melbourne's Chinese tenants, the same microphone asks the same question: How've you been lately?

Going overseas isn't just language translation. The channel's community management faced international audiences early: official Facebook and Instagram post bilingual recruitment calls; overseas specials solicit "astonishing daily life" from locals on rent prices, cultural quirks, and working-holiday visas [^14]. And the channel's engagement with Taiwan's diverse society isn't limited to the lens: since 2020, invited by the Taiwan Love and Hope Association, they've spoken at Taichung's Southern Rainbow Street and Kaohsiung's Sunshine Queer Center, using off-camera time to connect with friendly equality organizations [^21].

This is perhaps HahaTai's most important legacy over ten years: it demonstrated that online content can be both a business and social observation. That 2019 Daan District video that nearly got Sun-nu fired let the team discover Taiwan's most precious yet overlooked scenery — the passersby on the road willing to say three more sentences to a stranger. The channel started with mockery but survived on "heart-to-hearts"; cursed for being too aggressive, it learned gentleness because of those curses. In 2025, HahaTai returns to the Walk Bell Awards with five nominations on the strength of Singapore and Australia specials [^18] [^20] — that microphone fought over by RJ, Jenni, and Gary on the Songyan stage, wanting to "marry" it, is still on the road [^13]. And the hardest lesson it leaves for Taiwan's content industry might be: algorithms change again and again, platform dividends come and go, but the passersby willing to stop are forever more worth pursuing than the fingers willing to swipe away.
