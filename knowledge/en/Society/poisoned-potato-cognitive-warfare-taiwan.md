## The Morning of the "Letter of Blood"

On April 29, 2026, at a routine press conference in Beijing, spokesperson Chen Bin-hua read out a criticism directed at Taipei:

> "Facing economic bullying and product dumping by the US through the so-called 'Taiwan-US Agreement on Reciprocal Trade,' the DPP authorities have acted stubbornly against the wishes of the Taiwanese people, openly abandoning the bottom line of food safety. After opening the door to ractopamine pork, they are now serving 'poisoned potatoes' to the Taiwanese people's dining tables, once again sacrificing the lives and health of the people as a 'letter of blood' to curry favor with the US."[^1]

The three characters "letter of blood" (投名狀, _tóu míng zhuàng_) are the key to this passage. Over the past three years, this term has become the PRC's overarching framing for the entire series of "Taiwan-US Trade Agreements": every time Taipei signs something with Washington, it is translated as "the DPP handing over a letter of blood to the US."[^2]

It must be clarified first: the "letter of blood" is not exclusively a PRC invention. Its etymology comes from the story in _Water Margin_ where Lin Chong must present a "letter of blood" (a head taken as proof) to join the Liangshan outlaws. In modern political contexts, both the blue (KMT) and green (DPP) camps use it. In the same week, the DPP also used "letter of blood" to criticize the KMT: on April 28, during negotiations on the Military Procurement Act, the DPP caucus spokesperson stated, "The Blue-White camp uses oversight as a pretext, but is actually showing favor to the mainland as a 'letter of blood'."[^32] The power of a framing word lies not in exclusive vocabulary, but in the conditioned reflex formed by repeated application. The uniqueness of the PRC version lies in framing consistency: for three years, every Taiwan-US agreement has been translated as "the DPP handing over a letter of blood to the US." The same word, different directions, but only one direction has an accumulated narrative across years.

Hours later, Taipei responded. The spokesperson for the Mainland Affairs Council used equally direct language:

> "The government will take responsibility for ensuring the health of the Taiwanese people. We do not need the Taiwan Affairs Office to say three words."[^3]

The Ministry of Health and Welfare, the Council of Agriculture, and the Executive Yuan issued a joint statement on the same day, essentially stating: the government has not opened the door to sprouting, rotting, or moldy potatoes. A double-check mechanism. "We will not allow unqualified or toxic potatoes to enter the market."[^4] Premier Cho Jung-tai had already said a week earlier at the Legislative Yuan's "Hou Sheng Hui" (a weekly open session): "If the Executive Yuan did such a thing, how would I dare stand here today?"[^5]

The two narratives are clearly walled off: one side is "poisoned potatoes on the dining table, the DPP abandoning the bottom line"; the other is "scientific evidence, strict quarantine, ensuring food safety." On the surface, it looks like yet another cross-Strait war of words. But if you look one layer deeper, this narrative war is no longer binary.

## 200 ppm is an Arithmetic Problem

First, let's talk chemistry. Let's establish a common basis for everything.

When potatoes sprout or their skin turns green, they accumulate a natural defense toxin called **solanine** (more precisely, total glycoalkaloids), primarily composed of solanine and chaconine. It is a chemical weapon evolved by solanaceous plants to combat insects and pathogens. In 1820, French pharmacist Desfosses first isolated it from belladonna berries.[^6]

![Solanine (α-solanine) molecular structure — a natural neurotoxin of solanaceous plants, non-decomposable by heating](/article-images/society/du-mali-solanine-structure.svg)

Countries basically align on the upper limit for solanine in edible potatoes: Taiwan's _Hygienic Standards for Pollutants and Toxins in Food_ sets it at 200 ppm.[^7] When the Executive Yuan refuted the PRC accusations, its phrasing was "200 ppm aligns with international standards" and "consistent with CODEX."[^14]

But **this claim of "international standard" itself has a reporting flaw**. Codex Alimentarius **has not** set a food safety standard for solanine.[^41] The true source of "200 mg/kg" is national legislation from the Swedish National Food Agency in 1990 and Health Canada in the early 1990s. Health Canada's own 2021 update proposal verbatim states: "The current 200 ppm TGA upper limit is a joint announcement by the Ministry of Health and the Department of Agriculture from over thirty years ago."[^42]

The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) 2020 scientific opinion (DOI 10.2903/j.efsa.2020.6222) provides the dietary dose "Lowest Observed Adverse Effect Level" (LOAEL): **1 mg total glycoalkaloids per kg body weight per day**.[^43] This is a different dimension of indicator from concentration standards (mg/kg potato). Converting back: for a 70 kg adult, consuming 70 mg of total glycoalkaloids in a day reaches the LOAEL. If the potato hits the 200 ppm boundary, this is equivalent to 350 g.

The same EFSA report explicitly states: "The MOE (Margin of Exposure) for young populations (children) in the highest average consumption survey and the P95 consumption in all surveys show health concerns."[^43] In other words, **for the P95 exposure (high-consumption group) of children, EFSA has officially marked health concerns**: this is at odds with the common-sense intuition that "below 200 ppm is absolutely safe."

The academic consensus on toxic doses (synthesizing EFSA 2020, JECFA 1992, and Mensinga 2005 human ascending dose studies):

| Indicator        | Value (per kg body weight)     |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Safe consumption | < 1 mg                         |
| LOAEL            | 1 mg/day (EFSA 2020)           |
| Toxic dose       | 1.2–5.1 mg (JECFA human cases) |
| Lethal dose      | 3–6 mg                         |

So, do the arithmetic: for a 70 kg adult, to reach an "immediately lethal" dose requires 210–420 mg of solanine. If the potato hits the 200 ppm boundary, one would need to consume approximately 1.05 kg of potatoes at once to reach an acute lethal dose.[^9] But this arithmetic only answers the dimension of "acute poisoning": the risk of chronic exposure to children, EFSA has already officially marked as a concern.

This arithmetic is not to say "so eat with confidence." The point is in the other direction: the imagination compressed in the six characters "poisoned potatoes on the dining table" is a danger level of "people will die immediately." But the 200 ppm boundary + lethal dose arithmetic says something else: under strict adherence to the boundary, the risk of acute poisoning will not reach the level that collapses the social medical system. **The true risk spectrum is things other than acute poisoning: chronic exposure to children at P95, acrylamide formation after processing, CIPC residues**: these scientific branches are not suitable for "letter of blood" level political mobilization, but they are real.

However, speaking of "under strict adherence to the boundary" has a premise: the government's stance must be consistent across years.

Here lies an internal contradiction rarely mentioned. The Food and Drug Administration (FDA) of Taiwan released an official health education article on May 17, 2016, titled "Potatoes Sprout and Are Toxic: Heard That Cutting Off the Sprout Eye Allows You to Eat the Rest, Is This True?" This article is still hanging on the FDA website's rumor-busting zone (fda.gov.tw) today (2026-04-30), with maintenance done on November 24, 2022. Its core conclusion is written firmly:[^33]

> "Once potatoes sprout, they should not be eaten. Even if the sprout eye is cut off or heated, they will still contain solanine." "Large amounts of solanine are produced in the whole potato, concentrating especially in the sprout eye and potato skin." "It is not edible after just cutting off the sprout eye; even high-temperature heating cannot remove the toxicity."

Put this together with FDA Food Division Chief Hsu Chao-kai's 2026-04 statement of "discard the whole potato" and "send to processing facilities for removal," and you see something strange: the same FDA, the 2016 health education website says "not edible after cutting off the sprout," while the 2026 policy says "circulates after processing selection." Ten years pass, old pages not removed, no updates issued, no time limits marked.

This is the second structural contradiction outside the 28-day signing: the government's internal stance is misaligned across years. DPP legislator Wang Ching-hui directly used this 2016 official page as evidence during her April 23 inquiry[^22], citing government-published health education, not some expired old rumor.

What about scientists' concerns? They are not in this internal contradiction: they are in another, more technical place.

## The Real Gap is Not 200 ppm, It's Called CIPC

To clarify the scientific core of the ART agreement controversy, one must bypass 200 ppm and look at the sprout inhibitor.

Imported potatoes, to prevent sprouting during long-distance transport, need to be soaked in a "sprout inhibitor" after harvest. The most commonly used in Taiwan, the US, and Japan is Chlorpropham, scientific name chlorpropham, industry abbreviation **CIPC**. CIPC slowly decomposes at room temperature into the metabolite 3-chloroaniline (3-chloroaniline)[^10].

This is where the real story lies.

The EU announced under Regulation 989/2019 that it would "not renew CIPC authorization," revoked member state authorization on January 8, 2020, and cleared all stock by October 8, 2020.[^11] The reason must be found in EFSA's 2017 chlorpropham peer review report (DOI 10.2903/j.efsa.2017.4903). This report gives three numbers that make one sit up:[^44]

- Dutch children's chronic chlorpropham dietary exposure reaches **180%** of the ADI (Acceptable Daily Intake)
- 3-chloroaniline chronic exposure reaches **195%** of the ADI
- Single potato acute chlorpropham exposure reaches **797%** of the ARfD (Acute Reference Dose)
- 3-chloroaniline acute exposure reaches **2360%** of the ARfD

EFSA's conclusion verbatim: "Due to multiple data gaps and uncertainties regarding food crop uses, a final consumer dietary intake risk assessment cannot be performed" "Experts could not exclude an endocrine-mediated mode of action for Leydig cell and thyroid effects."[^44] The recital of EU 2019/989 directly cites this EFSA conclusion as the legal reason[^11]: it is scientific numbers pushing it forward, cannot be dismissed with a single word "political decision."

Regarding the carcinogenic classification of 3-chloroaniline, here we must clarify a detail often misattributed: the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) classifies 4-chloroaniline (para-chloroaniline) as Group 2B "possibly carcinogenic to humans," but IARC **has not** individually assessed 3-chloroaniline (meta-chloroaniline, the metabolite of CIPC)[^45]. They are isomers, structurally similar but toxicological behavior may differ. The accurate statement is: 3-chloroaniline is a metabolite that EFSA has identified as having significant dietary exposure risks and endocrine disruption concerns; IARC has not directly classified this molecule. Media reports often mix the two into "3-chloroaniline IARC 2B" — this simplification makes the narrative useful, but lacks precision.

Taiwan / US / Japan / South Korea / Canada / Australia / Codex have not followed the EU's lead. The current CIPC residue limits for each country/region are summarized[^13]:

| Country/Region      | CIPC Residue Limit         |
| ------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Codex Alimentarius  | 30 ppm                     |
| Taiwan              | 30 ppm                     |
| US (40 CFR 180.181) | 30 ppm                     |
| Japan               | 50 ppm                     |
| **EU (Post-2020)**  | **0.01 ppm (default MRL)** |

The actual gap between the EU and Taiwan/US is **3000 times** (0.01 vs 30). The default MRL after EU 2019/989 is 0.01 ppm, which effectively **means "US CIPC-treated potatoes cannot be exported to the EU"**[^46]: US USDA FAS data shows exports to the EU approaching zero post-2020.

This gap was never a problem "fabricated out of thin air by the Taiwan Affairs Office." It is a scientific decision already made by the EU, which the Taiwan FDA currently chooses not to follow. But it is rarely discussed in this controversy: the DPP government's refutation focuses on "aligning with international standards" (i.e., the US/Codex 30 ppm route). The Taiwan Affairs Office's dissemination stops at the emotional impact of the three words "poisoned potatoes." Both sides avoid CIPC, this more technical but more substantial gap.

The EU banning CIPC is also not a political decision. Counting from the EU starting its review in 2015 to the stock clearing in October 2020, the whole process took 5 years. The most critical node in between was February 2019: the EU Standing Committee on Plants, Animals, Food and Feed (SCoPAFF) received 16 votes in favor of the ban out of 28 member states, but failed to pass due to the threshold of "favoring countries' total population must reach 65% of the EU." On June 17, 2019, the EU Appeal Committee directly decided not to renew authorization. On June 18, 2019, Regulation 2019/989 was officially announced. On January 8, 2020, member state authorization was revoked. On October 8, 2020, the stock clearing deadline.[^34] 5 years. This is the result of cumulative steps after EFSA pushed up the number of acute ARfD exceeding by 2360%.

If this article only wants to do one thing, it is this: pull CIPC out of the blind spot of this narrative war.

## The 28 Days from January 16 to February 13

Looking from another dimension: governance.

The timeline is indeed rushed. Economic Minister Cheng Li-chun went to Washington to sign the Taiwan-US Trade Negotiations MOU on January 16, 2026. On January 21, the Council of Agriculture announced the proposed adjustment of US potato import quarantine conditions, **shortening the comment period from the original 60 days to 14 days**. The announcement procedure was completed on February 6. On February 13, the "Agreement on Reciprocal Trade" (ART) was formally signed in Washington.[^15]

From MOU signing to ART final signing, a total of 28 days. The comment period was shortened by 46 days.

Premier Cho Jung-tai answered this timeline question during the Legislative Yuan's general inquiry on April 28:

> "There is no direct relationship. The text only mentions importing potatoes from the US. If changes to current relevant content are needed, they must be agreed upon by both parties."[^16]
> "This did not form overnight... It has nothing to do with this negotiation, it was talked about for a long time before."[^17]

The statement "has nothing to do with the negotiation" was questioned on the same day. KMT legislator Niu Hsi-tung revealed a detail during the same inquiry: the ART agreement text explicitly states "future quarantine conditions, if to be modified again, cannot be modified unilaterally, must be discussed by bilateral representatives."[^18]

If it has nothing to do with the negotiation, why does the treaty lock future quarantine changes into a bilateral treaty? Agriculture Minister Chen Chun-chi added in the same session: "In the past two or three years, we have continuously discussed with the US side, and all standards have not been relaxed."[^19]

The two statements are not contradictory, but together they present an image: technical details (200 ppm + processing procedures + individual removal) were discussed for several years. The final act of writing these technical details into a bilateral treaty with the US and completing the signing happened within 28 days. The former is fact, the latter is a political decision.

The point is not whether there is a conspiracy here, but in the structural fact that "no decision truly has nothing to do with the negotiation." When Cho Jung-tai says "has nothing to do with the negotiation," he might be referring to "substantive discussions predate the negotiation," while Niu Hsi-tung hears "the result happened to be written into the treaty" — both are talking about true things, but it sounds like they are fighting.

## "Check Every Single Potato" and 85 Border Inspectors

The third layer is a more concrete governance issue: border inspection capacity.

The Executive Yuan's official version says: if sprouting potatoes are found to be too severe during border quarantine, "we can still choose to return the whole batch." If only a small amount is sprouting, "send to processing facilities for removal."[^20]

Premier Cho Jung-tai's original words are more intense. His verbatim public commitment on April 24 was:

> "We will check the whole container, take every single potato out to check."[^35]

The four characters "take every single potato out to check" triggered internal backlash within the green camp on the same day. "Card God" Yang Hui-ju's Facebook post directly wrote:

> "Cho Jung-tai, this guy who says potatoes must be checked one by one for sprouting, why is he the Premier?"[^35]

Neither blue nor green buy it. Why? Let's look at the numbers.

DPP legislator Wang Shih-chien revealed two numbers during his Legislative Yuan inquiry on April 15: "There are only 2 facilities in Taiwan with solanine testing certification."[^21] Legislator Chen Ching-hui then revealed border inspection manpower: 105 on the roster, 85 actually hired.[^22]

KMT legislator Luo Ting-wei (relayed by Wu Tien-jung, Storm Media columnist) did the arithmetic:

> "A 40-foot container has about 150,000 potatoes. If flipping each one to check takes only 2 seconds, one container takes about 83 hours (3.5 days) of continuous man-hours. Moreover, Taiwan imports up to 300 million potatoes annually."[^23]

150,000 potatoes / container × 2 seconds / potato = 83 hours. One border inspector works 40 hours a week. Just looking at the physical time for one container, it exceeds the weekly hours of 2 inspectors. Annual import of 300 million potatoes converts to 2,000 40-foot containers.

The promise of "check every single potato out" is physically impossible.

But more noteworthy is another thing: the phrasing "check every single one" is record-breaking in previous Taiwan government food safety commitments. During the ractopamine pork period (2020-2021), the government's phrasing was "batch sampling," "100% border sampling." During the fake food oil period (2022), it was "batch inspection for radioactivity." During the egg import controversy (2023), it was "batch inspection, batch release." Every time it was "batch." Only this time in 2026 did it upgrade to "check every single one," "every single potato."

The upgrade in commitment phrasing often corresponds to political pressure. The pressure behind this upgrade is: blue camp calling the shots + green camp internal backlash + Taiwan Affairs Office simultaneous pressure + media commentator dissemination chain full spectrum: the pressure is stronger than previous times, so the commitment is pushed to a physically impossible position.

Cho Jung-tai himself changed his tune on April 28 at the Legislative Yuan to "sampling." Between "every single one" and back to "sampling," 4 days passed.

It is worth detouring to the neighbor: how Japan and South Korea handle the same thing.

Japan's answer is: **not open at all**. Japan's Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries (MAFF)细则 negotiated with the US for 19 years, and as of 2026 still only allows US chipping potatoes (processing use) import, not table stock (fresh eating)[^47]. MAFF细则 Article 5 verbatim states "inspect more than 1% of the output container units, pay special attention to damage, deformities, etc., cut open for inspection when appropriate, confirm no quarantine pests (especially nematodes)" — **the core of inspection is nematodes, no sprout inspection clause**. But Japan's safety guarantee comes from "imported varieties are limited to processing use," not "not checking sprouts." Agriculture Deputy Minister Hu Chung-i cited "Japan doesn't even check for sprouts" as a defense on 4-22, only saying half the truth.

South Korea's answer is: **mandatory standardization**. On January 23, 2026, South Korea expanded US potato access to 11 states, but conditions include "mandatory use of CIPC as sprout inhibitor (per defined specifications)," "packing shed registration," "psyllid trapping, zebra chip bacterial disease testing."[^48] The part where South Korea is stricter than Taiwan is not "lower CIPC limit," but putting responsibility **upstream to the packing stage**: US batches that potentially do not use CIPC simply cannot enter South Korea.

Comparing them, Taiwan is another path: border sampling + post-processing selection, no Korean-style packing shed registration upstream management, no Japanese-style table stock refusal to open. The physical impossibility of "check every single potato" is because Taiwan chose a middle path with neither upstream management nor use restrictions. The political responsibility Cho Jung-tai took when he said "take every single potato out to check" was essentially trying to use one sentence to make up for this structural gap.

What about the processing plants? Wu Tien-jung wrote more directly in her Storm Media column:

> "The government actively abandons the quarantine power to block sprouting potatoes at the border... Not blocking at the border, but letting processed food operators self-select, government supervision is almost non-existent."[^24]

This criticism is not directly responded to in the government's version. FDA Food Division Chief Hsu Chao-kai's explanation is: "If corruption is found, or solanine content exceeds the hygienic standard limit, etc., violating hygienic standards, the whole potato should be discarded, ensuring safety before entering subsequent processing or circulation."[^25]: But this describes the border stage. The supervision mechanism for operators self-managing after entering the processing plant is currently a blank in public data.

These are not problems that disappear automatically because of PRC accusations.

## From the 2011 Plasticizer to the 2024 Ractopamine Pork

The fourth layer: perhaps the most important one: is time.

To understand why the three words "poisoned potatoes" could explode such a large reaction in Taiwanese society in April 2026, we must return to 2011.

In March of that year, during the execution of the "Strengthen Crackdown on Counterfeit and Fake Drugs Project" by Yang Ming-yu, a senior official at the Food and Drug Administration of the Department of Health, an意外 discovery of the plasticizer DEHP was made in a probiotic product. On May 23, the Department of Health held a press conference. Digging down this line, it was found that the upstream "cloud agent" (legal food additive) of Taiwan's food industry had long been illegally mixed with industrial plasticizers by Yushen Fragrance and Binhan Company. DEHP was subsequently detected in beverages, juice, pastries, health foods, and medicines: the number of manufacturers potentially using Yushen or Binhan's toxic cloud agent expanded from 119 to 155.[^26] Taiwanese people discovered that the source of what they had been drinking for the past twenty years contained a toxic additive.

On September 4, 2014, the rancid oil incident. Pingshan's Guo Lie-cheng's recycling oil factory recycled rancid oil, made into products with a ratio of "33% low-quality oil + 67% lard," sold to Charkuan Enterprise, made into "Quantong Fragrant Lard" entering the downstream food supply chain.[^27] In the same year, another 759 tons of non-edible lard imported from Hong Kong and Japan was exposed; estimated over 200 tons of gutter oil flowed into the market.

2020 to 2021, ractopamine pork. The Tsai Ing-wen government announced the opening of US pork containing ractopamine for import, the KMT launched a referendum to oppose it. On December 18, 2021, the referendum result: "Anti-Ractopamine Pork" Case 18 agreed 3,936,386 votes (48.79%) against disagreed 4,131,371 votes (51.21%), failed by a margin of about 195,000 votes.[^28] During the ractopamine pork period, IORG (Taiwan Information Environment Research Center) compiled 8 key PRC narratives on the referendum issues, of which **7 were related to ractopamine pork**[^29]: this ratio itself is data evidence for "food safety issues as the main axis of cognitive warfare."

The legal endings all dragged on for a long time. The plasticizer case was finalized on August 3, 2018, with civil joint liability compensation of 3.95 million NTD: distributed to 561 consumers, about 7,000 NTD each. Lai Chun-chieh (Yushen head) 15 years finalized, Jian Ling-yuan 12 years, Wang Fen 10 years; Chen Che-hsiung (Binhan head) already dead, public prosecution not accepted.[^36] The rancid oil case: Charkuan's Ye Wen-xiang 22 years finalized in September 2017, Guo Lie-cheng 20 years finalized in February 2020: longer than the common sentence for robbery and murder. Charkuan Enterprise was fined 120 million NTD and dissolved in 2017.[^37]

Ractopamine pork is another ending. After the referendum failed, over 4 years: 2021 (the year of the referendum) US pork imports 922 metric tons, 2023 surged to 16,313 metric tons, a 17-fold growth. No collective acute poisoning events: but the Consumer Foundation tested 600 market foods, **only 2 items** labeled with US pork ingredients.[^38] Health risks did not explode, the trust gap did not heal. This is the question the following article wants to answer.

The common point between the 2011, 2014, and 2020 events: none were exposed by foreigners, all were discovered by Taiwanese society itself that it had been deceived. The plasticizer was accidentally discovered by a Department of Health inspector; the rancid oil was reported by the public to the Pingshan County Police; the ractopamine pork is the structural contradiction of the Taiwan-US trade negotiation.

After each event, the "lesson" Taiwanese society learned is in the same direction: **government and food industry commitments are untrustworthy**.

Dr. Shen Cheng-nan used a concept in his April 29 YouTube commentary on the poisoned potato controversy:

> "It is a collective narcissism, a part of excessive attention to health preservation." "No country is worried about poisoning from eating sprouting potatoes!"[^30]

The sentence "no country is worried" is **not tenable** on the factual level.

The US FDA's official guidance on fda.gov for Acrylamide and Diet explicitly states: "Potatoes should be used that are as fresh as possible, have no green spots or sprouts, and are stored above 6°C (not in the refrigerator)."[^49] The EU 2017/2158 acrylamide regulation **mandates** food operators "sprouting shall be suppressed in long term stored potatoes."[^50] Health Canada sets a 200 ppm legal upper limit and attaches "minimising exposure to glycoalkaloids" official guidance.[^42] JECFA 1992 warns: "The available epidemiological and experimental data from human and laboratory animal studies did not permit the determination of a safe level of intake" "Lethal dose 1.2-5.1 mg/kg bw."[^51] EFSA 2020 gives a health concern determination for children's P95 exposure.[^43]

Advanced countries are not "not afraid." The difference is in the handling mechanism: using sprout inhibitors (CIPC, 1,4-DMN) + 200 mg/kg legal concentration upper limit + acrylamide prevention guidance + storage protocol multi-layer defense line to disperse risk. The core of this Taiwan controversy is not "should we be afraid," but which of these layers **Taiwan has done, which it has missed**.

Dr. Shen Cheng-nan's medical description itself ("no country argues about this to this extent") is too far from the actual regulatory gap: when he uses the words "collective narcissism," "excessive health preservation," he inadvertently does the thing of psychologizing the public's distrust of food safety: translating it from "reasonable suspicion of institutions" to "excessive anxiety about one's own body."

This translation direction is problematic. The 2011 plasticizer was a real twenty-year deception; the rancid oil was a real 645 tons of gutter oil; the ractopamine pork is a real bilateral negotiation structure. When the public in 2026 hears "US potato relaxation" and immediately becomes alert, the memory they mobilize comes from the accidental discovery of the 2011 Department of Health FDA.

The scars accumulated over 15 years are real. It is not written by the PRC, but it has become the foundation the PRC narrative can step into.

## How the "Letter of Blood" Steps In Precisely

Putting the previous four layers together, the precision of the "letter of blood" narrative becomes clear.

The framework of PRC cognitive warfare studies on Taiwan (Shen Bo-yang, IORG, Taiwan Democracy Lab) is roughly as follows:[^31]

```
Food Safety Concerns (Physiological Layer)
   → Government Incapacity (Governance Layer)
     → DPP Abandoning Sovereignty (Political Layer)
       → "Currying Favor with the US," "Letter of Blood" (Diplomatic Layer)
         → Unification Narrative: Only cross-Strait unification can protect Taiwanese people's health (Ultimate Framework)
```

Chen Bin-hua's April 29 sentence "once again sacrificing the lives and health of the people as a 'letter of blood' to curry favor with the US" is exactly the compression of the above five layers: pushing from food safety concerns all the way to the diplomatic framework. The word "letter of blood" is particularly important, being the PRC's overall framing for the family of Taiwan-US trade agreements (ractopamine pork, chips, ART, arms sales) in recent years: every agreement = DPP handing over a letter of blood to the US.

Shen Bo-yang, who has studied PRC cross-Strait public opinion warfare for 8 years, gives a quantitative judgment:

> "80% of the PRC's public opinion warfare against Taiwan has nothing to do with 'true or false,' but is mostly 'narrative attacks,' that is, fabricating a perspective."[^52]

Poisoned potatoes is a textbook example. 200 ppm, solanine, CIPC, 85 border inspectors, EU 0.01 ppm, Cho Jung-tai "take every single potato out to check," MOHW 2016 health education article: every one is a true fact. But after being strung together by the narrative framework of "letter of blood," "Lai government currying favor with the US," it becomes material for the Taiwan Affairs Office's 4-29 press conference. Shen Bo-yang's analysis of the PRC's five cross-Strait lines is clear: "The information warfare initiated by the Communist Party of China has 5 lines, namely the Ministry of State Security, the United Front Work Department of the CPC Central Committee, the Taiwan Affairs Office of the CPC Central Committee, the PLA, and the Communist Youth League."[^53]: This poisoned potato is the specific task of the Taiwan Affairs Office line.

Shen Bo-yang also mentioned the concept of "online and offline, top and bottom pincer attack": "For a cognitive warfare to produce effects in Taiwan, it must be done both online and offline... ## So What is the Problem

Scientifically, the 200 ppm solanine boundary + processing selection + sampling: in terms of **acute poisoning risk**, the gap between the new system and the old system is smaller than the degree of concern. But scientific assessment is not the true battlefield of this controversy.

But they are the true shape.

The most honest description of this controversy might be this: scientifically, there are no "poisoned potatoes that kill immediately" entering the Taiwanese dining table; in governance, there are real relaxations and supervision breaks; cognitive warfare precisely hits the 15-year food safety scars; and the scars themselves — should not be treated as emotion, nor as evidence.
