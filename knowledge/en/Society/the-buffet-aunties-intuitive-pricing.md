# The Mysterious Visual Estimation and Calculation Skills of Buffet Aunties

Taiwan's first buffet restaurant in 1962 lasted only two years, but the visual pricing techniques it left behind remain a human skill that is hardest for AI to replicate.

---

> **30-Second Overview:** The visual pricing used by "Buffet Aunties" (the women running these establishments) is not random guesswork; it is a real-time decision-making system integrating experience, observation, and social judgment.
> Even though AI can now achieve 96.3% accuracy in food recognition, it still cannot replicate the dynamic pricing logic of these aunties, which considers "the customer's appearance," "their plating technique," and "the density of oil."
> This is a "Human Fuzzy Pricing System" more complex than machine learning.

---

## A Forgotten Starting Point

In June 1962, at No. 52 Guanhua Street, the "Taipei First Quick-Service Restaurant" officially opened. With 150 seats, fixed prices, self-service trays, and sanitized utensils—this experimental restaurant, funded by the Taiwan Provincial Materials Bureau, was the starting point of Taiwan's buffet culture.

However, it lasted less than two years. In February 1964, it closed due to building renovations.

Yet, the seeds planted by this short-lived restaurant continue to sprout in tens of thousands of buffet shops across Taiwan today. However, something went awry during the evolutionary process: **Fixed pricing disappeared, replaced by a mysterious system of visual estimation.**

> **📝 Curator's Note**
> Taiwan's first buffet restaurant in 1962 had fixed prices, but modern buffet shops have instead abandoned this "modernized" feature. Is this a technological regression or a cultural evolution?

If you ask a buffet aunty, "How do you calculate it?" she will say, "You just get used to seeing it."

But this "getting used to seeing it" is actually one of the last professional skills that cannot be fully replicated by AI.

---

## When AI Meets the Buffet: 96.3% vs. Human Intuition

In 2021, a Taiwanese scholar published a paper titled _A Framework of Visual Checkout System Using Convolutional Neural Networks for Bento Buffet_. The team used six AI models, including AlexNet, VGG16, and ResNet50, to train the recognition of 22 types of buffet dishes.

The final result: **9-6.3% accuracy, with a recognition time of 0.108 seconds per image**.

Sounds impressive? But the research team admitted one problem: "Customer disputes regarding price calculation" remain unresolved.

Why? Because AI can only perform "food recognition"; it cannot perform "price judgment."

> **💡 Did You Know?**
> This study used 22 types of dishes and 2,025 training images, requiring fixed lighting and standardized partitioned plates. But in a real buffet shop, the aunties face: countless variations in dishes, random plating styles, different lighting conditions—and most crucially, **the different social backgrounds of every customer**.

---

## The Aunty's "Multi-Variable Real-Time Algorithm"

You might think she is looking at weight, but she is actually running a complex decision tree:

### Variable 1: Food State Analysis

- **Rice density**: Fluffy rice looks like more; compressed rice weighs more.
- **Oil content in vegetables**: High oil causes volume expansion, but nutritional density is high.
- **The "presence" of meat**: It's not about weight, but visual proportion.
- **Stacking technique**: Whether they are intentionally creating air gaps or pressing it down tightly.

### Variable 2: Observation of Customer Behavior

- **Plating technique**: Novices scoop politely; veterans use techniques to pile food high.
- **Eye contact**: Direct eye contact indicates confidence; avoiding eye contact suggests guilt.
- **Judging by attire**: A student? An office worker? A tourist?

### Variable 3: Social Dynamic Corrections

- **Regular customer discounts**: "Let's just call it 80 [TWD]."
- **Student compassion**: Automatic price reduction of 10–20 TWD.
- **Greed penalty**: Adding a "service fee" if they pile too much on.

> **⚠️ Controversial Viewpoint**
> This "person-dependent" pricing logic carries obvious social bias. For the same amount of food, someone in a suit and someone in flip-flops might be charged different prices. But this is how human society truly operates—an "unfair" system filled with emotion, bias, and compassion.

---

## Fuzzy Consistency: The Divine Skill AI Cannot Achieve

The most magical part is that the same aunty will charge "roughly" the same price for the same portion—usually within a range of ±10 TWD.

This ability is called a "Human Fuzzy Pricing System." She is not performing precise calculations, but rather **pattern recognition** + **situational judgment**.

Robotics pioneer Rodney Brooks once said, "Common sense is the hardest thing for AI to replicate." The pricing of buffet aunties is the perfect illustration—they do not use mathematics; they use "social common sense."

### Fundamental Differences: Humans vs. AI

| Human Aunties                      | AI Systems                             |
| :--------------------------------- | :------------------------------------- |
| Fuzzy logic + Social judgment      | Precise recognition + Fixed rules      |
| Considers _who_ is buying          | Only looks at _what_ is being bought   |
| Dynamic situational adjustment     | Requires retraining                    |
| 30 years of accumulated experience | Requires thousands of annotated images |
| Quotes a price in 0.5 seconds      | Recognizes in 0.108s, but cannot price |

## When the Buffet Becomes the Last Human Bastion

Amazon Go, unmanned supermarkets, QR code ordering—the retail industry is being taken over by AI. But buffet shops still need "humans."

Not because the technology is backward, but because the core of a buffet is not "selling food," it is "selling flexibility."

> **📊 Data Source**
> According to statistics from the Ministry of Economic Affairs, the number of chain restaurants in Taiwan increased from 28,800 in 2015 to 32,800 in 2020, yet traditional buffet shops remain tucked away in street corners and alleys, having not been replaced by chains or automation.

The aunty's pricing logic includes:

**Compassion** (cheaper for students)
**Sense of fairness** (charging more for greed)
**Human touch/warmth** (discounts for regulars)
**Survival wisdom** (standard price for tourists)

These "inefficient" human elements are exactly what AI will never be able to learn.

---

## A Craft About to Vanish?

You might say, "This fuzzy pricing should have been phased out long ago; it's unfair and inefficient."

But imagine if one day all buffets were replaced by QR code ordering and AI pricing—what would you miss?

**You would miss that 0.5-second human gaze.**

In 그 moment, she is not just estimating weight; she is evaluating your situation and understanding your needs. She might give you a little extra meat, or she might charge you 10 dollars less.

> **✦** "In a world ruled by algorithms, the buffet aunty is the last human decision-maker."

---

## Conclusion: An Algorithm More Complex Than AI

You think she is just calling out a random number.

But in that instant, she completes: a real-time decision-making system integrating experience, observation, social judgment, and fuzzy logic.

And as you stand there, holding your lunch box, the only thing you can do is one thing:

"Okay... thank you, Auntie."

Perhaps, this is the last kind of warmth that cannot be digitized.
