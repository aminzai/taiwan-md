# Watchout: A Decade of Taiwan's Civic Tech Practice, From Legislative Oversight to Whole-of-Society Defense

> **30-Second Overview:** Watchout (Watchout), founded in November 2013, marks a significant milestone in the development of Taiwan’s civic tech and independent media[^1]. As the first online platform in Asia dedicated to monitoring government operations, Watchout has continuously lowered the threshold for citizen participation in politics through projects such as "Legislative Yuan Watch," "Mayor, Can We Ask?", "Philosophy on the Grill," and the _Active Action Guide_ released in collaboration with international NGOs[^1] [^2] [^10]. This article provides a comprehensive梳理 of its founding intentions, role in the Sunflower Student Movement, core projects, community management, and organizational governance challenges, showcasing its historical position as Taiwan’s "digital sentinel."

In contemporary Taiwan’s civil society, **Watchout Co., Ltd.** holds a highly symbolic position[^1]. Its name is derived from the English phrase "Watch out," originally meaning "be vigilant and alert." Its core mission is to urge elected representatives and government officials to make public issues transparent through digital tools[^1].

## Key Turning Point: The Sunflower Student Movement and Civic Awakening

Watchout was founded just in time for the **Sunflower Student Movement (318 Movement)** in 2014. In this movement that reshaped Taiwan’s political landscape, Watchout played a key role in information dissemination. When traditional media struggled to convey complex details from the legislative chamber in real time, Watchout collaborated with the civic tech community, such as g0v, to transform rigid legislative disputes into understandable digital information, encouraging more young people to care about legislative operations[^9].

To make politics less solemn and distant, Watchout created the virtual character **"Ah Cao"**. Through anthropomorphic narratives, humorous social media interactions, and visualized information, Watchout successfully "softened" complex public issues, giving them high viral potential on social media and attracting groups previously indifferent to politics to participate in discussions[^12].

---

## Core Project Practices: Deepening Democracy and Civil Defense Resilience

Watchout transformed rigid political agendas into information accessible to the public through "Legislative Yuan Watch." During the 2014 Taipei mayoral election, Watchout launched the interactive platform "Mayor, Can We Ask?," breaking the limitations of one-way communication in traditional policy announcement events and setting an important example for digital participation promoted by various sectors in Taiwan subsequently.

To deepen the foundation of rational critical thinking in civil society, Watchout established the "Watchout Civic Academy" in September 2014, with philosopher **Ju Chia-an** serving as editor-in-chief, developing the "Philosophy on the Grill" community[^6]. This community explores the core values and ethical principles behind public policies through accessible philosophical articles and comics.

_Image: Watchout’s collaboration with international NGOs to release the "Active Action Guide" marks its strategic shift toward strengthening social defense resilience._

In recent years, facing geopolitical threats, Watchout collaborated with the US NGO **"Spirit of America" (SOA)** to release the _Active Action Guide_[^10]. Unlike official versions, this manual focuses more on the psychological impact, material preparation, and practical response for citizens when war breaks out, aiming to reduce public fear of "hybrid warfare" and war threats, transforming them into concrete action power[^4] [^11].

---

## Organizational Governance and Challenges: The Friction Between Ideals and Systems

As a private institution aspiring to be a social enterprise, Watchout has also faced severe internal governance tests during its development.

In June 2015, Watchout was embroiled in a financial controversy involving its founder and then-CEO, **Liu Lin-wei**, who was suspected of embezzling funds without authorization. This sparked high public concern over the financial transparency of crowdfunding[^5]. Watchout promptly dismissed him and commissioned an accounting firm to conduct a comprehensive financial audit, publishing relevant reports to clarify the flow of funds[^5].

In May 2021, Watchout faced another major management crisis, with then-CEO **Lin Tsu-yi** suspected of abnormal financial flows[^3]. The Watchout board of directors dismissed him immediately and filed a lawsuit with judicial authorities[^3]. Although these two incidents impacted the brand image, they prompted the Taiwan civic tech community to deeply reflect on the importance of organizational governance and public accountability.

---

## Historical Position and Future Outlook

Looking at Watchout’s development over more than a decade, it is not just an independent media outlet but also a microcosm of the evolution of digital democracy in Taiwan. Facing the proliferation of disinformation and the threat of hybrid warfare, whether Watchout can continue to function as a digital sentinel tests not only the internal governance wisdom of its management team but also serves as an important indicator of whether Taiwan’s civil society can continuously deepen democratic resilience.
