## From Oil Crisis to OEM Wave: The Emergence of the Kingdom of Bicycles

The rise of Taiwan’s bicycle industry is imbued with the spirit of the times and resourcefulness. In the early 1970s, the first oil crisis broke out globally, causing a surge in demand for energy conservation, carbon reduction, and recreational sports in European and American societies, triggering the first wave of the bicycle boom [^4]. At that time, small and medium-sized enterprises centered in Taichung leveraged their flexible mechanical processing capabilities to secure a large number of OEM (Original Equipment Manufacturer) orders from the United States and Europe.

In 1972, Liu Chin-piao founded Giant in Taichung, followed by Zeng Dinghuang founding Merida [^4]. These two future global dominators started from obscure parts and basic OEM work. However, Taiwanese manufacturers did not settle for simple low-cost OEM manufacturing. As Taiwan’s economy boomed in the 1980s and the New Taiwan dollar appreciated significantly, traditional labor-intensive industries faced severe pressures of offshoring and rising costs, forcing Taiwan’s bicycle industry to move toward technological upgrading and automated production. In this process, research institutions such as the Industrial Technology Research Institute (ITRI) actively transferred composite materials and aerospace-grade technologies to the private sector, laying a solid technical foundation for Taiwan’s future dominance in the global carbon fiber frame market.

## The Co-opetition Miracle of A-Team: An Organizational Revolution Breaking the OEM Destiny

Entering the late 1990s, with the rise of low-cost manufacturing bases in mainland China, Taiwan’s traditional bicycle export volumes faced severe shocks, and the industry once faced the fatal crisis of “hollowing out” [^9]. It was at this critical juncture of life and death that Taiwan’s bicycle industry demonstrated a responsiveness that amazed global business scholars.

In 2003, under the joint advocacy of Giant Chairman Liu Chin-piao and Merida’s senior management, Taiwan’s domestic bicycle frame manufacturers and more than ten core component suppliers formally allied to establish the famous “A-Team (Taiwan Bicycle Industry Association)” [^1] [^2] [^3]. Breaking the traditional taboo of “competitors are like enemies,” A-Team introduced Toyota-style Lean Production and concurrent engineering, tightly linking the previously fragmented supply chain.

Industry history researchers point out that the key at that time was not price-cutting competition, but how to shorten delivery times and minimize defect rates. A-Team lifted Taiwan’s supply chain from price competition to a new track of value competition.

## The Rise of Carbon Fiber Craftsmanship and Invisible Champions: Leading the Global High-End Track

Entering the 21st century, Taiwan’s bicycle industry experienced another structural technological leap. In addition to the two bicycle giants, Taiwan has nurtured countless “invisible champion” component manufacturers dominating the globe, such as global chain leader KMC, tire giant Maxxis, high-end pedal specialist Wellgo, and saddle manufacturer Velo. These enterprises have mastered global pricing power and technical standards in their respective niche fields.

In the field of carbon fiber frame manufacturing, Taiwanese manufacturers have mastered core processes from mold design and multi-axis lay-up technology (Lay-up Tech) to high-strength sintering, making it almost impossible for top European and American road bike, mountain bike, and emerging gravel bike brands to operate without the support of Taiwan’s supply chain [^7]. Furthermore, with the recent global outdoor leisure trend shifting toward multi-terrain riding gravel bikes, Taiwan’s supply chain seized the opportunity for global new product launches first, leveraging extreme product flexibility and mold development speed.

Facing the recent global net-zero carbon emission and silver-haired leisure trends, Taiwan’s two giants and component giants have quickly entered the high-end electric bicycle (e-bike) market [^10]. By perfectly combining precision motors, battery management systems, and traditional mechanical craftsmanship, Taiwan has maintained steady growth in revenue and technology despite the turbulent global trade environment.

## From Manufacturing to Culture: Island-Wide Cycling Networks and Taiwan’s Soft Power

The uniqueness of Taiwan’s bicycle industry lies not only in world-class manufacturing capabilities but also in the “cycling culture” cultivated jointly by industry, government, academia, and research. With the government promoting the “Island-Wide Route 1” and a complete island-wide bicycle path network, Taiwan has nurtured a unique nationwide “Island-Wide Cycling” sport. This deep riding culture, in turn, has become the best laboratory for Taiwan’s brands to test new products and collect real Big Data.

Additionally, the Taipei Cycle, held annually in Taiwan, has become the barometer of the global bicycle industry. From an early OEM hub to now leading the global innovation center for micro-mobility and green smart vehicles, Taiwan has turned the island’s resilience with two wheels, earning global respect.

## From A-Team to A+Team: Embracing the New Era of ESG and Smart Manufacturing

In recent years, with the restructuring of global supply chains, the implementation of the European Union’s Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM), and strict net-zero carbon requirements, Taiwan’s bicycle industry is facing another historic upgrade. New-generation industry leaders are actively promoting the transition from “A-Team” to “A+Team,” extending the scope of cooperation from pure production efficiency to digital transformation and ESG green manufacturing [^8] [^10]. After surviving the global inventory destocking test in the post-pandemic era, Taiwanese manufacturers have demonstrated high resilience, successfully standing firm in the next wave of green transportation through smart production and dynamic supply chain adjustments.

Under the promotion of the Taipei Cycle and the Taiwan Smart Electric Bicycle Association (TSEBA), Taiwan’s supply chain is actively building a green ecosystem, from low-carbon aluminum applications and recycled carbon fiber technologies to the introduction of vehicle IoT smart logistics systems, demonstrating that Taiwan’s manufacturing sector is not just an OEM but a key driver of global sustainable transportation (Green Mobility) [^8] [^11].

## Conclusion: Wheel Tracks Extending to a Sustainable Future

Looking back at the iron oxen bicycles and small parts processing workshops from half a century ago, Taiwan’s bicycle industry has turned a globally watched miracle with its hands in the fields of Taichung. This is not just a commercial story about manufacturing and exports; it is a contemporary legend written by Taiwan’s small and medium-sized enterprises in the face of globalization shocks, through innovative cooperation, lean production, and an indomitable craftsmanship spirit.

As the next wave of green transportation and smart vehicles sweeps in, Taiwan’s bicycle tracks remain steadily at the forefront of the global industry.
