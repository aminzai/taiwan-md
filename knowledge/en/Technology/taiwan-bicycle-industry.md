## From Oil Crisis to Manufacturing Boom: The Genesis of the Bicycle Kingdom

The rise of Taiwan's bicycle industry is steeped in the spirit of opportunity and resilience. In the early 1970s, the first oil crisis erupted globally, sparking a surge in demand for energy-efficient transportation and leisure activities in the West, igniting the first wave of bicycle enthusiasm [^4]. At that time, small and medium enterprises centered around Taichung leveraged flexible mechanical processing capabilities to secure a flood of OEM (Original Equipment Manufacturing) orders from the United States and Europe.

In 1972, Liu Jintiao founded Giant Machinery (Giant) in Taichung, followed by Niu Dinghong who established Merida [^4]. Both would later dominate the global market, yet in their early days, they too started as obscure component suppliers and basic contract manufacturers. However, Taiwanese manufacturers were not content with mere low-cost manufacturing. As the 1980s saw Taiwan's economy take off and the New Taiwan dollar appreciate significantly, traditional labor-intensive industries faced severe challenges of relocation and rising costs, pushing Taiwan's bicycle industry toward technological upgrades and automated production. During this period, research institutions like ITRI actively transferred composite materials and aerospace-grade technologies to the private sector, laying a solid technological foundation for Taiwan's eventual dominance in the global carbon fiber frame market.

## A-Team's Collaborative Miracle: An Organizational Revolution Breaking the Contract Manufacturing Paradigm

By the late 1990s, as low-cost manufacturing bases in mainland China rose, Taiwan's traditional bicycle exports faced severe impacts, and the industry teetered on the brink of a "hollowing out" crisis [^9]. It was precisely at this life-or-death moment that Taiwan's bicycle industry displayed an adaptive capability that left global business management scholars in awe.

In 2003, under the joint initiative of Giant Chairman Liu Jintiao and Merida executives, Taiwan's complete bicycle manufacturers and over a dozen key component suppliers formally united to establish the renowned "A-Team (Taiwan Bicycle Industry Association)" [^1] [^2] [^3]. Breaking the traditional taboo of "treating competitors as enemies," A-Team introduced Japanese Toyota-style lean production (Lean Production) and concurrent engineering, tightly integrating previously fragmented supply chains.

Industry historians note that the key at the time was not price competition, but how to shorten delivery times and minimize defect rates. A-Team propelled Taiwan's supply chain from price competition to value competition, charting a new course.

## Rise of Carbon Fiber Craftsmanship and Hidden Champions: Leading the Global High-End Track

Entering the 21st century, Taiwan's bicycle industry experienced another structural leap in technology. Beyond the complete vehicle giants, Taiwan has nurtured countless "hidden champion" component suppliers that dominate their respective global niches, such as the world's chain leader KMC, tire giant Maxxis, high-end pedal specialist Wellgo, and saddle manufacturer Velo. These companies command global pricing power and technical standards in their specialized fields.

In carbon fiber frame manufacturing, Taiwanese manufacturers master core technologies from mold design, multi-axis lay-up techniques (Lay-up Tech), to high-strength sintering, making it nearly impossible for top-tier European and American road bike, mountain bike, and emerging gravel bike brands to operate without the support of Taiwan's supply chain [^7]. Additionally, as the global outdoor leisure trend shifts toward multi-terrain riding with gravel bikes, the Taiwanese supply chain leverages exceptional product flexibility and mold development speed to seize the initiative in global new product launches.

Facing recent global trends toward carbon neutrality and silver-aged leisure, Taiwan's leading brands and component giants have swiftly moved into the high-end electric assist bicycle (e-bike) market [^10]. By perfectly integrating precision motors, battery management systems, and traditional mechanical craftsmanship, Taiwan continues to maintain steady growth in revenue and technology even amid turbulent global trade environments.

## From Manufacturing to Culture: The Island-Wide Cycling Network and Taiwan's Soft Power

What makes Taiwan's bicycle industry unique goes beyond world-class manufacturing prowess—it also includes the "cycling culture" jointly cultivated by industry, government, and academia. With the government's promotion of the "Island Ring No. 1" cycling route and the completion of a comprehensive cycling path network across the island, Taiwan has fostered a distinctive "cycling around the island" mass movement. This deep-rooted cycling culture serves as the ideal testing ground for Taiwan's brands to launch new products and collect real-world big data.

Moreover, the annual Taipei International Bicycle Show (Taipei Cycle) has become a barometer for the global bicycle industry. From early days as a contract manufacturing hub to today's role as a leading innovation center for micro-mobility and green smart mobility, Taiwan has spun its two wheels into a respected island of resilience.

## From A-Team to A+Team: Embracing the New Era of ESG and Smart Manufacturing

In recent years, as global supply chains undergo restructuring, the European Union implements carbon border adjustment mechanisms (CBAM), and carbon neutrality requirements tighten, Taiwan's bicycle industry is undergoing a historic upgrade. Pushed by a new generation of industry leaders, the transition from "A-Team" to "A+Team" is extending collaboration from mere production efficiency to digital transformation and ESG green manufacturing [^8] [^10]. After weathering the global inventory digestion challenges of the post-pandemic era, Taiwanese manufacturers have demonstrated remarkable resilience, stabilizing their position through smart production and dynamic supply chain adjustments to ride the next wave of green transportation.

Under the promotion of the Taipei International Bicycle Show and the Taiwan Electric Bicycle Industry Association (TSEBA), the Taiwanese supply chain is actively building a green ecosystem, from low-carbon aluminum applications, recycled carbon fiber technologies, to vehicle IoT smart logistics systems, showcasing the powerful strength of Taiwan's manufacturing—not just as a contract manufacturer, but as a key driver of global sustainable mobility (Green Mobility) [^8] [^11].

## Conclusion: The Journey Extending Toward a Sustainable Future

Looking back half a century from iron-head bicycles and small component workshops, Taiwan's bicycle industry has spun a globally acclaimed miracle in the fields of Taichung. This is not just a story of manufacturing and export—it's a contemporary legend of Taiwan's small and medium enterprises facing global challenges with innovative cooperation, lean production, and indomitable craftsmanship.

As the next wave of green transportation and smart mobility surges forward, Taiwan's bicycle journey continues to lead the global industry at the forefront.
