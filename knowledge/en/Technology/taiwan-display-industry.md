# Taiwan's Display Industry: A Pivot from TFT-LCD to Micro LED

> **30-Second Overview:** The Taiwanese optoelectronics industry took shape in the 1970s; the Industrial Technology Research Institute (ITRI) actively invested in display technology starting in 1987, completing Taiwan's first 10.4-inch TFT-LCD panel in 1996. By 2002, Taiwan was the world's second-largest panel producer and supplier, holding over 30% market share. Today, this industry—which once pursued scale based on large-format LCDs—is shifting its competitive arena toward Micro LED, smart automotive displays, transparent displays, panel-level packaging, and system integration.[^1]

## How a Glass Substrate Became an Industrial Chain

The Taiwanese display industry did not suddenly emerge from a single panel manufacturer. ITRI reviews indicate that the optoelectronics industry in Taiwan began developing in the 1970s, subsequently establishing a complete industrial chain. Starting its investment in display technology in 1987, ITRI built a display industry chain comparable to the semiconductor industry within seven years. The key to this history was not just making a panel, but gradually connecting components, materials, processes, equipment, and downstream products.[^1]

The milestones listed by ITRI gave concrete shape to this process of "connecting." In 1996, ITRI completed Taiwan's first 10.4-inch TFT-LCD panel and transferred the technology to companies such as Chance, Nanya Plastics, China Steel, and Sharp; in 2000, ITRI also co-formed the Taiwan TFT LCD Association with Acer Display Technology, Chance, HannStar, E Ink, Unilux Optoelectronics, Chemix, and Wistron, providing a collaborative platform for upstream and downstream industries.[^1]

The Industrial Economics Research Institute (IERI) placed another node of industrialization in 2000: Dagori Technology, the predecessor to AUO, produced Taiwan's first 17-inch TFT-LCD panel module; mass production of this production line began in February 2001, which the institute called Taiwan's first fourth-generation factory. These two time points are not contradictory: the former is a milestone for R&D and technology transfer, while the latter is an industrialization node where the panel module entered mass production.[^8]

## Scale Was Once the Answer

The year 2002 was a moment that clearly defined the Taiwanese panel industry. ITRI's industrial history data indicates that Taiwan became the world's second-largest panel producer and supplier in that year, with a market share exceeding 30%, and the display industry output reaching trillions of NTD.[^1] In 2018, when the Presidential Office reviewed the industry, it stated that Taiwan's panel output was still ranked second globally, having reached 1.35 trillion NTD in the previous year, driving over one hundred thousand jobs. This is historical data from 2018, not current statistics, but it shows how panels once served as a pillar of Taiwanese industrial policy and employment structure.[^3]

In 2017, the National Research Institute's Technology Policy Research and Information Center organized a Micro LED process and equipment symposium involving over 50 companies and nearly one hundred experts from panel, LED, solutions, equipment, and materials sectors. This event showed that industrial transformation did not begin with a single panel company changing products; rather, it required the participation of panel, LED, equipment, material, and solution companies together. The paper also noted that at the time, challenges such as mass transfer, repair, particulates, and optoelectronic testing still existed for Micro LED; the assessment of future commercialization timelines in this article reflects the industry views from the 2017 event and should not be taken as a realized prediction.[^2]

> **📝 Curator's Note:** Panels are not merely an appendage to semiconductors. They also rely on cleanrooms, materials, process integration, precision equipment, and long-term capital investment, yet they place the technological results directly into objects people see every day: TVs, mobile phones, car windows, e-paper signs. What is most memorable about Taiwan's display industry is not just its former ranking as second in the world, but how it demonstrated Taiwan's ability to transform R&D institute technology into manufacturing capability shared by many companies. This is why the next transformation cannot be accomplished by a single panel company.

## The Cost of Scale Competition from LCD Success

The difficulties faced by the panel industry are another side of its former success. An IERI 2021 industrial analysis pointed out that after 2018, technological advancements and capacity expansion by Chinese panel manufacturers led to oversupply in the global market; data compiled in the paper showed that China's LCD panel production share reached 33.7% in 2018 and exceeded 50% in 2020, creating competitive pressure on Taiwan. These figures are historical analyses compiled by IERI in 2021 and cannot be used as market shares for 2026.[^8]

The same analysis also noted that Micro LED was still in the early stages of commercialization in 2021, with cost, mass transfer, and testing technology remaining challenges. In other words, the industry's shift to Micro LED is not just about finding a prettier screen material; it requires handling how millions of tiny light-emitting elements are precisely placed, tested, repaired, and maintained in yield across different sizes and applications.[^8]

This transition changed the question being asked. The problem during the LCD era was closer to "Can we build bigger factories and make more panels?"; the problem in the Micro LED era is "Can we integrate the chiplets, backplanes, transfer, testing, driving, materials, and applications into a deliverable system?" Taiwan's accumulated process and supply chain capabilities are therefore not just historical assets but necessary conditions for entering the next stage; however, they do not guarantee commercial success.

## Micro LED: Rewriting Panel Composition, Not Just Changing Backlighting

ITRI describes Micro LED technology with characteristics such as high brightness, high efficiency, fast response, and self-emission without a backlight. ITRI also states that this technology can be used in small-pitch displays, transparent displays, AR/MR, wearable devices, and automotive displays, and has integrated Micro LED into various substrates like glass, flexible substrates, silicon, and PCBs.[^4]

These applications are important because they no longer treat the panel merely as a flat surface. Curved surfaces in cars, store windows, museum display cases, and non-rectangular surfaces on wearable devices all require the display to handle brightness, transparency, shape, durability, and interactivity simultaneously. The display has transformed from a "component that outputs an image" into "part of an environment," extending industrial value from single panel price to the integration of design, control, sensing, content, and services.

ITRI's technological history shows that the Micro LED Alliance was established in 2016, the mass transfer of Micro LED to PCB was a technical breakthrough completed in 2020, and in 2022, they collaborated with 50 supply chain companies, including Innolux and Jorjin, to develop high-precision color Micro LED display modules for applications such as TVs, indoor displays, flexible automotive panels, flexible wearables, and smart glasses. This path shows that the development of Micro LED is not a single research outcome but a collective advancement driven by alliances, processes, and applications.[^1]

## Panel Manufacturers Start Selling "Scenarios"

AUO stated on its Micro LED technology page that Micro LED can mass transfer millions of micro LEDs onto LTPS backplanes for use in automotive, wearable, transparent windows, and foldable displays. The 14.6-inch foldable Micro LED display listed on AUO's page has a folding hinge radius of 4 mm and a pixel density of 202 ppi. These are descriptions of AUO's technology and products; this article treats them as corporate capability claims, not independent testing conclusions.[^5]

Innolux, on its official technology page, stated that its Micro LED centers on LTPS backplane driving technology, focusing on color conversion, selective mass transfer, mass repair, seamless tiling, and direct patterning. The company categorizes applications into smart entertainment, smart mobility, smart healthcare, and smart retail. These technical terms indicate that the competition in the panel industry has expanded beyond "can the screen light up" to whether the process can be stably replicated, if the module can be tiled, and if the product can enter different environments.[^9]

In September 2025, the Taiwan Display Industry Federation reported a Micro LED application event co-hosted by the Information Technology Industry Association (TDUA) and the Federation: five domestic companies shared their work, and 22 potential demand companies participated in matchmaking. The participating fields included displays, retail, amusement, healthcare, mobility, and system integration. The exhibits mentioned were not just screens but combinations of applications such as transparent displays, naked-eye 3D, mirror displays, adjustable transparency, and AI mass transfer.[^6]

This reveals a subtle but important shift: panel manufacturers are attempting to sell "scenarios," not just "panels." A transparent window requires the display, optical structure, and retail context to work together; smart automotive needs the display to match car curves, sunlight, and user interfaces; medical displays require reliability and workflow to be defined in one product. Technology only becomes an industry when it enters an environment.

## The Direction for 2026: Beyond the Panel Factory

A Micro-LED industrialization review published in _Nanomaterials_ in 2025 pointed out that standardized Micro LED production processes are still lacking in the industry, and major manufacturers adjust equipment and materials based on product applications. The study recorded that most displays before 2021 were smaller than 10 inches; while there were tiled displays exceeding 100 inches from 2022 to 2024, single panels were still around 12 inches, and AUO displayed a 42-inch panel made with the fourth generation substrate in 2025. This study clarified the difficulty of "environmental integration": the next stage for displays is not just screen size but whether processes, materials, and costs can form a repeatable industrial process.[^7]

Therefore, panel-level technology is being re-examined: large-area precision coating, thin films, materials, and packaging capabilities may find markets outside of displays. This does not mean Taiwan has successfully entered all new fields, but it means the next question for the display industry has expanded from competing on single product specifications to whether equipment, processes, and system solutions can be delivered together.[^7]

```tw-versus
Scale Competition | Environmental Integration
Large-format LCD production lines | Micro LED, e-paper, transparent displays, and automotive applications
Core focus on shipment area and production efficiency | Core focus on integration of process, materials, equipment, software/hardware, and services
Product is a panel module | Product is a solution that can enter retail, transportation, medical, or public spaces
```

## Can This Pivot Be Stable?

The Taiwanese display industry has proven itself once: government research institutes can develop display technology, companies can turn it into production lines, and supply chains can transform a single panel into an entire industry. The world first saw this path in 2002.[^1]

But the next competition will not merely repeat that history. LCD's scale war allowed China to gain greater capacity; Taiwan cannot answer by simply increasing glass substrate area; Micro LED also has challenges like mass transfer, testing, cost, and yield, which IERI's 2021 analysis clearly outlined.[^8] Therefore, the real question is not whether Taiwan can declare "re-leading" one day, but whether it can integrate existing panels, LEDs, semiconductors, equipment, and systems into a product that customers are willing to adopt long-term.

The five sharing companies and 22 demand companies from the 2025 event, combined with the 42-inch display recorded in the academic review and the non-standardized production processes, reveal an industry landscape still under assembly, not a completed declaration of victory.[^6] [^7] The next chapter of Taiwanese displays may not be written by one large panel, but by a group of companies that can make transparent windows, car curves, e-paper signs, medical interfaces, and new packaging truly function.
