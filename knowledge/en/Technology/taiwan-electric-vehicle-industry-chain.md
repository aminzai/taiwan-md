# Development of Taiwan's Electric Vehicle Industry Chain

## 30-Second Overview

Taiwan's electric vehicle (EV) industrial chain encompasses a complete ecosystem spanning from battery materials and components to system integration. Driven by the government's 2050 Net Zero transition policy, there is a target for EV adoption in public transport by 2030, with a priority on public transit. Taiwan has established a comprehensive charging and swapping network for electric motorcycles, and its fast-charging standards have been incorporated into international IEC standards. Industry advantages are concentrated in semiconductors, battery materials, and motor control systems; however, breakthroughs in complete vehicle manufacturing and cell technology are still required, which are being accelerated through international cooperation and technology transfer.

**Keywords**: EV Industrial Chain, Battery Technology, Charging Infrastructure, Net Zero Transition, Vehicle Electrification

## Why It Is Important

The development of the EV industry holds multiple strategic significances for Taiwan. The transportation sector accounts for 14% of Taiwan's greenhouse gas emissions; electrification is a primary path to carbon reduction and a key component of the 2050 Net Zero goal. For the industry, shifting to the EV supply chain means transitioning from traditional auto parts manufacturing to high-value electronic system integration, while also reducing reliance on imported petroleum.

- **Air Quality Improvement**: The zero-emission characteristics of EVs help improve urban air quality.
- **International Competitiveness**: Seizing opportunities in the global EV market and maintaining manufacturing advantages.
- **Job Creation**: Related industries are estimated to create 200,000 jobs.

## Global Electric Vehicle Market Trends

### Rapid Market Growth

The global EV market surged from 3.1 million units sold in 2020 to 14.2 million units in 2023, nearly quintupling over three years with a Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) of 35%. China leads the global market with a 60% share, while the EU and the United States rank second and third, respectively.

**Major Market Distribution (2023)**:

China accounted for 8.5 million units, holding 60% of the global EV market; the EU followed with 3.1 million units (22% share); the US was third with 1.5 million units (11%); other regions such as Japan, South Korea, and India totaled approximately 1 million units.

### Technological Development Trends

The evolution of EV technology covers three dimensions—battery chemistry, autonomous driving, and business models—which are accelerating convergence.

**Battery Technology Breakthroughs**:

Lithium iron phosphate batteries (LFP) have seen a 70% reduction in cost; the commercialization timeline for solid-state batteries is advancing concurrently; fast-charging technology has been reduced from 30 minutes to 15 minutes, with battery density reaching 300 Wh/kg.

**Autonomous Driving Integration**:

Level 3 autonomous driving has entered commercialization, leading to deep integration between EVs and intelligent driving systems, with Vehicle-to-Everything (V2X) technology becoming mature.

**Business Model Innovation**:

Three new business models—Battery as a Service, Charging as a Service (CaaS), and Mobility as a Service (MaaS)—are being implemented in various markets, changing traditional car purchasing and usage habits.

## Taiwan's EV Industry Layout

### Complete Industrial Ecosystem

Starting from the foundation of semiconductor manufacturing, Taiwan has built a complete EV industrial chain that covers upstream key materials, midstream component systems, and downstream vehicle manufacturing.

#### Upstream: Key Materials

**Battery Materials**:

Cathode materials are supplied by ProLogium and Lichi-KY; anode materials come from Formosa Plastics and Changxing Materials; electrolytes are provided by Shangwei and Chemico; separator membranes are handled by Mingji Materials and Huaxia Haiwan.

**Rare Earth Materials**:

Taiwan has an advantage in rare earth processing, with Taiwan Magnetics Technology being a major domestic supplier of permanent magnetic materials, establishing supply chain partnerships with Japan and Australia to diversify raw material risks.

#### Midstream: Components and Systems

**Battery Systems**:

Battery cells are primarily handled by ProLogium (a leader in solid-state battery technology); Battery Management Systems (BMS) are managed by Xinpu and Sunda; battery packaging is completed through system integration by Tainan Electric and Feihong Technology.

**Motor and Control Systems**:

Motors are supplied by Tainan Electric, Delta Electronics, and Datong; inverters are handled by Tainan Electric and Shihlin Electric; controllers are provided by Guangbao Technology and Wistron.

**Power Electronics**:

Power semiconductors are supplied by TSMC, United Ocean Semiconductor, and World Advanced; charging chips are developed by MediaTek and Richtek; power management ICs are mainly supplied by Richtek and Modtron.

#### Downstream: Vehicle Manufacturing

**Electric Motorcycles**:

Gogoro leads the market with its globally leading battery swapping system; KYMCO competes with the Ionex EV platform; Sanyang and Tainan Electric collaborate on development, while Chintail launches the eMOVING series.

**Electric Cars and Buses**:

In passenger vehicles, the Yung Leong Group utilizes the Najijie electric brand; Foxconn (Hon Hai) launched three models: Model C (sedan), Model E (SUV), and Model T (bus); Chintail also has commercial EVs like e-moving. Electric buses are supplied by Huade Dynamics and Tangrong Vehicles, while Kaishang Green Energy collaborates with BMW to import technology.

### Foxconn's EV Layout

Foxconn's comprehensive layout in the EV sector covers three levels—platform, product, and vertical integration—with the MIH open platform at its core, attracting ecosystem partners.

The MIH EV Open Platform was established in 2020, with over 2,000 companies joining the alliance[^9], serving as a core tool for Foxconn to build an ecosystem by lowering the barrier to entry for EV development through open hardware and software platforms.

Among the three mass-produced models, Model C is a luxury sedan (with a range of 700 km); Model E is a luxury SUV (mass-produced in 2024); and Model T is an electric bus (which has entered operational testing).

**Vertical Integration Strategy**:

Foxconn achieves vertical integration across three critical layers: batteries (cooperating with CATL to establish Taiwanese battery factories), semiconductors (supplying vehicle chips via TSMC), and software (developing vehicle operating systems and autonomous driving technology). In international cooperation, the company has established joint ventures with Stellantis and set up production bases in Indonesia, while also negotiating EV collaborations with Saudi Arabia.

## 2030 Vehicle Electrification Policy[^3]

### Net Zero Transition Strategy

Taiwan's path to Net Zero by 2050 designates vehicle electrification as a core strategy, advancing in three stages: 35% of public transport vehicles electrified by 2030, 100% new passenger cars being electric by 2040, and net-zero emissions for the transportation sector by 2050.

**Policy Goals**:

The phased goals are: 35% electrification of public transport vehicles by 2030; 100% electrification of newly purchased buses; 100% new passenger cars being electric by 2040; and net-zero emissions for the transportation sector by 2050.

The promotion strategy prioritizes public transport as a demonstration, coupled with infrastructure pre-deployment, localization of key industrial technologies, and improvement of the related legal environment.

### 2030 Public Transport Electrification Plan

The government has formulated separate plans for public transit, taxis, and freight to incentivize fleet replacement through subsidies:

For electric buses, the 2030 target is 8,000 vehicles, with a maximum subsidy of NT$10 million per vehicle, plus five years of operational cost subsidies and dedicated charging stations. The target for electric taxis in 2030 is 20,000 units, with purchase subsidies ranging from NT$1 to NT$1.5 million per vehicle, along with preferential rates and platform support for public charging stations. Freight vehicle electrification prioritizes light trucks, aiming for 30% by 2030, with demonstration projects in cooperation with the logistics industry focusing on last-mile delivery in urban areas.

### Charging Infrastructure Construction

The density and convenience of charging infrastructure directly influence consumer willingness to purchase. Taiwan currently lacks sufficient public charging stations, necessitating massive expansion between 2025 and 2030:

The construction target is 7,200 public charging stations by 2025 and 25,000 by 2030; fast-charging stations must be located every 20 kilometers on highways, with urban density reaching 2–3 stations per square kilometer.

Charging stations are categorized by scenario: DC fast chargers are placed on highways, expressways, and commercial areas; AC slow chargers cover residential communities, office buildings, and parking lots; ultra-fast charging is deployed along major trunk roads and transportation hubs; wireless charging is being tested in demonstration zones and specific road sections.

**Charging Service Operation Models**:

Taipower leads the construction of the basic charging network, while private operators are encouraged to invest in operations. Convenience is enhanced through composite services (combining convenience stores and gas stations), and smart management systems provide real-time information, reservation services, and dynamic pricing.

## Battery Technology Development

### Battery Technology Roadmap

Taiwan's battery technology development covers multiple technological routes:

#### Lithium Iron Phosphate Batteries (LFP)

**Technical Features**:

- High safety and long cycle life.
- Lower cost, suitable for large vehicles.
- Good high-temperature performance.

**Taiwanese Companies**:

- Lichi-KY: Cathode material supplier.
- ProLogium Materials: Battery cell manufacturing.
- Tainan Electric: Battery system integration.

#### Nickel Manganese Cobalt Batteries (NCM/NCA)

**Technical Features**:

- High energy density and light weight.
- Suitable for passenger car applications.
- Excellent fast-charging performance.

**Development Challenges**:

- Requires cobalt, which is expensive.
- Thermal runaway risk needs control.
- Cycle life is relatively shorter.

#### Solid-State Batteries

**Technical Advantages**:

- Energy density up to 400 Wh/kg.
- Significantly improved safety.
- Supports fast charging.

**Taiwanese Leading Technology**:

ProLogium (Huineng Technology) is a global leader in solid-state battery technology, with the Dunkirk factory in France scheduled for mass production in 2028[^1] (not 2026); construction is currently underway. In 2022, a cooperation agreement was signed with Mercedes-Benz and secured investment from Formosa Plastics and China Development Investment.

### Battery Industry Chain Challenges

**Technical Challenges**:

Battery cell manufacturing technology represents the most obvious gap between Taiwan and major manufacturers in China, Japan, and South Korea; economies of scale are yet to be established.

**Supply Chain Risks**:

Key raw materials such as lithium, cobalt, and nickel are highly dependent on imports. Price fluctuations combined with geopolitical risks create hidden concerns regarding supply stability. Solutions include strengthening international technological cooperation, establishing strategic raw material reserves, and developing battery recycling technology to reduce raw material dependence.

## Successful Model of Electric Motorcycles

### Gogoro Ecosystem

Taiwan holds a leading position globally in the electric motorcycle market; Gogoro's swapping model is particularly unique: it replaces "battery sales" with "Energy as a Service," allowing users not to bear the risk of battery depreciation.

**Innovative Business Model**:

Gogoro replaced traditional battery sales with "Energy as a Service": users pay monthly fees and use fully charged battery packs at GoStations, thus avoiding battery depreciation risks. Gogoro also partners with motorcycle manufacturers like KYMCO and Sanyang through an open platform strategy to share swapping infrastructure.

**Market Achievements**:

Gogoro holds over 90% of the market share in Taiwan's electric motorcycle sector; it has more than 2,500 swapping locations (as of 2024)[^2], performing over 400,000 battery exchanges daily, with approximately 650,000 Gogoro riders (as of 2024)[^2].

**Technical Standardization**:

Gogoro's fast-charging standards have been certified by the IEC international standard[^5], and battery specifications are open to other manufacturers, promoting alignment of Taiwan's electric motorcycle industry standards with international norms.

**International Expansion**:

Gogoro has entered markets in India, China, and Israel, exporting its swapping system through technology licensing models and establishing local networks in cooperation with local governments.

### Industrial Chain Completeness

The maturity of Taiwan's electric motorcycle industrial chain is considered advanced within the Asian market; there are local companies covering complete vehicles, components, and charging/swapping systems.

Taiwan's EV supply chain is highly complete: main manufacturers include Gogoro, KYMCO, Sanyang, and Yamasa; annual production capacity exceeds 500,000 units, with some exports to European and American markets. In terms of key components, motors are supplied by Delta and Datong, controllers by Tainan Electric and Guangbao, and batteries from Xinpu and Sunda. The charging/swapping ecosystem is supported by charging equipment from Tainan Electric and Feihong, the Gogoro Network operating platform, and a nationwide maintenance service network.

## Industrial Policies and Support Measures

Taiwan's government policies for EVs cover regulatory, financial, and industrial aspects. Market demand is driven through subsidies and demonstration projects, while local supply chains are nurtured to achieve mass production capabilities. Public transport electrification serves as the initial demonstration, driving technology verification and economies of scale.

### Government Promotion Strategies

The legal environment includes the enactment of the "Electric Vehicle Development Act," charging facility standards, battery recycling management systems, and vehicle safety certification norms. In terms of financial incentives, passenger car purchases receive subsidies up to NT$90,000, motorcycles receive NT$7,200, and they benefit from a 5-year reduction in license tax and exemption from fuel tax, plus preferential rates at public parking lots. Industrial support is multifaceted, including the EV industry development plan, key technology R&D subsidies, industrial cluster establishment, and talent cultivation programs.

### R&D Innovation Support

The R&D support system operates on two levels: government science projects (EV plans from the Ministry of Economic Affairs, Advanced Technology R&D from the Ministry of Science and Technology, civilian application of defense technology from CAS, technology transfer from ITRI) and industry-academia cooperation (university EV curricula, corporate internships, international technological exchange, patent layout). The verification side is provided by the Vehicle Research and Testing Center (ARTC), which offers battery testing, charging facility inspection, and intelligent driving test fields, filling the certification gap between R&D and mass production.

## International Cooperation and Investment

### Foreign Investment in Taiwan

**Major Investment Cases**:

Taiwanese factories are deeply embedded in the Tesla supply chain: Waseda Industries supplies drive shafts, Tainan Electric provides charging pile equipment, Quanta handles autonomous driving computers, and TSMC undertakes vehicle chip manufacturing. European automakers also participate; BMW collaborates with Kaishang Green Energy on electric buses, Mercedes-Benz signs a solid-state battery cooperation agreement with ProLogium, and Stellantis partners with Foxconn for production. Japanese automakers are also actively expanding: Toyota promotes EV cooperation through Wasei Motors, Honda cooperates with Sanyang, and Nissan established a battery testing center in Taiwan.

### Overseas Investment by Taiwanese Companies

Taiwanese overseas expansion focuses on two axes: Southeast Asia and the United States. In Southeast Asia, Foxconn has established an EV production base in Indonesia, Gogoro entered the Indian market, and Tainan Electric set up a charging equipment factory in Thailand. The US market is pursued simultaneously through Tainan Electric's charging network construction, Quanta's autonomous driving R&D center establishment, and Waseda's electric vehicle parts manufacturing plant.

## Technological Innovation and R&D

### Key Technology Development

**Automotive Semiconductors**:

Taiwan holds a global leading advantage in automotive chips: TSMC provides advanced processes for vehicle chips; MediaTek is responsible for in-vehicle infotainment chips; Richtek specializes in automotive Ethernet chips; and Richtek supplies power management chips.

**Intelligent Driving Technology**:

ITRI leads autonomous driving system R&D, the Information Commission handles V2X communication technology, Foxconn launches an open platform for self-driving vehicles, and Quanta provides AI computing platforms—the four organizations divide labor to complete the full technological stack.

**Lightweighting Materials**:

Formosa Plastics supplies carbon fiber composites; China Steel provides high-strength steel; Taiwan Chemical and Nanya are responsible for engineering plastics and composite materials, respectively, jointly supporting the lightweighting needs of EVs.

### Innovative Application Models

**V2X Integration**:

Bidirectional charging (V2G) turns EVs into energy storage nodes for the grid. V2X communication, smart traffic system integration, and remote diagnostics upgrade EVs from mere transportation tools to intelligent mobility platforms.

**Integration with Sharing Economy**:

EV sharing services, battery sharing networks, and charging pile sharing platforms are being tested in urban areas, aiming to reduce private car dependency through Mobility as a Service (MaaS).

## Challenges and Solutions

### Major Development Challenges

The challenges facing Taiwan's EV industry span technology, market, and supply chain, all interconnected. The gap in battery cell manufacturing and insufficient experience in complete vehicle design raise the barrier to entry for the passenger car market; market limitations hinder the rapid establishment of economies of scale, which affects cost competitiveness.

Technical challenges are mainly concentrated in four areas: battery cells (still lagging behind international giants), complete vehicle design (lacking comprehensive model development experience), autonomous driving (AI algorithms and sensor technology need improvement), and breakthroughs in fast charging/wireless charging technology.

Market challenges include the relatively small domestic market size, the technological and cost advantages held by Chinese and European/American companies, insufficient charging network density, and the barrier of consumer purchasing costs and habit conversion.

In terms of the supply chain, reliance on imports for key raw materials like lithium, cobalt, and nickel, a lack of large-scale production experience, high requirements for automotive-grade certification, and the failure to establish economies of scale mean that cost control pressure persists.

### Solution Strategies

**Technological Breakthrough Strategy**:

Strengthening international technological cooperation and licensing, and establishing vehicle chip design centers are short-term priorities. Investing in next-generation technologies like solid-state batteries and developing Taiwan's unique advantages are medium-to-long-term directions.

**Market Development Strategy**:

Focusing on Southeast Asia as a key overseas market and building supply chain partnerships with international automakers. Niche markets such as electric motorcycles and electric buses are the most advantageous entry points for Taiwan, allowing the establishment of brand recognition.

**Industrial Ecosystem Construction**:

The construction of EV industrial clusters, expansion of charging infrastructure, improvement of battery recycling systems, and cultivation of related professional talent—all four elements are indispensable to determine the long-term competitiveness of Taiwan's EV ecosystem.

## Future Development Outlook

### 2030 Industry Vision

The industry scale target for 2030 is NT$3 trillion in output value, 200,000 jobs, and a 30% EV penetration rate. Technological milestones include the commercialization of solid-state batteries, maturity of Level 3 autonomous driving, charging time reduced to 10 minutes, and range reaching 600 km. In terms of international positioning, the goal is to become an important supplier base for APAC EVs and establish a Taiwanese technological brand through the export of electric motorcycle technology and participation in international standard setting.

### Emerging Technology Trends

**Solid-State Battery Commercialization**:

Solid-state batteries are expected to exceed 400 Wh/kg post-mass production, with charging times reduced to 15 minutes, and significantly better safety than traditional liquid electrolyte solutions. The progress of ProLogium's French factory is a key indicator for observing the timeline of solid-state battery commercialization in Taiwan.

**Hydrogen Fuel Cells and Smart Charging**:

Hydrogen fuel cells are mainly targeted at commercial vehicles and long-haul transport as a supplement to pure EVs, requiring the establishment of a hydrogen supply chain. In terms of smart charging technology, wireless charging commercialization, dynamic charging roads, solar charging stations, and integration with smart grids are being tested in demonstration areas and are expected to enter mainstream markets sequentially after 2030.

### Policy Evolution Directions

Policy evolution covers two aspects: regulatory refinement (autonomous driving regulations, mandatory battery recycling norms, standardization of charging, data privacy protection) and deepening international cooperation (participating in international EV alliances, establishing technological partnerships with sister nations, promoting internationalization of technologies, and building supply chain security mechanisms), providing institutional support for long-term industry development.

## Conclusion

Taiwan's EV industry stands at a critical turning point. Leveraging its existing advantages in semiconductors, precision machinery, and information and communication technology, Taiwan has the opportunity to occupy an important position in the global EV supply chain. However, facing intense international competition, Taiwan must accelerate technological innovation, expand market size, improve infrastructure, and deepen international cooperation.

The government's 2030 vehicle electrification policy established phased goals, and private enterprises have invested accordingly in R&D and factory construction. From Gogoro's electric motorcycle battery swapping model exported overseas to Foxconn attracting over 2,000 companies with the MIH platform, Taiwan is attempting to transform its existing manufacturing capabilities into a competitive advantage for the EV era.

In the next decade, whether Taiwan's EV industry can secure its position as an APAC supply chain hub depends on the mass production of solid-state batteries, the advancement of vehicle chip design, and the pace of charging infrastructure development.
