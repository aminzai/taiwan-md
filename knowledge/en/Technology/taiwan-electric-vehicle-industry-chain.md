# Development of Taiwan's Electric Vehicle Industry Chain

## 30-Second Overview

Taiwan's electric vehicle industry chain encompasses a complete ecosystem spanning battery materials, components, and system integration. Driven by the government's 2050 carbon neutrality policy, with public transportation prioritized first, the focus is on promoting the electrification of urban buses and government vehicles by 2030. Taiwan has established a comprehensive charging and battery-swapping network in the electric motorcycle sector, with its fast-charging standards even included in IEC international standards. Industry strengths are concentrated in semiconductors, battery materials, and motor control systems, but breakthroughs are still needed in complete vehicle manufacturing and battery cell technology, which are being accelerated through international cooperation and technology transfer.

**Keywords**: electric vehicle industry chain, battery technology, charging infrastructure, carbon neutrality transformation, vehicle electrification

## Why It Matters

The development of the electric vehicle industry holds multiple strategic significances for Taiwan. The transportation sector accounts for 14% of Taiwan's greenhouse gas emissions, and electrification is the main path to carbon reduction, as well as a key component of the 2050 carbon neutrality goal. For the industry, shifting to the electric vehicle supply chain means moving from traditional automotive parts processing to high-value-added electronic system integration, while reducing dependence on imported oil.

- **Air Quality Improvement**: The zero-emission characteristics of electric vehicles help improve urban air quality
- **International Competitiveness**: Seizing global electric vehicle market opportunities and maintaining manufacturing advantages

## Global Electric Vehicle Market Trends

### Rapid Market Growth

According to the International Energy Agency (IEA), global electric vehicle sales in 2023 approached 14 million units, an increase of 3.5 million units from the previous year, representing a 35% year-on-year growth[^13]. China dominates the global market with approximately 60% of sales, while Europe and the United States rank second and third respectively.

**Major Market Distribution (2023)**:

China approximately 8.1 million units (about 60%), Europe nearly 3.2 million units (about 25%) in second place, and the United States approximately 1.4 million units (about 10%) in third place[^13].

### Technology Development Trends

The evolution of electric vehicle technology spans three dimensions: battery chemistry, autonomous driving, and business models, which are converging with each other.

**Battery Technology Breakthroughs**:

Lithium iron phosphate (LFP) battery costs have dropped by 70%, and the commercialization timeline for solid-state batteries is accelerating simultaneously; fast-charging technology has shortened from 30 minutes to 15 minutes, with battery energy density reaching 300Wh/kg.

**Autonomous Driving Integration**:

L3-level autonomous driving has entered commercialization, with electric vehicles deeply integrated with intelligent driving systems, and vehicle-to-everything (V2X) technology becoming mature.

**Business Model Innovation**:

Battery rental, charging-as-a-service (CaaS), and mobility-as-a-service (MaaS) are three new business models being implemented in multiple markets, changing traditional car-buying and usage habits.

## Taiwan's Electric Vehicle Industry Chain Layout

### Complete Industry Ecosystem

Starting from semiconductor manufacturing foundations, Taiwan has built a complete electric vehicle industry chain covering upstream key materials, midstream components and systems, to downstream complete vehicle manufacturing.

#### Upstream: Key Materials

**Battery Materials**:

Cathode materials include Li-Cheng KY, and separator films are produced by Ming Kee Materials.

#### Midstream: Components and Systems

**Battery Systems**:

Battery cells are led by ProLogium (solid-state battery technology leader), battery management systems (BMS) are handled by Hsin Po and Shun Da, and battery packaging is completed by Delta Electronics and Phoenix Golden Technology.

**Motors and Control Systems**:

Motors are supplied by Delta Electronics, Dong Yuan Electric, and Da Tong; inverters are handled by Delta Electronics and Shilin Electric; controllers are provided by Lite-On Technology and Wistron.

**Power Electronics**:

Power semiconductors are supplied by TSMC, UMC, and World Advanced; charging chips are developed by MediaTek and Realtek; power management ICs are mainly supplied by Lite-On and Midea.

#### Downstream: Complete Vehicle Manufacturing

**Electric Motorcycles**:

Gogoro leads the market with its globally advanced battery-swapping system; KYMCO (Gogoro) competes with the Ionex electric vehicle platform; and China Motor Co. launches the eMOVING series.

**Electric Cars and Electric Buses**:

In the passenger car sector, the Yulon Group deploys the Luxgen electric vehicle brand; Foxconn launches Model C (SUV), Model E (sedan), and Model T (electric bus) three models[^14], and China Motor Co. also has e-moving commercial vehicles. Electric buses are supplied by companies such as Hwa De Motion, Tang Rong Vehicle, and Kai Sheng Green Energy.

### Foxconn's Electric Vehicle Deployment

Foxconn's comprehensive layout in the electric vehicle field covers three levels: platform, products, and vertical integration, with the MIH open platform as its core, attracting suppliers to join the ecosystem.

The MIH electric vehicle open platform was established in 2020, with over 2,000 suppliers joining the alliance[^9], using an open hardware and software platform to lower the threshold for electric vehicle development, serving as the core tool for Foxconn to build its ecosystem.

Among the three models, Model C is an SUV, Model E is a sedan, and Model T is an electric bus[^14].

**Vertical Integration Strategy**:

Foxconn attempts vertical integration of batteries, semiconductors, and software in the supply chain. In terms of international cooperation, it collaborates with the Indonesian government to establish production bases, and is negotiating electric vehicle cooperation with Saudi Arabia.

## 2030 Vehicle Electrification Policy[^3]

### Carbon Neutrality Transformation Strategy

Taiwan's 2050 carbon neutrality transformation lists "vehicle electrification and decarbonization" as one of the key strategies: prioritizing public transportation, leading by government example, focusing first on the electrification of urban buses and government vehicles by 2030, and setting a target for full electrification of market-available small passenger cars and motorcycles by 2040[^4].

**Policy Goals**:

The market share of electric small passenger cars should reach 30% by 2030, and electric motorcycles should reach 35%; by 2040, both should reach 100%[^4].

The promotion strategy prioritizes public transportation as a demonstration, combined with infrastructure-first approach, key technology localization in the industry chain, and improving relevant legal and regulatory environments.

### 2030 Passenger Vehicle Electrification Plan

The government provides subsidies for buses and taxis respectively[^4]:

Electric bus purchase subsidies are managed by the Ministry of Transportation starting from 2023, with a maximum of NT$3.7 million per vehicle for Class A and NT$3 million per vehicle for Class B, with additional operational subsidies provided by the Environmental Protection Administration. As of the end of 2022, there were only 464 electric taxis, accounting for about 0.5% of the approximately 90,000 taxis nationwide. The Ministry of Transportation promoted old taxi replacement subsidies in 2019, providing NT$350,000 per vehicle for replacing old taxis with electric vehicles, and approving an additional budget to subsidize 500 more electric taxis.

### Charging Infrastructure Construction

The density and convenience of charging infrastructure directly affect consumers' willingness to purchase electric vehicles. As of the end of 2022, Taiwan had approximately 8,174 public charging stations (6,172 AC slow chargers and 2,002 DC fast chargers), with the Ministry of Transportation planning to add 4,000 more AC slow chargers and 400 DC fast chargers by the end of 2025[^4].

Charging stations are assigned by scenario: DC fast-charging stations are located on highways, interchanges, and commercial districts; AC slow-charging stations cover residential communities, office buildings, and parking lots; ultra-fast charging is mainly deployed on main roads and transportation hubs; wireless charging is first tested in demonstration areas and specific road sections.

**Charging Service Operation Models**:

Taiwan Power Company leads the construction of basic charging networks, encouraging private operators to invest in operations, and using composite services (combining convenience stores and gas stations) to improve usability, with smart management systems providing real-time information, reservation services, and dynamic pricing.

## Battery Technology Development

### Battery Technology Routes

Taiwan's battery technology development covers multiple technical routes:

#### Lithium Iron Phosphate Batteries (LFP)

**Technical Features**:

- High safety, long cycle life
- Low cost, suitable for large vehicles
- Excellent high-temperature performance

**Taiwanese Manufacturers**:

- Li-Cheng KY: cathode material supplier
- Delta Electronics: battery system integration

#### Ternary Lithium Batteries (NCM/NCA)

**Technical Features**:

- High energy density, lightweight
- Suitable for passenger car applications
- Excellent fast-charging performance

**Development Challenges**:

- Requires cobalt, higher cost
- Thermal runaway risk needs management
- Relatively shorter cycle life

#### Solid-State Batteries

**Technical Advantages**:

- Energy density up to 400Wh/kg
- Significantly improved safety
- Supports fast charging

**Taiwan's Leading Technologies**:

ProLogium (Li-Cheng) invests in solid-state battery research and development, with the super battery factory in Dunkirk, France starting construction in February 2026, expected to begin small-scale trial production in 2028[^1]. In 2022, it already signed a cooperation agreement with Mercedes-Benz.

### Battery Industry Chain Challenges

**Technical Challenges**:

Battery cell manufacturing technology is where Taiwan's gap with South Korean and Japanese manufacturers is most obvious, and economies of scale in production capacity are yet to be established.

**Supply Chain Risks**:

Key raw materials such as lithium, cobalt, and nickel are highly dependent on imports, with price volatility and geopolitical risks forming concerns about supply stability. Solutions include strengthening international technical cooperation, establishing strategic raw material reserves, and developing battery recycling technologies to reduce raw material dependence.

## Electric Motorcycle Success Model

### Gogoro Ecosystem

Taiwan's electric motorcycle market holds a leading position globally, with Gogoro's battery-swapping model being particularly unique: replacing "battery sales" with "energy as a service," so users do not need to bear battery depreciation risks.

**Innovative Business Model**:

Gogoro replaces traditional battery sales with "energy as a service": users pay monthly fees, obtain fully charged battery packs through GoStation battery-swapping stations, and do not bear battery depreciation risks. Gogoro also uses an open platform strategy to cooperate with companies like KYMCO and San Yang, sharing the battery-swapping infrastructure.

**Market Results**:

Gogoro's battery-swapping network exceeds 2,500 locations (2024)[^2], with over 400,000 battery swaps per day, and approximately 650,000 Gogoro riders (2024)[^2].

**Technical Standardization**:

Taiwan's electric motorcycle fast-charging specifications have been included in international standards by the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC)[^6], and Gogoro opens its battery specifications to other car manufacturers, promoting alignment of Taiwan's electric motorcycle industry standards with international standards.

**International Expansion**:

Gogoro has entered markets such as India, China, and Israel, exporting the battery-swapping system through technology licensing models, and cooperating with local governments to establish local exchange networks.

### Industry Chain Completeness

Taiwan's electric motorcycle industry chain maturity ranks at the forefront of Asian markets, with complete vehicle, components, and charging/battery-swapping systems all covered by local manufacturers.

Taiwan's electric motorcycle supply chain is highly complete: complete vehicles are composed of Gogoro, KYMCO, San Yang, and Yamaha. Key components include motors supplied by Dong Yuan and Da Tong, controllers handled by Delta Electronics and Lite-On, and batteries from Hsin Po and Shun Da. The charging/battery-swapping ecosystem is supported by Delta Electronics and Phoenix Golden providing charging equipment, Gogoro Network operating the platform, and a nationwide maintenance service network.

## Industry Policies and Support Measures

Taiwan's government electric vehicle policies cover three aspects: regulations, finance, and industry, using subsidies and demonstration projects to stimulate market demand, while nurturing local supply chains to build mass production capabilities. Public transportation electrification is implemented first as a demonstration, driving technology verification and economies of scale.

### Government Promotion Strategies

Regulatory environment preparation includes charging facility installation standards, battery recycling management systems, and vehicle safety certification specifications. Financial incentives include extending the period for electric vehicle tax exemptions on commodity tax and license plates, with the Legislative Yuan passing the fifth reading in December 2025 to extend for five years until December 31, 2030[^15], and electric vehicles are additionally exempt from vehicle fuel usage fees, with some local governments providing parking fee or charging fee discounts for public parking lots[^4]. Industrial nurturing is carried out through multiple channels including the electric vehicle industry development plan, key technology research and development subsidies, industrial cluster establishment, and talent cultivation programs.

### Research and Development Innovation Support

The research and development support system covers two levels: government science and technology projects (Ministry of Economic Affairs's electric vehicle program, Ministry of Science and Technology's advanced technology research and development, National Defense University's defense technology transfer to civilian use, and Industrial Technology Research Institute's technology transfer) and industry-academia cooperation (university electric vehicle programs, corporate internships, international technology exchanges, and patent layouts). The verification end is provided by the Automotive Research and Testing Center (ARTC), offering battery testing, charging facility inspection, and intelligent driving test fields, filling the gap between research and development and mass production certification.

## International Cooperation and Investment

### Foreign Investment in Taiwan

**Major Investment Cases**:

Taiwanese manufacturers are deeply embedded in Tesla's supply chain: Andong Industry supplies drive shafts, Delta Electronics supplies charging station equipment, Gigabyte handles autonomous driving computers, and TSMC takes on automotive chip foundry orders. European car manufacturers: Mercedes-Benz signs a solid-state battery cooperation agreement with ProLogium. Japanese car manufacturers: Toyota promotes electric vehicle cooperation through Andai Motors.

### Overseas Investment by Taiwanese Companies

Taiwanese companies' overseas deployments focus on two axes: Southeast Asia and the United States. In Southeast Asia, Foxconn establishes electric vehicle production bases in Indonesia, Gogoro enters the Indian market, and Delta Electronics sets up charging equipment factories in Thailand. In the United States, Delta Electronics builds charging networks, Gigabyte establishes autonomous vehicle research centers, and Andong Industry sets up electric vehicle parts factories, all operating simultaneously.

## Technology Innovation and Research and Development

### Key Technology Development

**Automotive Semiconductors**:

Taiwan holds a globally leading advantage in automotive chips: TSMC provides advanced process automotive chips, MediaTek handles in-vehicle infotainment chips, Realtek specializes in automotive Ethernet chips, and Lite-On supplies power management chips.

**Intelligent Driving Technology**:

The Industrial Technology Research Institute leads autonomous driving system research and development, the Institute for Information Industry handles vehicle-to-everything communication technology, Foxconn launches autonomous vehicle open platforms, and Gigabyte provides AI computing platforms, with four institutions complementing each other to complete the technical chain.

**Lightweight Materials**:

Formosa Plastics supplies carbon fiber composite materials, China Steel provides high-strength steel materials, and Taiwan Chemical and Nan Ya respectively handle engineering plastics and composite materials, jointly supporting the lightweight needs of electric vehicles.

### Innovative Application Models

**Vehicle-to-Everything Integration**:

Bidirectional V2G charging makes electric vehicles energy storage nodes for the power grid, with V2X communication, smart transportation system integration, and remote diagnostic technologies further upgrading electric vehicles from transportation tools to intelligent mobile platforms.

**Sharing Economy Integration**:

Electric vehicle sharing services, battery sharing networks, and charging station sharing platforms are being tested in metropolitan areas, with the goal of reducing private car dependence through mobility-as-a-service (MaaS).

## Challenges and Solutions

### Main Development Challenges

Taiwan's electric vehicle industry faces challenges across three dimensions: technology, market, and supply chain, which are interconnected. On the technology side, the gap in battery cells and insufficient experience in complete vehicle design raise the threshold for entering the passenger car market; on the market side, limited domestic market size makes it difficult to quickly establish economies of scale, affecting cost competitiveness.

Technical challenges are mainly concentrated in battery cells (still lagging behind international giants), complete vehicle design (lacking complete vehicle development experience), autonomous driving (AI algorithms and sensor technologies need improvement), and breakthroughs in fast-charging/wireless charging technologies.

Market challenges include Taiwan's small domestic market size, technological and cost advantages of Chinese, European, and American manufacturers, insufficient charging network density, and consumer purchase costs and habit conversion thresholds.

In terms of supply chain, key raw materials such as lithium, cobalt, and nickel rely on imports, lacking large-scale production experience, high vehicle-grade certification thresholds, economies of scale not yet established, and continuous cost control pressure.

### Solution Strategies

**Technology Breakthrough Strategies**:

Strengthening international technical cooperation and licensing, establishing automotive chip design centers as short-term priorities, investing in next-generation technologies like solid-state batteries, and developing Taiwan's unique advantages as medium to long-term directions.

**Market Development Strategies**:

Focusing on Southeast Asia as the key overseas market, and establishing supply chain partnerships with international car manufacturers. Electric motorcycles and electric buses are niche markets where Taiwan has the strongest advantages, which can be used to build brand recognition.

**Industry Ecosystem Construction**:

The establishment of electric vehicle industry clusters, expansion of charging infrastructure, improvement of battery recycling and reuse systems, and cultivation of related professional talents are all essential and jointly determine the long-term competitiveness of Taiwan's electric vehicle ecosystem.

## Future Development Outlook

### 2030 Industry Vision

The 2030 targets that can be found in policies are: electric small passenger car market share of 30% and electric motorcycle 35%[^4]. In the industry, Taiwan's opportunity lies in becoming the supply base for Asia-Pacific electric vehicle components, and establishing its own technical brand through exporting electric motorcycle technologies and participating in international standard setting.

### Emerging Technology Trends

**Solid-State Battery Commercialization**:

After solid-state batteries achieve mass production, energy density can break through 400Wh/kg, charging time can be shortened to 15 minutes, and safety is significantly better than traditional liquid electrolyte solutions. ProLogium's French factory progress is a key indicator for observing the commercialization timeline of Taiwan's solid-state batteries.

**Hydrogen Fuel Cells and Smart Charging**:

Hydrogen fuel cells mainly target commercial vehicles and long-distance transportation markets, serving as a supplementary solution to pure electric vehicles, requiring the establishment of hydrogen supply chains. In terms of smart charging technology, commercialization of wireless charging, dynamic charging roads, solar charging stations, and smart grid integration are being tested in demonstration fields, expected to enter the mainstream market after 2030.

### Policy Evolution Directions

Policy evolution directions cover two aspects: regulatory improvement (autonomous driving regulations, mandatory battery recycling standards, charging standard unification, data privacy protection) and deepening international cooperation (participating in international electric vehicle alliances, establishing technical partnerships with friendly nations, promoting technology standard internationalization, and establishing supply chain security mechanisms), providing institutional support for long-term industry development.

## Conclusion

Taiwan's electric vehicle industry stands at a critical turning point. Leveraging existing advantages in semiconductors, precision machinery, and information and communication technology industries, Taiwan has the opportunity to occupy an important position in the global electric vehicle supply chain. However, facing fierce international competition, Taiwan must accelerate technological innovation, expand market scale, improve infrastructure, and deepen international cooperation.

The government's 2030 vehicle electrification policy has established phased goals, and private enterprises are investing in corresponding research and development and factory construction. From Gogoro's electric motorcycle battery-swapping model being exported overseas, to Foxconn's MIH platform attracting over 2,000 suppliers to join, Taiwan is attempting to transform its existing manufacturing capabilities into competitive advantages in the electric vehicle era.

Over the next ten years, whether Taiwan's electric vehicle industry can secure a pivotal position in the Asia-Pacific supply chain will depend on the mass production of solid-state batteries, automotive chip design capabilities, and the speed of charging infrastructure advancement.
