# Development of Taiwan's Software Industry

## 30-Second Overview

Taiwan has long been globally known for hardware manufacturing, but its software industry has been rapidly rising in recent years. From system integration and game development to AI applications, the Taiwanese software sector has transitioned from a "hardware OEM mindset" to a "software innovation model," finding a second trajectory in the digital economy era, supported by government policies.

According to the Ministry of Economic Affairs Statistics, the total operating revenue of the computer and information services industry in Taiwan reached approximately NT$570 billion in 2023, an 8.9% year-on-year increase, setting a new high[^3].

Keywords: Digital Transformation, Software Services, AI Applications, Gaming Industry, System Integration

## Why It Matters

As the global economy enters the digital age, the added value of pure hardware manufacturing has been squeezed; software and services have become the true creators of value. For Taiwan to transition from a "manufacturing giant" to an "innovation powerhouse," the software industry is the crucial battleground for this transformation.

The software industry possesses structural advantages that are difficult for the hardware sector to match: marginal costs approach zero, and business models can be rapidly replicated in international markets.

For Taiwan, the software industry represents:

- **Industrial Upgrading**: Moving from OEM production toward branding and innovation
- **Talent Value**: The culture of excellent Taiwanese engineers shines brightly in the software domain
- **Global Competitiveness**: Keeping pace with the international community in areas like AI, gaming, and FinTech

## Industry Status and Scale

### Overall Development Overview

According to the Ministry of Economic Affairs Statistics, Taiwan's computer and information services industry has shown stable growth in recent years:

- **Industry Scale**: Total operating revenue was approximately NT$570 billion in 2023, a record high[^3]
- **Growth Trend**: An increase of 8.9% in 2023[^3]

Compared to the traditional hardware industry, the software sector offers:

- Higher gross profit margins
- Greater scalability (marginal costs approach zero)
- More flexible business models (subscription services, cloud computing)

### Industry Structure Analysis

Taiwan's software industry is mainly divided into four major areas:

**1. System Integration Services (SI)**

- Represented by companies such as Jingcheng, Eiyang, and Zituo Hongyu
- Provides digital transformation services for traditional industries like finance, manufacturing, and government

**2. Game Development**

- Established game companies like Treasure and Rayplay
- Emerging mobile game studios such as Reia Games and Choku Games
- Annual output: PwC estimates the value of Taiwan's video game and esports industry in 2024 to be approximately NT$126 billion[^1]

**3. Enterprise Software Solutions**

- Enterprise management software like ERP, CRM, HRM
- Local vendors such as CITRON and Dingxin Computer
- Recently developing cloud-based and AI solutions

**4. Emerging Technology Applications**

- New technology applications such as AI/ML, blockchain, and IoT
- Collaboration between startups and research institutions
- Key focus areas of the government's "AI Taiwan" plan

## The Transition from OEM to Innovation

### Early Development (1980–2000)

The Taiwanese software industry began in the 1980s, with a primary model focused on:

- **Software Outsourcing**: Developing software for foreign vendors, similar to hardware OEM models
- **Localization Services**: Localizing foreign software, such as Microsoft Windows Chinese versions
- **System Integration**: Combining hardware and software to provide information systems for enterprises

The characteristic of this period was "technology orientation," emphasizing engineering implementation capabilities but lacking brand and innovation thinking.

### The Internet Era (2000–2010)

After the dot-com bubble, the Taiwanese software industry began searching for new development directions:

- **Rise of the Gaming Industry**: Online games became the first successful case for Taiwan's software sector
- **E-commerce Development**: Platforms like Yahoo Auctions and PChome emerged
- **Government Promotion**: The "Two Megabytes, Two Stars" policy designated digital content as a key industry.

### Mobile and Cloud Era (2010–2020)

The popularization of smartphones brought new opportunities:

- **Mobile Games**: Game companies began entering the mobile game market, such as Rayplay Network's _Resident Evil_ and Reia Games' _Cytus_
- **Cloud Transformation**: Traditional software vendors started developing SaaS services
- **Startup Ecosystem**: Accelerators like AppWorks nurtured startup teams.

### AI and Digital Transformation Era (2020–Present)

The pandemic accelerated digital transformation, leading the government to upgrade the "Digital Nation/Innovation Economy Development Plan" in 2021 to the "Smart Nation Plan":

- **AI Applications**: From predictive maintenance in manufacturing to medical image analysis
- **5G Services**: Innovative applications combining 5G and edge computing
- **ESG Software**: Solutions assisting enterprises in implementing sustainable operations

## Key Companies and Success Stories

The competitiveness of Taiwan's software industry is reflected across three dimensions: the digital transformation of traditional large system integrators, the international IP breakthroughs of the gaming industry, and the capital market achievements of AI startups.

These three dimensions collectively demonstrate one thing: the Taiwanese software industry has moved beyond serving only local markets, showing a genuine ability to establish global standing in specific niches.

The following representative cases showcase real results from different paths.

### Transformation of Traditional Giants

**Systex Corporation (Jingcheng)**

- Established in 1997[^2], starting from system integration
- Recently transformed into a "Digital Transformation Partner"
- Developed the "FinTech One-Stop Service Platform," assisting banks with digital transformation

**CITRON Computer (Zitong Diannao)**

- Holds the top market share for HRM software in Taiwan
- Successfully exported to markets such as Southeast Asia and China

### Gaming Industry Stars

**Reia Games**

- Internationally known for the music game series _Cytus_
- Insists on original IP, avoiding skin-swap games
- Its works have received international gaming awards, with fans worldwide

**Choku Games (Chizhu)**

- An independent studio famous for horror games
- _Huan Yuan_ showcases Taiwanese cultural characteristics and has received high international acclaim
- Represents the influence of Taiwanese independent games internationally

### AI Startup Representatives

**Appier Group**

- Founded in 2012, specializing in AI marketing technology
- Listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange in 2021, becoming a Taiwanese AI unicorn[^11]

**Taiwan AI Labs (Tâiwan AI Labs)**

- Founded by Du Yijin, focusing on medical AI applications
- Developed practical AI tools such as "Yateng Transcription"
- Collaborates with National Taiwan University Hospital to develop medical image AI systems

## Government Policies and Support

### From DIGI+ to the Smart Nation Plan

The Executive Yuan launched the "Digital Nation/Innovation Economy Development Plan (2017–2025)" (DIGI+ Plan) in 2016, which was renamed and upgraded to the "Smart Nation Plan (2021–2025)" starting in 2021, focusing on four main pillars[^5]:

1. **Digital Infrastructure**
2. **Digital Innovation**
3. **Digital Governance**
4. **Digital Inclusion**

### Asia-Silicon Valley Promotion Program

Centered on "IoT" and "Innovative Entrepreneurship":

- **Innovative Regulations**: Financial regulatory sandboxes, autonomous vehicle experiments, etc.
- **Capital Injection**: National Development Fund invests in startups; establishing angel investment schemes.
- **International Linkage**: Establishing cooperative relationships with innovation clusters like Silicon Valley.

### Taiwan AI Action Plan

The Executive Yuan promoted the four-year "Taiwan AI Action Plan" (2018–2021) starting in January 2018[^8]:

- **Talent Cultivation**: The government and private sector cooperated to train over ten thousand practical talents in AI technology and applications.
- **Industrial Application**: Promoting the introduction of AI into traditional industries.
- **R&D Capacity**: Establishing an AI Innovation Research Center at National Taiwan University, National Cheng Kung University, National Tsing Hua University, and National Chiao Tung University.

## Challenges and Opportunities

The challenges facing Taiwan's software industry mainly center on talent, market size, and funding, with the three elements having a mutually constraining relationship: lack of funding makes it difficult to attract talent, and a small market limits investor confidence.

The breakthrough lies in internationalization: reducing the talent gap requires a two-pronged approach—both enhancing salary competitiveness and creating more exportable software products.

However, demands from digital transformation, AI applications, and ESG software are rapidly opening up new opportunities.

### Major Challenges

**Talent Shortage**

- Salary levels still lag behind those in countries like the United States and Singapore, leading to the outflow of excellent talent overseas.

**Market Size Limitations**

- The domestic market in Taiwan is relatively small.
- Software companies must possess internationalization capabilities to grow strong.
- International barriers such as language, culture, and regulations exist.

**Difficulty in Securing Funding**

- Compared to hardware manufacturing, the software industry finds it harder to obtain bank financing.
- Although venture capital has increased, its scale is still not comparable to developed countries.
- The lack of large-scale software M&A cases means the exit mechanism is not yet sound.

### Future Opportunities

**Digital Transformation Wave**

- Corporate demand for digital transformation surged after the pandemic.
- Taiwan's software industry possesses deep practical experience in digitizing manufacturing.
- Taiwanese experience can be exported to other developing countries.

**AI and Emerging Technologies**

- Taiwan's advantages in semiconductors and precision manufacturing can be combined with AI.
- Areas like edge computing and AIoT are expected to become niches for Taiwan.
- The government actively promotes AI applications, providing good testing environments.

**Sustainability Demands**

- Issues such as ESG and carbon neutrality bring new software demands.
- Taiwan can develop solutions for sustainable management and carbon footprint tracking.
- Combining manufacturing experience, developing smart factory and green supply chain software.

## International Comparison and Positioning

In the Asia-Pacific software landscape, Taiwan's positioning is that of a "mid-sized professional market integrating hardware and software," rather than a large-scale outsourcing base (like India) or a platform ecosystem leader (like South Korea).

This positioning has inherent limitations but also possesses differentiated advantages that are difficult to replicate: in areas requiring hardware-software integration, such as IoT and smart manufacturing, Taiwan has practical experience that other regions struggle to quickly replicate.

The following comparisons with neighboring countries help clarify Taiwan's relative position.

### Comparison with Neighboring Countries

**vs South Korea**

- South Korea leads Taiwan in the gaming industry (e.g., NCSOFT, Nexon).
- Taiwan has opportunities to catch up in B2B software and AI applications.
- Both countries face competitive pressure from China and could consider cooperation.

**vs Singapore**

- Singapore excels in FinTech; Taiwan is stronger in manufacturing software.
- The Singaporean government invests heavily, requiring Taiwan to strengthen policy support.
- Singapore can be considered a springboard into Southeast Asia.

**vs India**

- India is known for software outsourcing; Taiwan focuses on high added-value applications.
- Taiwan's hardware integration capability is a differentiating advantage.
- Cooperation between the two could form a complementary relationship.

### Taiwan's Differentiated Positioning

**Hardware-Software Integration Advantage**

- Taiwan possesses both hardware manufacturing and software development capabilities.
- It has an advantage in areas requiring hardware-software integration, such as IoT and smart manufacturing.
- Can develop holistic solutions of "Taiwan Manufacturing + Taiwanese Software."

**Chinese Culture Market**

- Taiwanese software is highly adaptable to Chinese culture and Asian markets.
- It can serve as a springboard for international software companies entering the Asian market.
- Serves the markets of overseas Chinese in cross-strait regions, Hong Kong/Macau, and Southeast Asia.

**Refined Innovation Culture**

- The Taiwanese software industry emphasizes user experience and product detail.
- It demonstrates unique aesthetics in areas like gaming and creative software.
- Aligns with the trend of future software industries emphasizing design and experience.

## Outlook and Future Development

Taiwan's software industry is at a critical turning point. Under global digital economy trends, Taiwan has the opportunity to upgrade from a "hardware kingdom" to a "digital innovation powerhouse."

The core contradiction in this transformation is that while Taiwan has deep hardware thinking, software requires a completely different business logic.

The future of Taiwan's software industry depends on its ability to innovate and break through while retaining existing advantages. By learning from the success experience of hardware OEM, but not being bound by past thinking, the Taiwanese software industry will surely find its own place in the digital age.
